Sosrokartono’s Wisdom and the
Transformative Role of Educators

Sosrokartono’s century-old vision of the teacher as a life guide exposes a structural conflict in modern academia — publish-or-perish incentives and accreditation burdens pull professors away from exactly the mentorship role that made education transformative in the first place.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
A lecturer in industrial entrepreneurship at the School of Electrical Engineering and Informatics at the Bandung Institute of Technology and a Harvard Business School graduate.
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
01  ·  Introduction

A curriculum reform meets a declining PISA trend.

Indonesia’s Merdeka Curriculum aims to empower teachers and foster creativity — yet the numbers tell a more sobering story.

Situation

The government’s Merdeka Curriculum reflects a commendable forethought for educational reform, focused on empowering teachers and schools toward creativity and independence.

Complication

PISA results reveal a concerning trend, particularly in mathematics, where scores have notably declined from 2006 to 2022 — despite the reform’s intentions.

Question

What is missing between the reform’s intent and its results — and what does genuinely transformative teaching actually require?

Answer

Drs. RMP Sosrokartono’s century-old teachings frame the teacher as a life guide mastering both “science” and “action” — an ideal that modern academic incentives, focused on research output and administrative compliance, are structurally undermining.

02  ·  Gap

Reform targets curricula, not the teacher-student bond itself.

The advanced structures of education are built upon a relationship that current metrics do not measure.

Core Gap

Acknowledging the essential bond between guru and student, the piece asks whether educators are truly attuned to their fundamental mission — because the advanced structures of education are, after all, built upon this core relationship. The Merdeka Curriculum addresses structure and curricular freedom, but the deeper erosion of mentorship itself, driven by publish-or-perish and accreditation pressures, remains unaddressed.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
03  ·  Framework

Three Forces Shaping the Modern Teacher

Each force pulls in a different direction; together they explain the gap between Sosrokartono’s ideal and today’s classroom.

01
Cipta, Rasa, Karsa

Sosrokartono’s “The Teacher” defines mastery of “science” and “action” — creative force, sense, and will — as the teacher’s core, ensuring teachings are reflected in their own actions.

02
The Publish-or-Perish Collision

The prevalent research-output culture places undue emphasis on publication for career survival, often overshadowing the intrinsic value of teaching and genuine mentorship.

03
Real-World Experience as Pedagogy

Educators who have navigated the labor market or diverse professions bring practical wisdom and empathy that bridge theoretical knowledge with real-world relevance — a dimension textbooks alone cannot supply.

04  ·  Details

Three Cases That Ground the Argument

A classroom philosophy, a platform-absence observation, and the emeritus-faculty model each illustrate the mentorship ideal in practice or in its absence.

The fearless psychology classroom

A contemporary interpretation of Sosrokartono’s mutual respect between teacher and student, where judgment is suspended and free thought encouraged — requiring educators to embody humility and a willingness to learn alongside students.

The sparse presence of educators on LinkedIn

The limited engagement of Indonesian teachers on professional networking platforms is read as a missed opportunity to embody Sosrokartono’s vision of the educator as an ever-present, community-engaged guide.

Emeritus faculty as living ideal

Retired professors who continue advocating for foundational learning areas like early childhood education, out of genuine love for knowledge rather than institutional obligation, exemplify Sosrokartono’s ideals in practice.

05  ·  Evidence

Three Sources Behind the Argument

A national assessment data point and two conceptual anchors ground the piece’s claims.

EVIDENCE A
PISA results, 2006–2022

The Programme for International Student Assessment shows a notable decline in Indonesian mathematics scores over this period, cited as the sobering counterpoint to reform optimism.

EVIDENCE B
Sosrokartono’s “The Teacher”

Drs. RMP Sosrokartono’s writing frames the teacher’s guiding character as central to both the theoretical and practical facets of “science” and “action.”

EVIDENCE C
The Merdeka Curriculum

The government’s Independent Curriculum reform is the concrete policy backdrop against which the piece measures the persistence of Sosrokartono’s ideal.

EVIDENCE D
The “ivory tower” critique

The piece invokes the standard critique that academic research is sometimes perceived as disconnected from everyday challenges — the gap Sosrokartono’s real-world-experience ideal is meant to close.

06  ·  Implications

Diagnosis: What the Modern Academic System Rewards

The incentive structure and the mentorship ideal point in different directions.

✗ What Is Currently Rewarded
✗Constant publication for career survival and institutional recognition
✗Administrative labyrinths and accreditation criteria that pull educators from mentorship
✗Hierarchical structures positioning teachers as authority figures, not collaborative partners
✓ What Sosrokartono’s Ideal Requires
✓Mentorship weighted alongside research output in how educators are evaluated
✓Educators with real-world, cross-sector experience valued explicitly, not just academic pedigree
✓Humility and vulnerability modeled by teachers, not defensiveness in the face of criticism
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
07  ·  Conclusion

Recovering the Mentor in the Modern Professor

Three recommendations for narrowing the gap between reform intent and Sosrokartono’s ideal.

1

Rebalance faculty evaluation to weight mentorship and student engagement alongside publication output.

2

Actively recruit and value educators with real-world and industry experience, not academic credentials alone.

3

Encourage faculty presence in public scholarly discourse, including professional networks, as an extension of the mentor role.

Closing

Only by fostering a genuine partnership between teachers and students, and effectively bridging the gap between theory and practice, can we authentically transform the landscape of education for the better.
