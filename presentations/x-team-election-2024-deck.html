Election 2024: MIT and INSEAD’s
X-Team Insights in Navigating the Vote

Choosing Indonesia’s next president requires evaluating not just individual candidate leadership through the 4-Caps+ framework, but the strength and external orientation of their team through the X-Team model — because in a fluid political landscape, the team matters as much as the leader.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
A lecturer in industrial entrepreneurship at the School of Electrical Engineering and Informatics at the Bandung Institute of Technology and a graduate of Harvard Business School and MIT Sloan School of Management. The views expressed are his own.

01  ·  Introduction

Three candidates, one deluge of doxa.

As Indonesia’s general election approaches, voters face a muddle of information that makes candidate evaluation harder, not easier.

Situation

Three presidential candidates, each backed by political parties and investors, approach the general election amid an information environment blending truth and falsehood.

Complication

Today’s political industry dynamic increasingly shifts “from investors, by the people, to the investors,” and “doxa” (opinion) often overshadows “episteme” (knowledge) in how candidates are evaluated.

Question

What structured, accessible tool can help ordinary voters cut through the noise and evaluate candidates on substance?

Answer

The MIT/INSEAD 4-Caps+ Framework evaluates individual leadership capability, while the X-Team Model evaluates whether a candidate’s team can coordinate internally and engage effectively externally — together offering a fuller basis for the vote than rhetoric alone.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
02  ·  Gap

Voter evaluation stops at the candidate, not the team.

Leadership research has moved to team-level, externally engaged models — political commentary has not kept pace.

Core Gap

Traditional team models focused mainly on internal goals, roles, and processes are no longer sufficient in today’s rapidly changing world of fluid power structures and shifting interdependencies. Yet public evaluation of presidential candidates centers almost entirely on individual rhetoric and personality, ignoring the strength, dynamics, and external effectiveness of the teams candidates surround themselves with.

03  ·  Framework

Three Lenses for Evaluating a Candidate

Individual capability, credibility, and team externality together form a fuller evaluation than rhetoric alone.

01
The 4-Caps+ Individual Lens

Sense-making, Visioning, Relating, and Inventing — four capabilities (Ancona & Bresman, 2023) that assess how a leader creates meaning from uncertainty, crafts vision, engages stakeholders, and executes.

02
Building Credibility

The anchor beneath all four capabilities — leading with integrity, consistently acting in line with core values, and committing to the greater good over personal interest.

03
The X-Team Collective Lens

Rooted in the principle that “great ideas don’t sell themselves,” this model evaluates whether a team acts as ambassadors externally — building coalitions, negotiating, and coordinating internally at the same time.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
04  ·  Details

What Each Capability Actually Looks For

Three of the framework’s components, unpacked with the specific behaviors they assess.

Sense-making

The capacity to release entrenched mental models, view a changing landscape with fresh eyes, and synthesize complex information into a new map of reality — tested through experimentation and iteration, not just declared certainty.

Relating and Visioning

Relating means deep engagement with stakeholders’ perspectives and the ability to negotiate across polarized views; visioning means crafting a strategic, inspiring, shared direction rather than a list of promises.

The X-Team’s external mandate

Like skilled diplomats, an effective team must engage external networks — competitors, opponents, foreign counterparts — while maintaining superior internal coordination, ensuring the leader’s vision translates into execution.

05  ·  Evidence

Sources Behind the Argument

Two named frameworks and one candid limitation ground the piece’s claims.

EVIDENCE A
The 4-Caps+ Framework

Professor Deborah Ancona (MIT) and Professor Henrik Bresman (INSEAD) (2023) developed the framework’s four capabilities anchored in Building Credibility, the direct theoretical basis for the piece’s individual-leadership lens.

EVIDENCE B
The “political industry” concept

Porter (2020) frames politics itself as an industry with investors and returns — the concept the piece uses to explain the shift away from governance strictly “of, by, for the people.”

EVIDENCE C
The X-Team Model

Rooted in the principle that great ideas require external engagement to succeed, the X-Team Model is the direct source for the piece’s team-level evaluation lens.

EVIDENCE D
Acknowledged limitation

The piece explicitly acknowledges that even systematic frameworks cannot fully capture the unpredictable, non-rational realm of political decision-making — a candor evidence of intellectual honesty rather than framework overreach.

06  ·  Implications

Diagnosis: What Voter Evaluation Currently Misses

The gap is not a lack of information — it is a lack of structure for evaluating it.

✗ What Current Evaluation Centers On
✗Individual rhetoric, personality, and campaign messaging
✗Team composition and external engagement capacity, largely ignored
✓ What a Fuller Evaluation Requires
✓Assessing Sense-making, Visioning, Relating, and Inventing as distinct, observable capabilities
✓Evaluating the candidate’s team for internal coordination and external ambassadorship
✓Treating Building Credibility as the non-negotiable anchor beneath both lenses
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
07  ·  Conclusion

Voting With Structure, Not Just Instinct

Three recommendations for applying these frameworks before casting a vote.

1

Evaluate candidate teams’ external engagement capacity, not just the candidate’s individual rhetoric.

2

Popularize structured frameworks like 4-Caps+ and the X-Team Model as accessible civic tools, not academic abstractions.

3

Weigh Building Credibility as the anchor trait across both individual and team-level assessment.

Closing

Our responsibility as voters is to approach this task with both knowledge and an appreciation for the complexity of the choices before us.
