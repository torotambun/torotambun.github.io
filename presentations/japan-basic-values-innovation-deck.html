Elements That Determine
Innovative Nations: The Case of Japan

Innovation labs that copy Japan’s artefacts — co-working spaces, garbage sorting, spotless toilets — without the basic values underneath them will not replicate Japan’s innovation record. Basic values take two generations to build; there is no shortcut.

01  ·  Introduction

Copying the co-working space is not copying the country.

A SCQA reading of the core argument, drawn from a public holiday spent with Leave a Nest CEO Ryuta Takeda.

Situation

Japan is among the world’s most innovative nations. Its techno-parks, like Ryuta Takeda’s Leave a Nest, draw imitators from across the developing world, Indonesia included.

Complication

Indonesian “innovative labs” that try to learn from them mostly copy artefacts — warehouse-style co-working spaces, business models, event formats — without grasping why those things work in Japan.

Question

What actually produced Japan’s innovation record, and can it be copied faster than the two generations it took to build?

Answer

Basic values — not artefacts — explain Japan’s record: a Hofstede profile of high Masculinity, high Uncertainty Avoidance, high Long-Term Orientation and low Indulgence, instilled across generations. There is no shortcut.

02  ·  Research Gap

Innovation-ecosystem discourse counts artefacts, not substrate.

Co-working spaces, hackathons and accelerators are treated as sufficient inputs. The culture that makes them work is rarely asked about.

Core Gap

Existing Indonesian innovation-ecosystem discourse treats visible artefacts — co-working spaces, hackathons, start-up accelerators — as sufficient inputs for building an innovation nation. It never asks whether the surrounding culture supplies the discipline, restraint and multi-generational patience those artefacts assume in Japan. Edgar Schein’s distinction between artefacts, espoused values and basic assumptions is the gap this piece works through.

The making of an innovative ecosystem relies on the underlying basic values of a region — and the successful implementation of a corporation’s innovation depends on its corporate basic values, neither its artefacts nor its espoused values (Schein, 2010).

03  ·  Framework

What Actually Produced Japan’s Innovation Record

Three MECE pillars, read off Hofstede’s national culture index, explain what artefact-copying misses.

01
Predictability & Restraint

Uncertainty Avoidance 92, Indulgence 42. Constant exposure to earthquakes, tsunamis and typhoons built a culture where everything is prescribed for maximum predictability — and gratification is deliberately restrained, not indulged.

02
Long Horizon

Long-Term Orientation 88. Japanese see their life as a moment in a long history of humanity — guided by virtue and example, not quarterly results.

03
Respect Made Habitual

Masculinity 95, combined with mild collectivism. Queuing, cleanliness-as-gratitude, and respect for pedestrian space are not etiquette — they are values converted into daily, unconscious ritual.

04  ·  Details

The Host-Family Case: Values as Lived Practice

A 1988 exchange-year host family shows what “basic value” looks like when it is tested, not just declared.

The compounding crisis

Otosan was diagnosed with lung cancer; surgery left him unable to speak for a year; he then injured his back gardening and ruptured his kneecap. While caring for him, Okasan was diagnosed with breast cancer and lost her hair to radiotherapy.

The observed recovery

On a recent visit: their first dinner out in three years, Otosan’s first solo drive in years, a mini-climb managed for the first time since diagnosis, and a driving-license renewal passed “with excellence” two weeks after being wheeled in for the test.

The values named, not assumed

Growth mindset over fixed mindset. No time for self-pity. Acting on what is in their hands rather than complaining about what is beyond them. Support from their nucleus people. None of this is an artefact you can install in a co-working space over a weekend.

05  ·  Evidence

Four Artefacts, Traced to Their Underlying Value

Each artefact below is commonly copied by innovation labs. Each is evidence of a value that is not.

EVIDENCE A
Cleanliness as gratitude

“Not being wasteful is not just about avoiding waste — it also embodies a spirit of gratitude towards objects” (Matsumoto, 2018). Garbage segregation at every block is an ascetic practice, not a chore.

EVIDENCE B
Respect for pedestrians

An informal nine-commandment sidewalk etiquette — priority to pedestrians, no phone-checking while walking, take your trash home — observed consistently across a 12km routine walk in Tokyo.

EVIDENCE C
Fences, not walls

Japanese construction fencing harmonizes with its surroundings by design — “an intuitive understanding of the emotional relationship between human beings and their environment” (Sandrisser, 1982).

EVIDENCE D
National culture index

Hofstede’s Japan profile — Power Distance 54, Individualism 46, Masculinity 95, Uncertainty Avoidance 92, Long-Term Orientation 88, Indulgence 42 — gives the numeric substrate beneath every artefact above.

06  ·  Implications

Diagnosis: What Innovation Labs Get Wrong

Two generations cannot be compressed into two fiscal years by copying faster.

✗ Wrong Remedy
✗Copying co-working-space aesthetics and calling it ecosystem-building
✗Treating a single hackathon or accelerator cohort as sufficient input
✗Assuming Silicon Valley or Tokyo playbooks transplant directly
✓ Right Work
✓Auditing the cultural substrate with a Hofstede-style baseline before importing artefacts
✓Funding cohort programmes designed to outlive election cycles, not just budget years
✓Treating daily ritual — queuing, care for shared space — as a leading indicator, not decoration
© 2023 Dr. Toronata Tambun
07  ·  Conclusion

Do Not Jump the Queue

Three recommendations follow directly from the diagnosis above.

1

Measure innovation readiness by cultural substrate, not artefact count — adapt a Hofstede-style index for Indonesian regions and institutions.

2

Invest for two generations, not two fiscal years — fund programmes structured to survive changes in government and budget cycle.

3

Treat restraint and discipline as an asset in innovation policy, not a symptom of backwardness to be designed around.

Closing

The basic values are where you start, before any strategy, before any innovation roadmap. For a nation, that may take two generations to build. Do not jump the queue — if you do, you are at your peril.
