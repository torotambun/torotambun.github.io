The Wrong Remedy: Evaluating
University Study Program Closures

Closing a study program because its graduates are unemployed assumes the program's only legitimate output is a job. That logic sounds efficient in a ministry briefing and unravels the moment it meets any serious thinking about what education is for.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus.
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
01  ·  Introduction

A Staffing Agency With a Four-Year Queue

Education becomes valid only when the market immediately confirms it — a logic that relocates responsibility away from the economy built to receive graduates.

Situation

Kemendiktisaintek's Secretary General Badru Munir Sukoco announced in Bali on April 23 that study programs failing to demonstrate industry relevance will be reviewed and, if necessary, closed. The ministry counts 1.9 million graduates produced annually.

Complication

The state has not shown that these programs caused unemployment. It has only shown that their graduates are entering an economy unable to absorb them quickly enough.

Question

Is employment-placement rate the right metric for deciding which study programs deserve to live?

Answer

No. Formation comes first, specialization follows — and employment statistics measure only the second, narrower half of what education is for.

02  ·  Gap

A Distinction the Policy Has Collapsed

Special education trains for a specific occupation. General education prepares a person to think, judge, and function as a citizen.

Core Gap

The 1945 Harvard Report, General Education in a Free Society, was written when the United States faced a similar tension between mass education and economic absorption. Employment statistics measure the first kind of education. They do not measure the second — and Kemendiktisaintek has collapsed the distinction entirely.

03  ·  Framework

An Older, Deeper Educational Inheritance

Indonesia does not need Harvard to know this. Its own tradition gives the sharper answer.

1
Formation Before Specialization

A graduate without judgment becomes brittle when industries change. A graduate without competence remains vague when work must be done. The sequence matters.

2
Ki Hadjar Dewantara

Education forms a free and dignified human being — modeling from the front, building will from within, supporting from behind. A closure policy based on employment tables does none of these.

3
Sosrokartono

Education forms inner character: the capacity to serve, endure, and act with integrity. No employment metric can register that a graduate may still possess the formation that makes education worthwhile.

4
The Structural Risk

The Harvard Report warned that some nations respond to unemployed youth by regimenting them — a solution that cannot safely be adopted in a free society.

04  ·  Details

The Student in Kupang or Ternate

She is not a statistical inconvenience. She is the test case.

The burden falls on those least able to carry it

If closure reaches regional programs first because they lack the industry partnerships and institutional insulation of flagship universities, the policy has not solved graduate unemployment — it has merely moved the burden.

Educated unemployment is an economic, not academic, symptom

Carnoy's decades of research on developing economies establish that educated unemployment is produced by economies that were never built to absorb the graduates they demanded — the ministry has inverted the diagnosis.

A four-to-five-year cycle cannot track short-term markets

Asadiktisi chairman Prof. Susanto points to a basic constraint: fields dismissed today may be strategic tomorrow, and regional programs carry functions the metric cannot see.

05  ·  Evidence

Universities Have Already Pointed to a Better Path

Reform, not elimination, is the consistent recommendation from within the sector itself.

CONSENSUS
UMY, UGM, Unpad, Udayana, and UI

Have argued for curriculum reform, not elimination, as the appropriate response to programs facing weak industry relevance.

CREDENTIALED AUTHORITY
Impressive titles make the proposal more troubling, not less

It shows how easily credentialed authority can mistake a labor-market symptom for an educational diagnosis.

06  ·  Implications

One Number vs. Harder Diagnostic Work

A study program is not a stock item. It cannot be judged by one number over one short window.

✗ Closure by Employment Metric
✗Treating graduates as defective outputs of a malfunctioning production line, judged on one placement-rate number
✗Closure as the natural conclusion simply because graduates do not enter employment quickly enough
✓ Academic Diagnostic Work
✓Asking what a program actually teaches, whether faculty can teach it, and whether students are being formed intellectually
✓Closing programs only when academically indefensible, under-resourced, or producing credentials without competence
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
07  ·  Conclusion

The Cheaper Question, Mistaken for the Problem

Indonesia does not need fewer chances for young people. It needs more serious institutions behind those chances.

1

Ask what a program teaches and whether students are formed intellectually — not only whether graduates found work within a year.

2

Ask whether the economy around the university offers the kinds of work the closure policy assumes should exist.

3

Build a more serious economy capable of receiving the people its institutions form — the harder, slower, less visible work that actually addresses the problem.

Closing

Indonesia has not yet asked whether its universities are producing that kind of person. It has asked only the cheaper question, and mistaken that question for the problem itself.
