What the RI² Flags
Might Really Be Telling Us

I was shocked to learn my institution had been flagged yellow in the Research Integrity Risk Index. I submitted my own research 54 times to reach 10 acceptances, never once cutting a corner. The flag is not about individual ethics. It is about systemic saturation.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
2025 Ganesa Widya Jasa Utama Awardee, Institut Teknologi Bandung.
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
01  ·  Introduction

A flag that didn't match the facts

Either the instrument is flawed, or something deeper is happening.

Situation

The author's institution was flagged yellow in the Research Integrity Risk Index (RI²), despite a process he experienced as rigorous: compulsory ethical review, strict Scopus requirements, and no known institutional misconduct.

Complication

The author's own record — 54 submissions, 10 acceptances, years of unreturned reviews and resubmissions — contained no shortcuts. Yet the flag persisted at the institutional level.

Question

If individual integrity is intact, what is the RI² flag actually measuring?

Answer

Not ethics violations, but systemic saturation — the structural accumulation of performance pressure across rankings, funding, quotas, and promotion, which produces unintended outcomes even in fully compliant systems.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
02  ·  Gap

Compliant on paper, saturated in practice

Structures don't always produce what they intend to.

Core Gap

A system can be entirely compliant on paper, but if its feedback loops are overloaded, misaligned, or tightly compressed, it can still produce unintended outcomes. The saturation stems from multiple pressures layered onto universities: rankings, funding eligibility, publication quotas, promotion requirements. When they converge, they produce an Institutional Saturation Point — and alongside it, System Fatigue among faculty and students expected to deliver high output under tight constraints.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
03  ·  Framework

The Loop Structure Behind the Flag

Five reinforcing loops and one balancing response.

R1
Publication Pressure Loop

Rankings reward volume; promotion is tied to output; faculty absorb the demand and pass it down to graduate students, who feed the cycle further.

R2a/b
Shortcut & Student Pressure Loops

When demands exceed what individuals can realistically fulfill, faculty seek faster pathways (often predatory or delisted journals) while graduate students, tied to publication timelines, comply, break, or cut corners.

R4/R5
External Validation & Normalization Loops

Universities chase rankings and visibility, recursively increasing pressure to maintain it, while shortcut behavior spreads until what began as exception becomes culture.

B1
Integrity Response Loop

A balancing loop: shortcut behaviors trigger flags and audits meant to restore stability — but overly forceful correction risks defensive compliance or driving behavior underground.

04  ·  Details

54 Submissions, 10 Acceptances, No Shortcuts

The personal record behind the argument.

54 total submissions across every stage

Desk rejections, peer reviews, revisions, and eventual publications — not passive submissions, but years of unreturned reviews, resubmissions, and strict ethical compliance.

10 accepted: 6 non-Scopus, 3 Scopus Q2, 1 Scopus Q1

A roughly 1-in-5 acceptance rate achieved without a single shortcut — because, in the author's words, "I wouldn't know how to even if I wanted to."

Cost: years of time, family, and mental clarity

The process was real and it worked, but the personal cost illustrates exactly the saturation pressure the RI² framework is meant to detect.

06  ·  Implications

Diagnosis: Punishment or Diagnosis?

Only one prevents good people from walking away.

✗ Reading Flags as Accusation
✗Assuming compliance on paper equals institutional health, waiting for scandal before intervening
✗Tightening procedures further without examining how internal pressure loops actually work
✓ Reading Flags as Diagnosis
✓Reforming incentives, not just procedures — shifting from volume to coherence, from shortcuts to sustainable deep work
✓Auditing loop pressure points, pausing ranking-chasing incentives, and decoupling graduation timelines from publication quotas
✓Recognizing deep research that may not yet be published, rather than only rewarding volume
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
07  ·  Conclusion

Rewiring the Loops, Not Just Adding Rules

Three imperatives for policymakers, rectors, and academic leaders.

1

Stop assuming compliance means health. Saturation can produce outcomes indistinguishable from misconduct even in stringent systems — don't wait for scandal to intervene.

2

Reform incentives, not just procedures. Review KPIs that reward quantity without meaning, and examine whether promotion systems escalate publication loops unnecessarily.

3

Use RI² as diagnosis, not punishment. Don't just ban the shortcut — dismantle the pressure that made it inevitable.

Closing

The true danger is not red flags. The true danger is when good people begin to feel that playing by the rules is futile. If we ignore that message, we won't need an index to tell us we're in trouble — we'll already know, because the best among us will begin to walk away.
