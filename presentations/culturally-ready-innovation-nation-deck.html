Fostering a Culturally Ready, Innovative and Technology Nation

Indonesia’s enthusiasm for AI risks repeating an old mistake — adopting a technology without the cultural readiness to absorb it. Early education, not policy mimicry, is the actual precondition.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Dosen Industri, Sekolah Tinggi Elektro dan Informatika, Institut Teknologi Bandung (STEI-ITB)
© 2023 Dr. Toronata Tambun

Automation has been reshaping human progress since long before AI. The introduction of the printing press threatened the leaders of its day. Industrial automation in the 1800s raised fears of job displacement. The calculator, introduced in the 1970s, disrupted math education. The contemporary rise of AI triggers the same apprehension — but the term itself was coined in 1950, when Indonesia was still an infant sovereign nation. This is not new; it is a continuation.

The inexorable march of progress cannot be halted, and resisting it is like fighting the tide. What can be chosen is adaptability — reskilling, continuous learning, and channeling human cognitive ability toward what automation cannot efficiently do.

© 2023 Dr. Toronata Tambun

What if a country misses the boat? Geopolitical disadvantage as AI-capable nations gain military and economic leverage. Cultural erosion as traditional practices dilute under imported technology norms. A new imperialism, where economic and technological dependency substitutes for old-style colonial power. Wider cybersecurity exposure, and a domino effect where one nation’s AI-infrastructure breach cascades across borders.

What if a country jumps in without cultural readiness? Economic disparity widens as AI infrastructure costs outpace developing economies. Technological dependency compromises sovereignty. Job displacement hits labor-intensive sectors hardest. Weak regulatory frameworks let algorithmic bias and privacy violations go unchecked. A skills gap and a data-inequality gap compound each other.

1
Early childhood and STEM

Nurture creativity and problem-solving from early childhood; ensure equal access to quality education across urban and rural divides.

2
Teachers and infrastructure

Continuous professional development for teachers; investment in the facilities and technology to deliver it.

3
Basic needs first

Nutrition and healthcare as a precondition for engagement and creativity — the same logic that runs through every one of these arguments.

4
Entrepreneurship and R&D

Grants, incubators and capital access for AI-era startups; sustained investment in research that tackles national challenges directly.

The proposal is “parallel action”: build early-education readiness for the next generation while asking today’s workforce — through continuous learning, local innovation hubs, tech-policy advocacy, digital inclusion — to proactively embrace the current wave. But the honest tension sits underneath the plan, not resolved by it: readiness for AI is a multi-generational project, while the pressure to adopt AI now is immediate. “Parallel action” names the gap; it does not close it.

© 2023 Dr. Toronata Tambun

As the nation embraces technology and innovation, safeguarding the security and resilience of its digital infrastructure has to run alongside its education agenda, not after it. Indonesia can stand at the forefront of technological innovation only if it treats cybersecurity and cultural readiness as one investment, not two separate line items.
