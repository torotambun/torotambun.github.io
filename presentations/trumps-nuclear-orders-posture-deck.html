What Trump's Nuclear Orders
Reveal About Indonesia's Posture

Both countries cite 250 MW SMRs and decade-scale timelines. But Trump's executive orders mandate deadlines and reassign legal authority; Indonesia's RUPTL names a target and repeats vendor language. The United States acts as an author. Indonesia continues to behave like a host.

Dr. Toronata Tambun

01  ·  Introduction

Two announcements, one tempo, one posture

Superficial alignment conceals a structural asymmetry.

Situation

In May 2025, President Trump signed executive orders compressing licensing pathways and directing agencies to deploy microreactors for AI infrastructure and defense. Days later, Indonesia's RUPTL confirmed a 500 MW nuclear target by 2034.

Complication

Both announcements cite SMR-scale plants and 250 MW baselines. But Indonesia's plan lacks binding regulation, credible testing, or fuel chain clarity, while the country has yet to operate a single research reactor above experimental scale.

Question

Is Indonesia authoring its own nuclear future, or merely hosting someone else's?

Answer

Trump's orders demonstrate internal authorship; Indonesia's roadmap reflects strategic absorption. Until Indonesia writes its own nuclear tempo, the ambition remains vulnerable.

02  ·  Gap

Consenting site, not accountable authority

BAPETEN depends on vendor-supplied data rather than independent testing.

Core Gap

BAPETEN has no statutory timeframe for license issuance and no independent reactor testing infrastructure. It depends on vendor-supplied data and external expert verification. This structure disqualifies the nation from acting as a genuine operator. It places Indonesia in the role of a consenting site, not an accountable authority.

03  ·  Framework

Author vs. Host: The Structural Asymmetry

Same numbers, opposite institutional postures.

US
The United States: Authorship

Mandates deadlines, reassigns legal authority, links nuclear directly to national defense and AI infrastructure, and revives fuel reprocessing after decades of dormancy.

ID
Indonesia: Hosting

Names a target and repeats vendor-driven language but lacks binding regulation, credible testing, fuel chain clarity, or a TNI/BRIN civilian-military innovation interface.

04  ·  Details

What "Proven" Actually Looks Like

Two states that do not export what they have not used at home.

Linglong One (China) — 125 MW, under construction since 2021

Passed IAEA safety review in 2016, with key components installed in Hainan in 2025 — not yet operational, but reflecting a build-first, export-later strategy.

Akademik Lomonosov (Russia) — 70 MWe, operational since 2019

Two KLT-40S reactors at 35 MWe each, fully commissioned May 22, 2020, supplying power and heat to Arctic communities since.

Indonesia's vendor — zero 250 MW-class reactors built anywhere

The U.S. canceled its most advanced NuScale project in 2023 due to rising costs and investor withdrawal; Indonesia is committing on paper to platforms never built or operated, even in the vendor's home country.

06  ·  Implications

Diagnosis: Reactive Posture or Declared Tempo?

Only one produces a nation that governs what it builds.

✗ Reactive Posture
✗Treating a 250 MW reactor as "starting small," a light infrastructure project rather than a long-term intergenerational liability
✗Announcing site selections without publishing siting studies or licensing frameworks
✓ Declared Tempo
✓Writing Indonesia's own dates, terms, and pacing logic rather than absorbing vendor-driven timelines
✓Building fuel autonomy strategy, enrichment capacity, and a civilian-military innovation interface at BRIN
✓Giving BAPETEN statutory license timeframes and independent testing infrastructure

07  ·  Conclusion

The Real Milestone Is Ethical, Not Technical

Did Indonesia choose from conviction, or merely sign what was offered?

1

Publish siting studies and licensing frameworks before committing to reactor locations in Sumatra and Kalimantan.

2

Build institutional authorship — binding regulation, independent testing, fuel chain clarity — to match the ambition already declared on paper.

3

Define Indonesia's own nuclear tempo, not just the dates but the terms and pacing logic, before proceeding further.

Closing

The risk is not just that we fail. The risk is that we succeed in building something we do not govern. In that case, the reactor is not the danger. The danger is the silence around it.
