Calling drivers entrepreneurs won't create better jobs

Naming ride-hailing drivers entrepreneurs changes the statistic and the category, not the productivity of an hour of work or who carries the risk. Indonesia needs protection suited to platform work now, and firms in which equipment, organisation and learning raise the value of labour later.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Renaming ride-hailing drivers as entrepreneurs changes the count, not the work.

On 1 July 2026 the Ministry of UMKM announced that two-wheeled ride-hailing drivers would automatically be treated as pengusaha mikro transportasi online, with access to programs available to other micro-enterprises.

Situation

The column opens on a walkway in an old part of Jakarta, first described in July 2024. Motorcycles lined the path, and "Men sat on them looking at their phones, waiting for orders".

Complication

BPS already counts a person who works for pay during the survey reference week as employed, so a driver who starts on a platform leaves the open-unemployment count. The Ministry of UMKM can then count the same person again as a micro-enterprise actor. "Yet the work itself may not have changed".

Question

Does the new label change what an hour of driving earns, or who carries the risk of a slow day?

Answer

"Calling the driver an entrepreneur does not make an hour of work more productive or create a better livelihood."

What has not changed

"The driver still waits for orders. Fuel still costs money. The motorcycle still depreciates. Daily income still depends on demand and the platform."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Gap

Spare-hour income helps, but a household needs a livelihood that survives waiting, injury and old age.

Platform income is real. The open question is whether it can carry a household.

What the platform provides
✓"A platform can make useful income out of a motorcycle that would otherwise sit idle, or a few spare hours."
✓"Platform work can prevent unemployment from becoming destitution. That is valuable."
What a household still needs
✗"An order pays for a journey; the household must meet expenses even when no order arrives."
✗"Fares must cover fuel, depreciation and unpaid waiting, while leaving enough for daily needs, injury and old age."
✗"The work must sustain a livelihood across the working day and over the years, including periods when the driver cannot work"
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

Manufacturing can build capability through process learning and capital investment, while a driver's hour may remain close to where it began.

"Platform growth and the driver’s progress need to be examined separately".

A worker in a capable manufacturing company
✓"A worker who spends eight years in a capable manufacturing company may know processes in year eight that were unfamiliar in year one."
✓"The company may also learn how to organise workers better, invest in equipment and improve output."
✓"Manufacturing can build this kind of capability through process learning and capital investment"
A ride-hailing driver
•"A ride-hailing driver also learns. After years on the road, the driver knows routes, customers and the demands of the job. Those are real skills."
•"But the economic value of an hour of work may remain close to where it began"
•Platform totals "cannot show whether experience has made the driver better able to earn a living"
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun

Implications

Expanding platform work is not proof that the economy is creating enough higher-productivity employment.

"Platform work can prevent unemployment from becoming destitution. That is valuable. But Indonesia cannot treat the expansion of platform work as proof that the economy is creating enough higher-productivity employment."

The column returns to a problem it raised in December 2025, when examining projections that place Indonesia at high-income status by 2045: "An economy can put more people to work without making their working hours much more valuable."

The distinction matters "when a falling unemployment rate is presented as evidence that the country is moving toward prosperity".

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

The entrepreneur label blurs necessity with opportunity and can make the platform's risks look like normal business risks.

01
Necessity or opportunity
Self-employment is not the same as entrepreneurship
"Someone who sees an opportunity, gathers resources and builds a business is doing something different from someone who enters self-employment because there is no better job available." Entrepreneurship research has long separated necessity-based self-employment from entrepreneurship built around perceived opportunity.
02
Category or explanation
A category does not explain how a business operates
The column recalls a July 2021 argument that small and medium enterprises and innovation-driven enterprises "perform different economic roles and need different policy remedies". The same principle applies here: a category used to deliver assistance "cannot, by itself, explain how a business operates or what would help its workers become more productive".
03
Who carries the risk
The platform's uncertainty stays with the driver
"A driver pays for fuel, motorcycle depreciation and idle time while also carrying much of the uncertainty of earning through the platform." Calling the driver a micro-enterprise actor "can make those costs look like normal business risks even when the driver depends on the platform for a living".
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Recommendations: Now

Indonesia could protect platform-dependent drivers, drawing on Singapore, without declaring them opportunity entrepreneurs or automatically turning them into employees.

Protection and productive employment are separate problems, and "a driver who needs income this week" cannot wait years for better jobs to appear.

1  ·  Protect the work people already depend on
"Indonesia needs protection suited to the work people already depend on." Employment protection "could transfer some risks away from the driver".
2  ·  Draw on Singapore's Platform Workers Act
The Act "created specific protections for workers whose income depends on digital platforms, including social-security contributions, work-injury protection and representation".
3  ·  Protect without reclassifying
"Drivers who depend heavily on platform income can receive protection without being declared opportunity entrepreneurs or automatically turned into conventional employees. Platforms should share the costs created by the work while drivers retain flexibility."
4  ·  Start with injury cover, retirement contributions and clear cost rules
"Work-injury coverage and retirement contributions are practical starting points, drawing on Singapore’s framework." "Rules on deductions, compensation and waiting time should also make clear which costs belong to drivers and which arise from the platform’s way of operating".
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Recommendations: Longer Term

Protection does not remove what pushes people into platform work, so training must sit inside a plan for productive employment.

1
Ask why platform work is one of the best full-time options
Protection "does not remove the conditions that push people into platform work". "Indonesia must also ask why so many workers find this one of the best full-time options available."
2
Expand the firms and activities workers could move into
"The longer-term task is to expand the firms and activities into which these workers could move." Manufacturing is one route, but investment must create work in which equipment, organisation and learning allow people to produce more valuable output. The relevant question is "what an employer is preparing to produce, and why it will need workers with greater capability".
3
Treat productive demand as essential
"A course can teach a skill, but a firm must have a use for it." Without a connection between learning and a change in the work itself, "the burden falls back on individuals to acquire qualifications and hope that employers will eventually need them".
4
Put training inside a plan for productive employment
"Giving a driver a certificate while leaving no more productive job to enter changes the paperwork rather than the economic path." The test is what happens after training: "whether the worker enters a better position, uses the new skills and gains experience that makes those skills more valuable".
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

Neither an order count nor a category label shows where the driver stands in the economy.

The Ministry of UMKM can record another entrepreneur. The driver still has to make a day’s work support a household.

The scene returns
"The men on that Jakarta walkway may still be waiting for orders at ten on a Sunday morning."
What neither measure shows
"Their place in the economy cannot be understood from the number of orders alone, or from the category beside their names."
