What if Discrimination Is
Not About Religion?

Indonesia exports an image of tolerance at Davos and ASEAN summits. On Good Friday 2026, the public saw another story — and a harder question follows: why does the same suffocating pattern repeat, regardless of which faith holds majority power?

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Doctoral Researcher in Corporate Innovation Ecosystems, School of Business and Management, Institut Teknologi Bandung (SBM-ITB).

01  ·  Introduction

A Brittle Web Beneath the Permit

Authorities sealed a Christian congregation's prayer house in Teluknaga, Tangerang on Good Friday, April 3, 2026, citing a building permit regulation, only hours after worship had ended.

Situation

Indonesia's talking points at Davos and ASEAN summits describe Pancasila, Bhinneka Tunggal Ika, and the world's largest moderate Muslim democracy.

Complication

It was the second disruption faced by the same congregation in two years. The explanation referred to permits, but beneath it sat something more volatile — a brittle web of sensitivities that can ignite fast while still calling itself tolerance.

Question

Why does the same pattern appear even where the minority faith in one province becomes the majority faith in another, and applies the same pressure to its own minority?

Answer

Because persecution is not explained only at the level of doctrine. An ancient threat-detection reflex in the brain often makes its first judgment before doctrine begins to speak.

02  ·  Gap

Incomplete Diagnoses, Familiar Reflexes

Blame religious intolerance. Blame the majority. Blame the permit system. These are not wrong answers — they are incomplete.

Core Gap

Since 2016, the Laroma community, adherents of Lalang Rondor Malesung, the ancestral faith of the Minahasa people, has faced sustained discrimination from surrounding Christian-majority communities. The actors were different. The religious banner was different. The mechanism, however, looked painfully familiar.

03  ·  Framework

The Machinery Beneath Doctrine

Fear wins the first round. Moral formation decides whether it wins the second.

1
The Amygdala

An ancient threat-detection system that sorts danger fast, reacting in fractions of a second, long before conscious moral reasoning or theology can speak.

2
The Prefrontal Cortex

Slower, associated with deliberation, empathy, and restraint — capable of overriding first impulse, but only through repeated, trained practice.

3
The Early Childhood Window

Experiences between ages 3 and 7 contribute approximately 45% to the moral capacity expressed in adulthood, outweighing cognitive biases and external pressures (Tambun et al., 2025).

4
Majority-Condition Fear

Once a group holds cultural or institutional dominance, a quieter fear of being displaced or made irrelevant — mortality salience — can turn ordinary difference into perceived threat.

04  ·  Details

Three Audiences, One Shallow Diagnosis

If the diagnosis remains shallow, the remedy will remain shallow too.

For those blaming only one community

The evidence from South Minahasa demands a more serious accounting — majorities can reproduce the same pattern across very different settings. The amygdala does not ask for doctrine before it activates.

For policymakers

Brochures about tolerance are not enough. What matters more is repeated, ordinary, embodied contact across difference, long enough for suspicion to weaken and familiarity to grow.

For foreign diplomats and observers

Religious freedom in Indonesia should not be read only as a doctrinal dispute dressed in legal form. Much of it is a human threat response dressed in religious form.

05  ·  Evidence

Two Cases, One Mechanism

Different religious banners, same suffocating pressure.

TELUKNAGA
A congregation disrupted twice in two years

The first disruption came in July 2024, when residents dispersed worship held in a private home. The second came on Good Friday 2026, when the prayer house was sealed hours after worship ended.

MINAHASA
Wale Paliusan destroyed, June 2022

The Laroma community's sacred gathering place was destroyed by residents who branded them deviant — a year later, the discrimination had not ended.

07  ·  Conclusion

Tepo Seliro

The instinct to feel what another feels and act before being asked — older than the current language of coexistence, and more demanding.

1

Defend the Indonesia in which ordinary people interrupt their first instinct to divide, and choose instead to care — not the Indonesia that recites harmony abroad while managing exclusion at home.

2

Treat habit, not branding, as the measure of tolerance — tepo seliro asks for practice, not performance.

3

Judge the nation by what it does next, not by what it recites at interfaith dialogues with polished name tags.

Closing

The amygdala does not check religion before it activates. A decent nation is judged by what it does next.
