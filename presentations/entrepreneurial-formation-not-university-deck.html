Entrepreneurial Formation
Does Not Start in University

President Prabowo Subianto is right that Indonesia needs more innovative entrepreneurs. But the speech treats entrepreneurship as something producible mainly through courses, loans, and administrative encouragement — when entrepreneurial formation is shaped by cultural conditioning that shifts across two to three generations.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Doctoral graduate of Institut Teknologi Bandung; Harvard Business School alumnus; MIT Sloan School of Management affiliated alumnus.

01  ·  Introduction

The Engine Runs on Something Unmentioned

The President's speech addresses the two variables outside the engine. The engine itself runs on culture.

Situation

The President calls for faster licensing, wider access to credit, startup loans after graduation, and stronger cooperation between government, business, workers, MSMEs, and regional governments.

Complication

A recent ITB doctoral thesis, after seven years of longitudinal research, places funding and university as exogenous variables — secondary amplifiers that cannot initiate what culture has not yet sanctioned.

Question

Is funding and administrative encouragement the right lever to accelerate entrepreneurial formation?

Answer

No. Capital does not flow to aspiration. It flows to credibility, repayment probability, and evidence of persistence — and cultural legitimacy must exist before institutional support can hold.

02  ·  Gap

A High-Risk Borrower, Not Automatically an Entrepreneur

Pushing available financing into the system before cultural conditions exist does not accelerate formation. It burns dry powder.

Core Gap

A fresh graduate with no operating history, weak cash flow, limited collateral, and uncertain persistence is a high-risk borrower. If credit is pushed into weak ventures before credibility exists, policy may simply convert entrepreneurship rhetoric into non-performing loans — debt distribution with nationalistic language.

03  ·  Framework

Exogenous Amplifiers, Endogenous Core

Family expectations, social legitimacy, fear of failure, and civil-service prestige govern the system.

1
Founder Withdrawal Risk

A student may perform entrepreneurship inside a university program, then abandon the venture when a safer employment path appears. Banks price that risk because they are risk institutions, not motivational ones.

2
The Early Childhood Window

Roughly 45 percent of an adult's mental model forms between ages three and seven. A startup loan at age twenty-two cannot overwrite years of reinforced family expectation.

3
The Family Veto

In Javanese behavioural grammar, wedi, isin, and sungkan shape restraint around risk and public failure — entrepreneurship becomes acceptable mainly once success is already visible.

4
Cultural Lag Timescales

Observable cultural lag runs roughly 180 months; deeper resistance to behavioural change operates across roughly 720 months, or sixty years — timescales that do not fit annual policy cycles.

04  ·  Details

Rational Actors, Irrational System

Each actor behaves rationally within its own incentive structure, but the combined result is irrational at system level.

The policy-culture gap

The state promotes risk-taking while households promote security. Universities promote entrepreneurship in public while students treat programs as credential platforms for conventional employment.

Policy fatigue

Each cycle of government enthusiasm produces a spike of visible activity that recedes — institutions and founders learn that the next program will arrive before the last one is evaluated.

Obah mamah: movement without transformation

A startup name is easier to count than founder persistence. What cannot be counted at all is whether the founder is still there twelve months after the ceremony ended.

05  ·  Evidence

Financing the Wrong Stage

The thesis is unambiguous on sequencing.

SEQUENCING
Legitimacy, then support, then financing

Cultural legitimacy must exist before institutional support can hold, and institutional support must hold before financing finds traction. Skipping to the third stage funds an illusion.

SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS
Cultural amplification outweighs policy and funding

Cultural amplification coefficients have far greater systemic influence than policy cues or funding parameters — shared values for innovation are the strongest long-term governor of the system.

06  ·  Implications

Visible Activity vs. Persistence Metrics

Governments broadcast what they value. What they broadcast can be changed at little or no cost.

✗ Rewarding Visible Activity
✗Accreditation that counts enrollment, competitions staged, and certificates printed — none of which measures whether a founder was still operating twelve months later
✗Fast disbursement under political pressure that makes disciplined, milestone-linked staging impossible
✓ Tracking Persistence and Credibility
✓Tracking three-year founder persistence rates and staged, milestone-linked credibility financing
✓Ring-fenced public procurement for firms under three years old, converting aspiration into a paying customer with a name
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
07  ·  Conclusion

Where the System Is Most Responsive

Early childhood is where the system is most responsive to change, not university.

1

Insert structured problem-solving and low-stakes failure into early primary curriculum — a lever the government already controls at far less cost than post-graduation loan programs.

2

Amplify authentic founder stories of failure and persistence from non-elite backgrounds — a lever that costs nothing and moves the cultural input in the right direction.

3

Ask banks to be precise, not patriotic — staged, milestone-linked financing over politically pressured fast disbursement.

Closing

Until the formation problem is addressed at the right stages, Indonesia will keep producing entrepreneurship programs, startup statistics, and ceremonial optimism while the deeper system quietly pulls young talent back toward the safest available job. The President's speech will not change that. A loan at graduation never could.
