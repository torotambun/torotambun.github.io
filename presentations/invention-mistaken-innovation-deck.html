When Invention Is
Mistaken for Innovation

Indonesia does not suffer from a lack of ideas. It suffers from a confusion about innovation. Innovation is invention multiplied by commercialization — and in Indonesia, even corporations cannot start that process.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Doctoral Researcher in Corporate Innovation Ecosystems, School of Business and Management, Institut Teknologi Bandung (SBM-ITB).
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
01  ·  Introduction

Presence Is Not Activation

Shelter first. Ideas first. But markets later — and innovation-driven enterprises are not born from shelter.

Situation

Indonesia keeps multiplying incubators, buildings, cohorts, mentors, and demo days — much of what is called innovation still stops at ideas, prototypes, programs, and facilities.

Complication

The Dynamic Feedback Theory of Entrepreneurial Formation in Loss-Averse, Short-Horizon Cultures reveals something uncomfortable: even corporations, structurally central to markets, cannot start the innovation process in Indonesia.

Question

If corporations concentrate capital, talent, and opportunity, why can't they originate innovation-driven enterprise?

Answer

Corporations are structurally anchored inside a deeper cultural control system. They can scale and absorb innovation, but origination requires a prior cultural shift they cannot generate on their own.

02  ·  Gap

The Dominant Talent Sink

Prestige, job security, and clear career ladders generate the strongest gravitational pull in the ecosystem.

Core Gap

The internal logic of corporate behavior is not neutral. Capital is structurally routed back into existing markets. Performance systems reward certainty. Risk systems penalize ambiguous bets. As long as this logic dominates, talent is continuously pulled into stable corporate roles before new ventures can form — even when corporate innovation budgets are raised, shared societal commitment to innovation barely moves.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
03  ·  Framework

The Dynamic Feedback Theory

A cultural field beneath visible institutions governs what corporate policy alone cannot move.

1
Innovation = Invention × Commercialization

Innovation only exists when something is accepted by the market, meets paying customers, and survives competitive pressure — not when it merely exists as an idea or prototype.

2
The Cultural Field Beneath Institutions

Shaped by family expectations, early career morality, and institutional signaling, this layer governs what work is respectable, what risk is tolerated, and what failure is forgiven.

3
The Policy–Culture Gap

When policy enthusiasm and corporate signaling outpace cultural absorption, the system undergoes endogenous reversion — potential founders pulled back into secure paths once the hype period ends.

4
Time Asymmetry

Corporate renewal is constrained by approval and investment cycles measured in years; shared values for innovation evolve over decades. Corporations can never get ahead of the curve — they respond to legitimacy, they do not create it.

04  ·  Details

Innovation Theater

The system generates a spike. The long-term trajectory does not change.

Immediate interest, program launches, visibility

Institutions mistake visibility for value — the hype movement fails to achieve takeoff because it does not cross the Policy–Culture Gap.

The deep preference for stable jobs reasserts itself

Fear of reputational loss pulls potential founders back into secure career paths as soon as the hype period ends — incubators multiply, but origination does not increase.

Corporate venture units emerge as surface structures

Without cultural alignment, they operate as branding devices and recruitment channels rather than genuine origination engines.

05  ·  Evidence

Sharpening an Earlier Position

The author's own prior argument, revised under new evidence.

PRIOR CLAIM
Universities are not the center of innovation-driven enterprise

The center is the market who will purchase — the corporation, the buyer, the moment when someone is willing to pay.

REVISED CLAIM
Corporations can scale innovation, but cannot originate it

They are structurally subordinated to deeper cultural forces — origination requires a prior cultural shift that weakens the early pull of stable employment.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
06  ·  Implications

Building Container vs. Solving System

Two very different responses to Indonesia's innovation gap.

✗ Asking Corporations to "Do More Innovation"
✗Raising corporate innovation budgets and expanding programs without addressing the cultural field beneath them
✗Treating incubators, cohorts, and demo days as the innovation problem itself, rather than downstream symptoms
✓ Intervening at the Cultural Layer
✓Reducing the gap between policy rhetoric and lived protection for those who take entrepreneurial risk
✓Allowing entrepreneurial identity to consolidate before corporate recruitment captures it
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
07  ·  Conclusion

A Question, Not Yet an Answer

Until the cultural base and government stance move together, the pattern repeats.

1

Stop measuring innovation policy success by program count, building count, or cohort size — measure it by origination, not presence.

2

Recognize that corporations scale and absorb innovation but cannot originate it — direct cultural intervention elsewhere in the system.

3

Identify and act on the one cultural layer that does not wait decades to move, rather than waiting passively for generational change.

Closing

Until the cultural base and government stance move together to legitimize long-horizon risk, Indonesia will keep building incubators, keep launching corporate programs, keep announcing innovation initiatives — and innovation-driven enterprises will remain rare. The next question is whether Indonesia is willing to intervene at the one cultural layer that does not wait decades to move.
