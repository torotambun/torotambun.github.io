Nuclear Power:
Secure It with Proven Technology

At 80 years of independence, political freedom without energy sovereignty is incomplete. The choice of which nuclear technology to adopt must be guided by one principle: refusing to be a test-bed for unfinished experiments.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Doctoral Researcher, School of Business and Management, Institut Teknologi Bandung (SBM-ITB).
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
01  ·  Introduction

Energy independence is national independence

Culture decides safety as much as machines do.

Situation

Indonesia's energy demand grows each year, yet the country still relies on fossil fuels that compromise both climate commitments and sovereignty. At 80 years of independence, energy sovereignty remains unresolved.

Complication

Thorium has been promoted as "promising" since the 1950s, yet no nation has built a proven, sovereign reactor from it. India's KAMINI is a 30 kWth non-commercial research reactor; China's Wuwei test reactor reaches criticality but delivers no power to the grid.

Question

Which nuclear technology should a sovereign Indonesia actually choose?

Answer

Proven reactors — pressurized water, boiling water, and CANDU designs with over 18,000 reactor-years of safe operation — should anchor Indonesia's grid. Thorium and SMRs remain worth monitoring, not adopting as the backbone.

02  ·  Gap

A Design May Cut Risk to One in Ten Million

Yet corruption or shortcuts erase that margin.

Core Gap

Reactors may be advanced, but people run them — and without discipline and integrity, no design is safe. That is why Indonesia cannot afford the added risk of gambling on unproven technologies on top of the human and institutional risk that already exists. If sovereignty required a real thorium path, it would have been found sixty years ago when uranium reactors scaled worldwide. As one nuclear specialist warned: "the world should not hold its breath."

03  ·  Framework

Sovereignty as a Sequencing Discipline

Not every proven-sounding technology is proven at commercial scale.

1
The Proven Path: PWR / BWR / CANDU

Over 18,000 reactor-years of safe operation worldwide. Available, insurable, and already powering Korea, France, and Canada.

2
SMRs: An Evolutionary Step, Not Yet Ready

More than 80 designs in development, a handful under construction. China's 210 MWe HTR-PM connected in 2023, demonstration scale only; Russia's Akademik Lomonosov is the most commercially proven so far, but limited to Arctic towns.

3
Thorium: Research, Not Energy Backbone

Fabrication requires higher temperatures, chemical inertness complicates processing, and short-term radioactivity demands costly shielding — decades of research have not resolved these for commercial power.

4
Culture as Safety Infrastructure

Tapping the proven-reactor legacy brings not just electricity but the safety culture and operator discipline that come with it — assets Indonesia cannot import through unproven designs.

04  ·  Details

What "Thorium-Proven" Examples Actually Are

Fairness requires precision about what each cited reactor really demonstrates.

KAMINI (India): 30 kWth, non-commercial

A research reactor fueled by U-233 bred from thorium — not a commercial power source.

Kakrapar-1 (India): 220 MWe PHWR, mainly uranium

Sometimes cited as thorium-fueled, but in fact ran mainly on uranium, with only limited thorium bundle trials.

Wuwei (China): 2 MW test reactor

Reached criticality but still delivers no power to the grid — a demonstration of physics, not of commercial viability.

05  ·  Evidence

Fukushima Did Not Disprove Defense-in-Depth

It showed where older plants had underestimated compound risks — and the industry responded.

DESIGN
Core damage probability: 1 in 10 million per year

New-generation reactors calculate risk far beyond the original 1-in-10,000 benchmark once set by the U.S. NRC, with passive safety systems, stronger flood and seismic protections, and automatic decay-heat removal.

ENFORCEMENT
Japan's rejection of Tsuruga No. 2

Under revised seismic rules, regulators rejected the reactor — proof that safety is enforced, not just promised. The OECD/NEA's FRAME initiative further strengthens preparedness.

06  ·  Implications

Test-Bed vs. Rightful Adopter

The difference between chasing novelty and claiming a right.

✗ Becoming a Stage for Foreign Experiments
✗Leaping ahead of richer, deeper-budgeted countries that remain cautious on thorium — not pioneering, but reckless
✗Treating supplier countries' permission as the deciding factor in Indonesia's nuclear future
✓ Claiming Nuclear as a Right
✓Anchoring the grid in proven reactors while monitoring thorium and SMR progress abroad
✓Activating roughly 1,500 trained but underutilized nuclear engineers into a serious national program
✓Securing nuclear technology as a right of an independent nation, not a favor to be granted

07  ·  Conclusion

Independence Is Ours, and Never for Trade

To surrender choice is to surrender sovereignty.

1

Anchor the national grid in proven pressurized water, boiling water, or CANDU reactors — technologies with 18,000-plus reactor-years of safe operation.

2

Treat thorium and SMRs as technologies to monitor and consider for remote archipelago regions in the future — not as today's energy backbone.

3

Activate Indonesia's dormant nuclear workforce as an industrial policy choice, not merely an energy one, securing human capital sovereignty alongside technical sovereignty.

Closing

Indonesia must not be a stage for foreign experiments. Proven nuclear power is the path we claim — not as a gift, but as a right. That choice secures our energy, our safety, and our dignity, and it seals one truth: independence is ours — and never for trade.
