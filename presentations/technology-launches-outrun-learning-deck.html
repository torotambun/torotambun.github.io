When new technology launches outrun learning

Recognition for launching a technology can arrive years before staff learn to make it work. Institutions should be judged on retained capability and verified operating results rather than inaugurations, while the claim that launches interrupt learning remains a hypothesis to be tested.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Leaders can be recognised for adopting a technology before anyone establishes that it improves the work.

"Technology can work well in an advanced economy, but for an Indonesian institution it can be a poor investment."

Situation

"Since the 1990s, Indonesian institutions have channeled substantial resources into successive tech waves, from internet businesses and digital start-ups to smart cities, blockchain and artificial intelligence."

Complication

"Leaders may receive recognition for adopting it before their staff establish whether it improves the work."

Question

"How much of that work remains useful, and how much knowledge have subsequent initiatives inherited?"

Answer

"Answering those questions requires following the people, investments and operating results beyond the announcements."

Framework

Gumun culture names the habit of adopting technology to signal modernity before checking fit or building capability.

"The Javanese warning aja gumunan cautions against being too easily impressed. Gumun means astonishment, while gumunan describes someone readily impressed."

Gumun culture "illustrates how institutions adopt advanced technologies performatively—chasing recognition by signaling modernity before assessing internal fit or building capability."

Koentjaraningrat illustrated a related concern in Rintangan2 Mental Dalam Pembangunan Ekonomi di Indonesia, describing "attention to the publicly visible parts of a house while its kitchen and toilet remained neglected." The column uses it as an analogy only: applying it to institutions "requires evidence about what their leaders recognize, fund and maintain."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

An institution can run the activities it promised without showing that they build the competence it promised.

Bromley and Powell (2012) separate two kinds of decoupling.

Policy-practice decoupling
•Commitments "go unimplemented".
Means-ends decoupling
✗Activities occur "but their connection to intended outcomes remains uncertain."
✗"A university can operate a laboratory and conduct training without demonstrating that either improves the technical competence it promised to develop."
✗"Counting facilities and participants cannot establish that result."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

A launch fits one reporting cycle while reliable operation takes years, which may push leaders toward new rollouts over maintenance.

1  ·  The timing mismatch
"Leaders can deliver a launch within a single reporting cycle, while staff need years to ensure reliable operation."
2  ·  The incentive
"When recognition rewards inaugurations over sustained performance, leaders naturally favor new visible rollouts over maintenance."
3  ·  Legitimate reasons to change course
"Legitimate factors—such as funding cuts, obsolescence or failed experiments—can also warrant shifting course."
4  ·  What would prove it
"Interrupted learning is supported only if leaders pulled funding or personnel from viable projects without sufficient justification."
Status of the claim

"This incentive dynamic remains a hypothesis." The author's doctoral work motivates the concern, but "they do not establish how commonly Indonesian institutions interrupt useful work to pursue another technology."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

The unfinished institutional S-curve describes learning that may be lost when support is withdrawn before staff have documented it.

"After purchasing a system, staff may still need substantial experience before they can diagnose failures and improve its performance."

"If managers withdraw support during that period, the organization may lose knowledge before staff have documented it or passed it to successors."

For civil servants the stakes are concrete: "Completing a purchase is meaningless unless citizens can reliably use the service."

A stated limit

"No national institutional S-curve has been measured here. The concept identifies a process to investigate rather than a finding about Indonesia as a whole."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

Completion means a usable result, which can include a failed experiment or a retired system whose lessons are kept.

"Completion also depends on the purpose of the commitment."

01
Public services
A service citizens can use
"Effective handovers require more than procurement paperwork."
02
Research
A finding others can use
Researchers can complete an experiment "by establishing that an approach does not work, provided they record findings that others can use."
03
Replacement
Knowledge kept for the successor
Managers replacing an obsolete system "should explain their decision and preserve knowledge useful to its successor."
04
Review
No reward for hiding failure
Reviews should recognise these outcomes "so that staff are not encouraged to continue unsuitable projects merely to avoid reporting failure."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Evidence

Schneider Electric's Batam factory and Polytron show adoption that goes beyond announcement, while export volume alone shows no engineering depth.

"Evidence of operational improvement challenges any claim that Indonesian institutions merely announce adoption."

EVIDENCE A
Downtime cut by 44 percent
AVEVA reports that Schneider Electric's Batam factory cut downtime by 44 percent via digital production systems, "a verifiable operating result, even if broader adoption rates remain unproven."
EVIDENCE B
In-house R&D at Polytron
Polytron's 2025 disclosures detail in-house R&D across software, printed circuit boards, electronic and mechanical engineering, "disproving claims that domestic electronics firms merely assemble foreign parts."
EVIDENCE C
518,212 vehicles exported in 2025
GAIKINDO's figure validates volume, but "it does not reveal local engineering depth or retained domestic value".
EVIDENCE D
Targets that run to 2030
Making Indonesia 4.0's five initial sector ambitions extend to 2030, "so an unfinished objective is not automatically an overdue promise."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

A newer technology can deepen earlier capability, but only if it is tested against the institution's own work.

Building on what exists
✓"AI engineering shows how a newer initiative can deepen earlier capability."
✓An organisation already using a language model may add authorised access to records, persistent task information and error recovery, the surrounding software "often called an agent harness".
✓"imported robots can still drive technical learning if local engineers learn to diagnose faults and adapt deployments."
What a demonstration cannot prove
✗"Demonstrating a task under controlled conditions proves neither multi-shift reliability nor cost-effective maintenance."
✗Foreign corporate adoption "cannot prove compatibility with a different organization's records, skills or responsibilities."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Recommendations

Baselines before rollout, longitudinal reviews and funding for continuity would test the hypothesis and protect capability.

1
Give employees standing to question new tools
"Employees should have the professional standing to question new tools."
2
Set the task and the baseline before rollout
"Before rollout, managers must specify the target task and establish baseline performance." Staff then track whether the tool cuts errors and workload, "factoring in time spent auditing and correcting output."
3
Weigh lifecycle costs against alternatives
"Any evaluation must weigh actual operating performance and lifecycle costs against conventional alternatives."
4
Review across technology cycles
"Policymakers should mandate longitudinal reviews across successive technology cycles." Retained teams, data and procedures with measurable gains "directly refutes the interrupted learning hypothesis."
5
Fund continuity and publish the audits
"Funding criteria must reward this continuity while allowing justified project wind-downs." Public institutions should publish the audits and boards "must ensure that operational capability outlasts management turnover."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

Rewarding adoption alone lets institutions buy the next technology before the last one has paid back.

When existing technology still has much to offer, why does adopting something newer satisfy us more than learning to use what we already have?

The cost of the reward
"Continually rewarding tech adoption lets leaders feign progress while neglecting capability building."
What gumun does next
"Gumun then drives premature acquisition before prior investments yield productive returns."
