Why Meritocracy Is Key to
Democracy, National Unity

A merit-based system that ignores unequal starting lines is not meritocracy at all — it is privilege in disguise, and left unaddressed, it corrodes the very trust that democracy depends on.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
A graduate of Harvard Business School and an affiliated alumnus of MIT Sloan School of Management.
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
01  ·  Introduction

An allegory of the puppet master’s mantle.

In the storied lands of Kelaraja, the rank of Dhalang was earned through talent and dedication — until the kingdom nominated a prince who had barely trained.

Situation

Becoming a Dhalang, the puppet master entrusted with Kelaraja’s tales and cultural memory, was long an embodiment of merit: ascending the rank required talent and dedication.

Complication

When the council nominates the inexperienced young prince Raden Lintang as the next Dhalang, rumors of King Wisnu’s influence spread — the revered tradition now appears tainted by politics.

Question

Is a captured meritocracy simply a matter of fairness, or does it threaten something larger?

Answer

It strikes at the foundation of democracy itself — when citizens sense that talent and effort are not the deciding factors, cynicism spreads, national unity fragments, and the collective belief in a fair shot at success erodes.

02  ·  Gap

Meritocracy is discussed as if the race starts from one line.

Public debate over fairness rarely accounts for the structural obstacles that determine who reaches the starting line at all.

Core Gap

The ideal of meritocracy envisions a fair race, where every participant stands shoulder to shoulder at the starting line. But Dr. Harry Passow’s (1916–1996) documented obstacles — poverty, limited language development, home–school value gaps, minority status, health, geographic isolation, indifferent environments, under-resourced schools, and eroded self-concept — mean many children never reach that line on equal footing. A merit-based system that does not account for these disparities is inherently flawed and biased.

03  ·  Framework

Three Forces That Determine Whether Merit Is Real

Each force operates independently; together they explain when a meritocracy is genuine and when it is captured.

01
The Allegory’s Warning

Raden Lintang’s elevation shows how systems can be quietly influenced by those already in positions of authority — not through outright favoritism alone, but through the ordinary workings of proximity to power.

02
The Starting-Line Fallacy

Passow’s obstacles — income, language, geography, health, school quality, self-belief — reveal that “equal opportunity” is often asserted rather than actually built into the system.

03
The Democratic Stakes

When large sections of the populace feel denied their rightful place, disillusionment breeds discontent and distrust — weakening the nation’s cohesion and collective hope, not just individual careers.

04  ·  Details

Four Starting-Line Disparities, Paired Against Privilege

The piece pairs each of Passow’s obstacles against Raden Lintang’s untouched advantage — making the disparity concrete rather than abstract.

Family poverty

A young woman from the slums of Jakarta struggles in a cramped home, while Raden Lintang, in his opulent palace, is surrounded by tutors and the best educational resources money can buy.

Language and mental-ability development

Children in remote Sumatra grow up with limited exposure beyond their mother tongue, while Raden Lintang, royally educated, fluently speaks multiple languages with instructors from across the archipelago.

Geographic isolation

Children on the remote Riau Islands have limited opportunities, while Raden Lintang, with his royal fleet, travels and learns from cultures across the world.

Self-concept

A young woman from inner-city Cikini begins to believe she is “not smart enough,” while Raden Lintang, buoyed by constant affirmation and lineage, grows up believing he is destined for greatness.

05  ·  Evidence

Three Sources Behind the Argument

A cultural allegory, an educational-obstacles framework, and a closing moral voice ground the piece’s claims.

EVIDENCE A
The Dhalang tradition

The Wayang puppet-master role is presented as an early, culturally embedded notion of meritocracy — ascension required talent and dedication, and the role carried collective memory and unity.

EVIDENCE B
Dr. Harry Passow’s obstacle framework

Passow (1916–1996) documented the multifaceted, often-overlooked obstacles — from poverty to school quality — that create disparities in access to opportunity well before merit is ever tested.

EVIDENCE C
Nyai Saras’s counsel

The wise counselor’s judgment that “the mantle of traditions should be passed based on dedication, passion, and true merit” frames the piece’s normative standard against which the prince’s elevation is measured.

EVIDENCE D
Bima’s closing wisdom

“A kingdom’s strength isn’t just in its fertile lands or mighty warriors but in the hearts of its people, their dreams, and aspirations” — the moral center the piece returns to in closing.

06  ·  Implications

Diagnosis: What Happens When Merit Is Captured

The damage is not confined to the individuals passed over — it compounds into the social fabric.

✗ What a Captured Meritocracy Produces
✗Cynicism and disillusionment among those who believed in the “fair game”
✗Resentment, reduced collective productivity, and stifled innovation
✗Fragmented national unity and diluted collective aspiration
✓ What a Genuine Meritocracy Requires
✓Explicit accounting for starting-line disparities in income, language, geography, and health
✓Selection processes insulated from capture by proximity to power
✓A genuine understanding of diverse life experiences built into how “merit” itself is defined
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
07  ·  Conclusion

Rebuilding the Starting Line

Three recommendations for closing the gap between meritocracy’s promise and its practice.

1

Audit high-visibility selection and succession processes for capture by proximity to power, not just for procedural compliance.

2

Invest early — in nutrition, language development, and school quality — to equalize the starting line before merit is ever tested.

3

Embed Passow-style obstacle mapping into education and youth policy design, rather than treating disparity as a footnote.

Closing

To rob even one citizen of the right to dream is to chip away at the very soul of democracy.
