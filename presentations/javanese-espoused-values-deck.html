Believe It or Not,
Javanese Values Breed Innovation

Indonesia already has, encoded in Javanese proverbs, the espoused values psychology links to entrepreneurial success. The unfinished work is converting them from espoused values into basic, muscle-memory ones.

01  ·  Introduction

The traits recruiters screen for already have Javanese names.

Four years of recruiting proto-entrepreneurs against the Big Five personality model.

Situation

Strong companies recruit for corporate fit using tools like StrengthFinder and the Big Five: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism. Entrepreneurs score higher on Openness and Conscientiousness, lower on Agreeableness and Neuroticism, than managers (Zhao & Seibert, 2006).

Complication

In four years of recruiting and co-learning with proto-entrepreneurs, fewer than 2 percent went on to become innovation-driven entrepreneurs. Brutal facts, in Admiral Stockdale’s sense.

Question

Does Indonesia already possess the cultural raw material for these traits, or must it be imported wholesale?

Answer

Javanese proverbs already encode Openness, Conscientiousness, Low Agreeableness and Emotional Stability. What is missing is not the value — it is its conversion from espoused value into basic, automatic value.

02  ·  Research Gap

Having the proverb is not the same as living it.

Espoused values and basic values are treated as interchangeable in most cultural-fit discourse. They are not.

Core Gap

Having espoused values is not enough to mobilize a country toward a greater cause. They need to be translated into muscle memory, into mental model, into system-1 thinking — into “basic values,” where the proverb becomes reflex rather than recitation. Without that translation, a culture can quietly vanish, absorbed as an inferior subculture the moment it meets a more inundated one.

It starts at the nucleus family, expands through schools, peers, and adolescent exposure — the myelination of a value until it sparks a live, inspired part of the brain. That pipeline, not the proverb itself, is what is missing.

03  ·  Framework

Four Entrepreneurial Traits, Four Javanese Value Clusters

Each Big Five trait linked to entrepreneurship maps onto an existing cluster of Javanese proverbs.

01
Openness to Experience

Ora rumongso bisa, nanging bisa rumongso — always feel yourself the stupidest in the room, so there is always room to learn. Manjing ajur-ajer — stay flexible, adapt to every condition.

02
Conscientiousness

Gemi, nastiti, ngati-ati — structured, careful, compliant, never wildly risk-taking. Alon-alon asal kelakon — not slowness, but calculated risk with a route of feedback.

03
Low Agreeableness

Tan ban cindhe ban siladan — uphold law and principle without discrimination, even against family and friends. Becik ketitik ala ketaro — integrity and reputation at all costs.

04
Emotional Stability

Yen kepergok ing pacoban aja lumaris — never give up, never quit. Sabar subur — patience is a virtue.

04  ·  Details

The Conversion Pipeline: Espoused → Basic

Where a value is stated matters less than where it is practiced until it becomes automatic.

Stage 1 — Nucleus family

“It takes a whole village to raise a kid” (African proverb, cited in the original). The proverb is first heard, then modelled, inside the household — long before any classroom or accelerator sees the child.

Stage 2 — School and peers

Repetition across school and peer exposure during adolescence is what myelinates a value — converts a heard proverb into a reflex response under pressure, not just a quotable saying.

Stage 3 — The recruitment mirror

The Big Five screen recruiters already run (Openness, Conscientiousness, low Agreeableness, low Neuroticism) is, structurally, a test for whether that myelination happened — whether the proverb became basic value or stayed espoused.

05  ·  Evidence

What the Recruitment Data Actually Shows

Four years of hands-on recruitment against a stated psychological baseline.

EVIDENCE A
Entrepreneurial personality baseline

Zhao & Seibert (2006): entrepreneurs score significantly higher on Openness and Conscientiousness, significantly lower on Agreeableness and Neuroticism, than managers. Extraversion shows no significant difference.

EVIDENCE B
Conversion-rate data

Fewer than 2 percent of recruited proto-entrepreneurs became innovation-driven entrepreneurs over four years; roughly 5 percent chose SME paths. Yet 80 percent graduated with measurably more entrepreneurial thinking than they entered with.

EVIDENCE C
The unscreened baseline

Without any recruitment criteria applied, the population splits: 93 percent enter linear industry-of-study careers, 6 percent seek government-backed employment, only 1 percent seek entrepreneurship or other paths.

EVIDENCE D
The proverb corpus

A working set of Javanese proverbs mapped directly onto Openness, Conscientiousness, Low Agreeableness and Emotional Stability — drawn from Javanese oral tradition, limited deliberately to the most protrude and prevalent values shared across Nusantara.

06  ·  Implications

Diagnosis: Where the Conversion Breaks Down

The proverb is rarely the missing input. The pipeline that converts it is.

✗ Wrong Assumption
✗Treating Javanese culture as lacking entrepreneurial values, and importing foreign frameworks wholesale
✗Teaching the proverbs as heritage content, disconnected from recruitment or curriculum design
✗Expecting a single accelerator cohort to instill what takes a generation of family and school exposure
✓ Right Work
✓Designing family and school programmes that model the proverb, not just recite it
✓Using the existing Big Five recruitment tools as a diagnostic for conversion, not just selection
✓Treating sub-culture factories — the way Japan, China, Korea, Singapore build theirs — as infrastructure, not accident
© 2023 Dr. Toronata Tambun
07  ·  Conclusion

Investing in Human Capital Takes Generations — No Shortcut

Three recommendations for closing the espoused-to-basic gap.

1

Build family-level programmes that model Openness, Conscientiousness and Emotional Stability through practice, not proverb recitation.

2

Use existing Big Five recruitment tools diagnostically — to measure conversion, not merely to filter candidates.

3

Invest in the sub-culture factory — the role-model pipeline — the way Japan, China, Korea and Singapore already have.

Closing

Without the right basic values, a culture may vanish — more subtly integrated as an inferior subculture — when confronted with other, more inundated cultures. Investing in human capital is the common theme among nations that avoided this. It takes generations. There is no shortcut.
