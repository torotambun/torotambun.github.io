Village Cooperatives Have Existed
Before. What Will Make the New Ones Last?

Indonesia is building Koperasi Desa/Kelurahan Merah Putih on a scale rarely attempted in the country's cooperative history. Its scale has made the number established an immediate measure of progress. Yet registration does not determine whether a cooperative belongs to its members.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus. Doctorate from ITB in innovation and entrepreneurial ecosystems.
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
01  ·  Introduction

Economic Democracy, Not Legal Form

Mohammad Hatta placed member control at the centre of cooperation — collective ownership, self-help, participation, and moral responsibility.

Situation

Presidential Instruction No. 9 of 2025 directed the accelerated formation of 80,000 cooperatives at a scale rarely attempted in Indonesia's cooperative history.

Complication

A cooperative could distribute goods or perform a public assignment, but those activities alone do not make it democratic. That depends on whether villagers can contribute in ways they recognize and retain control.

Question

What will actually make the new cooperatives last, beyond the registration count?

Answer

KDMP will last where villagers can contribute what they actually possess, see what happens to it, and control the institution through procedures they trust — the standard Hatta set for economic democracy.

02  ·  Gap

Collectivist, but Deferential to Hierarchy

The question is not whether Indonesians can cooperate. It is whether collective habits can be converted into member control without hierarchy taking over.

Core Gap

The Culture Factor's 2023 revision scores Indonesia at 5 for Individualism, 78 for Power Distance, and 29 for Long-Term Orientation — an extremely collectivist society with strong deference to hierarchy and a preference for visible results within a short period.

03  ·  Framework

What History and Culture Both Show

State initiation is part of both the old and new cooperative histories — it cannot explain which ones develop continuing economic lives.

1
Hatta's Economic Democracy

Members had to develop the discipline required to manage a shared economic institution — character comes from accepting ownership and participating in decisions.

2
Arisan as Cultural Clue

Participants contribute because the arrangement is familiar, the cycle is visible, and each person expects to recover the amount contributed — showing why visible contributions and principal protection matter.

3
The BUUD/KUD Precedent

KUD also grew through presidential instructions and public financing — what varied was local demand, management capability, and member commitment, not state initiation itself.

4
Japanese and German Comparisons

Cooperative federations lasted because members continued using them and retained authority over management — not because of how they were initiated.

04  ·  Details

Repayment May Outpace Confidence

These are loans, not permanent grants — and repayment may begin before members have developed trust in the business.

Rp3 billion at 6 percent, 72-month tenor

Minister of Finance Regulation No. 49 of 2025 allows each cooperative to borrow up to Rp3 billion, with a grace period of six months that may extend to eight. If early results are unclear, participation may weaken before a stable market develops.

Contribution need not be cash

In many villages, members may have little disposable savings. Their contribution may come through produce, livestock, labour, purchases, or modest regular deposits — what matters is a recognizable claim over the institution.

Village-head approval may overshadow membership

Because the loan requires village-head approval following deliberation, official authority may overshadow membership from the start — a cooperative may hold meetings without transferring real decision-making power to them.

05  ·  Evidence

Overlapping With Existing Institutions

The regulation recognizes the issue but does not decide which institution should operate which business.

INSTITUTIONAL OVERLAP
BUM Desa vs. cooperative ownership

A BUM Desa is owned through the village, whereas a cooperative is owned and governed by its members, yet both may operate similar businesses — pursuing the same customers, suppliers, and assets without local differentiation.

SPEED VS. AGREEMENT
Incorporation ahead of business consensus

Officials have strong incentives to complete incorporation quickly. The problem begins when incorporation moves ahead of agreement over the cooperative's business and membership.

06  ·  Implications

Judged at Registration vs. After Registration

Loan approvals and meeting records alone cannot establish whether a cooperative belongs to its members.

✗ Registration as the Measure of Progress
✗Treating the number of cooperatives established as the immediate measure of progress
✗Officials continuing to make decisions that belong to members after the cooperative is formed
✓ Gotong Royong as Collective Supervision
✓Members able to demand explanations for losses and remove managers who repeatedly fail
✓Judging the program by whether members continue using the cooperative and whether their contributions are protected

07  ·  Conclusion

Owners on Paper, or Owners in Practice

Where key decisions remain concentrated among officials, the cooperative may meet its legal requirements while members remain owners only on paper.

1

Let villagers contribute what they actually possess — produce, labour, or modest deposits — and give them a recognizable claim over the institution.

2

Build collective supervision into gotong royong, not just initial labour contribution — members must be able to challenge management.

3

Evaluate KDMP after registration, not at registration — the relevant evidence is continued use, protected contributions, and challengeable management.

Closing

KDMP will last where villagers can contribute what they actually possess and see what happens to it. They must also be able to control the institution through procedures they trust.
