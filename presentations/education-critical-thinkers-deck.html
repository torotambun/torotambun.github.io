Education should engender critical thinkers

Job placement shows that a graduate entered work, not that the graduate can evaluate a claim or justify a decision. Schools, assessments and teacher training should be held to evidence of critical thinking alongside occupational competence.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Orderly conduct and job readiness do not show whether a graduate can think critically.

The column begins at a vocational secondary school the author visited, where discipline, appearance and cleanliness were enforced through directive leadership.

Situation

The author has long advocated vocational education "precisely because it provides a reliable gateway to employment for families who cannot afford prolonged income uncertainty." The school's priorities "serve legitimate ends, particularly where physical safety hinges on strict adherence to protocol."

Complication

"Yet orderly conduct and job readiness do not indicate whether a graduate can think critically. Schools also prepare citizens whose duties extend well beyond the workplace."

Question

Has occupational placement been allowed to stand as a sufficient measure of educational success?

Answer

"Education must teach people both how to think and what to do."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Gap

A conformist student delivers the expected answer, while a critical thinker defends it or shows why it should be challenged.

"Critical thinking demands sufficient domain knowledge to interrogate a claim, test its underlying logic and revise a conclusion when evidence demands it."

What compliance and fluency show
✗"A conformist student merely delivers the expected answer"
✗"A student can rehearse a convincing pitch while leaving a mistaken customer assumption untouched."
✗"Confidence of delivery supplies little evidence of the quality of the argument."
What critical thinking requires
✓"a critical thinker defends why that answer holds merit or demonstrates why it should be challenged."
✓"Teachers have to examine the student's understanding and reasoning to discover that weakness."
✓"Knowing what action to take also requires weighing consequences for others."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

Sixteen cohorts of entrepreneurship teaching raised the question of what participation in activities actually proves.

Following entrepreneurship education at Babson College and the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, and drawing on industry experience, the author taught over 16 cohorts, each lasting one semester.

"Despite intensive lectures, client interactions and practitioner presentations, the curriculum failed to consistently cultivate the entrepreneurial judgment I envisioned." That gap prompted his doctoral inquiry into a corporate-backed, university-based innovation ecosystem.

The column keeps the limit of this evidence in view: "My classroom experience cannot isolate the effects of particular methods. They forced me to question what participation in educational activities proves."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

Classical education separated comprehension, validity and expression, and trained minds to see relationships beyond immediate use.

"While this historical schema cannot serve as an off-the-shelf curriculum for contemporary Indonesia, it exemplifies an educational ambition that transcends vocational training".

01
Trivium
Grammar
"Grammar governed the comprehension and mechanics of language".
02
Trivium
Logic
Logic "interrogated validity". A learner "might grasp the literal meaning of a proposition yet miss a logical fallacy".
03
Trivium
Rhetoric
Rhetoric "structured and communicated compelling arguments". A learner may spot the flaw "but lack the skill to articulate it."
04
Quadrivium
Arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, music
"These disciplines trained minds to perceive abstract relationships beyond immediate utility."
A stated limit

Dorothy Sayers later linked foundational knowledge to inquiry and articulate expression, "though her framework was philosophical rather than an empirically validated sequence of cognitive development."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Evidence

Retrieval and spaced practice improve retention, but recalling a proposition is not the same as evaluating it.

Christopher Perrin's modern synthesis of classical pedagogy emphasizes depth of study, measured pacing and deliberate practice, "though each element rests on differing bodies of evidence."

What the research supports
✓Cognitive research by Roediger and Karpicke, alongside Cepeda and colleagues, "demonstrates the power of retrieval and spaced practice for retention."
What retention leaves open
✗"Yet recalling a proposition does not mean a student can evaluate it."
✗"Instructors must deliberately engineer opportunities for students to interrogate assumptions and update their views."
✗"Pedagogical methods must be evaluated by whether they cultivate these higher-order capacities."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Evidence

Indonesian educators already tied independent, capable learners to obligations toward society.

"Indonesian educators discovered this intellectual independence could develop through practical schooling."

01
INS Kayutanam
Mohammad Sjafei
In Sutan Takdir Alisjahbana's 1966 account, INS Kayutanam "combined agriculture and manual trades with artistic activities to advance autonomous, practically capable learners."
02
Teacher responsibility
Ki Hadjar Dewantara
His principles "placed responsibility on teachers to encourage initiative and guide learners toward directing their own work."
03
Social obligation
Dr. Soetomo and Dr. Radjiman Wedyodiningrat
Alisjahbana discussed Soetomo's interest in cooperatives and community education, while Radjiman "questioned individual advancement detached from responsibility toward society."
A stated limit

"These educators held different positions, and their intentions do not prove what their institutions achieved." Their work still supplies precedents, a concern that "belongs in education for citizens as well as workers."

Implications

Teachers reward conformity when they treat a reasoned objection as disobedience.

The author proposes "performative culture" to describe "a possible pattern in which status and apparent compliance receive greater attention than the capabilities they supposedly represent."

The column does not claim this pattern is settled: "Schools where discipline and independent thought coexist could challenge a simple account of conformity."

Placement figures cannot settle the question either. They "measure entry into work and depend partly on the opportunities an economy provides. They do not show whether graduates can evaluate a claim or justify a decision."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Recommendations

Authorities, assessments and teacher training should require observable evidence of critical thinking alongside occupational competence.

1
Evaluate schools on evidence of critical thinking
"Education authorities should require evidence of critical thinking when evaluating schools." Vocational programs "should be accountable for demonstrating those capabilities alongside occupational competence."
2
Make assessments ask why, when it fails, and who bears the impact
"Assessments must require students to explain why a protocol functions, identify the conditions under which it fails and substantiate an alternative approach with evidence." Students must also say who bears the impact of a proposed decision and justify its broader intent.
3
Test whether learners can correct their own logic
"Instructors should evaluate whether learners can detect and rectify flaws in their own logic". These exercises give critical thinking "an observable meaning beyond fluent speech or confident disagreement."
4
Train teachers to welcome reasoned challenge
"Grading rubrics should reward thoroughly defended conclusions, even when they contradict an instructor's stance." Evaluators should check "whether dissent is investigated on its merits or met with administrative reprisal."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

Schools that put conformity above judgment risk leaving citizens waiting for instructions when jobs disappear.

If schools prioritize conformity over critical judgment, will they supply obedient employees when jobs are available and leave conformist citizens waiting for instructions when jobs are unavailable?

Where the column began
"Training a competent factory floor operator is an achievement."
What it asks of schools
"Schools also prepare citizens whose duties extend well beyond the workplace."
