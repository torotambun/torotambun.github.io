Vietnam’s Secret Sauce:
A Blend of Tradition and Tomorrow

Vietnam’s climb to 46th in the Global Innovation Index is not explained by its visible artefacts — the roads, the clean water, the digitally fluent students. It is explained by shared values Indonesia, despite near-identical Hofstede scores, has not converted into the same tolerance for uncertainty.

01  ·  Introduction

Twenty-six years of visits, one ranking that keeps climbing.

A SCQA reading, from a first 1998 visit as a Schlumberger employee to a 2023 high-school workshop.

Situation

Vietnam has risen two places to 46th in the 2023 Global Innovation Index (WIPO) — one of only three nations outperforming expectations for its development level, improving both input and output performance.

Complication

Nations admire and try to replicate Vietnam’s visible artefacts — infrastructure, literacy, digital fluency — while sharing near-identical Hofstede scores on power distance, individualism, masculinity and long-term orientation with Indonesia.

Question

What actually distinguishes Vietnam’s innovation trajectory, if the visible artefacts and most cultural dimensions are shared with regional peers?

Answer

Uncertainty Avoidance is the one dimension that diverges sharply — Vietnam at 30, Indonesia at 48 — and that single divergence, combined with visionary leadership, explains the gap the artefacts alone cannot.

02  ·  Research Gap

Emulation targets the artefact, not the variable that moves it.

Regional development discourse treats Vietnam’s achievements as a replicable checklist rather than a specific cultural variable.

Core Gap

In the pursuit of emulation, nations aspire to replicate the visible list of Vietnamese achievements. What remains hidden beneath the surface is the decades — centuries — invested in the values that fuel them. The objects of admiration are merely artefacts (Schein).

03  ·  Framework

Three Artefact Clusters, One Shared-Value Substrate

Visible achievement, and the cultural substrate beneath it, read as two separate layers.

01
Rural Transformation

Mekong Delta villages expected to be rudimentary instead had paved roads, prosperous homes, and modern electrical infrastructure — rural growth pursued alongside, not after, urban growth.

02
Agricultural Evolution

From subsistence rice terraces to coffee plantations, fruit orchards, and advanced irrigation — Vietnam moved from primary importer to global agricultural powerhouse.

03
R&D and Entrepreneurship

An innovation-friendly environment tapping the Vietnamese diaspora and securing global R&D funds — sitting atop a literacy rate near 95 percent.

04  ·  Details

The Cultural Substrate Beneath the Artefacts

Edgar Schein’s framework separates the visible from what actually drives it.

Resilience and Confucian duty

A history of resilience against foreign adversaries fostered a robust national identity; Confucian teachings on respect and collective duty establish an inclusive environment conducive to innovation.

Agricultural roots and family bonds

Agricultural roots cultivated resourcefulness that became a precursor to contemporary innovation; strong family and community bonds carry knowledge exchange beyond formal settings.

Reverence for ancestors

A deep reverence for ancestors lets innovators harmonize past lessons with present challenges — a compass, not a constraint, on forward motion.

05  ·  Evidence

The Number That Diverges

A direct Hofstede comparison isolates the one variable that actually differs.

EVIDENCE A
GII 2023 ranking detail

Vietnam improved input performance to 57th from 59th, and output performance to 40th — one of only three nations outperforming expectations for its development level (WIPO).

EVIDENCE B
Shared Hofstede dimensions

Vietnam and Indonesia share similar scores on power distance, individualism, masculinity, and long-term orientation — ruling these out as the explanatory variable.

EVIDENCE C
The Uncertainty Avoidance divergence

Vietnam scores 30 on Uncertainty Avoidance against Indonesia’s 48 — high-UAI societies prioritize rules, structure and convention; the lower score frees Vietnam for comfort with ambiguity.

EVIDENCE D
Direct workshop observation

A July workshop with Vietnamese high-school students produced case-study-level presentations within 30 minutes, with both parents present even with younger siblings in tow — rare across 51 countries of comparable workshops.

06  ·  Implications

Diagnosis: What Emulation Gets Backward

Copying the artefact list without addressing the UAI gap will not close the gap.

✗ What Emulation Targets
✗Infrastructure checklists — roads, clean water, digital literacy programs
✗Literacy-rate and R&D-spend benchmarking against Vietnam’s published figures
✓ What Actually Needs Addressing
✓The Uncertainty Avoidance gap itself — policy tolerance for ambiguity and non-rule-bound experimentation
✓Leadership models that pair long-term vision with comfort under uncertainty
✓Family and community structures that carry knowledge exchange the way Vietnam’s do

07  ·  Conclusion

The Journey Is Solitary and Uncertain — That Is the Point

Three recommendations for policymakers tempted by the artefact checklist.

1

Measure and track Uncertainty Avoidance as a policy variable, not just infrastructure and literacy benchmarks.

2

Design rural-growth investment to run alongside urban growth, not after it — following the Mekong Delta pattern.

3

Study and adapt the leadership model, not just the outcomes, behind Vietnam’s rise.

Closing

The journey to reproduce Vietnam’s achievements is solitary and uncertain, with real potential for failure — and the hallmark of national greatness is embracing that path regardless. Combined with visionary leadership, Vietnam’s shared values have propelled its innovative rise, setting a precedent the rest of the region, Indonesia included, has yet to fully absorb.
