Borrowed Standards, Buried Sovereignty:
Indonesia's Nuclear Dilemma

Of 52 nuclear reactor construction starts worldwide from 2017 to 2024, none used American technology — yet Indonesia has aligned itself with an unproven U.S. small modular reactor platform. This is not classic espionage. It is what intelligence professionals call shaping: steering a country's choices through soft tools before decisions are even made.

01  ·  Introduction

Not readiness. Positioning.

What looks like cooperation is often someone else's strategy.

Situation

Indonesia's nuclear plans are often explained as a matter of technical readiness — whether the country can build and operate a reactor.

Complication

In reality, it is not only about energy or engineering but about where Indonesia is placed in the global order. In 2023, Indonesia Power signed a deal to study NuScale's SMR, a design that has never reached commercial deployment anywhere in the world.

Question

Can Indonesia govern its nuclear future without being steered by the terms of whoever is offering the technology?

Answer

The real question isn't whether Indonesia can build nuclear — it's whether it can govern it without being steered. Once a country adopts an outside standard, it takes on audits, fuel shipments, and dispute mechanisms it no longer controls.

02  ·  Gap

Epistemic drift, not occupation

The vulnerability is not financial. It is deeper.

Core Gap

Indonesia is not under occupation, but it is being steered. The vulnerability isn't financial; it is deeper. We're fluent in borrowed language but disconnected from our internal tempo. This is epistemic drift, when the ability to frame decisions slips away. It's a kind of institutional disorientation, where familiar processes mask a loss of orientation. And it makes steering easy.

03  ·  Framework

Three Platforms, One Pattern

Whoever the vendor, the structure of dependency repeats.

01
NuScale SMR (United States)

A small modular reactor design that has yet to operate commercially anywhere, adopted through a 2023 study agreement carrying a narrative of trust and modernity rather than proof.

02
ThorCon Molten Salt (Thorium)

A U.S.-based firm has signed early agreements to deploy a thorium molten salt reactor in Bangka — a technology that has not reached global maturity even in its home market.

03
Akademik Lomonosov-Style Barge (Russia)

A floated alternative mobile reactor platform — the vendor changes, but the underlying issue stays the same: Indonesia does not set the terms.

04  ·  Details

How Shaping Actually Works

No cloak, no covert entry — just platforms, advisors, and timed offerings.

The Intelligence Cycle, Applied Openly

Direction, collection, processing, analysis, dissemination — combined with "phase zero" shaping operations, these tools influence environments before decisions are even made, and none of it requires secrecy.

Soft Tools of Influence

Grants, training, partnerships, and embedded consultants shift a country's future choices without classic espionage — workshops, study tours, and MOUs achieve placement without resistance.

Performance Replacing Reflection

As imported models entered, Indonesia accepted urgency; visibility became performance and reports replaced reflection — but nuclear energy doesn't forgive performance.

Singapore's Counter-Example

Singapore delays its nuclear path not because it cannot build, but because it won't hand over the long game to others — a contrast in strategic patience.

06  ·  Implications

Diagnosis: Visibility or Sovereignty?

The two are easily confused, and only one protects Indonesia's terms.

✗ Mistaking Visibility for Sovereignty
✗Treating a signed MOU or study agreement as proof of readiness, regardless of who actually drew the map
✗Accepting urgency imposed by outside offers rather than pausing to ask who benefits from the timeline
✓ Preserving the Judgment to Pause
✓Asking, regardless of vendor origin, who sets the terms and who draws the map before signing
✓Treating BRIN, PLN, and BAPETEN's readiness claims as separate from whether the environment has been shaped externally
✓Acting from conscience and clarity rather than borrowed pride or external praise

07  ·  Conclusion

The Reactor Isn't the Problem. The Terms Are.

Whose clock is Indonesia's nuclear program actually running on?

1

Ask, before any agreement, who sets the terms and whose long-term interests the platform actually serves.

2

Preserve the institutional capacity to pause and stop, rather than treating urgency as proof of progress.

3

Govern any nuclear commitment from conscience and clarity, not from the narrative momentum of a foreign offer.

Closing

If we go ahead with systems others won't use at home, we aren't leading. We're being used. Let it not be from borrowed pride or external praise, but from conscience and clarity — the only path that won't leave us repeating someone else's plan, on our own soil, with someone else's clock ticking.
