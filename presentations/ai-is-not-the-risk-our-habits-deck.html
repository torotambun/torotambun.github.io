AI Is Not the Risk.
Our Habits Are.

AI attracts attention because it looks powerful. The real test is not the machine. It is the culture that tries to operate it. Sophisticated tools break when placed inside institutions that lack discipline and honesty.

Dr. Toronata Tambun, Director of Yayasan Mens et Manus, and James Kallman, Co-Founder of the Foundation for International Human Rights Reporting Standards.
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
01  ·  Introduction

Technology Reveals. It Does Not Repair.

A new machine placed on top of an old mindset is outwardly modern, inwardly fragile.

Situation

Many countries want advanced AI systems and announce digital transformation initiatives, but wanting is not readiness. Meetings still rely on improvisation; documentation is still avoided.

Complication

Technology exposes the level of discipline, integrity, and critical thinking in the institutions that use it. When these are weak, the system fails no matter how advanced the tool is.

Question

What actually determines whether AI raises a nation's capability or amplifies its weaknesses?

Answer

Culture will decide AI's outcome, not AI itself. A society that strengthens discipline, integrity, and critical thinking will use AI to raise its capabilities; a society that tolerates shortcuts will see AI amplify them.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
02  ·  Gap

Reactive to Symbolism, Hesitant Before Real Suffering

AI does not correct an uneven sense of responsibility. It amplifies it.

Core Gap

In some situations, urgent human needs wait while responses move slowly and unevenly, yet highly visible actions are taken quickly when the spotlight is external. This contrast reveals a deeper structural habit: systems can be reactive to symbolism but hesitant in the face of real suffering. A nation that does not prioritize human dignity in its everyday institutions will struggle with any advanced technology.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
03  ·  Framework

Where AI Readiness Actually Begins

The weakness does not originate in adulthood. It begins much earlier.

1
Childhood Formation

A nation's relationship with technology is shaped in childhood, where habits of curiosity, perseverance, and independent reasoning first form. Environments that punish mistakes and reward compliance produce adults who hesitate to explore.

2
Rent-Seeking Dependency

A nation that imports advanced systems without nurturing its own talent becomes dependent on external expertise. Algorithms are purchased, not built. Sovereignty fades quietly as technical decisions move to external vendors.

3
Governance Amplification

When rules shift depending on personalities, automated systems replicate the inconsistency at scale. When data is adjusted to avoid embarrassment, the system learns to produce comfort instead of truth — and the distortion becomes permanent.

4
Gumun — the Javanese Guardrail

A warning against being easily impressed and displaying what is not yet mastered. Societies that lose this cultural anchor become reactive, insecure, and easily swayed by every technological wave.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
04  ·  Details

Machines Predict. Humans Decide.

Discipline is the silent pillar that holds the whole system together.

AI systems require precise data, consistent processes, honest reporting

These qualities cannot be purchased. They must be lived. AI does not correct conduct — it reflects it, and amplifies it once embedded.

Human in the loop vs. AI in the loop

Human in the loop places human framing, interpretation, and responsibility before the machine. AI in the loop means the system supports the process but never removes human oversight.

The human rights dimension

AI can deepen discrimination, reproduce bias, and expand surveillance when deployed without safeguards. Efficiency without justice creates decisions that are fast but unfair.

05  ·  Evidence

Convergent Findings from AI Scholarship

Across different viewpoints, leading thinkers converge on the same message.

GERRISH, 2018
AI is engineering and feedback

Systems fail when the human loop is weak — the machine cannot compensate for a broken feedback structure around it.

AGRAWAL, GANS & GOLDFARB, 2018
When prediction becomes cheap, judgment becomes the critical asset

Judgment does not grow in cultures that avoid reflective thinking — the scarce resource shifts from computation to discernment.

06  ·  Implications

Chasing Trends vs. Building Capability

One path protects existing advantage; the other expands national competence.

✗ Imitation as Progress
✗Adopting AI tools as symbols of progress rather than instruments of national strength
✗Purchasing algorithms and leasing infrastructure while no true capability is built and no broad-based wealth is created
✓ Discipline as Foundation
✓Building critical thinking and independent reasoning from early childhood, before the technology ever arrives
✓Protecting institutional integrity so automated systems replicate consistency, not inconsistency, at scale
✓Keeping the human at the start of the chain — human framing and responsibility before machine prediction
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
07  ·  Conclusion

What Kind of People Do We Want to Cultivate?

Not how fast AI can be adopted — but what habits a nation builds before it arrives at scale.

1

Strengthen discipline and critical thinking from early years, since AI accelerates processes but does not accelerate maturity.

2

Protect integrity in institutions so data, procedures, and enforcement remain stable inputs an automated system can actually trust.

3

Keep humans in the loop deliberately — machines can predict, but accountability must never be outsourced to the system.

Closing

AI will not decide a nation's future. Culture will. Technology does not create national weaknesses. It reveals them. It magnifies them. It turns them into system behavior.
