Why Vocational Education
Struggles for Respect in Indonesia

If Indonesia wants to become an innovation-driven country, it must stop treating mind and hand as if one were noble and the other secondary. Yet that is still the social code in many places.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Doctoral Researcher in Corporate Innovation Ecosystems, School of Business and Management, Institut Teknologi Bandung (SBM-ITB).
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
01  ·  Introduction

A Faculty Where the Bias Shows Concretely

Many agriculture students are educated around food systems, land, and production, yet too few imagine a future close to farming itself.

Situation

Vocational schools, polytechnics, and other applied pathways are too often treated as lower in status than university routes, even though the country's productive future depends on technical mastery.

Complication

Indonesia's farming base is aging badly, and research on agriculture students suggests agriculture is still seen as less promising, less prestigious, and shaped by social status.

Question

Why does a country that needs builders keep teaching its young that respectable success means distance from applied, technical work?

Answer

Because gengsi, title-hunger, and the preference for stable jobs turn career choice into a moral signal — respectability, safety, and family standing outweigh the path that builds the greatest capability.

02  ·  Gap

A Hierarchy That Is Neither Natural Nor Serious

MIT was built around mens et manus — mind and hand. Indonesia does not need to copy MIT, but it should stop treating applied work as less honorable.

Core Gap

The respectable child is too often imagined as the one who moves toward the desk, the title, the clean office. The child who remains close to tools, machinery, land, fabrication, or technical execution is too often seen as aiming lower. This is not a small misunderstanding — it is a cultural downgrade of applied competence.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
03  ·  Framework

Credential Platforms, Not Places of Mastery

Certificates satisfy social expectation. Titles reassure families. The slower discipline of building competence receives less symbolic reward.

1
Applied Competence, Downgraded

A country cannot build serious innovation on image alone. It needs technicians who understand systems and founders unembarrassed by the workshop, the field, or the customer complaint.

2
Innovation Theatre

It is easier to hold events, produce slogans, and announce road maps than to stay with the hard discipline of learning by doing. Visibility outranks function.

3
Externally Referenced Identity

The question shifts from "What can I build?" to "What will others say?" — once worth depends on external validation, applied paths become harder to defend.

4
Family as the Transmission Mechanism

Parents carry the culture's anxieties and pass them on, often without malice — steering children toward careers that look safe, respectable, and socially defensible.

04  ·  Details

Fluent in the Language, Uncomfortable With the Work

People learn to speak about disruption while remaining uncomfortable with dirt, delay, uncertainty, and technical correction.

Admiration in words, avoidance in practice

They admire entrepreneurship in words yet continue to rank stable salaried identity above risk-bearing creation — they praise productivity yet teach children the most respectable life is furthest from practical struggle.

The policy-culture gap

Governments build technoparks, incubators, vocational campaigns, and industrial road maps. But when these land on a culture still ruled by gengsi, programs appear and the deeper ranking does not move.

A country that becomes top-heavy

It may produce many graduates, certificates, and ceremonies, yet still lack depth where real production begins — it praises entrepreneurship while steering talent toward the safest respectable exits.

05  ·  Evidence

Society Rewards the Shell More Than the Engine

The cost is not abstract — it is visible in what gets built, and what does not.

OBSERVED
An aging farming base

Research on agriculture students suggests agriculture is seen as less promising and less prestigious — shaped by social status rather than by the country's productive needs.

CONTRAST
Mens et manus at MIT

One of the world's most respected institutions places hands-on, technical, applied, learning-by-doing work at the very center of institutional prestige — not beneath intellectual life.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
06  ·  Implications

Fallback Track vs. Honorable Path

Vocational education must stop being treated as a fallback for those who failed to enter the more glamorous lane.

✗ Vocational as Fallback
✗Treating applied and technical paths as lower-status options for those who could not enter university
✗Rewarding titles, certificates, and formal affiliation over demonstrated, functioning capability
✓ Vocational as Honorable Path
✓Modern equipment, serious pedagogy, strong industry links, and visible routes to income, enterprise, and dignity
✓Universities holding the same standard: a certificate platform detached from function is not superior to a strong vocational institution
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
07  ·  Conclusion

A Cultural Correction, Not Another Slogan

The hand is not inferior to the mind. It is one of the ways the mind proves itself.

1

Restore respect to useful competence — there is dignity in making things function and honor in being useful.

2

Reduce the worship of titles and weaken the pull of performative legitimacy across both vocational and university tracks.

3

Stop insulting the forms of education and work that produce builders, before the cost to farming, industry, and innovation grows even greater.

Closing

Indonesia says it wants stronger entrepreneurship, better food security, more industrial depth, and a more serious innovation ecosystem. Then it must stop insulting the forms of education and work that produce builders. Not another slogan, but a cultural correction.
