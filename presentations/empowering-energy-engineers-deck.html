Empowering Energy Engineers:
Indonesia's Path to Renewable Solutions

If large-scale nuclear power isn’t Indonesia’s near-term future, the country’s trained nuclear and physics engineers are not stranded — Golden Indonesia 2045’s renewable buildout, carbon capture ambitions, and 3,686 GW of untapped renewable potential offer them a bright, if redirected, future.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
MIT Climate Interactive EN-ROADS Simulation Ambassador and a Nuclear Engineering UGM alumnus.
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
01  ·  Introduction

A question raised by three columns of feedback.

If Indonesia's priorities are shifting away from nuclear power, what happens to the engineers trained for it?

Situation

Prior columns examined Indonesia’s nuclear energy option through political, cultural, and structural lenses, and audience feedback made clear that priorities are shifting toward economic growth, education, healthcare, and renewable energy.

Complication

Golden Indonesia 2045’s current energy policy does not prioritize nuclear power; fossil fuels still made up 86 percent of consumption as of 2023, and coal alone is projected to remain 47 percent of the mix by 2045.

Question

What does the future hold for Indonesia’s nuclear and physics engineers if large-scale nuclear power isn’t the path forward?

Answer

A bright one — if these engineers are redirected toward small modular reactors, renewable energy system optimization, and carbon capture, all central to Indonesia’s Golden 2045 ambitions.

02  ·  Gap

The engineer question is rarely asked out loud.

Energy policy debates address power plants and emissions targets; they rarely address the people trained to build the plants that don’t get built.

Core Gap

Indonesia has invested significantly in nuclear and nuclear engineering education, producing a workforce of highly trained scientists, engineers, and researchers. If the nuclear energy sector does not expand as anticipated, these professionals risk unemployment, brain drain, or emigration — consequences that energy-policy discussions rarely address directly, even though the research is clear that underemployment of skilled professionals weakens national innovation capacity.

03  ·  Framework

Three Emerging Sectors for a Redirected Workforce

Each sector draws on nuclear engineering expertise without requiring large-scale nuclear plants.

01
SMRs and Microreactors

Small Modular Reactors and microreactors offer a new frontier suited to powering remote areas, industrial sites, and energy-intensive data centers — a lower-footprint application of the same core expertise.

02
Renewable Energy System Optimization

Physics engineers’ expertise in system optimization, energy storage, and grid integration can significantly enhance the efficiency and reliability of Indonesia’s renewable energy projects.

03
Carbon Capture, Utilization, and Storage

Indonesia has an estimated 400 GT CO2 storage capacity in onshore and offshore sites; nuclear engineers’ expertise in handling complex systems and ensuring safety applies directly to CCUS projects.

04  ·  Details

The Scale of the Opportunity

Indonesia's own resource base and export ambitions define the size of the redirected pathway.

3,686 GW of renewable potential

With this much untapped renewable energy potential, Indonesia has the capacity to become ASEAN’s green energy export hub — already underway through a 2 GWac solar energy partnership with Singapore.

86 percent fossil fuels today, 31 percent renewables by 2045

Coal, petroleum, and natural gas made up 86 percent of Indonesia’s energy consumption as of 2023; the renewable share is projected to grow to around 31 percent by 2045, even as coal is expected to remain 47 percent of the mix.

The world's largest nickel reserves

Indonesia’s nickel, bauxite, and copper reserves are essential inputs for batteries, solar panels, and wind turbines — raw materials the country’s industrialization strategy aims to convert into high-value renewable technology products.

05  ·  Evidence

Sources Behind the Argument

National vision documents and workforce research ground the piece’s claims.

EVIDENCE A
Golden Indonesia 2045

Indonesia’s vision to become the world’s 4th largest economy by 2045 includes a commitment to net-zero greenhouse gas emissions intensity, framing the energy-transition opportunity for redirected engineers.

EVIDENCE B
Carrington & Detragiache (1998)

Research on brain drain shows that underemployment of skilled professionals leads to talent loss to other countries or sectors, weakening national innovation capacity and economic growth over time.

EVIDENCE C
Gallup (2017)

Research shows that frustration and dissatisfaction stemming from underemployment can lead to lower life satisfaction, higher stress, and greater likelihood of emigration — the human cost of failing to redirect this workforce.

EVIDENCE D
Indonesia's cross-border green power exports

The 2 GWac solar energy partnership with Singapore, and exploratory partnerships with Malaysia and Papua New Guinea, are concrete evidence of Indonesia’s emerging role as a regional green energy hub.

06  ·  Implications

Diagnosis: What Kind of Engineer Does the Transition Need?

Technical skill alone is not what determines whether this workforce thrives.

✗Trained engineers with no defined pathway if large-scale nuclear plants don’t materialize

07  ·  Conclusion

A Bright Future Beyond Traditional Nuclear Power

Four recommendations for turning a redirected workforce into a national asset.

1

Government, industry, and educational institutions should collaborate explicitly to integrate nuclear-trained engineers into Golden Indonesia 2045’s energy and environmental strategies.

2

Prioritize deployment into SMRs, renewable system optimization, and CCUS as concrete, near-term absorption pathways.

3

Cultivate an anti-fragile, lifelong-learning mindset among engineers so they can adapt as the energy landscape evolves.

4

Leverage Indonesia’s nickel, bauxite, and copper reserves domestically, not just as raw exports, to create demand for this redirected expertise.

Closing

Indonesia’s nuclear and physics engineers have the skills and potential to thrive in the evolving energy landscape — the shift toward renewable energy and the global fight against climate change require the very expertise these engineers bring to the table.
