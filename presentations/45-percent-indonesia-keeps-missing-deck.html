The 45 percent Indonesia keeps missing

Indonesia tries to shape conduct after people enter schools and workplaces, yet the years from pregnancy to age seven, including the parents' working conditions, carry the largest weight in the model the column cites. Existing machinery such as Posyandu, Puskesmas and BKB should audit those conditions rather than a new program being built.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Indonesia concentrates its effort on shaping conduct after people enter institutions, long after their formative years.

"Indonesia expends enormous effort attempting to shape human conduct only after citizens have entered formal institutions."

Situation

"Twelve years of primary and secondary schooling are designed to cultivate discipline, reasoning, critical thinking and habits of learning." Corporations run compliance seminars and public institutions require integrity pledges.

Complication

"Yet higher education routinely falters when it assumes students possess the capacity for independent judgment". Integrity pledges come "long after employees have spent their formative years observing how authority, rules, and rewards operate."

Question

If the state is spending heavily on human capital, is it spending at the right time?

Answer

"As the state is now spending heavily on human capital development, the timing of these interventions has become as critical as their scale."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Evidence

A 2025 model gives early neural pathways 45 percent of the weight in ethical decisions, ahead of biases, pressures, context and character.

"A 2025 peer-reviewed article on ethical decision-making points further back in that sequence."

1  ·  Early neural pathways: 45 percent
"Early neural development comes first."
2  ·  Cognitive biases: 22 percent
"Biases later affect what people notice and trust."
3  ·  External pressures: 18 percent
"Pressure introduces fear, reward and hierarchy".
4  ·  Context: 10 percent
"institutions reinforce what conduct is tolerated or punished."
5  ·  Character: 5 percent
"Character operates after years of exposure to all of them."
How to read the figures

"These figures are analytical weights derived from a literature-based model rather than census-level population estimates. Yet the policy lesson lies less in the exact arithmetic than in the underlying sequence."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

Ages three to seven refine the circuits for cognition and behaviour, and later institutions inherit what children learned then.

The article pays particular attention to ages three to seven, "when synaptic pruning and myelination help refine neural circuits involved in cognition and behavior."

"Children experience language, care, authority and conflict while systems involved in emotional regulation, impulse control and social cognition continue developing."

Later institutions "inherit years of experience about whether rules are fair, whether authority can be trusted and what happens when someone challenges power."

Not determinism

"Childhood does not determine the adult. Neuroplasticity persists throughout life; dedicated teachers, mentors, healthy relationships, and institutions can rewire earlier patterns."

Details

The policy window opens before birth and includes how the parents make a living.

"The policy window begins before age three."

01
Before birth
Pregnancy and maternal health
"conditions during pregnancy affect the environment in which development starts. Maternal health and nutrition matter".
02
Birth to three
Care, language and safety
Severe stress, infant nourishment, "responsive caregiving, language exposure and physical safety" all matter in the years that establish foundational capacities.
03
The household
Parental economic security
"Parental economic security is an inseparable part of the developmental ecology." "The state cannot claim to nurture the child while turning a blind eye to how the parents make a living."

Field Notes

Rosyid's afternoons beside his mother's tofu cart show how parents' jobs decide where a child spends his day.

"One such child is Rosyid, a primary-school boy I have seen grow up beside his mother's fried tofu cart in Menteng." His mother has sold there since 2003; his father earns through motorcycle ride-hailing.

"When nobody is home after school, his father brings Rosyid from Cawang to the cart. He can remain beside a busy road for hours, sometimes late into the evening."

"Both parents work, but their jobs give the family limited control over where their youngest child spends much of his day."

Why jobs belong here

"Having work is different from having work that reliably puts food on the table, gives parents some security and dignity, and leaves enough control over time to care for children."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Gap

Free meals now reach tens of millions, but food cannot make up for what a child's other conditions withhold.

"Getting the timing right is urgent because Indonesia is already investing in early-life intervention at unprecedented scale."

What the meal program reaches
✓"The free nutritious meal program has become one of the country's largest social programs, reaching tens of millions of beneficiaries."
What food alone cannot compensate for
✗"Yet food alone cannot compensate for sleep deficits, toxic air, unsafe public spaces, absent language engagement or the chronic stress of overstretched caregivers."
✗"parental income and job quality still shape whether families can provide those conditions."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Evidence

Indonesia once treated maternal health, child development and family income as connected policy, through BKB, Posyandu and keluarga sejahtera.

"Indonesia does not need to build all of this from zero."

1984
Bina Keluarga Balita (BKB)
A community-based parenting program introduced during the New Order.
1986
Posyandu
National rollout of the integrated health services post.
1989
Village midwives
The village-midwife initiative, anchored by the Puskesmas network.
1990s
Keluarga sejahtera
BKKBN paired family welfare with economic empowerment through UPPKS, Takesra and Kukesra, alongside Inpres Desa Tertinggal.
A stated limit

"These programs should not be romanticized, but they show that Indonesia once treated maternal health, child development and family economic capacity as connected policy concerns."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Recommendations

A prenatal-to-seven formation audit should run through existing institutions and include parents' work and the child's surroundings.

1
Build on Posyandu and Puskesmas
"A prenatal-to-seven formation audit could build on those institutions rather than create another bureaucracy." Measures could extend beyond nutrition, immunization and medical status to developmental screening, continuity of caregiving, sleep and physical safety.
2
Let BKB track interaction and authority
BKB and early-childhood services "could also track language-rich interaction, safe play, preschool quality and how adults use authority around children."
3
Put employment in the same audit
"Employment and social protection belong in the same audit." Government should ask whether parents earn enough, whether their work gives dignity and some security, and whether hours leave time for caregiving.
4
Count the child's surroundings
Local government should account for air quality, transport, public space and neighbourhood safety "because these conditions determine where children spend their days."
5
No new commission
"Indonesia does not need another commission or standalone early-childhood program."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

If early pathways carry the most weight, a child's daily conditions are formation itself, not background.

If early neural pathways carry the largest weight in the model, these are not background conditions. They are part of formation itself.

Where formation takes place
"caregiver availability, parental job security, sleep, linguistic exposure and the moral climate children absorb daily."
