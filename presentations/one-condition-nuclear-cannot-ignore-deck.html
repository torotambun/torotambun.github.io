The One Condition
Nuclear Power Cannot Ignore

Every technology has room for error except one. Should any society marked by massive, pervasive corruption ever attempt to build and operate a nuclear power plant? The answer is simple: not until the culture around it is clean enough to meet the discipline nuclear safety demands.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Doctoral Researcher, School of Business and Management, Institut Teknologi Bandung (SBM-ITB).
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
01  ·  Introduction

An Ethical Question First

Nuclear power does not forgive mistakes, bend to political pressure, or accommodate cultural habits.

Situation

Many societies with weak governance still operate complex, hazardous infrastructure — petrochemical plants, LNG terminals, aerospace projects — despite real risks, seemingly without catastrophe.

Complication

This fact leads to a misleading conclusion: if these systems can operate under weak governance, why should nuclear power be treated differently? The difference is consequence — nuclear accidents can redraw maps and displace communities for generations.

Question

Should a society marked by massive, pervasive corruption ever build and operate a nuclear power plant?

Answer

It should not — not until its institutions are strong enough to enforce rules consistently, oversight is real and independent, and reporting is honest and protected.

02  ·  Gap

The Danger Is Not the Reactor

It is the system that is supposed to protect it.

Core Gap

The technical probability of nuclear failure may be small — that is what modern reactor designs aim for. But the real world is not built on theoretical models. It is built on people, incentives, inspections, procurement choices, political cycles, and maintenance culture. When corruption is massive, pervasive, and prevailing, the safety assumptions behind nuclear technology collapse.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
03  ·  Framework

Why Nuclear Is Different

Consequence, pattern, and tolerance separate nuclear from every other hazardous industry.

1
Consequence Asymmetry

A chemical explosion is terrible but contained. Even catastrophic aviation failure affects limited geography. Nuclear accidents break this boundary — they turn operational failures into long-lasting environmental consequences.

2
Corruption as Pattern, Not Moment

A cheaper material substituted, an inspection signed without a site visit, a maintenance step skipped — no single shortcut breaks the plant immediately, but corruption is a pattern that weakens the entire safety chain.

3
Technology Depends on Integrity

Passive safety systems, advanced cooling, and automated shutdowns assume every component is genuine, every record accurate, every inspection real. When the system is not clean, the technology cannot compensate.

4
Minimized vs. Eliminated

Nuclear reactors do not accept minimized corruption. A 99 percent clean system can still collapse under the 1 percent that remains compromised.

04  ·  Details

Where Nuclear Still Fits

This is not a blanket rejection of the technology — it is a condition on where and how.

Modular reactors for stable, matched loads

Designed for specific industrial uses such as data centers or isolated facilities, modular reactors can be technically appropriate where demand is tightly matched to output.

Chronic overcapacity magnifies risk, not value

Installing reactors in environments with weak planning discipline or unreliable load forecasting does not create value — it only magnifies operational and governance risk.

Nuclear efficiency is technical; nuclear responsibility is cultural

The two questions — can it work, and should this society run it — are answered by entirely different evidence.

05  ·  Evidence

Stability on the Surface Is Not Proof of Safety

What looks stable may only mean information is not shared openly.

OBSERVATION
Some weak-governance states operate nuclear facilities without reported incident

These systems often work under unusual conditions, such as strict security control or limited public access to information — the absence of reported problems does not mean the system is safe.

REQUIREMENT
Civilian nuclear power requires open reporting and accountability

When corruption is massive, pervasive, and prevailing, these requirements cannot be met — a society shaped by systemic corruption cannot guarantee nuclear safety, even if the situation appears stable on the surface.

06  ·  Implications

Urgency Does Not Substitute for Integrity

Neither climate goals nor development ambitions change the underlying condition.

✗ Urgency as Justification
✗"The world needs clean energy" used to wave past the conditions required for nuclear safety
✗"Nuclear power strengthens national development" treated as sufficient on its own, ignoring that infrastructure built on unsafe foundations is risk disguised as advancement
✓ Integrity as the Prerequisite
✓Nuclear technology does not respond to urgency — it responds to integrity, built into the whole system, not applied selectively
✓Regulators who cannot be pushed aside, procurement that cannot be manipulated, deadlines that do not follow political calendars
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
07  ·  Conclusion

A Discussion, Not Yet a Construction

The day a society is ready for nuclear energy is the day it proves it can protect truth with the same discipline it expects to protect the reactor.

1

Treat corruption explicitly as a nuclear safety problem, not merely a political one — it belongs in the risk assessment, not just the ethics debate.

2

Build institutions strong enough to enforce rules consistently, with oversight that is real, independent, and protected from political interference.

3

Keep nuclear power as a discussion, not a construction, until the surrounding culture is clean enough to carry the weight of the responsibility.

Closing

A corrupt system cannot protect a nuclear reactor. And a nuclear reactor cannot survive inside a corrupt system. This principle is not political. It is not ideological. It is technical.
