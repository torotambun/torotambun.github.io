Philosophy as a Path to Knowledge: Insights from Nietzsche, Plato, and Beyond

Philosophy earns its place among the six modes of knowledge acquisition not by producing truth but by disciplining the claim to it — and that discipline reaches as far as the founder who thinks his authority is his own.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Philosophy sits alongside empiricism, not above it, and that is what makes it useful.

The article opens by placing philosophy in a set rather than on a pedestal: one of six foundational avenues of knowledge acquisition, alongside tradition, religion, empiricism, art expression, and culture.

Situation

Knowledge is acquired through six avenues, not one. Empiricism is a member of that set, not its arbiter.

Complication

In practice empiricism "often claims to be the most authoritative form of knowledge" — and a claim to authority is precisely what no mode of knowing can supply about itself.

Question

What checks a mode of knowledge that has no external referee?

Answer

Philosophy — whose "reflective and critical nature challenges assumptions, interrogates frameworks, and fosters humility." Its value is not a rival account of truth but a discipline applied to every account, including its own.

Nietzsche

Empiricism's claim to authority is itself an article of faith that life does not prove.

"Friedrich Nietzsche's critique of truth is especially relevant in a world where empiricism often claims to be the most authoritative form of knowledge."

We have arranged for ourselves a world in which we can live—by positing bodies, lines, planes, causes and effects, motion and rest, form and content; without these articles of faith, nobody now could endure life. But that does not prove them. Life is no argument; the conditions of life might include error.

— Friedrich Nietzsche, Beyond Good and Evil, quoted in the article
The claim being refused
While empiricism emphasises sensory experience and observable phenomena, "Nietzsche reminds us that no single mode of acquiring knowledge is infallible or omniscient."
Truths as forgotten constructions
Nietzsche famously described truths as "illusions which we have forgotten are illusions," underscoring how human constructs shape what we accept as truth.
What the critique is for
"His writings challenge the arrogance of assuming a monopoly on truth, cautioning against biases that blind us to alternative perspectives." It is "a call for intellectual humility" — a warning "against rigid adherence to any one framework, including empiricism".

Plato

The post-truth reader is not deceived; the shadows are more comfortable than the light.

The Allegory of the Cave read against the present, where the obstacle is not scarce evidence but unwanted evidence.

"Post-truth is characterized by the dominance of emotional narratives over objective facts, where personal beliefs and biases overshadow evidence and reason." And it does not thrive on ignorance. "This phenomenon thrives in an environment saturated with information, where individuals often choose comforting illusions over challenging truths."

In the allegory, "prisoners chained in a cave perceive shadows on a wall as reality." When one escapes and discovers the world outside, the realisation is painful as well as transformative — "the shadows were mere illusions, and the true forms of reality lay beyond."

That pain is the mechanism, not an unfortunate side effect. "This resistance mirrors the dynamics of the post-truth era, where deeply entrenched beliefs and emotional attachments often prevent individuals from accepting facts that challenge their worldview." Emotional narratives are powerful "because they provide psychological comfort and reinforce existing biases."

The consequence for anyone hoping to argue someone out of a position: more facts do not address the reason the position is held. "Philosophy serves as a bridge from ignorance to understanding, offering tools to critically engage with the shadows that dominate contemporary discourse."

The uncomfortable part of the remedy

"In a post-truth world, Plato's insights remind us of the importance of questioning our assumptions and seeking enlightenment, even when the journey is uncomfortable."

Hegel and Marx

The master's dominance is the source of his dependence, and Marx moved the dialectic into the factory.

The article turns here from the internal and epistemological to the relational: "Hegel shifts attention to the relational and societal aspects of human experience."

What the master appears to hold
✓Command over the relationship
✓Recognition, received and not returned
✓Freedom from the labour itself
✓The position from which terms are set
✓Standing that appears self-generated
What the dialectic actually produces
•"The master asserts dominance, but paradoxically becomes dependent on the slave's labor"
•Recognition from someone he has defined as unable to confer it
•"Meanwhile, the slave, through work and struggle, gains self-awareness and freedom"
•Marx reframes it as capitalists and workers — "a modern manifestation of this dynamic"
•"Marx emphasized the alienation of laborers from their work and the necessity of their contributions to sustaining the system"
Why the article says this belongs in a piece about knowledge

"The master-slave dialectic is relevant because power structures are central to how knowledge, labor, and societal roles are negotiated." Understanding them "helps illuminate not only historical struggles but also modern contexts, from political systems to workplace hierarchies."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Application

A founder directing labour occupies the master's position and inherits its dependency.

"Entrepreneurship is often seen as a domain of innovation and individual initiative, but it is deeply embedded in structures of labor, dependency, and power."

"Entrepreneurs, in their role as leaders, resemble the “master,” relying on the labor and creativity of their teams" — while their employees often gain self-awareness and growth through those same contributions.

"Entrepreneurs often occupy the position of “masters,” directing labor and resources to build innovative ventures. However, their success depends on the contributions of their teams, reflecting the interdependence at the core of Hegel's concept."

The move being made here is worth stating plainly, because it is the article's own contribution rather than Hegel's. It takes a dialectic written about lordship and bondage and points it at the founder — the figure whose entire cultural presentation is of authority that originates in himself. "This connection enriches the understanding of entrepreneurship by adding a critical lens to its ethical and social dimensions."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Implications

Recognising that dependency turns three ethical claims into practical ones.

"By integrating philosophical insights into entrepreneurship, several lessons emerge" — each one an operating consequence rather than a sentiment.

01
One
Ethical responsibility
"Recognizing the contributions of all participants fosters fairness and respect in organizational dynamics."
02
Two
Labor and innovation
"Valuing the creative efforts of team members strengthens innovation and morale." The dependency is not only moral; it is the source of the output being claimed.
03
Three
Power dynamics
"Understanding the interdependence between leaders and workers encourages a more equitable distribution of authority."
What this displaces

"Philosophy challenges the traditional profit-centric narrative of entrepreneurship, encouraging a broader view that incorporates ethical and social considerations."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

In a post-truth era the scarce resource is not evidence but the humility to be moved by it.

The tour ends where it began — with philosophy as one mode among six, and with what that placement is for.

In an era marked by emotional narratives and post-truth challenges, philosophy offers a path to clarity and intellectual humility.

— Dr. Toronata Tambun
What the three thinkers were doing together
"From Nietzsche's caution against arrogance in the pursuit of truth to Plato's exploration of enlightenment and Marx's analysis of labor, philosophy reminds us of the importance of questioning assumptions and embracing diverse perspectives."
The function, stated once more
Philosophy "provides critical tools for navigating the complexities of truth, power, and human understanding." Tools for navigating — not a destination, and not a rival set of facts.
Why the six-mode framing matters to the close
"It emphasizes the interplay of multiple forms of knowledge, fostering a balanced and reflective approach to understanding the world." The argument is not that philosophy wins. It is that any mode which believes it has won has stopped checking itself.
