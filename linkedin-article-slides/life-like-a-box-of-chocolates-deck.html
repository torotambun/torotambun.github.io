Life is like a box of chocolates

The account of one opening class argues that the university inherits a specific duty after twelve years of schooling: not to add subject knowledge but to hand over communication, meaning attentive listening and critical thought rather than fluency. The author tests that duty on himself. He is a nuclear engineer and oil and gas executive arriving as a lecturer from industry, mid-way through a doctorate he says he may not complete, and he teaches by refusing the monologue and going to the corners of the room. The Forrest Gump line is turned on his own trajectory, not the students'.

Opening

Students thirty years my junior arrive with curiosity intact, and English was never the barrier.

The room holds around eighty students, an International Class and a Regular Class combined, all of them more than thirty years younger than the man at the front. What he records first is not a deficit but a surplus: a spark of curiosity, boundless energy, and an anticipation of a future none of them can yet see.

The diagnosis that follows is precise about what is and is not missing. English itself is not a significant barrier for this group. What needs enhancing is the innate ability to communicate, which is a different faculty and a harder one to teach.

This is the author's second year teaching communication at the Faculty of Agriculture, Universitas Gadjah Mada, and the account is written as co-learning rather than instruction.

The frame he applies to himself

The Faculty has welcomed him as a lecturer from industry, a nuclear engineer and oil and gas executive, and he notes in the same breath that he is embarking on a PhD at a later stage in life, "a journey I may not complete." The Forrest Gump line in the title is aimed at his own path.

The Claim

After twelve years of schooling the baton passes to the university, and the tool it must hand over is listening and critical thought, not speech.

The article states the handover plainly. Twelve years are spent in elementary, junior and senior high school, and then the baton is passed to universities, where the responsibility is to arm students with the most crucial tool for their journey.

The definition of that tool is the load-bearing part. Communication here is not the ability to speak. The article specifies listening attentively and thinking critically as constituents of the same skill, which puts reception and judgement on the same footing as expression.

That definition is what makes the rest of the account a method rather than a diary. Everything the author then does in the room, from declining his own introduction to working the corners, follows from treating communication as something practised rather than delivered.

In the article's own words

"After 12 years spent in elementary, junior, and senior high school, the baton is passed to universities."

Field Notes

A professor opened by telling eighty students they could waste 1,400 minutes of their life.

The opening speech is the account's central observation, and the article treats it as a model of the thing being taught rather than as ceremony.

OBSERVED A
The entrance
Prof. Ir. Yohannes Andi Trisyono, M.Sc., Ph.D. enters in a classic white and brown batik. The article records a confident stride and an aura that hushed the ambient noise, and compares the speech that follows to a Marine General briefing troops before a final assault.
OBSERVED B
The words themselves
"Please find ways to make the most of your time with us. Otherwise, you will waste 1,400 minutes of your life. We are here to serve you and to learn together on how to better equip you in communicating, though not in English grammar."
OBSERVED C
Why the number matters
The figure converts a semester into a quantity that can be lost. It also states the course's own boundary in the same sentence: the subject is communicating, and explicitly not English grammar, which matches the article's opening diagnosis that English was not the barrier.
OBSERVED D
The author's response
He records the words as solemn, and the effect as shivers, noting that the professor's renown extends beyond the local community to the United Nations and academic circles worldwide.

What It Revealed

I declined the introduction slot and worked the corners of the room, because a monologue was never my forte.

Offered a formal slot for introductions, he declined it, saying he felt unworthy of the distinction, and chose instead to let aspects of his life enter as opportunity and relevance arose. He had prepared his curriculum vitae mentally as a radiant mind map rather than as a sequence to be recited.

The method in the room follows the same logic. He approached the students, who he judged were as uncomfortable as he imagined he would have been in their place, and went deliberately to the corners, to those he describes as metaphorically playing right and left field. What came back was their own accounts of a mix of schools, ethnicities and religions from across the country.

The room itself resisted. The article records over eighty students in a well-lit but cramped space, stuffy air, and a single whiteboard which he calls a poor substitute for the blackboards he prefers, limiting his teaching dynamics. He contrasts it with the six days at ITB, where blackboards were provided, and says the comparison filled him with a sense of purpose.

What is being tested

The refusal of the monologue is the article's own claim about communication put into practice on the first day. A course that defines communication as listening cannot open with an uninterrupted lecture about the lecturer.

What I Carry

Ninety minutes ran to a hundred and ten, and the mind map that came afterwards has six weeks of branches to spend.

The class overran, and what he recorded afterwards was not notes but a structure he intends to open one branch at a time.

May our presence on this planet elevate beyond mere existence, to become a profound blessing to the earth and all its inhabitants.

— Life is like a box of chocolates, 28 February 2024
The overrun
"Time flew in an instant, the scheduled 90 minutes stretching into 110." The article treats the extra twenty minutes as evidence of the exchange rather than of poor timekeeping.
What he wrote down
After the class he penned what he calls his "contributions," in the form of an attached mind map, and notes that the version shown is more beautifully presented than the one drawn on the whiteboard in the room.
The six weeks ahead
"I will exploit each branch in the coming 6 weeks." The map is a syllabus in the shape of a diagram, which is consistent with his stated preference for radiant over linear structure.
The closing principle
The article ends on rahmatan lil alamin, a mercy to all the worlds, extended from the classroom to stewardship of the earth and every creature on it.
