Driving Startup Success: Key Behaviors, Traits, and Frameworks for Aspiring Founders turn CEOs

Creating value and capturing it are different jobs, and the transition between them is where founders fail. The article assembles a composite guide for that transition — five attributes of operational excellence, ten behaviours that distinguish the best CEOs, and a mapping of those behaviours onto the 4-CAP+ capabilities and the Big Five traits — and closes by refusing the charge that publishing such a synthesis outside a journal makes it pseudoscience.

Introduction

Creating value and capturing it are different jobs, and the second is where founders fail.

The piece is written for students moving out of the creation phase, and it names the transition rather than the destination.

"As students transition from the creation phase of their startups to capturing value and mastering operational excellence, it is crucial to adopt a mindset that balances innovation with effective execution."

The distinction is held throughout: "The journey from ideation to operational success involves not just maintaining the drive to create value but also honing the ability to capture that value through superior operational practices." Creation is not the hard part being addressed here.

The article is explicit that the capability is learnable rather than innate — the attributes "are developed over time" but "can be accelerated by gaining comprehensive knowledge across various domains." The list runs from ethnography and design thinking through legal, tax, valuation and negotiation. These, it notes, "form the backbone of what many educational programs offer under different names: Venture Tactics, Mini-MBAs, or even full MBA programs and Corporate University offerings."

Framework

Operational excellence has five attributes, and one of them is choosing king or rich.

The author's own five-part symbol of operational excellence, each attribute carrying its source.

1  ·  Superb storytelling skills
"The ability to communicate a compelling vision and narrative is essential for attracting investors, customers, and team members. Great storytelling can differentiate a startup in a crowded market and build a strong brand identity." Cited to the author's own book on disciplined storytelling.
2  ·  King or rich?
"Founders must decide whether they want to prioritize control over their company (being the 'king') or prioritize growth and wealth creation (being 'rich'). This decision influences strategic choices, such as seeking external funding or pursuing partnerships." Attributed to Wasserman's founder's dilemmas.
3  ·  Inclusive teams
"Building teams with complementary skills and networks but aligned objectives is key to achieving operational success." The article ties it to a concept from outside management literature — "'Tikkun Olam' (repairing the world)" — as the reason diversity and unity are not in tension.
4  ·  Discipline — don't jump
In the 24 Steps of Disciplined Entrepreneurship framework, "each step builds on the previous one to ensure a systematic and thorough approach to developing a successful startup. Skipping steps can lead to missing critical insights or making premature decisions, ultimately jeopardizing the startup's potential for success." The failure mode is the shortcut.
5  ·  Profit through a solid value chain
"Operational excellence starts with a strategic statement that clearly defines the company's mission and vision. This must be supported by a robust value chain that ensures efficient and effective delivery of products or services, ultimately driving profitability."

Details

Ten behaviours separate the best CEOs, and the first is deciding at sixty-five percent certainty.

Each behaviour is paired in the article with the Big Five trait and the 4-CAP+ capability it draws on.

1  ·  Decisiveness
"Successful CEOs make decisions quickly and with conviction, even amid ambiguity." The threshold is specific: "Effective CEOs solicit multiple viewpoints and act with 65% certainty rather than waiting for perfect information". Conscientiousness and sensemaking.
2  ·  Engaging for impact
They "balance listening to others with making decisive calls and do not default to consensus-driven decision-making." The trait attached is the counter-intuitive one — low agreeableness, with emotional stability and relating.
3  ·  Proactive adaptation
"Effective CEOs are adaptable and spend significant time thinking about long-term strategies. They regularly scan diverse sources of information to sense changes early and adjust strategies accordingly". Openness, conscientiousness, inventing.
4  ·  Reliable execution
"Successful CEOs ensure that strategies are effectively executed by aligning talent, culture, and organizational design with the company's goals. They also manage their time and energy to maintain a sustainable pace".
5  ·  Boldness
"Boldness is a key predictor of CEO excellence. Effective CEOs make bold strategic moves early and frequently to drive significant performance improvements. They do not shy away from risk but manage it wisely to leverage opportunities".
6  ·  Humility and self-awareness
"The best CEOs exhibit humility, recognizing their own biases and skill gaps. They seek continuous learning and development and understand the importance of building and relying on a strong team".
7  ·  Focus on substance
"Successful CEOs prioritize key issues and rise above details to see the larger picture." But not at the cost of grip: "they are also adept at knowing the details when necessary, ensuring a deep understanding of critical aspects of their" organisation.
8  ·  Stakeholder relationships
"Effective CEOs build strong relationships with their boards, establishing trust and clear responsibilities. They use their boards as resources and ensure that board members are well-informed and engaged in strategic discussions". The board is treated as an asset to be worked, not a body to be managed.
9  ·  Resilience and drive
"They are original thinkers, capable of visualizing the future and catalyzing others to action. They maintain composure under pressure and are willing to engage in conflict when necessary".
10  ·  Execution skills
"Successful CEOs ensure coherence in management processes, align talent to value, and actively manage cultural elements that drive performance. They make timely and effective resource reallocations and foster organizational agility".

Framework

The behaviours map onto 4-CAP+ capabilities and Big Five traits, including low agreeableness.

Two established frameworks are laid across the ten behaviours: what a leader must be able to do, and what disposition makes it likelier.

4-CAP+ capabilities
•Sensemaking — "The ability to understand and interpret complex and ambiguous information"
•Visioning — "articulating a strategic direction that inspires and guides the organization towards long-term goals"
•Inventing — "developing new solutions, approaches, and strategies", ensuring they are "practical and actionable"
•Relating — "understanding and managing the needs and motivations of stakeholders, fostering trust"
•Building credibility — "demonstrating ethical behavior, moral values, and integrity"
Big Five traits
✓Openness — "intellectual curiosity, creativity, and preference for novelty and variety"
✓Conscientiousness — "organization, dependability, and discipline"
✓Extraversion — here narrowed to "taking initiative and driving engagement without necessarily being extroverted"
✓Agreeableness — deliberately inverted: "Lower agreeableness in CEOs can be beneficial for making tough decisions and pushing back when necessary"
✓Emotional stability — "being calm, composed, and resilient under pressure"
What the composite is for

"By integrating these traits, behaviors, and capabilities, the combined framework offers a robust guide for teaching founder classes on leadership and execution." The article is candid that it is teaching material: "Special emphasis is placed on the inventing capability, highlighting the ability to deliver and plan effectively, alongside maintaining ethical standards to build credibility."

Conclusion

Despite the charge of pseudoscience, I remain unfazed.

The article ends on a disclaimer that is really a position on where knowledge is allowed to be published.

My priority is the practical value and usefulness of this writing for my beloved students and audiences. I believe in delivering real-world insights over academic elitism.

— Driving Startup Success, 1 July 2024
The charge, as he states it
"Despite condemnation from esteemed academicians with long titles in this country, who label an article in LinkedIn or in newspaper (even in a national newspaper) as "pseudo science," I remain unfazed." The accusation is about the venue, not the content, and he answers it on that ground.
It recurs a month later
On 1 August 2024 he published a paper in a journal that is not Scopus-indexed and wrote out the dismissal he expected in the critic's own voice — not pseudoscience anymore, but still not one of us. Same objection, same refusal, one month apart. Both decks are in this archive.
What the disclaimer costs
It sits at the end of a synthesis of fifteen sources including Harvard Business Review, McKinsey and two peer-reviewed personality papers. The material is not the issue; the platform is. That is the asymmetry the disclaimer is written against.
