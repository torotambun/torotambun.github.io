One of My Early Milestones: AFS Exchange Student in Mombusho ASEAN Program 1988

The article recalls a 1988 AFS short-term exchange in Japan, one of six Indonesian students selected around November 1987, and the host family, house, commute and school life in Hanno-shi and Tachikawa. It tells how choosing the national science finalist place over the AFS year program led to the short program while the medical-school invitation route closed anyway, and how English and Japanese speeches at school led to a speech contest on 6 February 1988. It closes on the author's claim that the exchange myelinated his firing neurons, shaped his mental model and gave him an edge when Schlumberger recruited him in 1994.

Opening

The 1988 exchange began with a farewell song and a delegation of six students selected around November 1987.

The article opens on a sound. The author writes "I can still hear my own a-cappella voice" singing Sandiwara, a 1950s song by Mochtar Embut, at the farewell party for the inaugurated AFS Short-Term Mombusho delegates at the family home of Ms. Mien Uno. The committee asked him to perform because he was one of the winners of the 1987 Youth Radio Television Star Seriosa competition in DKI Jakarta.

A few months before the farewell, six students had been selected to represent Indonesia in the AFS Short-Term Mombusho Program, around November 1987: Toronata Tambun and Yulianti Rahmi of SMA 8 Jakarta, Awan Kurniawan and Mitha Guritno of SMA Lab School, Fatma Dian of SMA 6 Jakarta and Reza Anglingkusumo of SMA 34 Jakarta.

They were given intensive Japanese lessons at the Japanese Cultural Center at Summitmas Tower with Yamada sensei. A photograph caption names Awan, Mitha, Kak Ita Azly, Reza, Yamada Sensei, Dian, Yuli and Toro.

The Scene

A host family in Hanno-shi, a house of music and books, and a commute of several train changes set the daily rhythm.

The author lived with the Arais in Hanno-shi, in Saitama-Ken, about an hour by the Seibu-Ikebukuro line from Ikebukuro Station.

1  ·  The family
The host father, Eiji Arai, was a high school English teacher at Tachikawa High School, described in the article as claimed to be the best, one of the oldest and the most prestigious high schools in Tokyo. The host mother, Masayo Arai, was and is a private piano teacher. Their son Kazuhiro, the author's AFS younger brother, played football and tried the violin, and their daughter Michiyo, already very good at the piano, later became a professional flutist in Tokyo after a master's programme in the Netherlands. In the late 1960s, at Waseda University, both parents had majored in linguistics, Indonesian and Hebrew.
2  ·  The house
A two-storey town-house with two rooms downstairs and three on the second floor, one of them for the author, one for the younger siblings and the main room for the parents. It had a shared bathroom with a soaking place, a hot water machine and a dry sitting toilet. Otosan's desk held a word processor and a computer, the walls of the study were full of books and photo albums, and the tiny living room held a fine-tuned Kawai baby grand piano. A dog named Chiro lived there, a rocky river with very clear water ran behind the house, and the bedroom window looked over tea plantations.
3  ·  The morning and the commute
The author woke to his mother's greeting, classical radio playing Mendelssohn, Schumann, Schubert and often Mozart, and the smell of Japanese rice and miso soup. At 6:30 he cycled to the station with a bento box and a heavy bag of textbooks, then changed trains several times, through Tokorozawa and Higashi Murayama, to reach Tachikawa. He says he made the trip every morning without being late or missing a train. Only once did he ride to school on the father's 250 cc Kawasaki motorbike, in one of the coldest winters in Japan in decades, arriving with his lips cracked.
4  ·  The earlier guests and the return visits
The author was not the first foreign student the Arais hosted. Chacha, from Indonesia, came four years earlier, Prof. Dr. Fabian Cremaschi, a neurosurgeon from Argentina, one to two years earlier, and a female student from Malaysia whose name the author could not recall. He met Chacha in 1991 and Prof. Cremaschi in person in 2013, and says the feeling was surreal, as if they were his brothers. He returned to visit the Arais in 2001 and again in 2015, 2019 and 2022.

The Turns

Choosing the national science finalist place over the year program gave up one option and led to the short program, and the medical-school route closed anyway.

The author tells this story because, he says, it is relevant to how he came to the short program. The article dates the choice only to the period of the finalist place, and the deck follows its order.

August 1987
A science finalist's month
The author's biological research, from the science club at SMA 8 Jakarta, was chosen as one of the finalists of the LIPI-TVRI Youth Scientific Contest. It brought him to meet President Soeharto, Minister of Research and Technology BJ Habibie, Minister of Education and Culture Fuad Hassan and Menpen Harmoko, to sit in on a Presidential State Speech at the DPR-MPR on 16 August, and to attend the Independence Day Commemoration at the Merdeka Palace on 17 August.
1987
The choice
At that time he was in the selection process for the AFS Bina Antarbudaya year program and had to choose between being a LIPI-TVRI finalist and continuing to the third phase of the AFS year program selection. He chose the first, and withdrew from the year program selection.
Around November 1987
The short program
A few months later he was selected for the AFS Short Program Mombusho. He calls it a better option, because he did not have to leave his second grade, which the year program would have required.
1988
The route that closed
The author had been programmed to hold first rank in class at least until the fourth semester, to earn an invitation from the Faculty of Medicine of the University of Indonesia without an entrance test (PMDK). He thought, in his word naively, that the year program would end that dream. In 1988, in his third grade, he heard the devastating news that the minister had abolished the PMDK invitation.

Field Notes

At school the author spoke in English and Japanese, and the English speech contest of 6 February 1988 was the climax.

1  ·  The school day
School ran from 8 am to about 5 or 6 pm, with many extracurriculars. He joined the choir club, headquartered on the fourth floor of the landmark Tachikawa school tower, and the basketball, piano and biology clubs. From the school window he could see Fujisan on a clear day. Long days were no stranger, since at SMA 8 Jakarta his day ran from 7 am and often to 9 pm because of the science club.
2  ·  The speeches
His days at Tachikawa High School were spent with class-to-class speeches in English and several times in Japanese, often telling stories about Indonesia, Indonesian students, Indonesian history and his family.
3  ·  The contest and the press
The climax was a five-minute Speech Contest in English on 6 February 1988. He also remembers being interviewed by a local journalist about his impression of Tachikawa High School and Japan, and a photograph shows him in the school newspaper.
4  ·  The lines he learned
During the orientation at the Sony Japanese language school in Tokyo he was taught a speech in Japanese, which he says he still remembers: he introduced himself, thanked Monbusho and AFS from the heart, and promised to study more Japanese so that the people of ASEAN and Japan could understand each other, and to work with his ASEAN friends for the development of the Asia-Pacific region. As his fluency grew he added a more impressive closing sentence, of thanks for what he had been able to learn and a wish that the friendship would last.

What I Carry

The author credits the exchange with shaping his mental model and with an edge when Schlumberger recruited him in 1994.

The last page turns from the scene to what the exchange became, and ends in thanks.

Well, my firing neurons were myelinated with the AFS experience. This shaped my mental model and provided some foundation to be an anti-fragile, agile, rebel talented person.

— One of My Early Milestones, 2 May 2021
An edge in 1994
His status as an AFS returnee, he says, gave him extra points when Schlumberger recruited him in 1994, right after he graduated in Nuclear Engineering at UGM.
The career since
As printed in 2021, he had worked in 46 countries as an oil and gas executive, started thinking about retirement at 46, and left corporate life at 49 after executive education between 2016 and 2019, earning alumni status at Harvard Business School and affiliated alumni status at MIT Sloan School of Management.
The thanks
He thanks Bina Antarbudaya (Kak Iried, Kak Tini, Kak Ita, the late Kak Arno, his orientation friends and his batch of Mitha, Yuli, Dian, Reza and Awan), the late Pak Samsidar Malik, his English teacher at SMA 8 Jakarta, and Otosan, Okasan and his late parents, "without whom I would not have the courage to take this journey".
