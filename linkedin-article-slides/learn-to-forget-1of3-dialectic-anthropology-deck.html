(1 of 3) Learn to Forget: The Dialectic Process in Anthropology

Prof. PM Laksono’s “learn to forget” is not memory loss but a dialectic of Recall, Forget and Newness: prior knowledge must be recovered before its authority can be loosened, so that difference, resistance and evidence can change the person studying. The dialectic cannot stop at a label or a quotation, each encounter begins from a changed understanding, and before anything can be recalled it must first have been learned.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

“Learn to forget” turned out to describe a dialectic for changing understanding, not a statement about losing memory.

Prof. PM Laksono, trained at Leiden and Cornell, used the phrase in an STEI ITB entrepreneurship seminar years earlier.

Situation

A guest lecture introduced a phrase not then fully understood: “learn to forget.”

Complication

"To forget something, a person must first remember what is to be forgotten."

Question

What does forgetting mean if it "does not begin with absence"?

Answer

"It begins with recall." The sequence Recall, Forget, Newness, learned from his Cornell professor James T. Siegel, "became the organising idea behind almost everything else he explained to me."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

Recall makes prior knowledge visible, Forget loosens its authority, and Newness yields an understanding the old frame could not produce.

Reflexive anthropology rejects the idea of the researcher as a neutral observer (Dietz, 2011; Markom & Tošić, 2021; Feder, 2019).

01
Recall
Bring assumptions into view
Knowledge, beliefs and past misunderstandings are made "visible enough to be examined rather than allowing it to continue operating only as an unquestioned background to interpretation."
02
Forget
Make authority provisional
"what has been learned is retained, but its authority becomes provisional and open to reconsideration."
03
Newness
Produce history
"Newness produces history because something has changed, whereas an inquiry that leaves the interpretive structure untouched merely reproduces what was already known."
Why Recall comes first

"Forgetting without that prior recovery would amount to absence or ignorance rather than a deliberate loosening of what was previously known."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

Studying other people should be able to change the person studying, which requires tolerating difference and distrusting settled labels.

A lecturer can speak Bahasa Indonesia to Indonesian students and still be misunderstood, "because sharing a language does not guarantee that meaning is shared in the same way."

Anthropology adds "the reciprocal possibility that the lecturer has also failed to understand how the students receive, interpret, and situate those words." Prof. Laksono asked what anthropology is for if the person studying others cannot tolerate what is different. "Tolerance in this context does not require agreement or abandonment of judgment".

Derrida’s différance, the differing and deferring through which meaning stays unfinished, warns against naming too soon. "the analytical problem begins when naming is treated as if it has exhausted understanding." Categories "should not acquire more authority than the phenomena they are intended to describe."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Gap

When difference cannot be tolerated, interpretation turns coercive, and the Javanese moves of nggiwar and ngalah offer another path through resistance.

If difference cannot be tolerated, he said, the tendency is toward kekerasan, violence, a warning about research as well as social life.

Interpretation as coercion
✗Participants allowed to speak only within the researcher’s categories
✗"An unexpected response may be reclassified as ignorance, resistance may be treated as error, and difference may be converted into deviance"
✗The participant "loses the capacity to disturb, revise, or complicate the researcher’s categories."
Moving with resistance
✓Nggiwar: move aside, like sharpening a saw along the form of its teeth
✓Ngalah: yield, "refusing to confuse intellectual progress with domination"
✓Menang tanpa ngasorake: prevail without humiliating the other
✓"Resistance can therefore become analytically useful by indicating where an existing interpretation is being challenged"
Scope of the support

"Dietz does not establish Prof. Laksono’s claim about violence, but his reflexive framework supports the methodological requirement that researchers remain alert to the authority they exercise when interpreting other people."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

The dialectic cannot stop at a quotation, which is why a question the professor himself cannot answer earns an A.

"Citations are necessary for establishing intellectual provenance", but they "do not by themselves establish that a particular concept adequately explains the phenomenon currently being studied."

Having Derrida, Bourdieu, Geertz or Siegel available "does not remove the researcher’s obligation to continue examining the evidence, the context, and the adequacy of the interpretation." Citation can support inquiry or close it prematurely.

An abstract earns a B; a question, however weak, earns a B; a question he cannot answer earns an A. "The A therefore does not reward ignorance for its own sake. It rewards the capacity to open a problem in a way that makes the limits of current understanding visible".

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

Drawn as repeated S-curves, the dialectic shows each new encounter starting from a changed understanding rather than the same point.

"A circle, however, can also suggest that the process simply returns to the same starting point. That is not how I understand the consequence of Newness."

Once an encounter has produced new understanding, "the researcher has changed in some respect". A later encounter may reactivate the dialectic, "but Recall now operates on a different body of accumulated understanding."

"the loop explains how the dialectic operates within an encounter, while the S-curves show how repeated encounters can move understanding over time."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

Before anything can be recalled it must first have been learned, which adds a prior condition to the dialectic.

"Only after the conversation ended did another question occur to me. If the dialectic begins with Recall, then something must already exist to be recalled."

A person cannot reconsider knowledge that was never acquired, examine an assumption that was never formed, or loosen the authority of a framework that was never learned.

— Dr. Toronata Tambun, Learn to Forget (1 of 3)
