(1 of 3) When Anti-Fragility Emerges - Lessons from Maria João Pires' Experience

Maria João Pires sits down to play a concerto she has rehearsed for months, and the orchestra begins playing a different one entirely — one she hasn't touched in eleven months — and a single reassuring glance from the conductor is what steadies her enough to keep from walking off stage.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus

Opening

In 1999 at the Concertgebouw, the orchestra began playing a concerto she hadn't rehearsed in eleven months.

Pires had been called from Portugal late the previous night to replace another pianist at the last minute.

As the first notes rang out, they didn't match what was in her mind. Something was wrong. The orchestra was playing Mozart's Piano Concerto No. 20 in D minor, K. 466, not the concerto she had prepared. A cold, creeping panic spread through her. She hadn't rehearsed this piece in almost 11 months — eleven long months since she had last played K. 466.

Her body stiffened, her fingers hovering just above the piano, frozen. The conductor, Maestro Riccardo Chailly, continued leading the orchestra, oblivious to the tidal wave of fear crashing inside her.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
The Scene

A single reassuring glance from the conductor is what steadied her enough not to walk off stage.

In a desperate moment, Pires glanced toward Maestro Chailly, searching for some sign, some reprieve.

He continued conducting, his baton cutting through the air, guiding the orchestra through the bars of K. 466 as if everything was perfectly normal. As she locked eyes with him, the Maestro smiled — a calm, reassuring smile that seemed to cut through her panicking moment. His demeanor mirrored the unspoken message of his smile: you can do this. I know you'll recover. That external act of seeking help, however brief, was enough to steady her resolve.

In that brief glance, lasting no more than two seconds, Pires shrugged slightly, a gesture that communicated her resignation to the impossible situation she was facing. It was as if she was saying, I'm out of options. I'm stuck. The intensity of her emotions, from shock to the realization of being 'fucked' (in her own candid expression), was evident.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
The Turn

There was no way out but through, and her hands began to move.

The shift wasn't clarity or calm — it was acceptance.

She was going to play. No matter what.

— The author's own narration
What she found
She had played this piece before, eleven months ago. Somewhere, buried deep in her muscle memory, was the knowledge of K. 466. She could feel it, just out of reach. She just had to find it. She exhaled, a shaky breath that rattled through her chest, and slowly let her fingers drop to the keys.
