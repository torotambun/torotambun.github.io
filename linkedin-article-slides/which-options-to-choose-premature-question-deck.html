Which options to choose? That is a premature question! A lot of prerequisites to deliver prior

Which university to choose is the wrong first question for a coach to answer. The job is to build the student's own decision-negotiation framework — Strategic Negotiations plus behavioral-bias checks plus a weighted scoring model — before any option is compared, because a student who reasons through BATNA, ZOPA and their own biases ends up with a choice that is both rational and one they can live with, which a ranked list alone never delivers.

Opening

Empathy means building the student's framework, not handing them an answer.

How as a coach? Using empathy instead of sympathy.

Situation

A student faces a choice between universities and asks, in effect, which one to pick.

Complication

"Empathy in this context means guiding the student to construct their own framework rather than imposing one." A pre-made solution short-circuits the reasoning the decision is supposed to build.

Question

If the coach won't supply the answer, what does the coaching actually consist of?

Answer

Asking reflective questions that make the student visualize their experience at each university, challenging cognitive biases before they become emotional traps, and calibrating their reasoning toward a structured, strategic framework. "Empathy here means acting as a thinking partner, not a decision-maker."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

Treat the decision as a negotiation with your own future.

A decision-making framework rooted in Strategic Negotiations and Behavioral Science.

BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement): what is the fallback if the student chooses neither university?

Interests vs. Positions: instead of university rankings, what are the real interests — career prospects, faculty quality, networking?

Zone of Possible Agreement (ZOPA): what factors must be present for the student to feel satisfied with the choice?

Opportunity Cost Analysis: what would they give up by choosing one over the other?

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Bias Check

Four biases distort the comparison before any framework is applied.

Since human decision-making is prone to biases, behavioral science principles keep the comparison rational.

01
Familiarity
Status Quo Bias and Anchoring
Are they leaning toward a university just because they have heard of it more? Are they over-relying on a single data point, such as rankings, without considering other factors?
02
Fear and hindsight
Loss Aversion and Regret Minimization
Are they avoiding a university due to perceived risk rather than evaluating actual benefits? The Regret Minimization Framework — Jeff Bezos' method — asks the student to imagine their future self: what would they regret not choosing?

Evidence

A three-point weighted-score gap between two real universities is the whole point of the exercise.

A worked example using the Weighted Criteria Decision Making (WCDM) model, factors weighted 1–10 by personal importance, universities scored 1–10 per factor.

WORKED A
Edinburgh — weighted score 183
Career Prospects 8, Research & Faculty 9, Location & Lifestyle 7, Personal Fit 6, Opportunities & Flexibility 7, against weights of 9, 8, 6, 7 and 5.
WORKED B
Warwick — weighted score 186
Career Prospects 9, Research & Faculty 8, Location & Lifestyle 5, Personal Fit 8, Opportunities & Flexibility 6, against the same weights.
The question the model itself can't answer

"The higher total score suggests a better fit, but then : how significant if the delta is only 3?" The framework produces a number; it does not tell the student whether a three-point gap is decisive.

Conclusion

Objectives, long-term horizon and mindset should have been the coach's focus since step one.

The article's own closing point — arriving last, but meant to run underneath everything before it.

What would you do when you return from school? what is you dream for 5 years to come, 10 years to come? What do you envision yourself ?

— Closing section of the article
What the coach is actually assessing
"This is where the coach can see the maturity and critical thinking ability of the mentee."
Why it isn't really the last step
"Even though this the last point, but this should have been lingering since step A or step 1." The weighted model and the bias checks are only useful once the student's own long-term sense-making is already active.
The outcome the framework is built for
"A Data-Driven and Emotionally Aligned Decision" — ensuring rationality, strategic thinking and long-term perspective without falling into common decision-making traps.
