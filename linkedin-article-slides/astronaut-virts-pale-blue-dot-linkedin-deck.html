LinkedIn Article  ·  Reflection · The Astronaut's Perspective  ·  4 July 2019

An astronaut "The universe humbles us, if we are willing to step back and realize our place in it. IT MAKE THINGS LIKE POWER AND WEALTH SEEM SILLY"

The article recalls a session at the Harvard Business School GMP-26 with Commander Terry Virts, a fellow participant who commanded the International Space Station. A photo of a tiny Earth brings the author to Sagan's Pale Blue Dot and to his father's teaching from Tehilim 103, that people are dust. He reports a personal message from Virts that the universe humbles a person who steps back and realises their place in it, which makes power and wealth seem silly. He sees society acting with posturing, slander and character assassination, thanks Virts for a noble perspective, and closes on regret that people do not know they are dust on this tiny pale blue dot.

Opening

The author's memory of GMP-26 is a session with a fellow participant who commanded the International Space Station.

Situation

One of the author's reminiscences of GMP-26, the in-campus Executive Education programme of Harvard Business School, is the session with Commander Terry Virts, a fellow GMP participant from GMP-11. The article, citing Virts (2019), says he took command of the International Space Station as Commander of Expedition 43 after 200 days on his Expedition 42 and 43 flight, and is one of only four astronauts to have piloted a space shuttle, flown on a Russian Soyuz spacecraft, performed spacewalks and commanded the station.

Complication

In a personal chat, for which the author says he had Virts's permission in WhatsApp to share publicly, Virts made a remark that the author says inspires his very recent writing on the world-ready citizen. The author recalls that in the session he almost burst into tears at Virts's photo of the earth, which looked so tiny.

Question

What does a view of the earth from space do to the way power and wealth look?

Answer

According to the remark as reported, the universe humbles a person who is willing to step back and realize their place in it, and it makes power and wealth seem silly.

The Turns

A photo of a tiny Earth, Sagan's lines and a father's verse lead to one point: people are dust on a small planet.

The article moves through five turns. Its quotations from Sagan and from the psalm are described here and not reproduced.

1  ·  The session
At GMP-26 the author heard Commander Terry Virts, a fellow GMP participant, who commanded the International Space Station.
2  ·  The photo
The author says he almost burst into tears when he saw the photo of the earth that Virts took, because it was so tiny.
3  ·  Sagan's lines
The session brought him to Carl Sagan's Pale Blue Dot: the earth as a very small stage in a vast cosmic arena, the blood spilled by generals and emperors to be momentary masters of a fraction of a dot, and self-importance challenged by that point of pale blue light.
4  ·  The thanks
The author thanked Virts for bringing a noble perspective on how fragile lives are, and says it humbled him.
5  ·  The father's verse
He recalls what his father taught him, a simple framework borrowed from the past and everlasting: a verse from Tehilim 103, verses 14 to 16, that people are dust and their days are like grass.

What It Revealed

Society shows the posturing that the view from orbit makes seem silly, in slander and character assassination.

The author says many people in general society act as Sagan described, without knowing that they are just as dust on this tiny pale blue dot.

✗Posturing and imagined self-importance
✗The delusion of a privileged position in the universe
✗Conduct like the generals and emperors, not with swords but with slander
✗Character assassination
✗Not knowing that all are dust on a tiny pale blue dot
What the view from orbit gives
✓A noble perspective on how fragile lives are
✓Humility: the author says it humbled him
✓Power and wealth seen as silly, in the astronaut's reported words

What I Carry

The astronaut's remark humbled the author, who calls the perspective noble.

The article closes on regret that so many people do not know they are dust.

— Commander Terry Virts, in a personal chat, as reported by the author
