When Internships Become a Detour Instead of a Bridge

A student is in a fifth undergraduate year because of internship overload, not academic failure. The stacking is not pretentiousness — it is a rational response to signalling pressure, disappearing junior roles and institutions that never drew the line between training and experience. When internships become the goal instead of the gateway, students do not just delay careers. They delay completion.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus

Introduction

A student is in a fifth year, and not because of academic failure.

"One of my students is now in their fifth year of undergraduate study—not because of academic failure, but because of internship overload."

Situation

"From one brand to another, they kept chasing prestige. Many of these roles are paid—yes—but only at standard intern rates." "Not illegal, just disproportionate when weighed against the long-term cost of delaying graduation."

Complication

The student's own explanation was short: "Big brand, Pak. I couldn't miss the chance."

Question

"This isn't rare. More and more young people are stacking internships and portraying them as full-time work. Some of it feels like overclaiming." Is that the whole of it?

Answer

"But underneath the surface, there's something deeper going on." What follows is not a charge of vanity but a list of the pressures that make the behaviour rational.

The Mechanisms

Seven mechanisms, and not one of them is the student's vanity.

"Here's what the research shows…"

1  ·  Internships as signalling
"In crowded job markets—especially in places like Indonesia—youth use big-brand internships as proof of employability. This is classic signaling theory (Spence, 1973): the brand name matters more than the skill gained."
2  ·  The LinkedIn effect
"CVs today are performative. Marwick & boyd (2011) call it “context collapse”—where a single resume must impress peers, recruiters, and personal networks. So internships, no matter how short, get exaggerated into “roles.”"
3  ·  Junior jobs are disappearing
"Many industries have replaced entry-level jobs with unpaid or semi-paid internship cycles (Curiale, 2010). Interns now play the role of junior staff—with none of the long-term development."
4  ·  Meritocracy pressure
"Under capitalist meritocracy, “grind culture” is internalized. Internships become a form of “hustle capital” (Cabanas & Illouz, 2019)—a currency in the self-optimization race."
5  ·  University silence
"Many career centers don't explain the difference between training, exposure, and real work. So students overstate, not out of arrogance—but because no one taught them the boundary."
6  ·  Searching for identity
"Arnett (2000) calls this “emerging adulthood”—a phase where internships are used not just to earn, but to become someone. The danger? Identity formation turns into delay."
7  ·  Brands become meaning
"A short, low-responsibility internship at a famous firm gets more attention than building something real. Why? Because in symbolic terms (Baudrillard), the logo confers legitimacy."

The Judgment Withheld

The issue is not dishonesty; it is structural ambiguity.

"Many list these internships under “Work Experience” on LinkedIn—sometimes without indicating the short-term or training nature of the role. Is that wrong?"

"Not necessarily. It reflects how blurred the categories have become. When junior roles are scarce and signaling pressure is high, even a 6-week internship can be framed as a “position.”"

"The issue isn't dishonesty—it's structural ambiguity. Platforms, universities, and employers have not agreed on where training ends and experience begins."

That refusal to convict is the article's method, not a softening of it. The behaviour is explained by the incentives it answers, which is a harder claim than calling it overclaiming — because it locates the fault upstream of the student.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
The Cost

No one taught the boundary, so students overstate without arrogance.

What the accumulation actually does to a degree.

"Some stay in limbo, bouncing from one internship to another while thesis deadlines, capstone requirements, and even graduation dates are repeatedly pushed back."

"In extreme cases, the very purpose of school—to graduate and earn a diploma—gets quietly sidelined. Students keep collecting brand names, but lose sight of the finish line."

The word the article chooses is quietly. Nothing announces the drift; each individual decision looks like ambition rewarded, and the aggregate looks like a fifth year.

Conclusion

When internships become the goal instead of the gateway, completion is delayed.

"So what's really happening?"

When internships become the goal instead of the gateway, students don't just delay careers. They delay completion.

— When Internships Become a Detour Instead of a Bridge
What the behaviour actually is
"This isn't pretentiousness. It's a rational—but misdirected—response to broken systems, confusing signals, and institutional neglect." Three causes named, none of them located in the student's character.
What the loop feels like from inside
"Without reflective structures, many spiral into “CV loops” that feel productive—but quietly undermine the very purpose of school." Feeling productive is the trap: the loop supplies the sensation of progress while the degree recedes.
