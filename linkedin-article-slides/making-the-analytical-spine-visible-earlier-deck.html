Making the Analytical Spine Visible Earlier

The correction that came out of examination was not methodological but sequential: the analytic spine was already there, and became explicit too late for an unfamiliar reader. In autoethnography propositions are outcomes of immersion rather than inputs to inquiry, rigor is established by traceability rather than prediction, and beginning with propositions is not a neutral choice — it signals a misalignment between method and epistemology.

Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus

Opening

Progress came from making existing logic visible earlier, not from adding material.

From immersion to proposition: on sequencing in autoethnographic research.

Situation

"Sometimes progress in research does not come from adding new material, but from making the existing logic visible earlier." Recent feedback during examination helped surface a simple but important issue.

Complication

"The work itself had a clear analytic spine, but that spine became explicit too late for an unfamiliar reader." "What followed was not a methodological correction, but a sequencing correction."

Question

If the logic was sound and the material was sufficient, what exactly needed to change — and what did not?

Answer

"The substance did not change. The clarity did." Autoethnographic material, translated into analytic constructs, needs early signposting — not to change its epistemic origin, but to help readers track how lived experience becomes propositions, how propositions inform modelling, and how models are then used for policy reasoning.

Sequencing

Propositions are outcomes of immersion, not inputs to inquiry.

"This methodological position should not be read as a rejection of propositions, causal explanation, or analytical rigor. The distinction is one of sequencing rather than substance."

"In this study, propositions are treated as outcomes of immersion rather than inputs to inquiry." Given the exploratory nature of the phenomenon and the absence of stable prior theory at the level of lived entrepreneurial experience, "introducing propositions at the outset would have risked premature closure."

The decision was therefore to allow empirical material to stabilise through prolonged immersion, reflexive documentation and comparative interpretation before articulating analytic claims.

The propositions that appear later are grounded propositions — derived from observed regularities across autoethnographic accounts, functioning as provisional causal mechanisms that guide subsequent modelling and analysis. "They are not framed as hypotheses to be empirically tested in a confirmatory sense."

Postponed, not demoted

"propositions are postponed not because they are unimportant, but because introducing them too early would have reduced validity in an under-theorised domain."

Rigor

Rigor is achieved through traceability rather than prediction.

The standard the work asks to be held to, stated so that it can be applied against the work.

"Rigor in this approach is achieved through traceability rather than prediction." That is a demanding criterion rather than a lenient one: it requires that the chain from episode to claim survive inspection at every link.

"Every analytic claim can be traced back to documented episodes, reflexive memos, and iterative comparison over time." Nothing in the analysis is permitted to arrive from outside that record.

"Narrative operates here as a mode of representation, not as the method itself. The method consists of systematic reflexive analysis across temporal, institutional, and positional shifts." The distinction matters because the two are routinely conflated by readers unfamiliar with the tradition — which is precisely the legibility problem the examination surfaced.

Grounding

The epistemology is interpretivist; the orientation is pragmatist.

"The sequencing described above is not idiosyncratic, but reflects the epistemic commitments of auto-ethnography as a qualitative research tradition."

01
Epistemic grounding
Interpretivist and constructivist
Traditions that treat "meaning as emergent, situational, and co-constructed through experience and interpretation rather than discovered as objective fact." Knowledge claims are contextual and provisional, shaped by interaction between researcher, participants and setting, while remaining subject to systematic reflection (Schutz, 1967; Berger & Luckmann, 1966; Geertz, 1973; Lincoln & Guba, 1985).
02
Research orientation
Pragmatist
"the overarching research orientation is pragmatist, emphasising usefulness, problem-solving, and methodological fit over philosophical purity" (Dewey, 1938; Morgan, 2007; Morgan, 2014; Biesta, 2010; Johnson & Onwuegbuzie, 2004). The two commitments are stated as layered, not competing.
03
What follows from both
Fixed propositions are ruled out at the start
"In autoethnography, it is methodologically inappropriate to begin with fixed propositions." "Starting with propositions assumes stable variables and causal direction in advance, an assumption that autoethnography explicitly rejects" (Ellis, Adams, & Bochner, 2011; Chang, 2008).
What the canonical scholars say

Ellis and Bochner describe autoethnography as a process in which "the researcher writes into understanding," allowing analytic insight to surface through narrative, memory work and emotional recall rather than imposing explanatory frames at the outset (Ellis, 2004; Bochner, 2012). In this tradition, "experience is not evidence for propositions. Experience is the primary site of inquiry from which meaning is later interpreted."

The Risk

Early propositions become filters that silence disconfirming experience.

"The use of early propositions is explicitly cautioned against in qualitative methodology because it risks premature closure."

Beginning With Propositions
✗Charmaz notes that "forcing analytic frames too early distorts meaning and shifts the inquiry from discovery to confirmation" (Charmaz, 2014)
✗"Autoethnography intensifies this risk because the researcher and the field are inseparable."
✗"Early propositions function as filters that silence disconfirming or uncomfortable aspects of lived experience."
✗Stable variables and causal direction are assumed before anything has been observed
✗"Beginning with propositions is not neutral."
Beginning With Sensitising Concepts
✓"Where some orientation is necessary, the accepted practice is the use of sensitising concepts rather than propositions."
✓"Sensitising concepts provide general points of attention without specifying relationships or outcomes" (Blumer, 1954; Charmaz, 2014)
✓They allow empirical material to talk back to the researcher
✓"These concepts remain provisional and are expected to evolve or even dissolve during the research process."
✓What precedes propositions is immersion and reflexive documentation — fieldnotes, analytic memos, narrative fragments (Emerson, Fretz, & Shaw, 2011)
Where propositions belong when they do appear

"When propositions do appear in strong autoethnographic dissertations, they are typically located in later analytic chapters and framed carefully as interpretive outcomes. They explain what the immersion revealed, not what the researcher intended to demonstrate" (Chang, 2008; Adams, Holman Jones, & Ellis, 2015). "Only after repeated cycles of reflection and comparison do patterns begin to stabilise" — and even then "they are retrospective constructions, not starting assumptions."

The Line

Immersion comes first; propositions come later, if at all.

"So the hard methodological line is this."

In autoethnography, immersion and reflection come first. Propositions come later, if they appear at all.

— Making the Analytical Spine Visible Earlier
Why the choice is not neutral
"Beginning with propositions is not neutral. It signals a misalignment between method and epistemology, and examiners tend to catch it immediately." The consequence is not a stylistic penalty. It is a reader concluding that the method and the epistemology were never reconciled.
What examination actually did here
"This was a useful reminder that good examination is not about forcing conformity, but about improving legibility across traditions. When that happens, everyone wins. The reader, the work, and the argument."
How the note closes
"Grateful for careful reading and precise questions. They sharpen thinking more than agreement ever could."
