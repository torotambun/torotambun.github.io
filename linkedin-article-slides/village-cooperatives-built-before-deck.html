Indonesia Has Built Village Cooperatives Before. What Will Make This One Last?

State initiation is part of both the KUD history and the KDMP present, so it cannot explain why some village cooperatives developed continuing economic lives and others did not. What will decide this one is whether members commit their own capital, transact recurringly and hold authority over decisions — none of which registration, buildings or an approved loan can demonstrate.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Eighty thousand cooperatives are being formed, and the count of them has become the measure of progress.

Koperasi Desa/Kelurahan Merah Putih, on a scale rarely attempted in the country's cooperative history.

Situation

Presidential Instruction No. 9 of 2025 directed the accelerated formation of 80,000 cooperatives. The programme is intended to strengthen village economies and improve access to essential goods and services.

Complication

"Its scale has made the number of established cooperatives an immediate measure of progress." Yet Indonesia is returning to institutional territory it has occupied for more than half a century, and the national target gives officials a strong incentive to complete incorporation quickly.

Question

If the number formed is what gets counted, what would actually show whether a cooperative has become an economic institution rather than a legal one?

Answer

Recurring member transactions, financial performance, disputes over governance, and business results after the first period of government support. Registration, buildings and an approved loan show none of these.

Gap

Indonesia has built village cooperatives from above before, so "created from above" cannot be the diagnosis.

The modern history began with the Unit Desa framework in 1973. The special policy framework for KUD remained until Presidential Instruction No. 18 of 1998 revoked the 1984 instruction.

In 1978 the government adopted the combined Badan Usaha Unit Desa and Koperasi Unit Desa structure, known as BUUD/KUD, and national policy shifted more clearly toward KUD in 1984. A 1981 presidential instruction directed the provision and development of post-harvest facilities for KUD to support domestic food procurement. KUD became visible through services and government programmes that connected national agricultural policy with village economic activity.

This history complicates a common description of the present programme. "KUD is sometimes remembered as a community institution, while Koperasi Desa Merah Putih is criticized as a cooperative created from above. Yet BUUD/KUD and KUD also grew through presidential instructions, public financing and assigned economic functions."

KUD varied across locations and periods. Some served farmers through activities tied to production and marketing. Others weakened when public assignments changed, managers performed poorly, or members no longer found sufficient reason to participate.

Core Gap

"State initiation is therefore part of both histories. It cannot explain why some cooperatives developed continuing economic lives while others became inactive or dependent on government assignments." The relevant inheritance is not a verdict on KUD. It is the record of what happened when state-supported organizations met different levels of local demand, management capability and member commitment.

Framework

What made cooperatives last elsewhere was recurring member business and retained member authority.

Hatta sets the demanding standard; Japan and Germany show what it looks like when the standard is met.

01
The standard
Hatta's economic democracy
He viewed cooperation as part of economic democracy and connected it with collective ownership, self-help, participation and moral responsibility. "A cooperative could distribute goods or perform a public assignment, but those activities alone did not make it democratic." Its character came from members accepting ownership, participating in decisions and learning to sustain their common enterprise.
02
The Japanese case
Recurring business, not membership on paper
Japanese agricultural cooperatives became useful because farmers conducted recurring business through them, including marketing, purchasing, finance and insurance. Use, repeated over time, is what made the institution real.
03
The German case
Federations that audit and advise
Germany built cooperative federations that audit member organizations and provide advice. "They endured because farmers kept using them and retained authority over their management."
04
The test that follows
Ownership begins with capital and authority
"Registration gives a cooperative legal existence. Buildings and equipment allow it to operate. Government financing may ease the start. None of this shows whether villagers have become active owners." Ownership begins when members commit their own capital and exercise authority over the cooperative's decisions.

Details

A Rp3 billion loan at 6 percent falls due whether or not members have started trading.

Minister of Finance Regulation No. 49 of 2025 sets the financing structure, and the structure makes the ownership question immediate rather than theoretical.

1  ·  The terms
Each KDMP may borrow up to Rp3 billion at an annual rate of 6 percent, with a maximum tenor of 72 months and a grace period of six months that may be extended to eight. Up to Rp500 million may be used for operating expenses.
2  ·  These are loans, not grants
"A cooperative that lacks recurring member business may therefore face repayment obligations before it has developed a stable market." The financing does not wait for the trading relationship that is supposed to service it.
3  ·  The village head sits inside the credit decision
A loan requires approval from the village head following a village deliberation, and may be backed by Village Funds for repayment. "That arrangement may strengthen public oversight. It may also give village officials considerable influence over an organization that is supposed to be governed by its members."
4  ·  Prescribed models may not fit the village
Officials have developed standard business models, digital monitoring and financing arrangements for new cooperatives. "These may help them begin operating. They may also constrain local decisions when prescribed models do not match village production, consumer demand or existing commercial relationships."
5  ·  Rapid formation inverts the sequence
"Rapid formation becomes problematic when the legal entity precedes a shared business purpose among its members." The target creates the entity first and leaves the purpose to be found afterwards.

Evidence

Where a BUM Desa already works, two publicly supported bodies may chase the same customers.

The institutional problem is sharpest where the village already has an economic institution doing the same work.

EVIDENCE A
Different ownership, overlapping business
A BUM Desa is owned through the village, while a cooperative is owned and governed by its members. "In practice, both may sell basic goods, market farm produce, manage storage, provide credit or operate village services."
EVIDENCE B
The regulation names the issue without settling it
Minister of Finance Regulation No. 49 of 2025 requires KDMP business activities to consider economic institutions already present in the village. "The regulation recognizes the issue but does not determine which institution should operate which business." Without local differentiation, two publicly supported organizations may pursue the same customers, suppliers and assets.
EVIDENCE C
The village is not empty ground
Farmer groups, traders, savings groups and older cooperatives may already perform parts of the economic role assigned to KDMP. "A new cooperative may fill an unmet need or duplicate work already done by others."
EVIDENCE D
Support can substitute for member investment
"Government funding can help a sound enterprise begin, but heavy dependence on it may weaken member investment and financial discipline." Village officials can convene meetings and solve early coordination problems; the risk begins when they continue to control decisions that belong to members.

Implications

Registration, buildings and an approved loan show constitution on paper, not control in practice.

The documents record how the cooperative is constituted. They are silent on the questions that decide whether it lasts.

What the Documents Show
✓That the cooperative legally exists
✓That a loan was approved and a deliberation held
✓That premises and equipment were provided
✓That a membership list was recorded
What They Cannot Show
✗Who dominates meetings, selects suppliers or challenges managers
✗Whether members joined voluntarily and still use the cooperative
✗Whether the business can repay what was approved
✗Whether members control it in practice
Where the Difficult Work Begins

"Indonesia already knows how to create village organizations through national policy. The more difficult work begins after registration, when members must decide what the cooperative sells, contribute capital, choose its board, supervise hired managers and replace leaders who fail."

Conclusion

Judge it on recurring member transactions after the government support ends.

Hatta's idea of economic democracy placed the decisive powers in the hands of members. The measurement should follow the powers.

1
Measure transactions, not incorporations
Recurring member business is the variable that separated the KUDs that lasted from the ones that did not. It is also the variable a loan repayment schedule depends on.
2
Publish financial performance and governance disputes
Disagreement over suppliers, boards and managers is evidence that members are exercising authority. Its complete absence is not a sign of health.
3
Differentiate KDMP from an existing BUM Desa locally
The regulation requires that existing village institutions be considered but does not allocate the business. Where both are publicly supported, someone local has to decide who sells what — before both chase the same customers.
4
Set the assessment point after the support period
"That evidence will show whether the cooperative has become commercially viable beyond its initial government support." Assessment inside the support window measures the support.

Where key decisions remain concentrated among officials or managers appointed by higher authorities, the cooperative may meet its legal requirements while members remain owners only on paper.
