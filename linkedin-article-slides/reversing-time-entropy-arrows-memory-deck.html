(2 of 5) Reversing Time: A Technical Return to Entropy, Arrows, and Memory

The same answer as the first instalment, now carrying the mathematics that makes it binding: the Second Law, Boltzmann's statistical reading of it, the light cone and the cosmic background all point one way, and every construction that would permit a loop is either unobserved or unstable. Returning to first principles here is not an academic exercise but a response to a culture in which memory is short and causality is confused.

The Law

Local order is always purchased, and the receipt is written somewhere else in the universe.

"Not really — at least not globally, not in any closed system." That is the article's answer to whether entropy can be reversed, and the qualification is the whole of it.

"The Second Law doesn’t forbid local decreases in entropy, but it demands a price: any local reversal must be offset by a greater increase in entropy elsewhere."

"Even if the system’s entropy decreases (ΔSsystem <0), the surroundings must absorb enough disorder (ΔSsurroundings> 0) to keep the total entropy increasing or, at best, constant in ideal reversible processes."

Carnot closes the same door from the engineering side. "no engine operating between two heat reservoirs can be more efficient than a Carnot engine", and the efficiency formula "quietly embeds the Second Law: you can’t get 100% efficiency unless the cold reservoir is at absolute zero — which, in our universe, is unreachable. So again, entropy wins."

The Arrow

Time's direction is not written into the equations; it is written into the odds.

"While microscopic laws (e.g., Newton’s or Schrödinger’s equations) are time-reversible, macroscopic irreversibility arises from statistical mechanics."

ARGUMENT A
The asymmetry is combinatorial
"The number of disordered microstates vastly outnumbers ordered ones, making forward entropy evolution overwhelmingly probable."
ARGUMENT B
Boltzmann's attempt, and its objection
The H-Theorem of 1872 tried to derive the direction from statistics. It ran into Loschmidt's paradox: "If the underlying physical laws are symmetric in time, why does entropy always increase? Why not decrease if we reverse all velocities?"
ARGUMENT C
The answer that survived
"entropy increases not because it must, but because high-entropy states are far more probable. While entropy decrease is not forbidden, it is vanishingly improbable in systems with a large number of particles — like the universe itself."
ARGUMENT D
Two independent confirmations
Relativity supplies structure: "The light cone separates events that can influence each other (inside the cone) from those that cannot (outside the cone)." Cosmology supplies history: the CMB is "a near-perfect blackbody spectrum at ~2.725 K, discovered by Penzias and Wilson in 1965", and "The early universe was in a low-entropy state (a smooth, hot plasma), and entropy has been increasing ever since".

The Machinery

Five constructions would permit or forbid a time loop, and not one of them has been observed.

The article works through the formal apparatus rather than the intuition.

1  ·  Closed timelike curves
"a solution to Einstein’s field equations of general relativity where spacetime loops back on itself." The Gödel metric of 1949, "a rotating universe solution", permits them — but "such spacetimes violate conditions like global hyperbolicity and may be physically unstable."
2  ·  Novikov self-consistency
"any event occurring on a CTC must be self-consistent — i.e., it must not give rise to paradoxes." "No inconsistent histories can evolve, no matter how complex the system."
3  ·  Many-Worlds
Everett's 1957 interpretation "eliminates wavefunction collapse from quantum mechanics" and holds that "all possible outcomes of a quantum event actually occur — each in a separate, non-communicating branch of the universe." "the universe is fundamentally deterministic, but observers experience probabilistic outcomes due to their entanglement with different branches."
4  ·  Chronology protection
Hawking, 1992: "quantum field effects near CTCs (especially vacuum fluctuations) would diverge, making the formation of CTCs physically impossible." "This divergence would destabilize any time machine-like geometry before it forms — thus protecting causality."
5  ·  Emergent time
In causal set theory, loop quantum gravity and AdS/CFT duality, "time and even spacetime itself may not be fundamental, but emergent". Then "causality might arise only as a large-scale approximation, similar to how temperature emerges from molecular motion."

The Fiction

One film's premise has an equation behind it and the other's has none.

The technical instalment revisits the same two films with the mathematics attached.

Interstellar — inside general relativity
✓"According to Einstein's general relativity, time passes more slowly in stronger gravitational fields."
✓On Miller's Planet, "While the 7:1 time ratio is highly extreme, it’s not strictly impossible if the conditions (spin, mass, proximity) are right."
✓The Einstein-Rosen bridge is "a solution to the Einstein field equations"
✓But it "is not traversable unless it is stabilized by exotic matter with negative energy density — a concept permitted by the Casimir Effect in quantum field theory but never observed at the macroscopic scale"
✓The tesseract's fifth dimension is the article's own notation, offered as speculative extension rather than as physics
Tenet — outside it
✗"Entropy inversion, as depicted in Tenet, has no basis in known thermodynamics or quantum mechanics."
✗It "would require precisely reversing the momentum, spin, and quantum state of every particle — a violation of statistical mechanics"
✗"While time-reversal symmetry exists in microscopic laws, applying it macroscopically is not feasible under the Second Law."
✗"particles in quantum field theory do not possess intrinsic time-directional identity"
✗"Particle-antiparticle annihilation involves quantum numbers and charge, not temporal orientation."

The Speculation

Three multiverse models are mathematically consistent and evidentially empty.

String theory "is only mathematically consistent in higher-dimensional spacetime" — ten dimensions for superstring theory, eleven for M-theory. Compactified at the Planck scale, these "may contain parallel branes — other “universes” with their own physics, separated by microscopic distances in higher-dimensional space."

In eternal inflation, "quantum fluctuations cause inflation to stop in some regions but continue in others, creating a multiverse of bubble universes. Each bubble may have different physical constants, vacuum energies, or dimensional properties."

"As of today, none of these multiverse models — MWI, string landscape, or inflationary bubble universes — have direct empirical support."

"Some indirect hints (e.g., fine-tuning of physical constants, observed vacuum energy) are sometimes cited, but these remain speculative. The multiverse currently resides at the intersection of theoretical physics, cosmology, and philosophy."

The Turn

What is irreversible turns out to be moral before it is thermodynamic.

"In the equations, time is asymmetrical. In the universe, entropy rises. And in this moment — in culture, in discourse, in memory — the decay feels personal."

Maybe the second law isn’t just about heat and disorder — maybe it’s a quiet echo of something older: That what is lost matters.

— (2 of 5) Reversing Time
The older statement of the same law
"You turn people back to dust, saying, ‘Return to dust, you mortals.’ A thousand years in your sight are like a day that has just gone by, or like a watch in the night... Teach us to number our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom." (Psalm 90:3–4, 12, quoted in the article)
What physics does not supply
"Physics teaches us that time has a direction. But Scripture reminds us that time also has a purpose." The article does not collapse one into the other; it sets them beside each other.
The instruction that follows
"That what is irreversible is not just physical, but moral. And that wisdom begins when we recognize how brief and fragile this timeline truly is." "Let those who measure entropy also remember to number their days — not just in hours, but in meaning."
