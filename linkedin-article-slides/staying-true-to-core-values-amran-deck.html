Staying True to Core Values: Lessons on Career and Life from My Steadfast Friend Amran, a Member of Jemaah Tabligh

A friend whose appearance invites the label radical made an empirical, reasoned choice to leave a corporate ladder that required compromising his values — and Schein's three levels explain why the appearance is exactly the wrong place to look.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Opening

A classmate who could have run a state enterprise decided in 2006 that the next rung required a compromise he would not make.

A reconnection with a classmate from the MM programme at IPMI Institute in the year 2000 — known then for his sharp intellect and empirical approach.

He climbed the corporate ladder to become a Vice President in one of Indonesia's state-owned enterprises. "However, by 2006, he faced a moral dilemma. To advance further, he realized that compromising his ethical integrity and personal values was inevitable."

"Instead of succumbing, he made a bold decision: corporate leadership was not his calling." He revisited the values instilled by his parents during his early childhood — "values rooted in simplicity, honesty, and service to others."

That exploration led him to Jemaah Tabligh, a global Islamic movement focused on personal spiritual growth and community engagement. On retiring a few years ago, he committed to it full time. The decision is the article's starting fact, and everything that follows is an attempt to explain why it is so easily misread.

What is being claimed here

Not that leaving was noble, and not that staying would have been corrupt. The claim is narrower and harder: that the advancement on offer had a price stated in values, and that he read the price accurately.

The Misreading

The robe, the beard and the skullcap invite a verdict his reasoning does not support.

"What surprised me during our conversation was how his outward appearance—a traditional Islamic robe, skullcap, and long beard—might lead some to stereotype him as a radical or ultra-conservative."

What the appearance suggests to an outsider
✗Radicalism, or at least ultra-conservatism
✗Ideological commitment displacing independent thought
✗Rigidity — a life narrowed by rules
✗Withdrawal from the world into an insular group
✗A break with the empirical mind he was known for
What the conversation showed
✓"I saw a deeply rational, empirical thinker who used his sharp mind to pursue a pacifist and purpose-driven life"
✓He "had not become an ideologue but had chosen to step out of his comfort zone to contribute to others' spiritual well-being"
✓The same intellect, redirected rather than surrendered
✓Movement outward — self-funded journeys to communities, not retreat from them
✓A choice reached by reasoning about cost, not by conversion away from reasoning
Where the article turns

"Then, as he explained his journey, I realized how misunderstood movements like Jemaah Tabligh often are, and how insights from Edgar Schein's framework can help us move past superficial judgments to appreciate their deeper cultural dynamics."

The Movement

Jemaah Tabligh's distinctiveness is not its dress but its refusal to enter politics.

Founded in early twentieth-century India by Maulana Muhammad Ilyas. Its core teachings are the Six Points, and the article sets them out in order.

1  ·  Faith in Allah
Iman. The first of the six, and the ground of the rest.
2  ·  Prayer with devotion
Salah — performing the daily prayers with attention rather than as observance alone.
3  ·  Knowledge and remembrance
Acquiring Islamic knowledge, ilm, while engaging in remembrance of Allah, dhikr. Knowledge and practice are paired rather than sequenced.
4  ·  Respect and service
Ikram al-Muslim — respecting and serving fellow Muslims.
5  ·  Sincerity
Ikhlas — maintaining sincerity in all actions.
6  ·  Time given to dawah
Tafrigh waqt li dawah — dedicating time to spreading Islam's message. Members embark on self-funded missionary journeys for set durations: three, forty, or 120 days.
What actually sets it apart

"What sets Jemaah Tabligh apart from other Islamic movements is its apolitical stance" and its grassroots missionary approach. "Unlike groups focused on systemic change or political activism" it treats self-reformation as the foundation for any broader change. The movement operates "across over 150 countries".

The criticism, as the article records it

"Critics sometimes argue that its avoidance of political issues overlooks the socio-political dimensions of Islam." And: "Others view its emphasis on personal spirituality as overly simplistic, potentially neglecting broader societal challenges."

The Method

It answers doubt with practice rather than argument, which is why Amran declined my question about the afterlife.

The movement's approach to skepticism is not a debating position. It is a refusal to debate.

Jemaah Tabligh addresses skepticism about the afterlife with a non-confrontational and experiential methodology, "avoiding debates (jadal) or attempts to “prove” doctrines through scientific means". The invitation is grounded in lived experience rather than intellectual argumentation.

"Rather than challenging individuals' beliefs head-on, Jemaah Tabligh encourages participation in Islamic practices such as prayer (salah), dhikr, and community life." The underlying belief is that "consistent engagement in these practices will naturally foster spiritual conviction".

Where afterlife appeals do not land, the movement points at present-day results instead — discipline built by prayer, family and social bonds strengthened by community life, inner peace and moral character. The article is specific about why this matters: "in many high-profile corruption cases, religious figures may face limited earthly punishment, undermining their moral credibility and highlighting the need for alternative approaches to inspire ethical behavior."

It also appeals to ground that does not require agreement. Ethical living, responsibility toward others and personal accountability "are principles that transcend religious boundaries. These values provide common ground for dialogue, encouraging mutual understanding and respect".

And then the personal instance. When the author offered his own article on Plato's Myth of Hades and the concept of Sheol, Amran declined to take the afterlife question further. Read against the method just described, that is not evasion — it is the method working exactly as documented.

The Framework

Schein separates what a culture shows, what it says, and what it assumes without saying.

"Edgar Schein's framework for organizational culture offers a powerful lens for dissecting and understanding the dynamics of any group, including movements like Jemaah Tabligh."

01
Level one
Artifacts
The visible and tangible elements — what an outsider can observe directly. Attire, observable behaviours, lifestyle, gatherings. This is the only level available to someone who has not asked a question.
02
Level two
Espoused values
What the movement states it strives for: adherence to the Sunnah, dawah as a duty for all Muslims rather than scholars alone, personal piety and reformation, non-engagement in politics, unity and brotherhood across cultural, ethnic and national boundaries.
03
Level three
Shared values
The unspoken, deeply held beliefs that "drive members' actions, often unconsciously" Not stated in any brochure, and not visible in any photograph — but the level that actually explains behaviour.
Why the model is the right instrument here

"By examining Artifacts, Espoused Values, and Basic Underlying Assumptions (Shared Values), this model provides a structured way to move beyond surface impressions to the deeper cultural forces that drive behavior".

The Visible

Every visible marker is a claim about simplicity that outsiders read as rigidity.

The artifact level in full, as the article sets it out — and the level at which the misreading is manufactured.

ARTIFACT A
Physical symbols
Adherence to the Sunnah reflected in dress — white robes, long beards and skullcaps for men; modest clothing, hijabs or niqabs, for women. "These symbols often vary according to regional norms but consistently emphasize simplicity and modesty".
ARTIFACT B
Observable behaviours
Ghast, door-to-door dawah inviting individuals to mosques and prayer. Group prayers, strict adherence to prayer schedules, and travelling in groups for missionary trips, khuruj, where members often stay in mosques.
ARTIFACT C
Minimalistic lifestyle
"During missions, members sleep on mosque floors and consume simple meals, demonstrating their commitment to humility and focus on their faith's essentials".
ARTIFACT D
Gatherings and activities
Large-scale Ijtima congregations "draw thousands to share spiritual insights and renew commitments, while smaller group discussions focus on personal and spiritual growth".
The failure mode, named by the article

"While these artifacts are easily visible, they often lead to misconceptions. For instance, the traditional attire or missionary focus might be misinterpreted as rigidity or radicalism, whereas they actually reflect devotion, humility, and a deep sense of purpose".

The Unstated

The assumptions doing the work are the ones no one says aloud: life is a test, and action outranks theology.

Schein's third level — "the unspoken, deeply held beliefs that drive members' actions, often unconsciously"

1  ·  Faith in Allah as the sole provider of success
Success, in this life and the Hereafter, "is seen as achievable only through complete obedience to Allah".
2  ·  The importance of tabligh for salvation
"Participating in dawah is believed to strengthen faith and secure spiritual rewards". The missionary journey is not outreach as a programme; it is understood as constitutive of the member's own standing.
3  ·  Universal brotherhood
"An intrinsic belief that all Muslims belong to a single, interconnected community, fostering collective responsibility and support".
4  ·  Practical Islam over theoretical knowledge
"The movement emphasizes action—living Islam through deeds—over intellectual or theological debates". This is the assumption that produced the declined afterlife conversation.
5  ·  Life as a temporary test
"Life is viewed as a preparation for the Hereafter, where deeds determine eternal outcomes". Everything visible at the artifact level follows from this one, and none of it announces it.

What It Settles

The level of a culture that is easiest to see is the level that misleads.

"Schein's framework reveals the depth of Jemaah Tabligh's cultural fabric."

It encourages us to seek the “why” behind the “what”—to focus on the shared human values that connect us, even in the diversity of our beliefs and practices.

— Dr. Toronata Tambun
The ordering of the three levels
"While artifacts such as attire or missionary practices are immediately noticeable, they represent only the surface of a much deeper structure." Espoused values clarify intentions, but "it is the shared values—those deeply ingrained, often unspoken assumptions—that truly drive and sustain its mission".
What that means for a stranger's verdict
A judgment formed at the artifact level is formed at the only level Schein says is not explanatory. The robe and the beard are real; they simply do not carry the information a reader takes from them.
Back to the classmate
The 2006 decision and the appearance that now provokes the stereotype are the same reasoning at two different levels. "In analyzing Jemaah Tabligh, this structured approach illustrates the importance of looking beyond what is visible or stated."
