Entrepreneur During Crisis

The article prints a 24-point speech to an entrepreneurship class at Universitas Gadjah Mada on 17 April 2020. It argues that the aim in a crisis is antifragile people, then antifragile teams, organisations and society, and that resilience alone falls short. Management systems over-designed for efficiency are exposed, and a crisis asks for the unnatural behaviour of leadership. A firm needs an optimal core and a fast agile unit. Where nobody knows anything, action runs through sensemaking, fast iteration and small steps. Leaders sustain people through a marathon with honest hope, self-care and empathy, and purpose that is real and consistent must drive the enterprise.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus

Introduction

The aim in a crisis is antifragile people, then antifragile teams and organisations, and resilience alone falls short.

Situation

The speech was given to the entrepreneurship class of the Electrical and Instrumentation Engineering department at Universitas Gadjah Mada on 17 April 2020. It opens with wishes that the students stay safe, keep their distance, eat and rest well and encourage others to do the same, because they are an important resource for family, university and society. It says it is inspired mostly by a webinar by Professor Bill Aulet of the MIT Martin Trust Center in an antifragile speaker series, and by a class on leadership during crisis by Professor Dutch Leonard of Harvard Business School.

Complication

The speaker says clients read the projects that create start-ups in universities or corporations as more efficient product development, management and marketing, or as a curriculum, and that this misses the bigger point, which is human capital rather than product. The aim is not necessarily start-ups but antifragile humans, then antifragile teams and organisations. People call this resilience, but resilience is going ahead without pause: fragile is the negative condition, resilient the neutral condition and antifragile the positive condition. The speech also says "The pace of change will never be slower than it is today", a line it credits to Sue Siegel, Chief Innovation Officer of GE, at MIT in April 2019, and it likens the line to Moore's Law and Metcalfe's Law.

Question

What must an entrepreneur be, and do, when a crisis makes the future unknowable?

Answer

The speech answers with leadership, not management: build antifragile people, act in small fast steps with honest hope, and let purpose drive the enterprise.

Evidence note

The speech is an oral address and gives no data of its own.

Gap

Over-designed management systems are exposed in a crisis, which asks for the unnatural behaviour that the speech calls leadership.

Systems over-designed by management, focused on earnings per share, supply chain efficiency, return on net assets and a fixed set of metrics, are exposed when conditions change. The speech quotes Warren Buffett: "It is only when the tide goes out that you learn who has been swimming naked". Because the expected actions in a crisis are unnatural, it says, they take what it calls leadership.

Natural behaviour
✗Blaming
✗Seeing others as obstacles
✗Advocating, in order to convince
✗Always offering statements and directives
✗Always referring to a limited mental model
What a crisis needs
✓No blame culture, but learning why this happened
✓Viewing others as resources
✓Seeking to understand
✓Offering questions and help
✓Creating new knowledge, all in this together
Leadership and management

The speech calls them fundamentally different. Leadership creates followers, is an ageless phenomenon, and has three core processes: establishing direction, aligning people, and motivating and inspiring. Management lets large and complex organisations reach order and consistency, has been analysed closely only in roughly the past 100 years, and covers planning and budgeting, organising and staffing, and controlling and problem solving. In a crisis, and in a time of faster movement, leadership is what is needed.

Framework

A firm needs two operating systems, and a crisis calls for more flow of ideas between the optimal core and the fast agile unit.

The speech pictures a giant ball and a marble separated by a wall, two operating systems behind a firewall, with a bottleneck, opportunities and certain exposures between them. It says the flow of ideas between the two must be greater in a time of crisis.

01
The large ball
The core business: optimal and repeatable
The bigger ball is the core business, which is optimal and repeatable. The speech says certain exposures are unbearable for the mother company.
02
The marble behind the firewall
The agile unit: fast, flexible and iterative
The much smaller unit is very agile and iterative and delivers innovation to the company. It has a very fast clock, is very flexible, has global exposure and can take a more calculated risk. By nature it is always validated and matured in finding innovative solutions.

Details

When nobody knows anything, act through sensemaking, fast iteration and small steps, one at a time.

Points 7 to 10 of the speech set out how to act when there are no off-the-shelf answers.

1  ·  Sensemaking
The speech calls it a key leadership capability: structuring the unknown so as to act in it. It means forming a plausible map of a shifting world, testing the map with others through data, action and conversation, and refining or abandoning it depending on how credible it is.
2  ·  Clock speed
How fast one can respond. In a normal phase being a first mover is often a disadvantage, but the speech says that in a crisis of this size it is not. Fast iteration and rapid feedback loops let one learn before others.
3  ·  Nobody knows anything
There are no off-the-shelf answers, and many problems have never been seen before. The speech says major emergencies are unprecedented, that whoever claims to know is wrong, and that crisis leadership is working it out in real time under stress. It cites Bill Aulet's account of the Netflix founder, whose wife always says things will never work, and who tests that over and over.
4  ·  Rules of combat
Simplify and prioritise. Choose a direction, take small steps in it and reassess after each one, as small experiments with a direction, not all in at once. The speech puts it as one step, see it, then move.
5  ·  The Navy SEAL routine
The speech says Navy SEALs train the frontal lobes to control the amygdala, the panic system, through goal setting, mental rehearsal or a mental model, positive self-talk at 300-1000 words per minute as printed, and arousal control by breathing and silencing the mind.
Evidence note

The Netflix anecdote is second-hand through Aulet and the founder is not named. The SEAL training claim is stated without a source.

Implications

Leaders sustain people through a marathon with honest hope, self-care and empathy that turns to the next play.

Points 11 to 16, 19 and 20 of the speech set out what leaders must do for people over a long crisis.

1  ·  The third crisis
The speech says this is not only a medical crisis but a humanitarian one, an economic one, with 33 percent said to be unemployed, and a mental health one, and that if hope is lost the fight is lost. All of these must be understood to be antifragile and to come out stronger while iterating a sustainable solution.
2  ·  Faith and hope
The power of believing it will be all right in the end is not to be underestimated. People must start with hope, and people without brutally honest hope or faith, whatever it is called, will not be as effective.
3  ·  Oxygen mask first
One must preserve oneself first and be strong in order to help others.
4  ·  A marathon, with routines
Nothing good happens quickly. The work takes mental, economic and physical health over a long time while helping others, and it is grinding it out, not a Eureka moment. The speech advises small things that sustain, meditation, hiking and routines, including the SEAL breathing techniques.
5  ·  Clear simple plans
Lead with four things: hope, strength, guidance and community.
6  ·  The Stockdale paradox
Taken from Good to Great as the speech cites it: never confuse the faith that one will prevail in the end, which must never be lost, with the discipline to confront the most brutal facts of current reality. In dire circumstances leaders must be brutally honest and give a rational basis for hope, and communication must be regular, transparent and truthful.
7  ·  Empathy, then the next play
First express human empathy for the humanitarian, economic and mental health catastrophe, then pivot to what can be made of the new reality. The speaker says the talk opened with wishes for safety for that reason, and warns that the anxieties and constant surprises are wearying, so sanctuary must be sought.
Evidence note

The 33 percent unemployment figure carries no source, and the Stockdale passage is attributed to a book whose author is not named.

Conclusion

Purpose, real and consistent, must drive the enterprise, because each antifragile level does not guarantee the next.

The final points turn from surviving the crisis to what should be built through it.

1
Use the new technology and understand the new norms
The speech names Zoom, Teams and Webex for online exams and classes and says not to hold onto the past. It adds that geography matters less than it did, which has been possible for a while and is now ubiquitous.
2
Be purposeful, not profiteering
Great sustainable companies, teams, products and people will, more than ever, be driven by purpose and not by money. The speech adds that the love of money is the root of all evil.
3
Make the purpose real and consistent
The purpose must be real and authentic, and so must the leadership's authenticity about it, or it will not last. The speech asks for walking the walk, not only talking the talk.
4
Build antifragility at each level
Individuals, teams, organisations and society each need the one before, but none is predestined: an antifragile individual does not guarantee an antifragile team, nor a team an organisation, nor an organisation a society. Crisis leadership is most needed.
5
Look for the opportunities on the other side
The final point turns to what lies beyond the crisis and cites the late rapper Biggie Smalls on going from negative to positive.
