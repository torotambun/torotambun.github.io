The Danger of Calling Students Heroes Too Early

Zero to hero is not merely a generous slogan. It is false, because it awards the verdict at the beginning. It survives because it pays the family, the institution and the student at once — and every payment is drawn against formation that has not happened yet. Heroism is a long verdict.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

A 3.96 GPA did not settle a life, and a one-point-something did not end one.

Two students, described in the same conversation by a lecturer the author admires.

Situation

One graduated with a GPA of 3.96 and still did not secure a good job. The other twice fell into what some called nasib satu koma, one point something GPA, recovered, graduated, joined an oil and gas service company, and eventually made it to Houston from Jakarta.

Complication

A high GPA may show discipline, intelligence and seriousness within a formal system. It does not settle life. It does not guarantee judgment, resilience, communication, maturity, or the ability to grow through difficulty.

Question

If the transcript does not predict the outcome, what exactly is being certified when a young person is called a hero at twenty-two?

Answer

Nothing that has been tested yet. The contrast between these two students should disturb any easy theory of education — and it is the reason the author has become suspicious of a slogan.

The Slogan

The slogan is not merely too generous; it is false, because it gives false formation.

Why zero to hero fails as a description before it fails as encouragement.

The slogan sounds too generous. That is the charitable reading, and it is the wrong one. "In fact, it is false. It gives false hope because it gives false formation."

"It tells young people that early hardship plus early recognition equals arrival. It mistakes a beginning for an ending. It mistakes applause for character. It mistakes visibility for substance."

The falsehood is not random. It fits too well with a wider performative culture in which being seen often outruns being formed — a culture where what can be displayed quickly is rewarded more easily than what must be built slowly.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Why It Travels

It travels because it pays the family, the institution and the student at once.

The slogan satisfies a short-term imagination on three sides simultaneously. That is precisely why it is dangerous.

01
The family
Visible proof that sacrifice paid off
Families want evidence that the years of cost produced something. A degree that arrives with a heroic label supplies that evidence faster than a decade of quiet competence would.
02
The institution
Visible proof that uplift happened
Institutions want proof that mobility works and that compassion had an effect. A student who can be narrated as risen is more useful to that account than a student still being formed.
03
The student
Early signs that the crossing is over
Students want confirmation that the struggle has ended and the crossing has already been made. The slogan grants it before anything has been survived long enough to count.
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
The Conversion

Once the degree carries the family's dignity, it stops being long discipline.

Higher education then becomes more than education. "It becomes symbolic redemption."

For many families, especially where the undergraduate degree is the first in the family, the degree carries emotional weight far beyond learning. It becomes evidence of dignity restored, of sacrifice rewarded, of a family that has risen.

That hope is understandable. But once it becomes too heavy, formation begins to weaken. The degree is no longer approached mainly as long discipline. It is approached as early proof of worth.

Then title worship enters. Education is treated less as the slow work of becoming substantial and more as convertible prestige. The question quietly changes from what am I becoming to what standing does this give me — and the conditions are now perfect for premature symbolic inflation.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
The Romance

A precarious student turned into inspirational material becomes an emotional trophy.

Support for precarious students is proper. What happens next to the story is not.

What the Romance Produces
✗The student becomes proof that the institution is compassionate
✗The student becomes proof that mobility works
✗The student becomes proof that a neat story can now be told
✗Precarity is converted into inspirational material for an audience
✗The student stops being seen as someone still in need of formation
What Support Would Actually Mean
✓Seeing the student simply as a student who requires formation
✓Refusing to hand out crowns before anything has been tested
✓Recognising that hardship is not automatically beautiful
✓Declining to make the student carry the institution's account of itself
✓Treating the difficulty as difficulty, not as narrative material
The line the author draws

"Education should not turn people into emotional trophies." The romance is unfair to the student and corrupting to the institution — and it is corrupting precisely because it is well-meant.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
What Is Lost

The culture wants return before ripening, and the slogan is what supplies it.

What gets lost in the zero-to-hero script is long formation.

The culture wants return before ripening. It wants signs of arrival before substance exists. It wants closure before maturity.

— The Danger of Calling Students Heroes Too Early
Discipline weakens
Symbolic arrival comes too early, and the reason to keep working past the applause disappears with it. The work was never the point once the label had been awarded.
Calling gets blurred
Students are pushed to seek what looks rewarding and respectable rather than what they are prepared to build patiently and deeply. The two are not the same and only one of them lasts.
The moral timeline is taught wrong
A young person of twenty-two or twenty-three who has escaped hardship, entered a respected university, or collected awards may deserve respect. That person is not yet a hero, and saying otherwise is inflation, not kindness.
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
The Alternative

Anti-fragile formation is the harder ambition, and it does not worship suffering.

What the lecturer seemed to understand, and what he did not do.

He did not rely on zero to hero. He did not hand out crowns too early. He seemed to understand that the real task is anti-fragile formation — not romanticism, not theatre, not premature heroic language.

Anti-fragility does not pretend hardship is beautiful. It does not assume every wound becomes wisdom. It recognises only that some students, when formed well, become stronger through difficulty instead of flatter through praise.

What they develop instead is "resilience instead of image, judgment instead of drama, range instead of entitlement." That is a much harder educational ambition, and a much more honest one.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

Heroism is a long verdict, and the young do not need crowns too early.

The one prescription the piece makes, stated as narrowly as the author states it.

The young do not need crowns too early.

— The Danger of Calling Students Heroes Too Early
What the word actually requires
Heroism is tested over time through sacrifice, restraint, responsibility, and usefulness for others after strength and visibility have already arrived. To use it before then is not generosity.
What education should return to
If education is serious, it should stop rewarding early symbolic ascent and return to the slower labor of building adults. Zero to hero promises something faster. That is exactly why it is false.
