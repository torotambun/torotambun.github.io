What’s the Most Promising Climate Solution? A Personal Journey through Public Discussions, En-ROADS, and Theories of Multi-Solving

Every audience names the same two climate solutions, and the simulation prices each of them at one-tenth of a degree. What is promising is not a better single solution but multi-solving — an approach that treats emissions, equity and public health as one problem rather than three.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Every audience I ask names the same two solutions.

A question asked repeatedly, of very different rooms, over several years.

Situation

Speaking at public events and running coaching sessions, I have made a habit of asking one recurring question: "What is one promising climate solution you think will have the highest impact in reducing the climate change temperature?"

Complication

The audiences differ completely — engineering physics students, startup founders, NGO workers, policy advocates, a coaching session with Ahmad Al Rasyid — and the answers do not.

Question

Do the two answers that always come back reflect a grasp of what actually moves the temperature?

Answer

Two solutions dominate regardless of background: electrifying transport with nickel-battery EVs, and renewables — solar, hydro and algae — replacing fossil fuels. Both are real, and neither is what the question asked for.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Gap

The answers track the media rather than the model.

Where the shared answer comes from, and why it went unchallenged for so long — including by me.

The responses tend to mirror what is trending in media and public discourse. Without fail the same two candidates arrive, in the same order, from rooms with nothing else in common.

I used to take those answers as evidence of a solid grasp of the possibilities. Electrification and renewables are, after all, critical pieces of the puzzle. It was only on digging into the data that the mismatch became visible: the enthusiasm surrounding these solutions was disproportionate to their actual impact — at least when each is viewed in isolation.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Evidence

En-ROADS gives full transport electrification one-tenth of a degree, and every renewable subsidy another tenth.

The simulation that made the mismatch concrete.

EN-ROADS A
Electrify all transport: 0.1°C
Electrifying all transport through EVs and nickel batteries results in a temperature reduction of just one-tenth of a degree Celsius.
EN-ROADS B
Subsidise every renewable globally: 0.1°C
Ramping up all renewables and subsidising them globally yields another one-tenth of a degree decrease. The two headline answers, taken together and taken to their maximum, move two-tenths.
EN-ROADS C
Why single levers behave this way
En-ROADS, developed by Climate Interactive and MIT Sloan, is grounded in system dynamics — the field pioneered by Jay Forrester, which shows how feedback loops and interactions between variables in complex systems often produce counterintuitive outcomes (Forrester, 1971).
EN-ROADS D
The surprise was mine as well
Presenting these findings in a workshop, the data was not just surprising to the audience but to me. My own approach had been centred on technological solutions.

Framework

A solution that addresses one problem at a time cannot be the most promising one.

Three positions that together replace the search for a single lever.

01
Sawin, 2018; 2024
Multi-solving
Solutions must address multiple issues simultaneously — health, equity, sustainability — rather than optimising one. Carbon reduction alone, however critical, will not achieve broad-based progress while the interconnected issues around it go unaddressed.
02
Freeman, 1984
Stakeholder theory
Value creation extends beyond shareholders to communities, workers and the environment. Climate solutions have to incorporate the perspectives and needs of all stakeholders, particularly marginalised and vulnerable populations.
03
Jay, 2017
Breaking through gridlock
A purely technical solution will falter unless it is supported by a culturally aware, equity-driven communication approach that bridges conflicting stakeholders instead of dividing them.
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Implications

Equity is not an add-on to a climate solution; it decides who the solution reaches.

The reason multi-solving and equity are the same argument rather than two.

Climate change impacts different populations unequally, and solutions that do not address these disparities will perpetuate existing inequalities. That is not a moral footnote to the technical work; it is a statement about whether the technical work lands.

Equity is a practical framework rather than an abstract concept. Incorporating equity-driven stakeholder engagement into climate solutions produces strategies that address not only emissions but public health, economic development and social justice — which is exactly what multi-solving means in practice.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Reflection

The holier-than-thou posture in these debates is one I held myself.

The part of the diagnosis that is not about the audience.

In both my own journey and the sessions I have facilitated, discussions around climate change become divisive easily. People hold strong opinions about their chosen solutions, often with an "I'm holier than thou" attitude that creates unnecessary tension.

My own bias used to lean heavily toward technology-driven solutions. What changed was an appreciation of inclusive collaboration — bringing different stakeholders together without judgment. Jay's framework argues for moving beyond the polarisation of ideas toward dialogue that bridges differing viewpoints, and that matches what I found: climate solutions are not a zero-sum game, and no approach earns its place at another's expense.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

I stopped looking for the next technological fix and started looking at interdependencies.

What changed across the workshops, coaching sessions and speeches.

What was once a search for the next big technological fix has turned into an understanding of complex interdependencies.

— What’s the Most Promising Climate Solution?, 19 September 2024
Not instead of electrification and renewables
Both remain important. The claim is about arrangement, not exclusion: the question is how each contributes to a broader goal rather than which one wins.
Multi-solving is the unit of ambition
Approaches that incorporate equity, collaboration and systems thinking, in place of isolated technologies — climate solutions seen as interconnected systems requiring a comprehensive and equitable approach.
Where the thinking came from
The article closes by pointing readers to the work of Climate Interactive, Andrew Jones, the MIT Sloan Sustainability Initiative and Elizabeth Sawin, whose insights shaped this shift.
