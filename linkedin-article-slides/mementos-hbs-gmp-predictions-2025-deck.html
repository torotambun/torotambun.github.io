Mementos from HBS GMP Annual Meeting: Predictions for 2025

The defining story of 2025 will not be a single AI breakthrough but adaptation — how businesses, governments and individuals respond to accelerating change across the economy, AI governance, wellness, innovation, sales and finance. The same six shifts double as a map of where entrepreneurship students should be building, because the opportunity in each case is applying the technology to a real problem, not showcasing the technology itself.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus

Introduction

2025 will be defined by adaptation, not by any single technological breakthrough.

The framing the article opens on.

Situation

Companies face higher-for-longer interest rates, aging populations in the U.S., Japan and Europe, and geopolitical pressure reshaping supply chains toward Vietnam, Mexico and India.

Complication

AI will replace routine white-collar tasks in law, finance and IT operations, but industries requiring creativity and emotional intelligence will see rising demand for human oversight — and the AI ethics gap between regulated and unregulated markets will widen.

Question

If the technology is already diffusing everywhere, what actually separates the companies that benefit from it?

Answer

"The defining theme of 2025 will not be a single technological breakthrough or economic trend, but rather how businesses, governments, and individuals respond to accelerating changes in AI, work, ethics, and human well-being."

Framework

Six shifts run across the economy, AI governance, wellness, innovation, sales and finance.

Each shift names both the disruption and the limit of what AI actually replaces.

1  ·  The economy — cost pressures over expansion
Corporations focus on profitability and automation rather than aggressive growth; labor shortages in caregiving push aging economies to rebalance workforces while Indonesia, India and parts of Africa become talent hubs.
2  ·  AI and automation — governance becomes behavioral, not just technical
"AI deployment will not just be a technical issue but a psychological and societal challenge." Companies that recognize behavioral adaptation — trust, bias, dependency — will lead the transformation ahead of those focused purely on the technology.
3  ·  Wellness — human care expands alongside AI, not instead of it
AI-driven mental health support will expand, but demand for human therapists and caregivers will rise, not fall; AI-driven wellness tracking will raise new privacy concerns as it begins to monitor psychological states, not just work performance.
4  ·  Innovation — the era of "innovation theater" is over
Companies shift from showcasing AI pilots for hype toward deep tech — biotech, quantum computing, materials science — that solves real problems; the most valuable AI applications will be invisible, optimizing supply chains and compliance rather than making headlines.
5  ·  Sales and marketing — some B2B deals stay human by necessity
AI enhances lead generation and personalization, but high-stakes enterprise, defense and pharmaceutical sales still require in-person trust; "AI fatigue" among consumers will drive a resurgence of authentic, human-led branding.
6  ·  Accounting and finance — efficiency arrives faster than governance
AI-powered financial modeling becomes standard even as AI-generated financial manipulation becomes a regulatory concern; ESG reporting moves from voluntary to legally enforced; crypto faces more regulation without disappearing.

Implications

The same six shifts become five startup opportunity areas for entrepreneurship students.

For entrepreneurs, "the real opportunity in 2025 lies not just in adopting the latest technology, but in applying it strategically to solve real-world problems."

01
Build with it
AI as a productivity and decision-making engine
Low-cost AI development models will democratize access; the opportunity is not "AI for AI's sake" but AI applied to decision-making in healthcare, logistics and financial services.
02
Augment, don't replace
Human-augmenting technology and deep tech
Tools that assist rather than replace professionals — legal research, medical diagnostics, mental-health and wellness platforms — alongside a genuine next investment wave in biotech, quantum computing and clean energy.
Where AI-resistant niches still hold

"Not all industries will be disrupted by AI-some will be AI-assisted." High-trust industries — legal, consulting, B2B sales, corporate governance — still require human expertise, creating startup opportunities in AI-powered but human-led services.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

The real winners will treat AI as a tool for judgment, not a replacement for it.

The article's own closing frame.

The real winners in 2025 will be those who understand that technology is not a replacement for human judgment, trust, and adaptability—but a tool that must be integrated wisely.

— Closing conclusion of the article
What determines the outcome
"AI governance, trust, and behavioral adaptation will determine whether the technology is embraced or resisted."
For entrepreneurs specifically
"AI is a tool, not the product. The best startups will use AI to solve problems, not just build AI for AI's sake." The next unicorns are expected in biotech, clean energy and industrial automation rather than apps alone.
Web3's own test
"Web3 will be about function, not speculation. Blockchain must have a real-world use case to be valuable in 2025."
