“Berfilsafat” and Ontology: Charting the Depths of Existence and Knowledge Creation, Starting an S3 Journey

This is the article two later decks defer to, and what it holds is the axiology of the research stated in the author's own voice rather than borrowed from a framework. It opens on a Levi-Strauss line about the world completing itself without man, and reads it not as pessimism but as an argument for shared values, since what outlasts any individual is the cultural bedrock a society lays down. Berfilsafat, the Indonesian term, is defined as active and systematic contemplation rather than passive reflection, and paired with ontology as its natural partner. A correction follows that the later instalments quietly drop: epistemology and axiology are branches of philosophy in their own right while ontology is a sub-branch of metaphysics, so the familiar triad is not three of a kind. Then the axiology itself: a nation's strength lies in deep-seated shared values rather than in technology or economics, innovation is a value rather than a process, values installed early become reflexive, and the outcome sought is a generation with an innate disdain for corruption, framed by three traditions integrated rather than chosen between.

Introduction

A line about the world outlasting humanity is read as an argument for shared values.

The article opens on Levi-Strauss and then does something unexpected with him, turning a statement about human transience into a statement about what endures.

The world began without man, and it will complete itself without him.

— Claude Lévi-Strauss (1962), quoted in “Berfilsafat” and Ontology, 25 August 2023
What the quotation is taken to mean
The article reads it as "the importance of the transient nature of human existence and the lasting legacy of collective cultural values and beliefs", and borrows it deliberately: to "provide depth to my writing by emphasizing the foundational and enduring nature of cultural values, much in line with Edgar Schein's views on organizational culture."
The inference drawn from it
"Just as the world continues independently of human presence, shared values transcend individual lifetimes, influencing the course of societies across generations." Shared values are treated as the layer that outlives the people holding them.
How Schein's three layers are stacked onto it
Shared values are "so deeply embedded in the collective psyche that they often remain unarticulated but profoundly influential". Espoused values are what a society states explicitly, "like the visible tip of an iceberg". Artifacts are "the tangible expressions of shared values and espoused values", whether art, architecture, traditions or practices.
The closing move of the opening
The quotation "serves as a reminder of the impermanence of individual existence and the enduring influence of shared values in shaping societies and their cultural expressions." The article's whole programme follows from treating values, not people or artifacts, as the durable unit.

Framework

Berfilsafat is an active pursuit, not a contemplative mood, and that is why it pairs with ontology.

The Indonesian term is defined before it is used, and the definition is what distinguishes the article from a general essay on philosophy.

It is "deeply rooted in Indonesian linguistic and cultural nuances" and "embodies the profound act of contemplation about life, the universe, and the multifaceted phenomena encapsulated within."

The emphasis falls on the activity rather than the mood: "Far from being a passive reflection, it denotes an active, systematic, and critical pursuit to unveil answers to foundational philosophical questions about existence, knowledge, values, reason, mind, and language."

Ontology is its counterpart because both ask the same question from different directions. Derived from the Greek for being, it "parallels “berfilsafat” in its quest for comprehension about our existential positioning in the universe", asking after "the true existence of entities, their inherent nature and essence".

What knowledge creation is held to be

Not accumulation. "It isn't a mere aggregation of information but a meticulous synthesis of experiences, observations, and insights." And the article expects that synthesis to break things: the result gives "rise to coherent structures that often defy existing paradigms, ushering in novel viewpoints."

Evidence

The dissertation's axiology is stated here, and it is the only place it appears in full.

This is the section the later articles point back to. It sets out what the research holds to be valuable, which is a different claim from what it holds to be true.

1  ·  The foundational premise
"a nation's genuine strength isn't merely in its technological or economic advances but in its deep-seated shared values." And these are not declarations: "These aren't just words on paper; they're principles so ingrained they guide instinctual behaviors, particularly during challenging times."
2  ·  Innovation reclassified as a value
The reframing the article calls pivotal: "innovation isn't just a process or a product — it's a value." Once a society treats it that way, "it moves from being an activity to a deeply ingrained principle." And it carries a formula, "invention multiplied by commercialization", so that creativity is required to reach practical application.
3  ·  Why early formation is the mechanism
"Values integrated early in life don't just influence actions; they shape neural pathways, becoming reflexive responses." The entrepreneurial spirit that follows is explicitly not confined to business, and it is built on resilience, adaptability and what Nassim Taleb calls anti-fragility, prospering amidst disruptions.
4  ·  The outcome the research is actually aimed at
"A profound axiological outcome of this research is the molding of generations with an innate disdain for corruption." The distinction matters: such people view "corrupt practices not just as illegal but as fundamentally against their core principles."
Three traditions integrated rather than chosen between

The Islamic principle of Rahmatan lil alamin, "being a blessing for the entire universe"; Tikkun Olam from Jewish teaching, which "emphasizes repairing or perfecting the world"; and the Javanese Memayu Hayuning Bawono, which "underscores the importance of striving for the harmony and balance of the world." The article states what the combination is for: "not merely innovating for innovation's sake, but fostering a world characterized by harmony, equity, and shared prosperity."

Gap

The familiar triad is not three of a kind, and the article corrects itself in public.

Section four exists to fix a categorisation the author had been using loosely, and it is worth surfacing because the later instalments of the series do not repeat the correction.

Branches of philosophy in their own right
•Epistemology, focusing on the nature and scope of knowledge
•Axiology, dealing with the study of values
•Each stands alone as an area of study
•Each addresses its own distinct set of questions
•Neither sits underneath another branch
Not a branch but a sub-branch
✗Ontology, which the article marks emphatically as a sub-branch
✗It sits within metaphysics
✗Its focus is the nature of being and existence
✗Metaphysics is the branch; ontology is part of it
✗So the three are not parallel categories
Why the correction still leaves them connected

"one's epistemological views on what constitutes valid knowledge could influence one's ontological views on what kinds of entities exist, as well as one's axiological views on what is good or beautiful." The article's summary keeps both halves: "these are three distinct but interrelated branches of philosophy."

Details

Epistemology is the challenge the research has to survive, and six problems are named.

Before the taxonomy, the article sets out what makes knowledge creation difficult, treating epistemology as a guard rather than a subject.

1  ·  Sources, and objectivity
"Is it primarily derived from our senses, as the empiricists suggest? Or do reason and innate ideas play a predominant role, as rationalists argue?" Then the harder one: "How do we ensure the knowledge we gather is objective, free from the influence of personal biases, emotions, or societal pressures?"
2  ·  The limits of knowing
"Can human beings, with our cognitive limitations, ever fully grasp the vastness of the universe or the intricacies of consciousness?" The article accepts that some frameworks posit an inherent boundary, "urging a humble acceptance of our cognitive boundaries."
3  ·  Justification
"Simply holding a belief does not make it knowledge. For a belief to be considered knowledge, it must be justified." The open question is how to decide when justification is sufficient, which the article says reaches into evidence, reasoning and consensus.
4  ·  Culture, and change over time
"What's considered knowledge in one culture or context might be seen differently in another", and the article names the difficulty of respecting that "without resorting to relativism". Then the temporal problem: "What was once considered irrefutable might now be seen as outdated or even incorrect", which makes knowledge provisional.
The position it settles on

"Knowledge, in all its grandeur, is not a simple construct. Its creation and validation come with a myriad of challenges that demand rigorous examination and introspection." The stated purpose is to become better "not only as thinkers but also as responsible knowledge creators and consumers".

Conclusion

This is the text the later instalments defer to rather than repeat.

The article's subtitle names it as the start of the doctoral journey, and two later pieces treat it as the place where the philosophical grounding already lives.

Axiology, much like its counterparts in philosophy – epistemology and ontology – delves deep into the foundational aspects of human understanding.

— “Berfilsafat” and Ontology, 25 August 2023
The three questions, in the article's own phrasing
"if epistemology asks “how do we know?” and ontology asks “what exists?”, axiology posits the question, “what is of value?”". The same formulation reappears almost word for word in Stop 2 of the philosophy of science series, seventeen days later.
Who points back here
Stop 1 of the Stop series (8 September 2023) cites this article in its reference list. S1 to S2 to S3 (4 September 2023) names it as the place where the ontology, epistemology and axiology of the doctoral research are set out at length, and declines to repeat them.
What makes it the anchor rather than an instalment
The later pieces survey philosophy as a discipline. This one states a position: that shared values are the durable unit, that innovation is among them, and that the research exists to produce people who refuse corruption on principle rather than on law.
Its own status, as the subtitle gives it
Starting an S3 Journey. The article is dated 25 August 2023, three weeks before the methodology series and a month before the theoretical framework thread, which places it at the head of the whole sequence this archive has been working backward through.
