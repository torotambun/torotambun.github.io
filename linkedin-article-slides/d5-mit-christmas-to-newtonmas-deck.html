D-5 MIT's Shift from Christmas to Newtonmas: A Cultural/Art Expression?

Renaming Christmas to Newtonmas is a claim about knowledge, and tested against the six modes it lands in the cultural or artistic domain rather than the empirical one an institute of technology would claim — which is why it cannot dislodge a tradition held religiously.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Renaming Christmas to Newtonmas is a claim about knowledge, so it can be tested as one.

The article's own framing of the question, applied to MIT's effort to reframe the season.

Situation

The Massachusetts Institute of Technology's "recent effort to reframe “Christmas” as “Newtonmas”" is a public act by an institution whose authority rests on a particular way of knowing.

Complication

The move "may raise important questions about its rationale. Is this an attempt rooted in scientific and empirical perspectives, aligning with their academic identity? Or does it reflect a cultural, artistic, or even political position?"

Question

Which mode of knowledge acquisition is actually doing the work here?

Answer

"Each of these motivations corresponds to one of the six recognized ways of acquiring knowledge: empirical, philosophical, religious, traditional, artistic, and cultural." Naming the mode settles what kind of claim is being made — and therefore what kind of answer it deserves.

Framework

Every motive available to MIT lands in the cultural or artistic mode, not the empirical one it would claim.

The six modes applied one at a time to the available explanations. The test is not whether the renaming is permissible but which domain it belongs to.

01
Ruled out
Religious
"However, a religious basis seems highly unlikely and, if claimed, could risk being perceived as a mockery." The article closes this door first and does not reopen it.
02
If political
Cultural
"If MIT justifies this change as stemming from leftist political ideology, it falls under the cultural domain of knowledge acquisition. Political ideologies, shaped by societal values, historical narratives, and collective beliefs, are an integral part of cultural expression."
03
If meme-making
Artistic
"Alternatively, if this is part of meme-making, it aligns with the artistic domain—a valid approach to knowledge that uses creativity, humor, and provocation to stimulate thought and spark dialogue." The article grants it standing: "the artistic domain allows for reinterpretation of tradition in a way that is both provocative and reflective".
What the classification does

It does not dismiss the act. It relocates it. An institution known for empirical authority has, on this occasion, made a move that draws its force from culture or art — domains where the reply is interpretation rather than evidence.

The Concession

Free expression makes the substitution legitimate, and legitimacy is not the same as force.

The article concedes the ground most likely to be contested, and concedes it early.

"At its core, this change can also be seen as an exercise of freedom of speech, which is celebrated in the United States. Freedom of expression allows institutions like MIT to challenge traditional practices, even if such actions provoke debate or resistance."

The concession carries a condition rather than a complaint: "such expressions are not without consequences—they invite scrutiny, interpretation, and varying responses based on cultural and societal contexts."

That is the whole of the article's objection to the act itself. Nothing is demanded of MIT, no retraction is sought, and no offence is registered. What follows instead is a description of what the renaming can and cannot reach.

The Longer View

For two thousand years the message of the cross has been foolishness to those perishing; this is not new.

"From a Christian perspective, such actions are neither surprising nor new. For over 2,000 years, Christians have faced ridicule, persecution, and dismissal of their beliefs."

For the message of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God

— 1 Corinthians 1:18, NIV, quoted in the article
What the verse is doing here
"This verse reflects the enduring experience of being misunderstood or marginalized while affirming the profound meaning the gospel holds for believers." It converts the renaming from an event into an instance.
The consequence for the reader's temperature
If dismissal is the expected condition rather than a new provocation, then surprise is the wrong response — and so is the indignation that surprise produces.
The title's own answer
The article's full heading gives its conclusion before the argument: the shift "It Doesn't Shake, but Inspires to Be Immersed in His Faithfulness Even More".

The Response

The called-for answer is not anger but forgiveness offered to people who do not know what they are doing.

"Believers are therefore called to respond to such situations not with anger but with understanding and grace."

The words "Forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing" offer, in the article's phrase, "a timeless reminder of the importance of humility and compassion."

The article extends this even to the least charitable reading of MIT's motive. "Even if MIT's actions were intended to provoke or engage in religious discourse—a possibility that seems doubtful but not impossible—the response should align with the principles of forgiveness, love, and a willingness to engage in meaningful dialogue."

Two things are worth marking. The article judges provocation "doubtful but not impossible" and does not resolve the question — it prescribes the same response either way. And the response it prescribes is not tolerance but forgiveness, which presumes something was done rather than nothing.

What It Settles

Tradition is itself a mode of acquiring knowledge, and a renaming does not touch it.

"Tradition, as a way of acquiring knowledge, holds profound value." This is the argument's closing move, and it returns to the six-mode framework it opened with.

Great is Thy faithfulness, O God my Father; there is no shadow of turning with Thee.

— Lamentations 3:23, quoted in the article
Why tradition cannot be renamed away
"For over two millennia, Christmas has evolved as a deeply rooted tradition in personal, familial, and cultural practices. For Christians, it is not just a celebration but a testament to enduring faith, passed down through generations. While some may seek to reinterpret or replace it, its essence remains unchanged for those who uphold its significance."
The principle held throughout
Aristotle's words — "It is the mark of an educated mind to be able to entertain a thought without accepting it" — "serve as a guiding principle in this discussion." The article has entertained MIT's move for its full length without at any point accepting it.
Where the two modes finish
"Considering differing perspectives, even those that challenge cherished traditions, fosters deeper understanding and meaningful dialogue. Christmas, as both a tradition and a religious cornerstone, continues to hold its place among the ways humanity acquires and cherishes knowledge."
The line addressed to MIT
"Thanks MIT, it is not enough to deter us to immerse ourselves in His Faithfulness". The article closes in gratitude rather than grievance, which is the position the whole classification exercise was built to make available.
