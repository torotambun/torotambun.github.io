Indonesia Cannot Intern Its Way to Productive Employment

Indonesia should retain apprenticeships but stop asking them to perform a task they cannot perform: create vacancies that do not exist. The binding constraint sits upstream of the labour market, so a labour-supply instrument needs a labour-demand counterpart.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Measured learning is much stronger than absorption.

An apprenticeship can improve a participant. Six months cannot manufacture a vacancy.

Situation

The National Internship Programme addresses a real problem. New graduates often lack experience and employers are reluctant to hire without observing performance. The state finances the allowance at the applicable local minimum wage, work-accident and death protection, and BNSP certification; the host supplies the placement, mentor and structured programme.

Complication

The 2026 material reports completion above 95 percent and competency improvement for 66 percent — against an offer-or-recruitment outcome of 29.2 percent. These are programme-reported results, not independent estimates of net job creation, and an offer is not twelve-month employment. They reveal the central imbalance.

Question

Suppose every safeguard worked and every certificate were credible. Would the programme create jobs — and if not, which instrument would?

Answer

Two linked reforms. An Apprenticeship Additionality and Learning Contract makes internships honest. A Production-to-Jobs Compact creates the productive capacity into which capable workers can move.

Gap

The binding constraint lies upstream of the labour market.

Unemployment policy usually begins with the person: train the graduate, certify the skill, improve the résumé, match the applicant, place the participant.

These interventions may improve an individual's probability of obtaining an existing position. They do not determine how many productive positions exist. The upstream decision belongs to the entrepreneur and investor: is there a buyer, can a site be secured, will permits and electricity and logistics allow reliable output, can machinery and working capital be financed, is the margin sufficient to compensate for risk?

The causal chain is demand → site → machinery → capability → output → revenue → wages. Training enters the chain, but it does not start it. If an announced investment never reaches production, the correct diagnosis may be a missing order, unfinanceable machinery, an unreliable site, a standards barrier or weak supplier capability.

Core Gap

Calling every blockage a "skills gap" is administratively convenient, but economically wrong. Indonesia cannot train its way out of a shortage of employers, machines, orders and productive assets — and a programme designed against the wrong constraint can run at full capacity, report strong completion, and leave the employment problem exactly where it was.

Low Unemployment, Weak Ladder

Headline unemployment was 4.85 percent in August 2025. That figure should be welcomed, but not mistaken for a complete measure. In the same labour market roughly 58.7 percent of employment was informal. A person who sells a little, drives intermittently or accepts unstable hours counts as employed. The economic question is whether the job produces enough value to support rising real wages, learning, safety and a durable career.

Framework

Four things a training instrument cannot reach

Each is upstream of the labour market, and each is already named in Indonesia's own development architecture.

01
The statutory mandate
The plans already say this
Law No. 59 of 2024 identifies early deindustrialization, low productivity, informality, weak technological capability and spatial inequality. The mandate is not merely to prepare workers — it is to transform the productive structure that employs them.
02
The interdependent targets
Formal jobs cannot rise alone
RPJMN 2025–2029 targets formal employment rising from 23.96 to 46 percent, manufacturing from 18.67 to 21.90 percent of GDP, and logistics costs falling from 14.29 to 12.50 percent. Formal employment will not almost double because a ministry issues more certificates.
03
The balance sheet
A productive job is expensive before it pays a wage
Someone must acquire a site, install machinery, pass tests, train supervisors, hold inventory, finance receivables, comply with standards, connect to a buyer and survive the period before stable sales. Concentrating only on the worker's readiness ignores the balance sheet that makes the position possible.
04
The AI question
Task composition changes; the physical economy does not vanish
When prediction becomes cheaper, the complementary tasks — defining objectives, choosing among trade-offs, acting under uncertainty, taking responsibility — become relatively more important. A model may identify a likely fault; a qualified person must reach the site, isolate the cause, protect people and accept responsibility for the result.

Details

The Apprenticeship Additionality and Learning Contract

One slot, one occupation, one curriculum, one hours ledger, one auditable outcome. Five gates before a host receives a publicly funded placement.

1  ·  Prove additionality
The host discloses headcount, entry-level hiring, recent layoffs, apprentice count and occupation-level vacancies for the preceding six months, cross-checked against BPJS Ketenagakerjaan and payroll records under appropriate privacy rules. A slot is refused where it replaces a recent employee, fills a normal roster requirement, or repeats a function whose prior cohort is unaccounted for. The statutory 20 percent cap is a ceiling, not a target, and applies to the corporate group and worksite — not one legal entity that can be subdivided.
2  ·  Distinguish learning time from productive time
Government support is easiest to defend when it buys a public good — structured learning, independent assessment, access for the otherwise excluded. It is hardest to defend when it pays for saleable output received by a private firm. Government funds protected learning hours; the employer pays all hours attached to production targets or regular operations. A firm willing to pay for productive time is more credible than one interested only when the state pays the entire allowance.
3  ·  Make hours and tasks visible to the participant
The participant receives the schedule, curriculum, mentor, allowance, host contribution, social-protection status and complaint channels before starting. Each week both participant and mentor confirm actual hours and tasks. A company cannot claim a full placement while using the participant in fragments, nor demand full employee performance while calling the time training.
4  ·  Make repeat access conditional on outcomes
No host receives an automatic new cohort. Future allocations depend on a published scorecard: curriculum delivery, verified mentor time, safety, complaints, competency gain, employer contribution, employment at three and six months, retention, wages, and whether ordinary hiring fell. Conversion at the same host matters, but a good apprenticeship may also lead to employment elsewhere.
5  ·  Protect voice and publish firm-level performance
Participants need a confidential channel independent of the host, anti-retaliation rules, rapid interim protection and access to worker representatives. Zero complaints can mean either excellent practice or inaccessible reporting. Large hosts should not remain anonymous in the published results.

Evidence

What is documented, and what is only predicted

Policy credibility requires distinguishing what is documented, what economic evidence predicts, and what the programme still needs to measure.

EVIDENCE A
The programme's own reported results
A target of 150,000 participants for the second cohort; completion above 95 percent; competency improvement for 66 percent; an offer-or-recruitment outcome of 29.2 percent, with manufacturing among the larger recruiting sectors at 2,022 outcomes. These are programme-reported figures, not independent estimates of net job creation.
EVIDENCE B
The Ministry's own legal evaluation
A 2026 analysis by Kemnaker's Legal Bureau describes apprentices performing core production functions, working as if they were regular employees while receiving only an allowance, unclear contracts, weak inspection, ineffective sanctions, quota non-compliance and complaint barriers including fear of retaliation.
EVIDENCE C
The safeguards that already exist
Minister of Manpower Regulation No. 6 of 2020 requires a training programme, facilities, a qualified mentor and a written agreement, and limits apprentices to 20 percent of a company's workers — a quota intended in part to prevent apprentices replacing permanent workers.
EVIDENCE D
The instruments have precedent
Singapore's Enterprise Financing Scheme shares risk through participating financial institutions while the borrower remains liable. The UK's Growth Guarantee Scheme provides a partial guarantee to accredited lenders. UK Export Finance's Supply Chain Discount Guarantee anchors liquidity in approved invoices. South Africa's Black Industrialists Scheme shows capped cost-sharing for productive capacity.
The Evidentiary Boundary

The legal analysis shows the abuse mechanism is real within Indonesia's wider apprenticeship system. It does not establish that every host in the current graduate programme behaves this way, nor quantify prevalence in the 2026 cohorts. Claims that firms systematically fragment hours, rotate every six months, or report participants who are not genuinely present should be treated as audit hypotheses until firm-level data are published. That caveat does not justify complacency — it tells government what to measure before expanding.

Implications

Measure capacity built, not activity delivered.

Better governance still cannot create a missing employer. Even a perfectly run programme remains a labour-supply intervention.

Activity Delivered
✗Completion rates and certificates issued
✗Placements filled and cohorts convened
✗Programme-reported offers at the moment of exit
✗Announced investment that never reaches production
Capacity Built
✓Employment at three, six and twelve months
✓Whether the host's ordinary hiring fell
✓Cost per sustained outcome, published and compared
✓Production lines operating and paying wages from sales
The Largest Risk

It is not a fraudulent time sheet. It is that a visible national programme creates the impression that unemployment is being solved while scarce fiscal and administrative attention moves away from investment, machinery, supplier development, industrial sites, inspection and job creation. Temporary placements can reduce immediate distress but leave the productive structure unchanged.

Transition Must Not Become Funded Waiting

A transition pathway should not mean another allowance while the graduate searches. It should mean one of two things: the participant moves into a verified existing vacancy at normal wages paid by the employer, or the participant is recruited into a new production project whose commercial foundations are already in place. The second is the missing policy instrument.

Conclusion

Make internships honest. Then build the capacity workers can move into.

The compact begins with a buyer, not a trainee. Its discipline is simple: order → machine → output → revenue → wages.

1
Contract every publicly funded slot
Every host signs an Apprenticeship Additionality and Learning Contract before receiving a slot: additionality proven, learning hours separated from productive hours, the ledger visible to the participant, repeat access earned on a published scorecard, and voice protected independently of the host.
2
Make the host pay for productive value
Government continues to finance the protected allowance and certification, but the employer co-finances productive hours from the beginning and bears a rising share as competence increases. A participant who creates commercial value should not be free labour.
3
Start the compact from demand, not from a trainee
Anchor manufacturers, major domestic buyers, state-owned enterprises or public procurement bodies identify what they are prepared to purchase. An entrepreneur proposes how to meet it, contributes equity and accepts commercial risk. A bank or leasing company independently appraises and finances the machinery.
4
Confine the state to the specific failure
Only after those commitments exist does government act — on supplier qualification, testing, permits, utilities, site readiness, a capped partial guarantee, or named-worker certification paid to the accredited provider, never to the employer as wage reimbursement. No credible demand, no project. No entrepreneur equity, no project.
5
Cap the exposure and pilot before publishing a formula
Public exposure should be capped per production line and per corporate group, covering only a limited share of verified asset risk, with claims requiring equity, independent appraisal, proof of installation, a covered default and completed recovery. No guarantee percentage, rupiah cap, fee or loss-sharing formula should be announced before actuarial costing, legal review and a controlled pilot.
6
Choose first value chains by score, not by fashion
Food processing and cold chain, textiles and footwear, furniture and wood products, electronics assembly, machinery repair and industrial services, construction materials and building services — selected on net formal jobs, time to operation, domestic procurement, site and anchor readiness, additionality and inclusion. Capital expenditure alone is not success.

A participant who is better prepared but still lacks a vacancy is a programme output, not proof of additional employment.
