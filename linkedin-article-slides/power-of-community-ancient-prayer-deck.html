The Power of Community and Mindfulness Through Ancient Prayer

A cake carried into a PhD classroom by six classmates arrives in the same week as automated birthday greetings from a dozen loyalty programmes and the end of a thirty-year airline relationship. The contrast is the argument: affiliation is transacted and community is chosen. What the author does with the difference is borrow an ancient prayer, say it once for people in war zones and once for himself, and ask specifically for the strength to forgive.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus

Opening

A birthday cake arrived in the middle of a PhD class, from six people who chose to notice.

A 54th birthday spent in a full day of doctoral classes with Batch 9, 2013, School of Business and Management ITB.

"Not so long ago, I celebrated my 54th birthday during a full day of PhD classes with six dear friends of Doctoral of Science and Management Batch 9 2013 in School of Business and Management ITB" — Rio Adrianto, Thomas Airlangga, Endyas Pratitajati, Arisona Lestari and Rosa Permata Sari, with Prof. Dermawan Wibisono. "The leader of the class Catherine Patinah took initiative on behalf of the class surprised me with a birthday cake, making me feel special and appreciated."

The year behind it is named without elaboration, and the deck does not elaborate either: "Their kindness contrasted with the challenges I faced over the past year, including betrayal, bullying, misunderstandings, and discrimination. Despite these hardships, their care provided a relieve and comfort."

That sentence is the hinge of the piece. Everything that follows is an accounting of which relationships showed up.

The Contrast

The loyalty programmes sent greetings the day after Singapore Airlines ended thirty years.

Two kinds of connection arrive in the same week, and the article sets them against each other without commentary.

Affiliation, transacted
✗"robotic messages from various loyalty programs" — Qatar Airways Privilege Club, Garuda's Fly Garuda, the Harvard Alumni Association, Harvard & MIT Coop, Cartier, Pakubuwono, Singtel, Panin Dai-Ichi Life and the MIT Club of Indonesia
✗"These messages, either personally crafted or automated, reminded me of the connections and affiliations I have formed over the years"
✗"Strangely, these bountiful greetings arrived after an anticlimactic end to my long relationship with Singapore Airlines a day earlier"
✗"I had flown with them since 1994 and had been a PPS member for the last 10 years. Now, it is over"
Community, chosen
✓Six classmates and a professor, in a classroom, on a working day
✓A class leader who acted "on behalf of the class" rather than on her own
✓"making me feel special and appreciated"
✓"The acts of kindness and heartfelt messages from my friends reminded me of the resilience and strength that community can provide"
What the ending cost

"I am left with a sense of bitterness over how it ended. The loyalty and dedication I showed seemed overshadowed by an abrupt and cold termination of our association, adding to my feelings of disillusionment." The bitterness is recorded as his and is not resolved in this piece.

The Turn

I have been waking at two and three in the morning, and it is not jet lag.

The article gives its reason for reaching for a prayer, and the reason is physical before it is theological.

"For the past two weeks, I've been waking up at 2 or 3 AM every day, initially thinking it was due to jet lag." A classmate offered the ordinary explanation — "Dr. Thomas Airlangga joked that it's a sign of aging, and I couldn't help but agree."

Then the sentence that rejects both: "However, I realized that this is no ordinary midnight awakening. There is a magnetic pull drawing me to a higher ground, a transcendental experience."

The piece is explicit that this is the first instalment of something larger, and points forward to a series on why the brain returns to ritual. It ends there, on the question rather than an answer.

The Practice

I said it once for the war zones before I said it for myself.

The prayer is adapted twice, and the order of the two adaptations is the piece's quietest decision.

The first adaptation is for other people, and it opens the article rather than closing it: "In thinking about the broader struggles faced by many, I extend this reflection to those in war zones. Amidst the chaos and uncertainty, may they find moments of peace and clarity through their own reflections and prayers."

Each petition is rewritten for that condition. On daily bread: "Provide for our needs in these trying times. Grant us safety, food, shelter, and the strength to endure each day." On protection: "Protect us from the dangers surrounding us and give us the wisdom and courage to make decisions that preserve life and honor you." And on forgiveness, the hardest of them: "In the face of violence and betrayal, help us to hold onto forgiveness and not be consumed by hatred."

The closing petition asks for something the situation does not supply: "Help us to remember that you are sovereign and that your love is everlasting, even when we can't see a horizon." Only after all of this does the article turn to his own year.

Who it is offered to

"Let us all strive for peace, understanding, and mindfulness in our daily lives and extend our support to those around us, regardless of faith or background." The prayer is Christian and the invitation is explicitly not.

What I Carry

The second time I asked for the strength to forgive the people who wished me harm.

The personal adaptation is more specific than the first, and the specificity is what makes it worth reading.

I ask for your help in dealing with those who have betrayed me, looked down on me, and wished for my failure and suffering. Give me the strength to forgive them and the wisdom to navigate these relationships.

— The Power of Community and Mindfulness Through Ancient Prayer, 14 May 2024
What he thanks for first
"Thank you for the blessings of health, relationships, and financial stability that sustain me." Gratitude is placed inside the same prayer as the grievance, not opposite it.
What he asks protection from
"Protect me from their negativity and help me to stay focused on your path for me. Fill my heart with peace and my mind with clarity so that I can rise above any hurt or anger." The request is to be unaffected rather than vindicated.
What the borrowing is for
"This ancient prayer, adapted to my personal journey, serves as a reminder of the power of mindfulness and the strength that comes from a supportive community." The two halves of the title, joined in one sentence.

Sources

The article carries no reference list. The ancient prayer is the Lord's Prayer, adapted by the author in two versions; no translation or source is named and none is supplied here. Its material is the author's own record of a 54th birthday spent in class, of birthday messages received, and of the end of a membership. Six classmates, a professor and the class leader are named in the article, in gratitude, and are named here. The airline is named in the article and is named here; the people described as having betrayed, bullied or discriminated against him are not identified in the article and are not identified here. The article gives the start of the airline relationship as 1994 and the membership as ten years; a later piece by the same author, of 16 July 2024, gives the start as 1995. Both figures are on the record with their dates, and neither is treated as an error here. The photograph of the class is an image and does not survive text extraction.
