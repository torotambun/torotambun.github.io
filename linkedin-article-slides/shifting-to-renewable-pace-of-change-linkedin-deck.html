LinkedIn Article  ·  Energy Transition · The Pace of Change  ·  1 July 2019

Shifting to Renewable : Do the Right Thing Right in The First Time. Trust the Process, or else

The article, written in reply to a professor who wants hydrocarbon stopped at once, argues that the shift to a renewable-dominant energy mix is a large-scale change that will take the slow-speed approach, because an abrupt stop is a utopia while no alternative fills the void. It calls for a soft-hard change, top-down with power and enforcement and then the soft substance of change management, allows a pilot city that stops private cars as a bottom-up concession, and says coal stays the main component in 2040 unless renewables become as cheap as coal.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

A professor argues that the country should stop hydrocarbon now, and the author answers that the change is too large for that.

Situation

A professor invited the author to discuss the author's article. He said, "the country shall not wait for 40 years, instead, need to push to the limit: stop hydrocarbon. This will speed up the development of renewable to its maximum without further delay".

Complication

He argues that the author's approach of "slow technology adoption or infiltration will cause people to resist the change and ends up in status quo or small improvements with lots of rationalization."

Question

Should the country stop hydrocarbon at once, or is the change too large for that?

Answer

The article says the change is too large. The shift from a predominantly hydrocarbon energy mix to a predominantly renewable one is a large-scale change, and an abrupt stop is a utopia.

Evidence note

The professor is not named, and the article under discussion is not named in the text. His view appears only as the article reports it.

Framework

The ADB framework ties the scale of change to the resources committed.

The article says that on studying the question it found a framework developed by the Asian Development Bank (ADB), and it refers to the ADB diagram for the scale of change.

01
Input
Resources
The amount of resources dedicated to any change effort determines the scale of the change. The article lists human investment, capital investment and time.
02
Process
Tedious and lengthy
Implementing the precise strategic intent of a mixed energy change involves a tedious and lengthy process and a lot of stake-holders.
03
Result
Large scale
The scale of change from a predominantly hydrocarbon energy mix to a predominantly renewable one is not small. The article calls it a large-scale change indeed.

Details

The country's ability to handle change, the complexities and the years before readiness point to a slow-speed change.

The article takes three considerations into account and concludes that the transition will take the slow-speed change approach.

1  ·  The ability of the country to handle change
The first consideration is the ability of the country to handle change.
2  ·  The complexities above
The second is the set of complexities from the framework: the scale, the tedious and lengthy process and the many stake-holders.
3  ·  The years before readiness
The third is the fact, in the article's words, that it took 30-40 years before actually being ready to implement a renewable-dominant energy mix.

Details

An abrupt stop is a utopia while no alternative fills the void, so the change is soft-hard.

Asked whether to choose a hard sequence effect or an abrupt change, the article answers that there is no chance.

Abrupt change (the professor)
✗Stop hydrocarbon usage at once
✗Rhetorical, if the alternative will not be available for now
✗No alternative energy is yet available to fill the void that hydrocarbon leaves empty in the mix
✗The article calls it rather a utopia
Soft-hard change (the article)
✓A soft-hard change process is what the article says is required
✓A top-down approach first, from the President and all central government bodies down to province, regency, district and village, with power and enforcement
✓Then the soft substance: broad strategic intent and an organisation-wide approach of change management (Nohria & Khurana, 1993)
✓The professor's challenge to execute a quick, radical change becomes obsolete, the article says, after scrutinising the change framework
Evidence note

The sentence that joins the top-down approach to the soft substance is broken across a page break in the source. The deck reads it as the top-down approach first and the soft substance after it.

Details

To entertain the professor, the article allows a pilot city that stops private cars, once a compelling event arrives.

The concession combines a bottom-up approach with a small-scale change approach, on the assumption that a change can only be bought in when there is a bold compelling event. The example is stopping the demand for hydrocarbon-fuelled private transport, starting in a pilot city with very vivid top-down commitment and leadership, provided the ADB recommendations are met.

1  ·  Public transport first
The stakeholders speed up making public transportation available vastly, very timely and safely, for the haves and the have-nots.
2  ·  A free-car zone
The municipality applies a strictly adhered city perimeter limit where no more private cars are allowed to enter.
3  ·  Public transport and bicycles only
All people use only public transportation and/or bicycles and tricycles within the city perimeters.
Expected result

Demand for hydrocarbon-based fuel falls, and the fuel provider is prompted to move to other environmentally friendly energy for transport. The article calls the electrical energy form another big question.

Evidence note

The article says a proper dynamic modeling simulation must be conducted to confirm further. It presents none.

Conclusion

Power generation cannot change bottom up, and coal stays the main component in 2040 unless renewables become as cheap as coal.

The article does not suggest relying simply on renewable sources for electrical power generation, because that cannot be processed bottom up.

Coal will still be the main component in the energy mix in 2040, therefore, as long as the coal price is the lowest among other energy in the mix, there is no commercial reason to neglect it and to jump into more expensive form of renewable energy.

— Shifting to Renewable, 1 July 2019
The only chance to speed up
Disruptive conditions, namely the complex and expensive renewable energy becoming simpler and as cheap as coal. The power company would then without doubt not choose coal.
The closing verdict
The article closes the argument by calling that condition very utopia.
What supports it
A personal argument in reply to a professor, with one ADB report and one Harvard Business School background note. The article gives no figures for the coal price or the 2040 energy mix.
