Managerial Economics - Refreshment

The article is a refresher on managerial economics, written as the Douglas textbook that the author first used in his MBA year in 2000 was being introduced again at the University of Brawijaya in July 2023. It lists ten reasons startup founders should learn the subject and seven limits on applying it, sets out six class questions with a long exchange with ChatGPT as the answers, and adds the author's own argument that diminishing marginal returns has limits and that non-linear dynamics and system dynamics theory must be considered. It closes on an eight-entry list of books the author has read and on relief that the reading was not in vain.

Introduction

The textbook studied in the 2000 MBA year is being introduced again in July 2023.

The article opens with a personal marker: "I firstly took a Managerial Economics class during my MBA year in 2000", using a textbook by Evan J. Douglas (1993). In July 2023, it says, the same book is being introduced at the University of Brawijaya by a professor whom the article calls esteemed and renowned and does not name.

It gives Douglas's definition of managerial economics as the application of business principles and methodologies to decision making within the firm under uncertainty, with economic aims that relate to costs, revenue and profits in both business and non-business institutions.

It then frames the subject as one that businesses and organisations commonly use to make informed decisions, and turns to what it offers startup founders.

Details

Managerial economics serves founders across ten decision areas and stays valuable despite seven limits.

The article lists ten reasons founders benefit from learning the subject, then seven reasons it is hard to apply, and ends the pair on the view that it remains a valuable tool for decision-making.

1  ·  Resource allocation
Tools to analyse costs, benefits and risks when resources are limited.
2  ·  Pricing strategies
Pricing dynamics, demand elasticity and competitive factors.
3  ·  Market analysis
Reading the market, customer preferences and competitive forces from trends and consumer behaviour.
4  ·  Business planning
Forecasting demand, estimating costs and assessing profitability.
5  ·  Strategic decision-making
Analytical tools to evaluate options against the startup's goals.
6  ·  Risk assessment
Risk analysis and contingency planning.
7  ·  Understanding competition
Market structures, competitive advantage and market share dynamics.
8  ·  Funding and investment decisions
Evaluating returns and risks, and strengthening the credibility of business plans with investors.
9  ·  Scalability and growth
Forecasting and scenario analysis for expansion.
10  ·  Negotiation skills
Bargaining power, cost structures and value propositions in dealings with suppliers, partners and investors.
Seven limits the article lists

Complexity of real-world scenarios, assumptions and simplifications, behavioural factors, data availability and accuracy, changing economic conditions, external factors such as policy and geopolitics, and ethical considerations.

Implications

The article argues that diminishing marginal returns has limits and that non-linear and system dynamics must be considered.

Under the heading My Further Observations, the article first explains total and marginal product curves, then says "I argue that there are limitations of the concept of diminishing marginal returns and the importance of considering non-linear dynamics and broader contexts, such as system dynamics theory".

1  ·  Non-linear dynamics
The relationship between inputs and outputs is often non-linear. Technology, innovation, better management practices and changing external conditions can produce non-linear patterns of productivity growth.
2  ·  System dynamics theory
A framework built on feedback loops, delays and interactions. Investing in human capital during a downturn may have delayed but substantial positive effects, such as a faster recovery.
3  ·  The cyclical nature of economic activity
Traditional models may not capture the cycles and the non-linear responses within them. Strategic investment during downturns can support a more rapid and robust recovery.
4  ·  Adaptability and innovation
Businesses and economies adapt and innovate, which shifts productivity patterns away from simple linear relationships.
5  ·  Long-term against short-term considerations
Traditional models may stress short-term cost-cutting in downturns, while a longer view of investment in human capital can give more sustainable and resilient outcomes.
Evidence note

The article gives no data or case for these five points. After them it prints a heading, Repenning and Sterman (2001), over an eight-point list on diminishing labor costs and continuous production growth. The extracted text does not say how the paper bears on that list, and the deck does not state what the paper concludes.

Eight entries: the two volumes of Klein and Bauman's cartoon introduction to economics, Krugman et al. on macroeconomics, Lipschitz and Schadler, Moss, two Ormerod titles listed with Indonesian titles, and an unpublished in-class distribution by Rigobon from an MIT Sloan executive course.
