TARIF and Good Corporate Governance: From 30,000 Feet to the Microscopic Level

TARIF says what desirable governance should exhibit but cannot explain why a governance failure occurs. Explanation comes from theory at three altitudes, and a doctoral question moves from principle to theoretical problem, mechanism, construct and evidence.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

TARIF states what good governance should exhibit, but it cannot explain why a governance failure occurs.

"TARIF can identify areas requiring examination. It does not by itself explain why the situation arose, what mechanism is operating, or why the same governance arrangement may work in one organization but fail in another."

Situation

"Consider a fictional Indonesian listed company, PT Nusantara Prima Tbk." The founding family owns 62 percent, the founder's son is CEO, a building is bought from a company owned by the founder's brother, and one independent commissioner previously advised the founder for fifteen years.

Complication

Minority shareholders learn of the transaction only after board approval. "These are useful governance questions. They are not yet theoretical questions."

Question

Which layer of analysis moves a governance question from principle to explanation?

Answer

"TARIF describes what desirable governance should exhibit." "But those principles do not themselves explain behaviour." Theory provides the explanation.

Framework

At 30,000 feet, systems theory and bounded rationality show that governance failures can come from information design and not only from misconduct.

The first altitude asks what broad organizational or decision problem exists.

01
General Systems Theory, von Bertalanffy, 1968
Governance is one system
"An organization is an open system." A remuneration structure that rewards revenue growth can encourage risk taking, then compliance failures, then reputational and financing damage. "General Systems Theory helps explain why GRCS should not be treated as four completely separate administrative functions."
02
Bounded rationality, Simon, 1955
Boards decide with limited information
"Some governance failures arise from weak information architecture, limited cognition, poor decision design, or organizational complexity." It prevents "a simplistic conclusion that every governance failure results from unethical behaviour."

Framework

At 10,000 feet, six theories explain the governance relationship, and each puts a different question to the same board.

The second altitude asks which theory explains the governance relationship.

1  ·  Agency Theory (Ross, 1973; Jensen & Meckling, 1976)
"The central problem is delegation under potentially conflicting interests and unequal information." It explains Transparency, Accountability and Independence, and its limit is an excessive emphasis on managerial opportunism.
2  ·  Stakeholder Theory (Freeman, 1984)
It raises the question "Whose legitimate interests should enter organizational decision making?" and gives depth to Responsibility and Fairness.
3  ·  Resource Dependence Theory (Pfeffer & Salancik, 1978)
With Hillman and Dalziel (2003), it separates a board's monitoring from its resource provision. "A highly independent board may lack the knowledge needed to govern effectively."
4  ·  Stewardship Theory (Davis et al., 1997)
Managers can behave as stewards, so "More control does not automatically produce better governance." Monitoring has costs.
5  ·  Institutional Theory (Meyer & Rowan, 1977; DiMaggio & Powell, 1983)
"Does the governance mechanism alter organizational behaviour, or does it primarily demonstrate conformity with institutional expectations?"
6  ·  Principal-principal conflict (Young et al., 2008)
Where ownership is concentrated, "It may become: controlling principal versus minority principal." This bears directly on Independence and Fairness.

Details

At the microscopic level, each TARIF principle becomes an observable mechanism with its own evidence.

The third altitude asks what exact mechanism, construct, relationship, transaction or evidence should be examined.

1  ·  Transparency: disclosure and information asymmetry
"Transparency should not be equated with publishing more information." Timing, materiality, completeness, beneficial ownership and related-party disclosure can be examined (Verrecchia, 2001).
2  ·  Accountability: authority, monitoring and answerability
"Accountability is therefore an evidence trail linking authority, decision, monitoring, and answerability."
3  ·  Responsibility: obligations beyond internal authority
Accountability asks "Who must answer for the decision?" Responsibility asks "What obligations should govern the decision?" (Freeman, 1984).
4  ·  Independence: formal versus substantive
"Independent according to which rule, and independent from whom?" Tenure, professional ties, conflicts of interest and actual board behaviour can be examined.
5  ·  Fairness: organizational justice
Colquitt (2001) validated four dimensions: distributive, procedural, interpersonal and informational justice. Unequal outcomes are not automatically evidence of unfair governance.
6  ·  Tunnelling: a principal-principal mechanism
Johnson, La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes and Shleifer (2000) used the term to describe "transfers of assets or profits from firms for the benefit of controlling shareholders."

Evidence

The same fictional case yields different questions at each altitude, while the facts stay unchanged.

"The facts have not changed. The analytical altitude has."

01
At 30,000 feet
System and information
How ownership, management, board oversight, regulation and incentives interconnect, and what information the board possessed, whether directors could understand it, and whether the process allowed serious evaluation of the transaction.
02
At 10,000 feet
Relationship and legitimacy
Agency, stakeholder, resource dependence, institutional and principal-principal theories ask about delegation, affected interests, board dependence, formal versus substantive oversight, and whether the controlling family can benefit at the expense of minorities.
03
At the microscopic level
Mechanism and evidence
When the information became available internally and externally, who challenged the transaction, and "whether economic value moved toward the controlling shareholder".

Implications

A governance mechanism existing does not establish that it works, so the research question must name the mechanism.

The prescribed reading operationalizes governance through specific mechanisms, and the note uses it to show the doctoral move.

"This is different from treating GCG as one abstract variable." The study examines board-related mechanisms, independent commissioners, audit committees and managerial ownership. "The existence of a governance mechanism does not logically establish its effectiveness."

A proposed dissertation question shows the problem: Does TARIF implementation improve firm performance? "The question can be tested empirically. Its theoretical mechanism remains unclear."

The expected movement is "TARIF principle → theoretical problem → explanatory mechanism → construct → evidence." The note's rule follows: "The theory should come before the regression equation."

Conclusion

Eight closing questions turn the word Transparency from a one-word answer into an inquiry.

"It should produce questions."

1  ·  Transparent about what?
"The object of disclosure must be specified."
2  ·  Transparent to whom?
"Different stakeholders possess different information needs and decision rights."
3  ·  When was the information disclosed?
"Disclosure after the relevant decision may satisfy a reporting requirement without solving the original information problem."
4  ·  Which party possesses information that another party does not?
"This identifies the information asymmetry."
5  ·  Why might that party disclose or withhold information?
"This introduces incentives and behaviour."
6  ·  Which theory explains that behaviour?
"The theoretical lens should now become explicit."
7  ·  Through what mechanism should disclosure change behaviour or outcomes?
"The causal process must be specified."
8  ·  What evidence would show that the mechanism actually operated?
"The proposition must finally meet empirical evidence."
Scope

"The same discipline applies to Accountability, Responsibility, Independence, and Fairness."
