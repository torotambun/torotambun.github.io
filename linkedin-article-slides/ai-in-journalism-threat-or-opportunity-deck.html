Artificial Intelligence in Journalism: A Threat Or An Opportunity?

The article answers a reporter's question on whether AI threatens journalists by drawing on the author's own record as a heavy AI user: earlier encounters with AI, a decision in 2023 to stop doubting and adopt the tools, and an inventory of seven tools started the previous semester. In three written answers it says AI works more as an assistant than as a replacement, lists five families of AI tools that already help journalists, and names five human strengths that keep journalists indispensable. It closes on the takeaway that AI assists with data and language tasks while the core strengths lie with human journalists, and points to a journalistic piece that the extracted text does not carry.

Introduction

The reporter's question prompted the author to answer from his own record as a heavy AI user.

Situation

After the author presented to the expert staff of Fraksi Partai Kebangkitan Bangsa in Dewan Perwakilan Rakyat Republic Indonesia, a reporter from an online newsmaker "with an audience of around 120 million people" asked to interview him.

Complication

The reporter's interest was in whether AI poses a threat to journalists and to journalism in general. Preparing written answers, the author says memories of "my exponentially heavy engagement as an AI user within a semester or so" came flooding back.

Question

Does AI threaten journalists and journalism, and from what experience does the author answer?

Answer

The article answers from a personal record: earlier encounters with AI, an inventory of seven tools started the previous semester, and written answers to the reporter's questions on replacement, complementarity and coexistence.

Evidence note

The interview itself is not in the extracted text. The article prints the author's written answers.

Details

AI was not new to the author, and three 2023 encounters led him to stop doubting and adopt the tools.

Evidence

Asked whether the concern is justified, the author answers that AI works more as an assistant than as a replacement.

The reporter's first question asks whether concern about AI replacing human roles is justified and how quickly the shift might happen. The answer has two parts.

What the answer says AI has done
•Places AI's origin "dating back to 1950 when computer scientist John McCarthy coined the term", and reports significant advancements since the Dartmouth Conference of 1956
•Says AI has found applications in various industries, optimising processes and changing tasks
•Names natural language processing in virtual assistants like Siri, computer vision for facial recognition and medical diagnostics, and recommender systems at companies like Netflix
What the answer concludes
•"I see AI functioning more as an assistant rather than fully replacing humans"
•Says human labour remains essential in developing, overseeing and using the technology
Evidence note

The answer does not address the second half of the question, how quickly the shift might happen. The date for the coining of the term is reported as the article prints it.

Framework

Five families of AI tools already help journalists with data, fact-checking, sentiment, drafting and translation.

The second answer begins "AI and journalists can complement each other" and gives five examples, each with named tools and the journalists who would use them.

1  ·  Data analysis
Tableau, IBM Watson Analytics and Google Cloud's BigQuery process large datasets so that journalists can uncover trends and patterns. Users: data journalists, investigative reporters and news organisations seeking data-driven insights.
2  ·  Automated fact-checking
Factmata and Full Fact verify information and identify misinformation. Users: fact-checkers, news organisations and journalists ensuring accuracy and credibility.
3  ·  Sentiment analysis
IBM Watson and Lexalytics analyse online content to gauge public opinion on social issues. Users: journalists covering elections, public opinion or social issues.
4  ·  Content creation
OpenAI's GPT-3 helps journalists draft news summaries or reports. Users: journalists looking for efficient writing or inspiration.
5  ·  Language translation
Google Translate and DeepL translate interviews, documents and news sources. Users: journalists reporting on international events or multilingual regions.
Evidence note

The article names the tools and their uses and gives no evidence of how well they perform in newsrooms.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Implications

Five human strengths keep journalists indispensable: context, critical thinking, empathy, creativity and ethical decision-making.

The third answer says journalists and AI "can coexist symbiotically", and that journalists remain indispensable because of strengths that the article attributes to them and not to AI.

1  ·  Contextual understanding
Journalists grasp the historical, cultural and societal context of a story, which helps them present rounded narratives.
2  ·  Critical thinking and analysis
Journalists evaluate information, identify biases and interpret complex data accurately, which protects the integrity of reporting.
3  ·  Human emotions and empathy
The article says AI lacks emotional intelligence, while journalists connect with sources, empathise with subjects and tell stories with compassion.
4  ·  Creativity and originality
Journalists find unique angles and storytelling approaches that engage audiences.
5  ·  Ethical decision-making
Handling sensitive information and protecting sources to ethical standards remains the responsibility of human journalists.
Evidence note

The five strengths are asserted. The article reports no comparison of journalists with AI tools on any of them.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

The takeaway is that AI assists with tasks while the core strengths lie with human journalists.

The last section states the takeaway and then points to a piece that the extracted text does not carry.

Takeaways: while AI assists journalists with data analysis and language tasks, the core strengths of critical thinking, empathy, creativity, and ethical decision-making uniquely lie within human journalists.

— Artificial Intelligence in Journalism, 27 July 2023
The rest of the takeaway
The article adds that AI processes data, and that journalists' abilities ensure trustworthy news coverage that contributes to a well-informed and empathetic society amid evolving media and technology.
