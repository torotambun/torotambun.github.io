Stop 4: Core Concepts in Science

The fourth instalment declares a deliberate omission in its first line and then earns it. The foundations of science are skipped because the subject here is science in the context of doing research, which is a different thing from science as a body of knowledge. Empirical research is defined by observation and measurement, and the article's useful move is to name seven kinds of research that are not empirical and are still legitimate, so that the empirical framework stops being the only door. The scientific method is set out in ten steps, with the literature review positioned as where a gap is found and seven distinct kinds of gap available. Then the method is opened up: six philosophical doctrines underpin it, which means it is loaded rather than neutral. Models must declare their assumptions, realism and objectivity survive as ideals under sustained challenge, and peer review is named as the only corrective mechanism the system actually operates.

Introduction

The instalment skips the foundations on purpose, and says so in its first line.

The opening sentence is a scoping decision made in public: "I skip the foundation of science and jump into the science in the context of doing research." Having spent Stop 3 on what science can and cannot claim, this stop is about what a researcher does on a Tuesday.

Empirical research is defined by what it rests on rather than by what it produces. It is "based on observed and measured phenomena. It derives knowledge from actual experience rather than from theory or belief."

Its scope is set wide: spanning natural sciences, social sciences and parts of the humanities, taking quantitative or qualitative data, running experimental or non-experimental designs, and conducted in laboratories, natural environments, or through surveys and interviews.

And its depth is a nine-step sequence, from formulating research questions through literature review, design, sampling, collection, analysis, interpretation and presentation, ending at "Evaluation and Replication", where "other researchers may attempt to replicate the study to verify the findings."

The same activity under six other names

The article lists the aliases empirical research travels under and what each emphasises: experimental research for controlled settings, observational research where no variable is manipulated, field research in natural settings, primary research as against analysis of existing data, empirical studies for individual instances, and data-driven research.

Gap

Seven kinds of research are not empirical and are still research, which keeps the door open.

This is the section that stops the instalment from being a manual for one method, and it matters for a dissertation that mixes argument with data.

1  ·  Theoretical and conceptual
Theoretical research "Involves the development and testing of theories or frameworks, often without new empirical data." Conceptual research is "focused on abstract ideas or theory, often using existing literature".
2  ·  Mathematical and philosophical
Mathematical research uses "mathematical theorems and algorithms to solve problems". Philosophical research "Encompasses a range of non-empirical methodologies, including conceptual analysis, thought experiments, and logical argumentation".
3  ·  Narrative, legal and critical
Narrative research, common in history and literature, studies texts, documents or artifacts to construct or understand a narrative. Legal research studies laws, policies and constitutions through legal texts and rulings. Critical research involves "the critique or deconstruction of texts, ideologies, or societal structures".
4  ·  Where the two meet
Methodological pluralism, since "a study in the philosophy of mind might include both philosophical argumentation and empirical data from neuroscience". Interdisciplinary research, with climate science named as a field needing empirical data, modelling, and ethical and policy work at once. And complementarity: the two kinds have different strengths on validity, replicability and generalisability.

Framework

Seven kinds of gap exist, and the literature review is where one gets chosen.

The scientific method is given in ten steps, and the third is where the article spends its attention, because that is where a research project acquires its reason to exist.

1  ·  Evidence gap
Evaluating existing studies to identify "what areas lack sufficient information, which is a fundamental part of research methodology". The absence is of findings rather than of thinking.
2  ·  Knowledge gap
"Like an evidence gap but broader(Rifkin, 2014), it involves not just scientific evidence but also theoretical or conceptual knowledge." The article places it as intrinsically related to philosophy of science while remaining operationally a methodology question.
3  ·  Practical-knowledge and methodological gaps
The practical-knowledge gap concerns "the applicability of research" and is cited to Tahir and Rithmire (2017). Of the methodological gap the article says only that "By its very name, it pertains to methods and would, therefore, fit under" research methodology itself.
4  ·  Empirical, theoretical and population gaps
The empirical gap is "focused on the lack of empirical studies or data in a particular area". The theoretical gap aligns with philosophy of science but is identified during research planning. The population gap "involves identifying the lack of research involving specific populations".
Why the taxonomy is worth having

"Identifying and understanding these gaps is essential for informing the design, focus, and methods of a research project, making them central to the methodology." The kind of gap chosen determines what the study has to produce in order to close it.

Evidence

Six doctrines sit underneath the method, so it is philosophically loaded rather than neutral.

The article's most consequential claim about the scientific method is that it carries commitments: "The scientific method doesn't operate in a philosophical vacuum."

01
Where knowledge comes from
Empiricism and rationalism
Empiricism, "The belief that knowledge is primarily gained through sensory experience", is what makes the method begin with observation. Rationalism, "The belief that reason is a primary source of knowledge, distinct from sensory experience", guides the theoretical work: the logical structuring of hypotheses and the interpretation of data.
02
What makes a claim scientific
Falsifiability and the three reasonings
Popper's criterion shapes how hypotheses are written, since they are "crafted to be falsifiable, enabling a rigorous test of their validity". Induction forms the hypothesis, deduction designs the experiment that tests it, and abduction proposes the best explanation where neither fits.
03
What limits the method
Objectivity and paradigms
The method "Strives for objectivity in data collection and interpretation but acknowledges the potential for subjective biases", blinding being the practical countermeasure. And Kuhn returns: "A prevailing paradigm often shapes the kinds of questions asked and the methods used to answer them."
Popper, given a special note and then qualified

"Popper argued that a scientific theory can never be proven true, but it can be falsified through empirical testing." The article does not leave it there: "some critics have argued that Popper's concept of falsification is too strict and does not account for the role of confirmation and corroboration in scientific inquiry."

Details

Models are simplifications, so what they leave out has to be stated.

Models and theories are treated together as the tools of explanation and prediction, and defined by their incompleteness: they "are simplifications of reality that capture essential features of phenomena for the purpose of explanation or prediction".

The worked example makes the tolerance explicit. "The Bohr model of the atom simplifies atomic structure to help explain electron orbits, even though it doesn't capture the full complexity of quantum mechanics." Being wrong in a known way is not disqualifying.

Which is why assumptions have to be declared. "The ideal gas law model assumes no interactions between gas particles, which is not true under conditions of high pressure and low temperature." A model used outside its assumptions fails silently.

Theories are distinguished by scope rather than by certainty: Newton's law of gravitation explains a wide range of phenomena, where treating the Earth as a perfect sphere is a simplification adopted for particular calculations.

What validation looks like when it works

"Einstein's theory of general relativity was validated during the solar eclipse of 1919 when light from stars was observed to bend around the Sun." A prediction that could have failed, made before the observation, and then tested.

Conclusion

Objectivity survives as an ideal under challenge, and peer review is the only corrective the system actually runs.

The closing section holds two things at once: that objectivity is what scientific method aims at, and that the aim is contested from several directions at once.

The argument that all observations are influenced by the theoretical framework of the observer, thus introducing some level of subjectivity.

— Stop 4: Core Concepts in Science, 11 September 2023
The three kinds it distinguishes
Observational objectivity, the belief that observations need not be tainted by personal or societal belief, with a thermometer against a subjective judgement of warmth. Interpretative objectivity, where data are read on statistical validity regardless of the researcher's view of the drug. And methodological objectivity, the standardisation that makes results comparable.
Where the challenges come from
The article names feminist epistemology, social constructivism and postmodernism together, and states their shared claim plainly: "science, being a human endeavor, can never be entirely free from social and cultural biases."
The corrective that exists
"Manuscripts submitted to scientific journals are often reviewed by multiple experts in the field to ensure that the research meets the standards of objectivity." Peer review is offered not as a guarantee but as the mechanism the system actually has.
And what it is for
The ethical stake is named directly: preventing fraudulent data manipulation, with retraction of papers found to contain fabricated or manipulated data as the visible consequence when it fails.
