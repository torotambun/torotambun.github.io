The Ruler's Word Versus Republican Duty

Presidential speech is institutional action: from the highest office a joke becomes a cue and a label becomes a classification. The courtly ideal must therefore be reversed — not the public bound because the president has spoken, but the president bound to answer for what his speech does.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Eight expressions, seven occasions, one presidency.

An argument about the ethics of presidential communication should begin by refusing the very imprecision it criticizes.

Situation

Eight expressions now form a troubling vocabulary around President Prabowo Subianto's public communication: rakyat jelata, antek asing, ndeso, bajingan, a suggestion that citizens who found Indonesia's future bleak could seek another country, londo ireng, rasain sendiri, and a remark about when a 10-year-old girl might be allowed to date.

Complication

They are not identical. They arose from seven occasions. Two — rakyat jelata and ndeso — were not spoken by Prabowo but by officials within his apparatus. The remark to the child was neither a curse nor an insult. Yet the differences do not dissolve the pattern; they reveal its institutional breadth.

Question

Is the problem that coarse words have been used around the presidency — or is it what the office does to language once it passes through it?

Answer

Presidential speech is institutional action. The remedy is neither silence nor courtly ornament but republican self-command: speech candid enough to name wrongdoing, precise enough to be answered, and disciplined enough to remain worthy of the office.

Gap

The presidency is not a private microphone.

Indonesians are not so fragile that one vulgar noun, one regional idiom or one sharp joke will destroy public life. Something else is at stake.

Nor should democratic politics be embalmed in the artificial politeness of the palace. Demands for refinement can disguise class prejudice: elite critics may tolerate brutal policy expressed in polished language while recoiling from a coarse word spoken with popular conviction. Indonesia should not confuse a court accent with moral virtue.

Several Javanese courtly principles can illuminate this thoroughly republican problem — sabda pandhita ratu, ajining diri saka lathi, empan papan, tepa selira, angon rasa, unggah-ungguh. They should not be used to turn the president into a king. Indonesia is not a court, citizens are not kawula, and political legitimacy does not descend from aristocratic tradition. These principles are valuable only after being democratically reinterpreted.

Core Gap

From the highest office, a joke can become a cue, a label can become a classification, and an insinuation can tell ministers, supporters and bureaucrats which citizens may safely be treated as backward, disloyal, contemptible or unserious. The ethical test changes with the power of the office, the vulnerability of the audience, and the direction in which the verbal force travels.

Not a Romanticised Tradition

Javanese courts could be hierarchical, exclusionary and coercive, and courtly language itself could conceal violence. The point is not that past kings always lived up to these ideals — history could not sustain such an absolute. It is that even a tradition built around concentrated authority understood that the ruler's tongue required restraint. A democratic president, governing equal citizens rather than subjects, should face a higher standard still.

Framework

Five categories, not one catalogue of profanity

Examining the expressions separately makes the cumulative pattern more serious, not less.

01
Rakyat jelata · Ndeso
Language of social hierarchy
Arranges society vertically — those below, spoken about by those above. Rural identity is made to do the insulting. An official apparatus cannot defend a relational failure solely through lexical definition.
02
Antek asing · Londo ireng
Language of suspected loyalty
Divides citizens by suspected allegiance. Neither is an evidentiary category. When the alleged agents are unnamed, the phrase creates a cloud of suspicion without offering the accused a claim they can answer.
03
Bajingan · Rasain sendiri
Language of contempt and taunting
Makes contempt available as a presidential register. Honesty and coarseness are not synonyms, and a joke that imagines every citizen as equally free to act turns real constraints into a punchline.
04
Cari negara lain
Belonging made conditional
Makes national belonging sound conditional on political optimism. In a republic, pessimism is not disloyalty and criticism is not a tenancy violation. A president does not own the country whose future he describes.
05
The remark to Citra
A vulnerable citizen diminished
An unsolicited, gendered and paternalistic diversion from a girl's educational and athletic achievement to her future romantic life — imposed on a child who could not reject the premise or redirect the conversation.

Details

What the courtly principles actually demand, once democratised

Properly reinterpreted, each imposes restraint before speech rather than submission after it.

1  ·  Sabda pandhita ratu — the word cannot be casual
Sometimes reduced to the claim that a ruler's word is law, which is dangerous in a constitutional republic. The useful meaning is ethical: the word of a sage-ruler must be dependable and cannot be tossed about or treated as disposable chatter. If an utterance carries unusual weight, the speaker has an unusual obligation to test it before release. The principle does not enlarge presidential authority; it enlarges presidential accountability.
2  ·  Ajining diri saka lathi — dignity is known through the tongue
Not a command to decorate every sentence with ceremony, but a recognition that speech reveals self-command. For a president the dignity at stake is not only personal: the office speaks in the name of the republic, so a verbal lapse can diminish the institutional dignity entrusted to its holder.
3  ·  Empan papan — the discipline of fit
Knowing the person, place, time, circumstance and social relation in which words are spoken. A phrase that produces laughter at a private table may be wrong at a national legislative session. A joke suitable among adults may be unsuitable when a child has just been presented as a symbol of educational struggle.
4  ·  Tepa selira, angon rasa, unggah-ungguh — relational intelligence
Tepa selira asks the speaker to consider another person's position; angon rasa to tend to feeling; unggah-ungguh concerns propriety; andhap asor associates authority with humility rather than verbal domination. These are not demands for aristocratic affectation.

Evidence

The record, and its gaps

Where an official transcript exists it is cited. Where it omits what citizens heard, that omission is itself evidence.

EVIDENCE A
What the transcripts preserve
Antek asing appears in the official presidential transcript of the 22 August 2025 address to Sekolah Rakyat teachers and principals, and returns in a 24 December address. Bajingan is preserved in the transcript of the 79th National Cooperatives Day commemoration on 12 July 2026.
EVIDENCE B
What the transcripts omit
The published Cooperatives Day transcript confirms the event but does not include the cari negara lain passage, which is preserved in contemporaneous reporting. The State Secretariat's speech text for the 13 August 2026 electric-motorcycle launch presents the event but does not preserve rasain sendiri.
EVIDENCE C
The presidential voice is distributed
Rakyat jelata came from Presidential Communication Office spokesperson Adita Irawati on 5 December 2024, while praising Prabowo for siding with an iced-tea seller. Ndeso and kampungan came from State Secretary Prasetyo Hadi on 30 October 2025, inside an anti-drug message. Neither was Prabowo's own words; both shaped the presidency's voice.
EVIDENCE D
The constitutional frame inverts the palace
Article 1(2) of the 1945 Constitution places sovereignty in the people, exercised according to the Constitution. Article 9 requires the president to swear to fulfil the duties of office justly. Articles 28E and 28F protect expression and communication as democratic activities, not gifts from the executive.
What This Does Not Claim

The eight expressions should not be inflated into criminal or constitutional charges without legal grounds, and no evidence justifies attributing a sexual motive to the remark made to a child. Criticism becomes easier to dismiss when it outruns the verified words. The issue is constitutional ethics, not criminal liability.

Implications

The distinction is between candor and degradation.

Candor names reality. Degradation assigns inferior standing. A precise president does not need euphemism.

Degradation
✗Defending a relational failure with a dictionary
✗Accusing unnamed antek asing or londo ireng
✗Treating national belonging as a reward for optimism
✗Publishing transcripts that omit what citizens heard
Candor
✓Naming the contract, the funding, the rule broken
✓Routing accusations through institutions that can prove them
✓Correcting openly, so the public knows which words bind
✓Protecting a child's dignity within an asymmetry she cannot negotiate
Why Precision Is Tougher Than Insult

A precise president can say which contract was corrupt, which funding relationship was hidden, which policy argument is wrong, which actor violated what rule, and which evidence supports the charge. Such speech is tougher than insult because it can be tested. Precision is not softness; it is accountable force.

The Unequal Distribution of Verbal Power

Those named or implied by the president do not possess an equivalent platform. A journalist can publish a rebuttal, an NGO can issue a statement, a citizen can post online — but none speaks from a lectern backed by the executive state. Humor does not neutralize that hierarchy; in some contexts it deepens it, because the laughter of the room makes objection feel humorless or disloyal.

Conclusion

Not censorship. A communication system proportionate to the power of the office.

These measures would not make presidential language colorless. They would make it more exact — forceful, humorous and recognizably himself, while distinguishing adversaries from traitors and criticism from disloyalty.

1
Apply an empan papan test to every major address
Three questions: from what authority is the speaker talking, who is being addressed or classified, and what action, stigma or permission could the wording produce once repeated by officials and supporters? The test must cover jokes and improvisations as well as prepared lines.
2
Target conduct and evidence, not categories of identity
"This organization failed to disclose foreign funding" is answerable. "There are londo ireng among NGOs" is elastic. Serious accusations should then be routed through the institution capable of proving them — a presidential label should never substitute for an evidentiary process.
3
Adopt a protected-audience protocol
Children and other vulnerable citizens appearing at state events should not be invited into adult speculation about romance, marriage, appearance or family roles. This is not humorlessness; it is recognition that a child cannot negotiate the encounter on equal terms.
4
Red-team high-risk language, and correct openly when it misses
A small function inside the Presidential Communication Office should test phrases against foreseeable readings across class, region, gender, profession and affiliation. The question is not whether a phrase can be defended — almost any can, after controversy — but whether a clearer formulation preserves the intended force without creating unnecessary stigma.
5
Preserve the record, and extend the code beyond the president
Official transcripts should include consequential off-script remarks rather than producing a smoother institutional memory. The communication code must cover ministers, spokespersons and senior aides — the rakyat jelata and ndeso episodes show the presidential voice is distributed.
6
Pair monologue with structured listening
Direct speech is healthiest when those affected can respond through press conferences, consultations and forums where questions are not pre-classified as hostility. A ruler who speaks forcefully but hears weakly will confuse the echo of authority with public understanding.

The ruler's word once bound the people. The republic requires the people's Constitution to bind the president's word.
