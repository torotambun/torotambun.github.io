(4 of 6) Purim: Venahafoch Hu – The Reversal of Destiny

Purim is not a story of endurance or of justice arriving on schedule. It is a story of reversal, and the distinction matters: salvation does not prevent the crisis or precede it, but overturns it using the machinery the aggressor built. Haman's every precaution — the lots, the gallows, the irrevocable seal — becomes the instrument of his defeat.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus

Introduction

Purim is not about surviving the crisis but about the crisis turning over.

Situation

"Some stories are about endurance. Others are about justice. But Purim is about something else entirely: reversal—a complete and sudden overturning of fortune that transforms destruction into salvation."

Complication

The phrase "appears in Esther 9:1, where it describes how everything was "turned upside down.""

Question

"This theme does not only apply to one moment in the story—it defines the entire narrative."

Answer

"Purim is not just about survival. It is about how fate itself can be upended in ways no one foresaw."

The Reversals

Every precaution Haman took became the mechanism of his defeat.

"Every turn of the Megillah unfolds in unexpected reversals, defying both human calculations and natural expectations."

1  ·  The lots he cast for safety
"his fate was already sealed the moment he cast lots (purim) to determine the execution date" (Esther 3:7). "Instead of securing his control, the delay created the very opening that led to Esther’s intervention." "Haman believed in fate, but he never saw the hidden counterforces at work against him."
2  ·  The reward that was withheld
"Mordechai’s recognition was improbably late—but precisely on time." His deed "had been recorded but not rewarded. The event faded into obscurity—until one specific night, the night before Haman planned to execute him." "That night the king could not sleep…" (Esther 6:1)
3  ·  The honour he designed for himself
"The reversal was instant. Haman arrived at the palace intending to hang Mordechai but left leading him through the streets in royal robes" (Esther 6:7-11). "Had Mordechai been honored earlier, it would have been personal recognition, nothing more. But at the perfect moment, it became a weapon of reversal".
4  ·  The gallows he built
"Haman had constructed a 50-cubit gallows, prepared specifically for Mordechai’s execution" (Esther 5:14). "Yet within hours, Haman was the one hanging from his own gallows" (Esther 7:10). "The very structure designed to destroy his enemy became the instrument of his own demise."
5  ·  The seal that could not be broken
The decree "was sealed with the king’s signet ring—a law that, by Persian rule, could not be revoked" (Esther 8:8). "But then, another reversal: A new decree was issued, allowing the Jews not to flee, but to fight back". "Instead of being slaughtered, they were victorious. Instead of mourning, they celebrated."

The Principle

The rabbinic reading is measure for measure, and the article states it as a structural rule rather than a moral wish.

Three rabbinic passages are used to name what the narrative is doing.

PRINCIPLE A
On what survives pressure
"The righteous are likened to trees—shaken by the wind but never uprooted. The wicked are likened to chaff—swept away in an instant" (Megillah 12a).
PRINCIPLE B
On timing
"Deliverance is not rushed, nor is it late. It comes in the appointed hour" (Berakhot 60b). The article uses this to argue that the lateness of Mordechai's reward is a feature of the story rather than a flaw in it.
PRINCIPLE C
Measure for measure
The fate of the gallows "aligns with the principle of Midah K'neged Midah" — measure-for-measure justice. The Midrash: "The trap one sets for another will become his own snare" (Vayikra Rabbah 26:7, Schwartz, 2004).
PRINCIPLE D
What Haman failed to account for
"Haman had played every move carefully, ensuring nothing could interfere with his revenge. Yet, he failed to account for divine reversal." And on the pattern as a whole: "This is not coincidence. It is divine precision."

The Distinction

Rescue would have prevented the day; reversal used it.

The instalment's sharpest claim is about what kind of deliverance this is.

What did not happen
✗The decree was not revoked — Persian law did not allow it
✗"Salvation did not come before the crisis"
✗"nor did it simply prevent it"
✗Haman's death alone changed nothing: "Even after Haman’s death, the decree still stood."
✗No plague, no parted sea, no named divine act anywhere in the book
What happened instead
✓"It overturned the crisis itself—turning destruction into a moment of divine intervention."
✓"The day chosen for their destruction became their day of triumph."
✓"On the very day that the enemies of the Jews had hoped to gain mastery over them, it was turned upside down (Venahafoch Hu), and the Jews gained mastery over their foes" (Esther 9:1)
✓"The timing of deliverance, rather than its mere occurrence, is what gives Purim its defining character."
✓"Haman builds the gallows—yet he is the one who swings from it."

Conclusion

Justice arrives, and it arrives by a route nobody was watching.

"Purim’s central message is not just that justice prevails, but that justice arrives in ways no one predicts."

It is not enough to endure hardship—one must trust in the unseen hand that turns the page.

— (4 of 6) Venahafoch Hu
The shape of the pattern
"The rising and falling of power in the Megillah follows an unmistakable pattern of divine orchestration" — and the article lists it as three inversions: the builder of the gallows swings from it, the condemned man is elevated, the marked people emerge victorious.
On history's direction
"The sages teach that history bends toward justice, but its path is rarely straight" — followed by "The wicked prepare, but the righteous inherit" (Proverbs 13:22).
The question it ends on
"This axiology of reversal remains one of the defining spiritual insights of Purim." "The Megillah is proof that everything can change in a moment." And then, directly to the reader: "Venahafoch Hu—when fate turns in an instant, will you recognize it?"
