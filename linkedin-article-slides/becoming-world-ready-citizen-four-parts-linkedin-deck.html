LinkedIn Article  ·  Leadership · The World-Ready Citizen  ·  3 July 2019

Becoming a world-ready citizen : by acknowledging that human are irrational creatures, teams are necessary, system dynamics explains life.

The article, a personal reflection, says a world-ready citizen acknowledges that humans are irrational creatures, that teams are necessary and that system dynamics explains life. In four parts it says to measure life by cherished moments, generosity and meaningful impact and not by fame, wealth or power; to rely on teams whose skills and networks complement each other and whose objectives are congruent; to expect the worst of humanity even in the most elite group while celebrating its best; and to treat life as a complex system, where humility comes from acknowledging unintended effects.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

A world-ready citizen acknowledges that humans are irrational, that teams are necessary and that life is a dynamic system.

Situation

The article is a personal reflection set among executive education participants, people who have climbed or are still climbing the corporate ladder. Its bibliography places it in Harvard Business School GMP 26 and MIT Sloan classes, and it sets out what becoming a world-ready citizen takes, in four parts: self, others, expectations, and life as dynamics and a complex system.

Complication

A person cannot control what others think and perceive of him, and the article says it is scientifically very human to make a reality out of a perception, since the brain evolved to work that way. Even the most elite group, it says, shows irrational behaviour from the most rational people.

Question

What does it take to become a world-ready citizen?

Answer

Acknowledging three things, as the title says: humans are irrational creatures, teams are necessary and system dynamics explains life.

Evidence note

The article is written from personal experience and gives no data. Its claim that the brain was evolutionarily wired to make a reality out of a perception carries no source.

Details

The self is measured by cherished moments, generosity and meaningful impact, and not by fame, wealth or power.

The article says a person must not surrender to what people say and think, and must keep improving and refining the self through a spirit of discernment, which it links to authenticity.

The measures to avoid
✗Level of fame
✗Level of wealth
✗Level of power
✗Surrendering to what people say and think
✗Situational leadership: cultivating a weak bias to portray it as a strong one
What the article measures instead
✓Cherishing every remaining moment: being healthy, surrounded by loved ones and having choices
✓Being always generous yet prudent, whether affluent or less affluent
✓Creating a life of meaningful impact to others
✓Blocking noise and combining the right and matching biases when an opportunity arrives
✓Waiting, and saying no to opportunities that do not fit the aspirations (Aulet, 2018)
Evidence note

The article says a leader who applies situational leadership may suffer a psychological breakdown, and can continue for 20 years before it takes its toll. Neither statement carries a source.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

Success rests on teams whose skills and networks complement each other and whose objectives are congruent.

The article says no successful people are mercurial individuals and that reliance on teamwork is a prerequisite. What counts is the composition of the team, not the number of people in it.

01
First
Skills
Skills complement each other among friends in a group.
02
Second
Networks
Networks complement each other.
03
Third
Objectives
Objectives must be congruent. The team's principles are to live life to the fullest and to treat money only as a means to an end, hence full integrity, 100 percent and not 99 or 98, with its derivatives: FCPA compliance, no toxic influence and a par value policy.
04
Choice
Who to empower with
A person must be independent in deciding with whom to be empowered: those who are world-ready, happy with other people's happiness and willing to share knowledge, genuine empathy, work opportunities and education financing. Friends who are offended by a person's progress can be disengaged.
Evidence note

The article puts the perception of arrogance in certain cultures down to friends' lack of a growth mental model. This is the author's own experience, with no source.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

Expect the worst of humanity even in the most elite group, and celebrate its best.

The article asks for both at once: expect the worst to come out even in the most elite group in the world, and celebrate the best that comes out of the same group.

Expect the worst
✗People being slandered
✗A dog-eat-dog approach and displays of political identity
✗Selfishness for survival
✗Lack of integrity in small measures: punctuality, promises and task completion
✗Favoritism and irrational behaviour from the most rational group of people
Celebrate the best
✓The best of humanity, which comes out of the same elite group
✓The moment
✓The togetherness
✓The learning struggle and the exercises
✓The gifts and the time of laughter
Evidence note

The article gives no example or count for these behaviours and does not name the group.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

Life is a complex system of delays and loops, where one person's actions reach people in other systems.

The article says there is nothing linear about life.

1  ·  Delays
Life is full of delays, which the article calls surprises.
2  ·  Effects on other systems
What a person does in his own system affects people in other complex systems.
3  ·  Loops
A decision can loop back opposite to what was expected, or respond as expected. A positive or negative loop can produce a balancing effect after an intervention, or a reinforcing effect.
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

Humility comes from acknowledging that the system leaves room for unintended effects.

The last paragraph turns the complexity into a point about humility and ends on a borrowed line.

However, the humility of our life comes from acknowledging that the system has always rooms for UN-INTENDED effects.

— Life is dynamics and a complex system, 3 July 2019
No design is right
The article adds that no matter how smart the design, it is always wrong.
The closing line
The article closes on a line it says Mike Tyson is thought to have quoted, that everyone has a plan until they are punched in the mouth.
What supports it
A personal reflection with no data. Its support is the author's experience and a bibliography of assessment reports, class materials and one textbook, of which only Aulet (2018) is tied to a claim in the text.
