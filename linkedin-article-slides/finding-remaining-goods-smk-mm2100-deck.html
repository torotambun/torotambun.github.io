Finding the Remaining Goods in Indonesia: SMK Mitra Industri MM2100

The officials responsible for vocational education have already seen a working SMK. What they have not copied — and cannot install — are the underlying assumptions that keep its artifacts alive on ordinary days.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

The most-visited vocational school in Indonesia has produced ceremonies — and no comparable institutions.

Near the entrance, two chairs bearing the names Naruhito and Masako stand behind rope stanchions, almost like a shrine.

Situation

SMK Mitra Industri MM2100 in Cikarang has hosted ministers, officials, industry leaders and the state visit of the Emperor and Empress of Japan. There are photographs, videos and ceremonies.

Complication

Among the government SMKs I have studied and visited, I do not see many operating close to this standard. In my research, SMK still appears as a second option, sometimes the third — entered because another school was unavailable or the family had already lowered its expectation.

Question

If the example is visible, accessible and already visited, why has nothing comparable appeared — and what exactly is it that cannot be copied?

Answer

The artifacts can all be bought and installed. What survives ordinary days are the shared values and underlying assumptions beneath them — and what remains missing is evidence that the visitors learned them.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Gap

A visitor can read the artifacts in an afternoon. That is the problem.

Thick description requires resisting the temptation to assign meaning too quickly — and so does policy.

The compound was green. Students walked a dedicated pedestrian lane. Motorcycles stayed in designated parking. The directors' toilet, hidden in the wall, was dry and spotless; so was the public one used by students and visitors. Students wore black blazers, greeted adults properly, and replied sharply when asked a question. They were not isinan.

These were still artifacts. A clean toilet does not prove a good school. A short haircut does not prove character. A green pedestrian lane does not prove discipline. A blazer does not prove confidence. So I kept looking — and what I found underneath was an institution that had been built around a problem rather than around a standard.

Core Gap

Everything a visiting delegation can see is reproducible. Paint can be bought, equipment installed, a programme launched. What cannot be installed are the shared values and underlying assumptions that make those artifacts survive ordinary days — and those are precisely what a study tour is least able to observe.

The Ontology Is Different

A toilet remains clean because people have decided what condition is acceptable. A teaching factory works because equipment is expected to be used. A dream pin means something only if the school takes the student's intended future seriously enough to organise learning around it. Government institutions can copy the visible form while preserving the assumptions that produced the old institution.

Framework

An industrial problem, not an educational one

The school exists because industry could open the factory gate but could not supply the formation needed to stay inside.

01
The founders
Built by people from the factory floor
Ms. Lispiyatmini came from Jotun. Mr. Musfir came from JFE. Mr. Darwoto was connected with the management of the MM2100 industrial estate. Mr. Kobi was part of the founding story. Their backgrounds matter because the school was born from an industrial problem.
02
The failure
Access did not become persistence
Local people wanted their children accepted into the factories inside the estate. That demand was understandable. But after some were hired, some left after a week, some after two weeks, some after a month. The companies could provide access to work; they could not automatically provide the preparation required to remain in it.
03
The response
A school inside the compound
Rather than accept the available explanation, the founders built a vocational school within the industrial environment, placing honesty, responsibility, discipline and cooperation at the centre of the model — dispositions that decide whether someone maintains a machine, reports a defect truthfully and stays with a difficult process.
04
The demand
Chosen, not settled for
Students wore pins carrying their dreams. Some told me their parents own PT companies — families with alternatives who chose an SMK anyway. A school populated mainly by students who believe they lost somewhere else begins from a different psychological position from one that students with alternatives are willing to choose.

Details

Work with consequences, not values printed on a wall

Four things the school does that a photograph of it would not reveal.

1  ·  A café with customers, revenue and consequences
Hospitality students brew the coffee, prepare the menu, cook, serve and operate the business, generating around Rp50 million per month. The coffee failed my own test — I prefer robusta, so I cannot praise everything merely because I liked the school. The business was different. It was not a classroom pretending to be a café.
2  ·  A teaching factory using real cars
Teachers and families who owned cars could bring them in so students could work on them. Students did not have to depend only on equipment that exists to demonstrate one predetermined exercise. The Paint School under industrial chemistry was built with Jotun, with involvement from Chris Adidharma.
3  ·  Destination decides the syllabus
The school uses BMW and now BMW+ language: Bekerja, Melanjutkan Kuliah, Wirausaha, Magang ke Jepang, and international options that include Germany. Students following different destinations can have different schedules and syllabi. That is a serious institutional choice, not an acronym.
4  ·  A dropout system school
Students who do not pass the required academic standard can be removed, as can those caught in certain unethical behaviour — and the rule applies regardless of who they are. Admission is selective; in the earlier period the ratio was approximately one student accepted for every five applicants. That is different from printing values on a wall.

Evidence

What I can report from one visit

One visit cannot prove that strict dismissal causes good behaviour. It can report the combination encountered.

EVIDENCE A
The repeated pattern
The toilet was clean. The pathways were controlled. Motorcycles were organised. Workshops were ordered. Students were groomed. The café was operating. Cleanliness alone proves little — but by then it was no longer an isolated observation, and the repeated pattern became harder to dismiss as decoration prepared for a visitor.
EVIDENCE B
A student whose parents own a PT
I had assumed an SMK serving students with fewer options. While talking with students in the café, I became suspicious of that assumption and asked. They told me some of their parents owned PT companies. A student whose parents own a PT still chose an SMK. That is the sentence I want to remember from the visit.
EVIDENCE C
Japan is inside the school, not in a seminar
The school has sent many students to Japan and prepares them through a pathway requiring schedule, language, behaviour and curriculum. I was told the students they send are absorbed — though I want to distinguish carefully between what I was told during the visit and what can be independently documented.
EVIDENCE D
The institutional independence
The school was willing to differentiate curriculum by student destination, maintain close contact with manufacturing, run a café as an actual business, use real cars in a teaching factory, build international internship pathways and enforce its own behavioural expectations. It did not appear to be waiting for government to tell it how to become useful.
Where I Am Withholding Judgment

The founders described a trip to Yogyakarta during the Merapi eruption period as connected to the discussions that led to the school. I want the exact chronology checked before treating that sequence as historical fact. I prefer to preserve what I heard rather than repair memory with a convenient date.

Implications

A ceremony proves a school was visited. It proves nothing about what changed afterwards.

They have seen it. The problem begins after they leave.

Wrong Signal of Success
✗Counting ministerial visits, photographs and ceremonies
✗Copying buildings, uniforms, workshops and cafés
✗Reading clean facilities as proof of character
✗Arranging another study tour to see what is known
The Right Work
✓Schools families with alternatives actively choose
✓Schedules and syllabi that differ by destination
✓Standards enforced with real consequences
✓Enough distance from bureaucracy to decide
The Harder Standard

I am less interested in whether an official was impressed during a visit than in what appeared afterward. Where are the comparable schools? Where are the institutions that families with options actively choose? Where are the government SMKs where a student whose parents own a company still says, voluntarily, I want to study here?

Room to Decide

Government can become destructive when an institution spends too much energy satisfying bureaucracy, ceremonial expectations, reporting rituals, titles and visitors. The school I visited appeared to have room to make consequential decisions. That may be one of the reasons it became useful.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

Stop touring the exception. Build the conditions that produced it.

The artifacts are reproducible and largely beside the point; the assumptions beneath them are neither. These measures move attention from the visit to what follows it.

1
Measure choice, not attendance
Ask where else a government SMK is actively chosen by families who had alternatives, instead of counting visits to the one that already is. Social position affects a school before its curriculum begins.
2
Give institutions room to decide
Reduce the reporting rituals, ceremonial expectations and approval layers that consume the energy a school needs to build pathways with industry, differentiate its curriculum and enforce its own standards.
3
Judge the year after the ribbon
Track what actually appeared inside a visitor's own institution afterwards — differentiated pathways, enforced standards, working industry partnerships — rather than what was admired during the visit.
4
Copy the assumptions, or copy nothing
If extraction, position and performance remain the underlying assumptions, copying the artifacts of a good school will not produce the same school. Performative culture survives very comfortably inside new buildings and new programmes.

The officials have already visited SMK Mitra Industri MM2100. What remains missing is evidence that they learned from it.
