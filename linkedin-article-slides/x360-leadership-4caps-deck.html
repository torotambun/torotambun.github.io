X360 Leadership Development Models (4CAPS +)

The x360 model starts from Signature Leadership — the claim that a leader's style is his own and that he is obliged to articulate it — and only then derives the four capabilities. That order matters: capabilities without an articulated philosophy produce compliance rather than an X-Team. And the model earns its place not in stable conditions but in the ones three successive acronyms have tried to name.

Introduction

Signature Leadership says your style is yours and you are obliged to articulate it.

The article is written to explain a framework to students, and it begins where the framework begins rather than with its list.

The purpose is stated plainly: "With the aim to illuminate this elegant yet straightforward framework, this article has been penned for students and those keen on exploring the intricacies of the X-Team concept." It follows a Jakarta Post column in which the author had discussed the same framework.

What distinguishes the model is not its taxonomy: "The xLEAD leadership model stands out for its streamlined approach to leadership, emphasizing the inherent uniqueness of each leader's style—what's often termed "Signature Leadership.""

And uniqueness carries an obligation rather than a permission: the concept "underscores the importance of leaders communicating their distinct philosophies to their teams, ensuring a collective and culturally coherent understanding within the organization." A style held privately does no organisational work.

The conditions that follow from it are specific. Each team member should be "part of an inclusive and skilled group" aligned "with a shared and noble objective", in a dynamic "where curiosity, a value for diversity, and comfort with various perspectives converge to bring forth innovative solutions."

Framework

Four capabilities follow from that, held together by integrity.

The article names the capabilities in prose and draws them in mind maps; the definitions live in the drawings.

The derivation is stated as a consequence of disposition rather than of training: "A leader’s role then is clear: to be purpose-driven, to embody resilience, and to maintain a high degree of open-mindedness. These traits, coupled with conscientiousness and an appropriate level of assertiveness, cultivate the four critical leadership capabilities of sense-making, inventing, relating, and visioning."

The fifth element is named where the framework is summarised: "the 4 CAPS+ framework (encompassing visioning, inventing, relating, sense-making, and a shared purpose of integrity)". Integrity is the plus sign — a shared purpose rather than a fifth skill.

What the four are for is given as a sequence of organisational acts: "the X-Team Framework's ability to establish a clear vision, assess opportunities, develop strategic plans, and build trust, all rooted in an unwavering commitment to integrity."

The definitions are in the drawings, not the text

Eight mind maps carry the article's substance, each captioned as the author's own conceptual distillation of a model developed by Ancona and Bresman, along with a "distilled list of traits" that "served as references for the creation of the mind maps". All of them are images and none survives text extraction, so what each capability is taken to contain is not recoverable from the record. This deck reports only what the prose states.

Details

An X-Team faces outward — ambassadorship, task coordination, scouting.

The definition that distinguishes an X-Team from a good team, and the reach the article claims for it.

The capabilities "set the foundation for an X-Team—a team not confined by inward focus but adept in engaging with the external environment through strategic sense-making, effective ambassadorship, and task coordination." Cohesion is not the differentiator; external orientation is.

The claimed reach goes past performance: "These X-Teams are poised to make significant impacts beyond their immediate roles, influencing society, the environment, and the broader welfare of stakeholders."

And the mechanism is a sequence rather than a quality: "Central to this is the X-Team Framework's ability to establish a clear vision, assess opportunities, develop strategic plans, and build trust, all rooted in an unwavering commitment to integrity."

The model is presented as one instrument among several. It "synergizes with valuable concepts like System Dynamics and Disciplined Entrepreneurship" and with "the transformative Three Lenses model from MIT Sloan Executive Education" — a toolbox rather than a doctrine.

Conditions

VUCA, TUNA and BANI name three generations of the same difficulty.

The article sets out all three vocabularies and does not choose between them, which is itself the observation.

VUCA, from the U.S. Army War College
•Volatility — "the rate of change in an industry, market or the world in general", with challenges "usually of short duration"
•Uncertainty — "the lack of predictability in issues and events", from "a lack of information or an inability to understand an event or its potential impact"
•Complexity — "Multiple key decision factors are typically involved, with some being interconnected"
•Ambiguity — "The lack of clarity about how to interpret something due to the "unknown unknowns.""
BANI, the newer frame
✗Brittle — "Things that are brittle appear to be strong or stable, but are prone to sudden and catastrophic failure when conditions go beyond what they can withstand"
✗Anxious — "a pervasive feeling of discomfort or worry about the unpredictable nature of the environment and its potential threats"
✗Non-linear — "the output is not directly proportional to the input, often resulting in disproportionate effects"
✗Incomprehensible — "Despite access to vast amounts of data and information, the inability to make sense of it all leads to confusion"
TUNA sits between them, and the article is honest about what it does not know

TUNA — turbulent, uncertain, novel, ambiguous — is "not as widely referenced as VUCA" and "is used in executive education". Turbulence "implies more chaos and dynamics that are harder to anticipate than those suggested by volatility." On BANI's origin the article states plainly that "specific details on the proponent of the BANI model were not found in the immediate search results, suggesting that it might be a concept that is still gaining traction". A hedge left standing rather than filled in.

Conclusion

The toolbox outlasts the career, which is why he says it can be savoured in retirement.

The article ends somewhere unusual for a leadership framework — after the working life it was built for.

These models serve as invaluable guides in both professional and post-retirement phases, offering leaders the joy of exploring deep-seated principles that can be beautifully applied to life beyond the workforce.

— X360 Leadership Development Models (4CAPS +), 2 April 2024
What is being assembled
"The process of learning and integrating concepts like Signature Leadership, the 4 CAPS+ framework (encompassing visioning, inventing, relating, sense-making, and a shared purpose of integrity), alongside the X-teams framework, equips leaders with a rich "toolbox.""
Retirement as a phase of application
"Retirement thus becomes a phase of life where the application of such profound leadership principles can be savored and explored in depth." A frame that treats leadership capability as something one keeps rather than something one held.
Where Signature Leadership is grounded
"In this leadership narrative, the Signature Leadership characteristic celebrates each individual's unique design—a challenge that leaders must embrace and articulate to their teams." The article grounds the claim in Psalm 139:13-14, quoting "For it was You who created my inward parts; You knit me together in my mother's womb. I will praise You because I have been fearfully and wonderfully made".
