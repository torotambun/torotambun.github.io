What the Government Counts as Entrepreneurship

The same state has described the same men twice — as openly unemployed and as micro-enterprise actors — while nothing in the work moved between the two descriptions. A productivity problem has been converted into an entrepreneurship statistic.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Two official descriptions of the same men, issued by the same state.

Between them, the pavement, the waiting time, the fuel cost and the day's takings stayed exactly where they were.

Situation

In July 2024 I wrote a field note about a walkway in a century-old part of Jakarta — motorcycles parked along its length, men sitting on them with their phones, waiting. By Badan Pusat Statistik's own definition of pengangguran terbuka, which counts people whose work does not match their skills, education or desired employment level, they remained unemployed.

Complication

On 1 July 2026 the Ministry of UMKM announced that two-wheeled ride-hailing drivers would automatically be treated as pengusaha mikro transportasi online, with access to programs available to other micro-enterprise actors. Both descriptions are official. Both were issued by the same state.

Question

What does the reclassification actually change — and who carries the cost when supplementary earning capacity becomes a permanent livelihood?

Answer

Only the administrative description changed, and it produces a better-looking economic fact. Indonesia should separate protection from classification: protect platform-dependent workers without declaring them opportunity entrepreneurs or converting them into conventional employees.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Gap

The pavement did not change. The paperwork did.

A driver whose working day is built around waiting for orders does not acquire productive capability because a ministry has recorded him as a business actor.

I did not name the sector in 2024, because I did not want a Sunday morning observation to arrive as an accusation. The government has now named the work differently. What interests me is not the label but the arithmetic underneath it, which is unchanged.

The reclassification does not settle whether these drivers are best understood as partners, entrepreneurs or employees. The more important economic questions are what kind of work has absorbed them, what happens to their productive capability while they remain in it, and who carries the cost when supplementary earning capacity becomes a permanent livelihood.

Core Gap

The state has taken workers whose labour was absorbed without any corresponding increase in productive capability, and recorded that absorption as micro-enterprise. The measurement improves; the economy does not. A statistic that counts absorption as enterprise cannot detect the problem it is concealing.

Source-Control Note

The 1 July 2026 Ministry of UMKM press release is the primary government source relied on here for the change in treatment. The official text of Perpres No. 27/2026 is not relied upon to establish micro-enterprise status — I had not seen the official PDF at the time of writing. That is a statement about source availability, not a claim about the instrument's validity.

Framework

Four things the reclassification does not touch

Each is independent of the label, and each survives it intact.

01
The pricing
Idle capacity is not a livelihood
A matching platform is economically strongest when it monetises capacity that would otherwise sit idle — a motorcycle already owned, a few spare hours. Once the same arrangement becomes a principal livelihood, the fare must carry fuel, depreciation, waiting time, household expenditure, accident exposure and old age.
02
The productivity
Transactions are not capability
A platform may generate more transactions without making an hour of its driver's work more productive. Productivity comes when workers learn better processes and firms accumulate capabilities that increase what an hour of labour can produce.
03
The typology
Necessity is not opportunity
Someone who identifies an opportunity, assembles resources and builds a business is doing something economically different from someone pushed into self-employment because the productive economy has no suitable place for him. The literature has long separated the two.
04
The risk
The label reassigns the bill
Treated as a micro-enterprise actor, the driver carries the depreciation of his motorcycle, his idle hours and much of the economic risk of earning through the platform — the same pattern I traced in 2023 among MSME suppliers on thin margins.

Details

The year-eight test

Not whether someone is economically active, but whether an hour of their work has become worth more.

1  ·  What eight years buys in a capable firm
A worker who spends eight years in a capable manufacturing firm may know a process in year eight that he did not know in year one. The firm may also have learned how to organise that worker, combine his skills with others' and improve what the group produces — process learning, capital deepening and organisational improvement compounding together.
2  ·  What eight years buys on a phone
A driver who spends eight years accepting orders certainly learns routes, customers and the demands of the job. Those are genuine skills. The problem is that the economic value of his hour may remain largely where it began.
3  ·  Why the structure makes this unavoidable
Manufacturing value added rose to 22.7 percent of GDP in 2000, fell to 18.3 percent in 2022, and recovered only to 19.1 percent by 2025. Ride-hailing and delivery expanded during a period in which manufacturing no longer carried the economic weight it once did.
4  ·  Why calling him an employee does not solve it either
An industrial employment relationship assumes a firm that accumulates capability in its workers because it needs that capability to compete. A matching platform sits in none of the three enterprise typologies I separated in 2021. Legislating an industrial relationship over a business that does not accumulate capability would impose much of the cost of industrial employment without the upgrading that ordinarily justifies it.

Evidence

What is on the record

Two of these are the state's own documents. Two are precedents from elsewhere.

EVIDENCE A
The state's two descriptions
Badan Pusat Statistik (2023) defines pengangguran terbuka to include people who are working, where the work does not match their skills, education or desired employment level. The Ministry of UMKM (2026) records the same drivers as business actors. Both official; nothing in the work moved between them.
EVIDENCE B
The productive structure
World Bank data: manufacturing value added at 22.7 percent of GDP in 2000, 18.3 percent in 2022, 19.1 percent in 2025. Platform work can prevent unemployment from becoming destitution, but it cannot by itself supply the cumulative productivity growth Indonesia's 2045 ambitions require.
EVIDENCE C
Singapore separated protection from classification
Singapore created a specific statutory framework for platform workers covering social security, work injury and representation — demonstrating that platform-specific protection can be designed as a distinct policy response rather than forcing a choice between ordinary employment and ordinary self-employment.
EVIDENCE D
Rivigo investigated before it legislated
Deepak Garg asked why India could face a shortage of truck drivers despite high unemployment and healthy wages. Field research in the recruiting villages found that long stretches away from home made the occupation unattractive. Rivigo reorganised long-distance trucking around relay operations so drivers could return home far more regularly.
What This Does Not Claim

None of this establishes that the drivers are worse off for having been reclassified, or that access to micro-enterprise programs is valueless. It establishes that the reclassification records a conversion in productive capability that has not taken place.

Implications

A better-looking statistic is not a better economy.

The government faces two distinct tasks, and only one of them is served by a change in classification.

Wrong Signal of Success
✗Counting reclassified drivers as new micro-enterprises
✗Reading platform transaction growth as productivity growth
✗Granting opportunity standing to necessity-based work
✗Issuing certificates with no productive job to enter
The Right Work
✓Asking whether an hour in year eight is worth more
✓Naming which costs belong to the driver, which to the platform
✓Protecting economic dependence without reclassifying it
✓Building firms that make labour more valuable over time
The Conversion

The treatment takes workers whose labour has been absorbed without a corresponding increase in productive capability, and records that absorption as micro-enterprise. A productivity problem is converted into an entrepreneurship statistic — and a converted statistic is harder to act on than an honest one.

The Practical Constraint

Government cannot industrialise these drivers by decree, and a driver who needs income this week cannot wait for productive jobs that may take years to build. Protection therefore cannot be deferred until the productive economy is fixed — but neither should it be mistaken for the fix.

Conclusion

Separate protection from classification.

Protect the people whose livelihoods already depend on platform work, and reduce the number for whom it is the best full-time option available. These are two tasks, not one.

1
Protect without reclassifying
A worker who depends substantially on a platform for income can receive social protection and clearer rules on the allocation of risk without being declared an opportunity entrepreneur or automatically converted into a conventional employee.
2
Begin with injury cover and retirement
These are among the protections incorporated into Singapore's platform-worker framework, and they are the obvious place to start. Platforms should bear part of the costs the work creates while drivers retain the flexibility that distinguishes platform work from conventional employment.
3
Make the cost allocation explicit
Rules on deductions, compensation and waiting time should state which costs belong to the driver and which arise from the platform's operating model. Ambiguity here is not neutral — it defaults the bill to the driver.
4
Investigate the condition before legislating the label
Garg examined what produced the shortage before reorganising the work. Officials should ask what makes platform driving unavoidable as a full-time livelihood, which costs the current model pushes onto drivers, and what productive work could offer a credible exit.

The test is whether an hour in year eight has become worth more than an hour in year one.
