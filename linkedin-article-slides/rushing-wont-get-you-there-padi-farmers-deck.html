(1 of 4) Rushing Won't Get You There: What Padi Farmers Teach About Long-Term Success

An Indonesian padi farmer plants walking backward, eyes fixed on the line already sown, because the straightness of that line is the only part of the harvest he owns. Everything else — rain, pests, the price of rice — is outside him. The discipline is not humility about outcomes; it is precision about process, and it is the premise the rest of this series will complicate.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus

Opening

The farmer plants walking backward, and it is not superstition.

The sky is still pale. He bends forward, drops seed into damp mud, and then does something that looks like the wrong direction.

Situation

An Indonesian farmer steps barefoot into the paddy at first light and begins placing seed into the soil with practiced precision.

Complication

He moves backward, not forward, eyes fixed on the line of seed behind him rather than on the ground ahead — a method that looks counterintuitive to anyone watching.

Question

Why plant facing what is already done, when the field ahead is where the crop has to grow?

Answer

Because the straight line is what gives the seedlings optimal spacing, and a line can only be kept straight while you can still see it. Correcting later is not available to him.

In his words

"He will not rush. He will not correct mistakes later. There is no later. The line must be straight now."

The Constraint

He cannot control the rain, the pests or the price, so he controls the one thing that is his.

The method is not tradition for its own sake. It is a boundary drawn around what is actually within reach.

The farmer moves back until his heel touches the edge between his field and his neighbour's — a marker of responsibility as much as of property. Behind him is a line he made; ahead of him is weather he did not.

"The farmer cannot control the rain. He cannot stop the pests or predict the market price of rice. But this, this process of planting, is entirely his. And he will do it right."

That sentence is the whole argument in miniature. The uncertainties are real and they are named — weather, pests, water quality, market. None of them is denied and none of them is managed. What is managed is the single stretch of the chain that belongs to him.

"By doing this one part right, they set the foundation for a successful harvest." The claim is deliberately modest: not that precision guarantees a harvest, but that it is the foundation one can lay.

The Turn

Looking back is how he keeps the next step straight, not how he dwells.

Kierkegaard's line about understanding and living is usually read as a consolation. In the paddy field it is a working instruction.

Life can only be understood backwards; but it must be lived forwards.

— Søren Kierkegaard, quoted in the article
Not hesitation
"The farmer is not stuck in the past. He is not moving backward out of hesitation or fear." The backward step is deliberate action, not retreat.
The same motion elsewhere
"A student nearing graduation reflects on past study habits to avoid making the same mistakes. A job seeker adjusts their approach after every rejection. A researcher revisits failed experiments, searching for overlooked details."
Where the wisdom lives
"Kierkegaard's wisdom doesn't live only in books—it lives in the muddy fields of Indonesia."

Framework

The business world calls this Total Quality Management; the farmer calls it planting.

TQM is built on doing things right the first time. Stripped of its vocabulary, it is the same ritual.

What TQM rules out
✗Shortcuts taken early and paid for late
✗Last-minute fixes standing in for method
✗Scattering effort in every direction and hoping
✗Treating quality as something inspected at the end
✗Assuming a careless first step can be absorbed downstream
What both the farmer and the leader accept
✓Careful, intentional action at every stage
✓Quality built into the process from the very beginning
✓One misstep in planting can cost the whole harvest
✓Some things stay beyond control — weather, markets
✓Focus on the method and the execution, and the odds improve
The equivalence he draws

"Just as the farmer doesn't scatter seeds carelessly, businesses must build quality into their processes from the very beginning."

Application

Precision now is what buys the student, the job seeker and the retiree their later freedom.

The article names three readers and refuses to give them the same instruction twice.

1  ·  Students and researchers
"In academic work or a PhD journey, rushing to complete tasks without precision or care often leads to wasted time and effort." The instruction is to structure the thesis carefully and refuse to rush the hard parts — the foundational work is the straight line.
2  ·  Job seekers
"it's not about sending out hundreds of generic resumes. It's about crafting each application thoughtfully, aligning your skills with the role, and showing genuine intent." Market conditions and employer decisions are the weather; preparation is the planting.
3  ·  Retirees and those in transition
"Look back at your experiences to understand what worked, what didn't, and what truly matters to you." Alignment with values takes the place of alignment of seed rows.
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

There is no later; the line must be straight now.

Three instructions, each one his own words, each one addressed to whoever is holding the seed.

1
Do the right thing right the first time
Set as a standalone line in the article, unqualified. Not do it fast, and not fix it afterwards — right, first time, because the correction window the farmer does not have is the one most people assume they do.
2
Control the process, not the outcome
"The world is unpredictable—just like the weather, pests, and market shifts the farmer cannot control. But the process, the care in each step, is yours to command." The uncertainty is not argued away; it is placed outside the boundary of responsibility.
3
Look back to learn, then step ahead with purpose
"Move forward, yes—but do so carefully. Look back to learn. Then step ahead with purpose" — the reflection is in service of the next step, never a substitute for it.

The discipline of planting well is the foundation for any harvest worth waiting for.

— Dr. A.R.Y. Toronata Tambun
