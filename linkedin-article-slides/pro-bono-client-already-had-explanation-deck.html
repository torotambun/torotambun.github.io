When a Pro Bono Client Already Had an Explanation

A client who arrives with a theory has already compressed the problem. Undoing that compression is the consulting work — and the audit of my own reasoning found that I applied the discipline to his explanation far more rigorously than to the theories I then accepted from an AI system.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

A colleague brought me a community and a theory that already explained it.

Names and selected contextual details have been removed or generalised, because I do not know whether he intended the conversation to extend beyond us. The mechanisms relevant to the analysis are retained.

Situation

A colleague who leads an Islamic institution contacted me and asked whether we could speak by telephone. He described more than one thousand people living amid overlapping forms of contestation: competing interpretations of the community's identity, rivalries among religious figures, business interests, family clans, religious organisations, boarding schools and places of worship, and a social division between those considered inside the community and those located outside it.

Complication

He had already begun considering theories that might explain the conflict. Some appeared immediately plausible; they gave language to unequal power and dependency. An established social theory came readily to mind for me as well, and I found myself beginning to organise his description around it.

Question

The explanation was coherent and supported by a respected body of scholarship. Was that sufficient reason to build on it — and if not, what has to happen first?

Answer

I set the theory aside and returned to what he had actually described. What arrived as one conflict in one community separated into several distinct mechanisms, each operating at a different analytical level and each requiring different evidence.

Gap

The theory was persuasive because I had begun selecting the details that supported it.

The problem was not that the theory lacked value. It was that I had moved too quickly from hearing the account to arranging it within a framework.

Returning to his account changed the direction of my thinking. Other details remained present in my notes, but I had not yet given them equal attention. My analytical habits were already influencing what I considered important.

That recognition made me more cautious. I was attracted to an elegant explanation before I had earned the right to use it. Setting the theory aside changed the question: rather than asking which theory best explained the community, I asked what my colleague had actually observed.

Core Gap

His account concerned identity, religious authority, family influence, educational institutions, business activity and community membership. "These were descriptions of people, relationships and events. They were not yet explanations."

The Symmetrical Error

After resisting one theory, I could easily create another total explanation in which identity, religion, kinship and business were all expressions of a single local system. "Coexistence was not proof of causation." The evidence did not yet justify that conclusion either.

Framework

Observations, reported relationships and causal interpretations are three different kinds of statement.

I began sorting the account mentally into three categories. The third required the greatest caution, because causal explanations often enter a conversation in grammatical forms that make them sound like observations.

01
Category one
Observable arrangements
Particular institutions existed. Businesses used common inputs. People distinguished among social groups. These can be checked, and they carry the least interpretive load.
02
Category two
Reported relationships
Certain actors were said to cooperate, compete or exclude. The relationship is asserted by someone. Whether my colleague observed it directly, inferred it from events, or received it from another person's interpretation is itself a question about the evidence.
03
Category three
Causal interpretations
One group acted in a particular way because of identity, religion, family interest or commercial advantage. "Each statement may be accurate, but the motive itself is not directly observable."
04
The discipline
Ask what would disconfirm it
"I noticed how easily I converted reported motives into analytical facts when they fitted a coherent explanation." So I asked what evidence would disconfirm each emerging mechanism. Those questions did not prove any mechanism; they prevented me from treating explanatory convenience as evidence.

Details

One story about one community broke into seven mechanisms that do not belong together.

The conflict had initially arrived as one story. Once I stopped treating it as a single problem, its components no longer appeared to belong naturally together.

1  ·  Boundary production and maintenance
To say people were divided by identity does not explain how the division was produced, who maintained it, or what followed from it. What qualified someone as an insider? Who had the authority to recognise that status? Could the boundary be crossed, or was it inherited?
2  ·  Social closure
Identity concerns how people understand themselves and others; closure concerns how a recognised group restricts access to positions, resources or standing. A person could be recognised as culturally connected to the community while remaining excluded from its institutional or commercial centres.
3  ·  Religious legitimacy
Religious authority is not identical to formal office. A doctrinal disagreement, a contest for followers, a struggle between institutions, and a dispute over who may represent the community are four different mechanisms. "Each mechanism required different evidence."
4  ·  Institutional competition
A pesantren can also function as a religious centre, a family institution, an employer, a landholder, an alumni network and a source of public authority. An institution could continue competing after the original personal disagreement had receded, because its need for students, income, staff and continuity gives the conflict an organisational life of its own.
5  ·  Kinship as a channel
A conflict within a family and a conflict organised through family networks are not the same thing. Where authority, property or institutional leadership passes through families, disagreement over a person may also be disagreement over continuity. "This did not make kinship the master explanation. It made kinship one channel through which several mechanisms could operate."
6  ·  Resource dependence on fabric
Most enterprises operated within the same broad industry and competed for materials, labour, designs, customers and overlapping sales channels. Fabric could not be treated merely as a product. The relevant issue was who could secure the required material, at what price, in what quantity and under what conditions.
7  ·  Supplier concentration and value-chain power
Many similar producers competed horizontally and remained fragmented, while a smaller group of larger businesses held stronger vertical positions through access to critical inputs. "The intensity of competition among producers did not mean that they possessed equivalent power within the supply structure."

Evidence

Supplier concentration is the defensible description; monopoly would require market shares he did not have.

Where the account supported a weaker claim than the one offered, I recorded the weaker claim. Each of these is a boundary I drew during the conversation, not a finding.

BOUNDARY A
Monopoly was not established
"My colleague had described the larger businesses as monopolising the supply of fabric. I could not adopt that classification from the number of firms alone." Monopoly or oligopoly would require evidence about market shares, purchasing volumes, barriers to entry, alternative suppliers and pricing power. Supplier concentration was the more defensible description at this stage (Cabral, 2017).
BOUNDARY B
Structural advantage is not deliberate exclusion
Stronger factory access might arise from purchasing scale, established commercial relationships, credit arrangements, family networks or exclusive agreements. "Large purchasing volumes could create an advantage without proving that the larger firms had actively closed access to others." Social closure would require evidence that access was being restricted, not evidence of unequal capacity alone.
BOUNDARY C
Specialisation is not proof of lock-in
Martin and Sunley (2006) caution against treating industrial concentration as sufficient evidence of lock-in. Evidence would be needed that earlier choices and accumulated capabilities constrained later alternatives. "Lock-in therefore became a proposition to investigate rather than a conclusion that could be inferred from specialisation alone."
BOUNDARY D
The prisoner's dilemma was set aside
Price cutting and design imitation briefly suggested a game-theoretic interpretation. The account did not establish identifiable strategic choices, expected responses, payoffs or a sufficiently specified pattern of repeated interaction. "Calling the behaviour a prisoner's dilemma would have supplied a familiar label without establishing the mechanism."

Implications

The first theory was not wrong; it had been assigned too much explanatory work.

A world-systems interpretation operates at a broad structural level. Once I distinguished the macro context from the mechanisms operating among local actors, it acquired a more limited and defensible role.

What One Grand Theory Cannot Do
✗Explain why particular religious leaders compete
✗Explain how an insider–outsider boundary is maintained
✗Explain why one local producer obtains better access to fabric
✗Move between individual, institutional and macro levels without an explicit analytical step
What Mechanism-Matched Theory Does
✓Boundaries and closure: Lamont & Molnár (2002); Weber (1978); Parkin (1979)
✓Contests over position and legitimacy: Fligstein & McAdam (2012)
✓Vulnerability to controlled inputs: Pfeffer & Salancik (1978)
✓Coordination and power across production stages: Gereffi et al. (2005)
✓Macro context, held at its own level: Wallerstein (1974)
The Restricted Version That Survives

A peripheral position within a wider economic and political structure might restrict access to capital, markets, institutional mobility and external opportunities, with local actors then competing more intensely over what remains available inside the community. World-systems theory could frame that external constraint — but a meso-level theory would still be needed to explain how the constraint became rivalry among local actors.

Conclusion

My diagnostic discipline was stronger than my discipline in moving from mechanisms to theories.

I had departed from my own Theory-Tiered Literature Review methodology and did not notice until I reviewed the conversation. The framework produced should therefore be treated as provisional: it identifies mechanisms and associates them with candidate theories. It does not establish a theoretical architecture.

1
Clarify the observations before accepting any explanation
Separate what was observed from what was reported and from what was interpreted. The client's theory is a hypothesis he brought, not a finding he made.
2
Identify the mechanisms and fix their analytical levels
An individual leader's conduct does not establish the conduct of an institution. A family relationship does not establish a community norm. A local pattern of supplier dependence does not establish a centre–periphery relationship. Each movement between levels requires an explicit step.
3
Construct the corpus the mechanisms define
Fifty or sixty studies on the community, religious authority, textile production, social boundaries, institutional rivalry, resource dependence and regional economic development — read comparatively, not sampled for fit.
4
Tier the theories and test the architecture
Separate grand theories from middle-range explanations and low-range contextual constructs, trace their genealogy, and run cross-theory synthesis and audit loops before treating any match as established.
5
Treat AI-proposed theories as candidates, not as results
"The conversation accelerated theory generation, but it also showed where methodological discipline still had to begin." Apparent explanatory fit and position within a theoretical architecture are different claims.

The next time someone asks me to explain a community, I suspect I will spend even longer deciding what the problem actually is.
