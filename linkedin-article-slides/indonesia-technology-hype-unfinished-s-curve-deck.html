Indonesia's Technology Hype Culture: The Unfinished S-Curve

Indonesia keeps adding technological commitments faster than it accounts for the capability earlier ones built. The unfinished S-curve is a testable hypothesis about interrupted institutional learning, not exhausted technology: the five-sector audit finds real achievements alongside unresolved ambitions, and completion should mean an accountable record of retained capability before the next beginning is celebrated.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Indonesia keeps adding technological commitments while the capability accumulating beneath them goes largely unaccounted for.

"The problem is the accumulation of technological commitments without a corresponding account of what institutions have learned to do."

Situation

"A new strategy, laboratory, application or demonstration can make institutional activity visible immediately."

Complication

"The ability to maintain a production system, improve its performance and develop the next generation of technology takes longer to establish."

Question

Do new commitments extend earlier learning, or begin again before earlier initiatives produced sustained operational competence?

Answer

"This is the unfinished S-curve: a hypothesis about interrupted capability development, not a claim that Indonesia has invented nothing or that every technology must reach its ultimate limit before another can be adopted."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

Each global wave since 1990 drew Indonesian roadmaps, platforms and prototypes, but a chronology cannot show whether earlier work was completed or displaced.

Twenty chronology windows pair each global wave with a documented Indonesian response, from globalisation to agent harnesses.

"These activities translated global technological possibilities into local promises, commitments and expectations." The chronology "does not establish whether earlier initiatives were completed, abandoned or developed into lasting capabilities." Cloud can support fintech and industrial data can support AI; successful integration would make the expanding list evidence of cumulative development.

Real achievements constrain the argument. OECD (2025) identifies Indonesia as Southeast Asia’s second-largest start-up hub after Singapore while describing fragmented support. "Commercial activity and institutional coordination problems can coexist."

What the table is not

"The initiatives are documented examples, not a census of Indonesian activity or proof that each wave generated nationwide hype." The author’s own experiences "do not constitute a representative measurement of Indonesian institutions."

Framework

Three different curves hide inside the S-curve, and buying a system can raise adoption while leaving operational capability almost unchanged.

"These have different units, mechanisms and relevant populations."

01
Technology
Performance curve
Improvement in a specified technical measure as development effort accumulates.
02
Market
Diffusion curve
The cumulative share of potential users adopting a technology over time.
03
Institution
Capability curve
"the ability to deploy, maintain and improve a system and obtain useful outcomes." Netland and Ferdows (2016) tied Volvo’s lean gains to implementation maturity, not elapsed time.
What finishing means

"Completion is accountable closure of a commitment or phase, not cessation of innovation." A research project might finish "with validated negative findings that prevent further waste."

Waiting is not the rule

Adner and Kapoor (2016) show ecosystems shape substitution. "Continuing an obsolete project merely to say it was completed can destroy value".

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Evidence

The five Making Indonesia 4.0 sectors show continuing output and exports, yet no available measure settles any sector’s original ambition.

"It is a bounded evidence audit, not a national impact evaluation." The strategy runs to 2030.

1  ·  Food and beverages
6.15% year-on-year growth in Q2 2025. "This is quarterly sector growth, not productivity growth." ASEAN leadership is not established.
2  ·  Textiles and apparel
Sritex stopped operating on 1 March 2025, while apparel exports rose from US$7.08 billion to US$7.27 billion. The two "cannot cancel one another."
3  ·  Automotive
518,212 built-up vehicle exports in 2025 against 472,194 in 2024. Export units do not measure design authority or value retained.
4  ·  Electronics
Polytron reports domestic R&D across design and prototyping, contradicting an assembly-only claim; it "is not an independently audited sector-wide capability series".
5  ·  Chemicals
LOTTE’s Cilegon complex, 1 million tonnes of ethylene capacity. "Nameplate capacity is not realized output, and petrochemical investment does not establish biochemical capability."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Evidence

Cases of operational upgrading, such as a 44% cut in downtime at Batam, narrow the question to how widely and durably improvement spreads.

"Indonesia contains functioning manufacturing, technical capability and instances of operational improvement."

AVEVA’s account of Schneider Electric’s Batam factory reports a 44% reduction in downtime: "a supplier-associated case report, not an independently replicated national estimate", but an operational outcome rather than an announcement. The INDI 4.0 readiness framework already exists and "should be interrogated and connected to outcomes".

Nickel separates location from control. OECD (2026) reports domestic-company control of less than 10% of production, from a 2024 ownership source that "should not be presented as though it were a fresh 2025 census." "To show that new priorities displaced old ones would require resource and decision records."

Absence of evidence

"the absence of a complete longitudinal evaluation in this evidence set is not proof that no such evaluation exists anywhere."

Details

Data centres, agent harnesses and humanoid robots deepen an existing curve only when capability is built around them.

"A new name can mark deeper integration of an existing system; a striking new machine can remain an isolated demonstration."

Deepens the curve
✓A harness that connects authorised records, logs actions and requires human approval, measured against the previous process
✓"The surrounding system must enforce the rule before an action occurs."
✓Imported robots used to build the ability "to diagnose, adapt and maintain the deployment"
✓Data-centre operators sustaining reliable service while Indonesian engineers acquire deeper responsibilities
Only looks new
✗"Renaming the initiative “agentic” without those changes would provide no corresponding evidence of progress."
✗"A claim of autonomy cannot replace a definition of acceptable performance."
✗A humanoid demonstration, which does not establish throughput, recovery or cost per completed task. "Human shape is not an industrial outcome."
✗Treating a campus "potentially scalable to 500 MW" as operating capacity

Gap

Repeated beginnings may be attractive because a launch earns recognition within a reporting period while maintenance earns little.

Bromley and Powell (2012) separate policy–practice decoupling from means–ends decoupling, where activities occur but their link to outcomes stays uncertain.

"Leaders can display a launch within a reporting period. Maintenance, integration and incremental correction may consume resources without producing an equally conspicuous event." A new initiative competes for the same technical staff and managerial time, and its opportunity cost may stay hidden when each program is reported separately.

Competing explanations remain: genuine shifts in demand, supply constraints, financing, or a firm rationally stopping a failed pilot. "Calling the problem “culture” should direct attention to repeatable institutional practices that can change, rather than an assumed permanent characteristic of Indonesians."

Status of the mechanism

"These are mechanisms to investigate, not motives established for particular officials or institutions."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Implications

Completion should leave an account of retained capability, from the baseline before launch to the system’s survival beyond its sponsor.

"An institution does not have to complete every experiment successfully. It does have to explain what happened to each material commitment."

1  ·  Before launch
The problem, the performance baseline, the beneficiaries, the intervention and the conditions for continuing or stopping.
2  ·  During implementation
Outcome measures that fit the work: defect rates and downtime, completed user tasks, or cost per verified AI result. Installations and users cannot substitute.
3  ·  Beyond the sponsor
"Can another competent team operate the system?" Are procedures documented, failures diagnosable locally and maintenance funded?
4  ·  At the next proposal
State whether it extends, replaces, explores or addresses a different problem, and what happens to earlier work.
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

Technological enthusiasm becomes cumulative capability only when each new beginning can show what earlier work now reliably does.

"The five-sector evidence does not sustain the claim that Indonesia has accomplished nothing. It reveals real production, instances of technical competence, serious losses and objectives that the available measures do not yet resolve."

Before celebrating the next technological beginning, an institution should be able to show what earlier work can now reliably do, what knowledge it has retained, and why the next investment will improve on it.
