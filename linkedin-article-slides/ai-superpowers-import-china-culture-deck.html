AI Superpowers: Can Indonesia Import China’s Technology Without Its Entrepreneurial Culture?

Lee credits China’s AI rise partly to entrepreneurs hardened by intense competition. Indonesia can import the artifacts of that success, its infrastructure and institutions, but seven years of Indonesian evidence show that cultural feedback constrains entrepreneurial persistence, so the test of any intervention is whether founders persist after support ends.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Indonesia can import China’s AI infrastructure, but Lee’s account does not show that the entrepreneurial culture behind it travels with the technology.

Revisiting Kai-Fu Lee’s AI Superpowers (2018) through doctoral research on entrepreneurial formation in Indonesia.

Situation

Lee "explains how China developed the entrepreneurial and technological capabilities to challenge American dominance in artificial intelligence."

Complication

Indonesian doctoral research finds that institutional support, funding and programs can stimulate entrepreneurial activity "while cultural feedback mechanisms constrain its persistence."

Question

"can a country reproduce the achievements of an innovation-driven economy by importing its technology and institutions without developing the shared values that sustain entrepreneurial behavior?"

Answer

"establishing innovation institutions does not necessarily produce a sustained entrepreneurial ecosystem."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

Lee traces China’s competitive edge to entrepreneurs forged by imitation, aggressive rivals and demanding consumers.

Chapter 2, Copycats in the Coliseum.

Companies confronted rapid imitation, aggressive competitors, demanding consumers and continuous pressure to improve. "Surviving entrepreneurs developed capabilities in commercial experimentation, adaptation, and execution." Lee connects these with China’s digital consumer market, data, engineering talent and government support.

"It does not establish that another country can reproduce those entrepreneurial capabilities by copying China’s technological infrastructure and institutional arrangements."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Evidence

Seven years of Indonesian evidence show founders abandoning ventures when secure jobs appear, as cultural feedback limits persistence.

Autoethnography, thematic analysis and system-dynamics modelling, 2018–2024.

FINDING A
Scope of the record
"16 autoethnographic reports, 16 accelerator runs, and experiences involving more than 500 founders and students."
FINDING B
Seven causal-loop domains
From innovation-driven entrepreneurship students to entrepreneurial culture and mental models; "two exogenous and five endogenous domains."
FINDING C
Founder withdrawal
Students developed concepts and showed potential, "yet abandon their ventures when secure employment opportunities emerged."
FINDING D
Cultural dispositions
"wedi (fear), isin (shame), sungkan (social reluctance), gengsi (status concerns), and nrimo (acceptance)." "These concepts are context-dependent".
Two time scales not to confuse

The 180-month perception delay "was a modelling parameter used to examine delayed adjustment, not an empirical finding that Indonesian culture necessarily changes within exactly 15 years." The 50–75 year proposition on shared values "should not be confused with the model’s 180-month parameter."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

Schein’s three levels explain why importing artifacts can leave the assumptions governing entrepreneurial decisions untouched.

Schein (2010) distinguishes "artifacts, espoused beliefs and values, and basic underlying assumptions."

01
Level 1
Artifacts
Visible structures: "technology parks, research laboratories, startup accelerators, university entrepreneurship programs, and AI infrastructure."
02
Level 2
Espoused beliefs and values
"what institutions publicly declare important." Governments promote innovation; universities endorse entrepreneurship.
03
Level 3
Basic underlying assumptions
Beliefs a group treats as normal. "They influence behavior even when actual decisions contradict publicly declared values."
The contradiction in practice

"A family may admire successful entrepreneurs while advising its children against uncertain entrepreneurial careers."

Scope of the extension

"Schein developed his framework for organizational culture. Its extension to national culture requires examining how expectations are reproduced through families, educational institutions, workplaces, and successive generations."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Gap

Interventions run on academic, investment and political cycles, while the expectations behind entrepreneurial choices persist across generations.

"Increased participation during an intervention therefore does not establish that entrepreneurial formation will continue after institutional support ends."

"An accelerator may operate for one academic year, a corporation may evaluate its investments within several years, and government programs may follow political cycles." Importing AI infrastructure changes the visible environment and innovation centres change institutional arrangements. "Neither intervention necessarily changes the underlying assumptions influencing entrepreneurial behavior."

"This does not establish that Indonesia and China possess uniform national cultures or that their cultural differences alone explain their technological outcomes. A comparative investigation would be necessary to test those propositions."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Implications

Indonesia should measure whether entrepreneurial persistence outlasts sponsorship, not how many centres and participants it funds.

Counts of research centres, accelerator participants, training programs and funded startups measure institutional activity. "Such indicators do not establish whether entrepreneurial capabilities persist."

1  ·  After sponsorship
Whether accelerator graduates continue developing ventures once sponsorship ends.
2  ·  After failure
Whether unsuccessful founders attempt new ventures.
3  ·  Incentives
Whether universities and employers change the incentives governing independent initiative.
4  ·  Institutional consistency
"An organization that declares innovation important but penalizes responsible experimentation has not aligned its practices with its stated values."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

Sustained AI capability depends partly on the shared values Indonesia reproduces, not on the artifacts it imports.

Whether investments produce sustained capability "depends partly on the shared values reproduced through families, educational institutions, corporations, and public institutions."

Can Indonesia develop the shared values required for sustained innovation-driven entrepreneurship, rather than repeatedly importing the artifacts of societies that already possess those capabilities?

— Dr. Toronata Tambun, AI Superpowers
What Indonesia needs
"Indonesia does not need to adopt China’s particular culture. It needs shared values compatible with sustained innovation-driven entrepreneurship within its own social circumstances."
