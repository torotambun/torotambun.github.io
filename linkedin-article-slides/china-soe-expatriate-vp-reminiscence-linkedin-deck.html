Reminiscence as an Expatriate VP in a China State Owned Company :

The author was invited as a guest speaker at IPMI to tell MBA students about his 3 years as an expatriate VP in a China state owned company. After the class explored theories on what Chinese companies want from international deals and on why China goes global, and frameworks on the cross-cultural determinants of Chinese employees and on China's national cultural values, he offered a meta-analysis: harmony is the utmost priority and a shared mind is the prerequisite of guanxi, leaders are expected to command by interpretation and to achieve without doing, and he disagrees with accepting a good result no matter which way. In his case corporate culture derived from the leader's personality, family values and previous company subdued geopolitical motives, face saving is not only Chinese, and understanding Chinese characters helps to understand Chinese values.

Dr. Toronata Tambun

Opening

A guest lecture at IPMI led the author to a meta-analysis of his three years as an expatriate VP in a China state owned company.

Situation

The author was invited as a guest speaker at IPMI to share with its MBA students his experience working as an expatriate VP in a China state owned company. A pre-reading on the China energy industry (McFarlan, Baroutas and Manty, 2009) was provided before the class.

Complication

After the author elaborated the milestones of his 3 years of service and the astonishing results delivered in the end despite all the dynamics, the class explored theories on what Chinese companies want from international deals (Ma et al., 2015) and on why China goes global (Enright, 2005). A class interaction then related his experience to frameworks on the cross-cultural determinants of Chinese employees (Jaw, 2006) and on China's national cultural values, explored through Hofstede's Country Comparison Tool.

Question

What did the author conclude from relating his experience to those theories and frameworks?

Answer

A meta-analysis in the form of a list of observations, on harmony and guanxi, on how leaders lead, on corporate culture, on face saving and on Chinese characters.

Evidence note

The article names no company, gives no date for the session and gives no figures for the results it calls astonishing. It names the Country Comparison Tool without an author. The attribution to Hofstede was supplied by the author when this deck was prepared and is not part of the 2017 text.

The Turns

Harmony is the utmost priority, and a shared mind and shared thinking are the prerequisite of guanxi.

These are the first two observations in the author's meta-analysis.

01
Observation
Harmony first
The author says being in harmony is the utmost priority. The article backs this with a saying from the Analects, cited to Wei, Li, p. 61, that a superior person seeks harmony and not sameness, while a petty person seeks sameness and not harmony (paraphrased).
02
Observation
Same mind, same thinking
The author says that having the same mind and the same thinking is the prerequisite of guanxi, and that together they are its key ingredients.

Field Notes

Leaders are expected to command by interpretation and to achieve without doing, while the author disagrees with accepting a good result no matter which way.

What the article says leaders are expected to do
•Command is expected to be elaborated by way of interpretation
•Cleary, p. 310, is cited for a chain that runs from ruling through finding winners, strength, using the power of others and winning hearts, to self-mastery and flexibility (paraphrased)
•Leaders aim to achieve something without doing anything
•Cleary, p. 330, and Sinolingua, p. 105, are cited for managing affairs without contrivance, instructing without speaking and teaching by saying nothing (paraphrased)
What the author does not tend to agree with
✗A case-by-case practice of accepting a good result no matter which way
✗Bojun, p. 31, is cited for a saying that haste undermines thoroughness and that fixing on small advantages keeps great affairs from being completed (paraphrased)
Evidence note

The article does not say whether leaders in these observations means Chinese leaders, the leaders in the author's company or leaders in general. The deck says leaders and attributes nothing further.
