The Electric Motorcycle Is Not a Transition

The electric motorcycle delivers a real machine-level efficiency gain and then disappears inside a fleet where it is a quarter of one percent. A working machine is being asked to carry claims about climate, fuel sovereignty and industrial renewal that the policy has not earned.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus · En-ROADS Climate Simulation Ambassador
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

The vehicle works. That is the point at which the national claim begins to fail.

A conclusion I did not expect, and one that changes which number the policy should be judged by.

Situation

On the grid Indonesia actually has, an electric motorcycle emits about 26 grams of carbon dioxide per kilometre against about 44 for the petrol machine it replaces. The advantage survives Indonesian coal because it comes from the drivetrain, not the fuel.

Complication

Then the national arithmetic empties the victory. Indonesians registered about 6.5 million new motorcycles last year and 99.5 percent burned petrol. Roughly 196,000 electric motorcycles were on the road in June 2025 inside a fleet above 120 million — a quarter of one percent. At current sales, Indonesian showrooms replace the entire electric fleet in about eleven days.

Question

If the machine works but the deployment rate empties the result, what is this policy actually for — and by which number should it be judged?

Answer

Name the objective and publish the number that measures it, shift attention from fleet headlines to the 6.5 million new sales a year, and tie every incentive to contracted clean generation and an industrial conversion plan in the same document.

Gap

A real gain, asked to carry claims it has not earned

Three different objectives — climate, fuel sovereignty, industrial renewal — are being measured with one instrument and reported with none of their own numbers.

I did not expect this conclusion. For two years I have argued in these pages against overclaiming for electrification, and I assumed that on a grid which is 82 percent fossil the climate case for electric two-wheelers would be thin. It is not. A small combustion engine turns about a fifth of its tank into motion; an electric drivetrain manages around 85 percent, and that difference is large enough to survive Indonesian coal.

The government's stronger case is fuel sovereignty. Electricity can be dirty and still domestic. Coal is ours, and each litre displaced is a litre Indonesia no longer buys from someone else's refinery at someone else's price. But that argument does not rescue the policy — it only changes the test.

Core Gap

Unit subsidies report administrative activity, which can rise while the real objective remains untouched. Carbon policy should report tonnes avoided; fuel sovereignty should report barrels not imported; industrial policy should report local value added, converted suppliers and workers moved into real production. Indonesia is publishing none of the three.

Aimed at the Smaller Target

If fuel sovereignty is the goal, the motorcycle is central — two-wheelers are 82 percent of the 173 million vehicles on the roads and they burn the imported gasoline that dominates the import bill. Yet policy has been shaped around cars: battery plants, local content rules, four-wheel tax incentives and summit slides about assembly capacity. The vehicle that can move the import bill received a part-price subsidy and a task force.

Framework

Four claims, four different verdicts

Each stands or falls on its own evidence. Collapsing them into one slogan is how the policy escapes measurement.

01
Climate
True, and nearly irrelevant at this scale
The eighteen-gram saving per kilometre is real and survives a dirty grid. At a quarter of one percent of the fleet it cannot move national emissions — and it shrinks further when the marginal kilowatt hour comes from coal.
02
Fuel sovereignty
Stronger, and pointed at the wrong vehicle
Around 120 million machines at a modest ten thousand kilometres a year burn something like twenty billion litres annually — on the order of a third of the shortfall Indonesia imports. Nothing else in the transport system can displace imported gasoline at that scale.
03
Industry
A change of supply chain, not an upgrade
The existing motorcycle economy was built around combustion engines, Japanese manufacturers, local assembly, component suppliers, dealers and repair workshops. Electric motorcycles shift value toward batteries, controllers, software and cell chemistry, where Chinese firms are stronger.
04
Labour
The loop nobody has costed
When factories do not absorb workers, platform transport often does. More full-time platform work means more motorcycles, more kilometres and more electricity demand. Without clean power, a weak manufacturing base feeds platform transport, and platform transport feeds coal demand.

Details

The average is not the number that matters

Four pieces of arithmetic that decide whether the policy does what is claimed for it.

1  ·  Where the advantage actually comes from
A measured Indonesian comparison puts an electric two-wheeler at 3.33 kilowatt hours per hundred kilometres — roughly 23 grams per kilometre, or 26 after charging and transmission losses. A petrol machine at a generous 60.6 kilometres per litre produces about 38 grams at the exhaust and about 44 by the time the fuel has reached the pump. The gain is the drivetrain, not the grid.
2  ·  Average demand versus marginal demand
The 680 grams per kilowatt hour figure is an average across everything the system produced, including hydro and geothermal. A new electric motorcycle does not draw the average; it adds demand, and added demand is met by the plant that runs next. In Indonesia that plant usually burns coal. Coal generation did not fall in 2024 — it rose by 12 terawatt hours.
3  ·  The import bill the policy is meant to move
Indonesia produces around 582,000 barrels of crude a day and consumes 1.7 million. Refined gasoline alone costs 21.3 billion US dollars a year, close to 60 percent of the entire oil and gas import bill, and fuel imports have risen 37 percent in five years.
4  ·  The creditor changes, the dependence does not
Fuel sovereignty weakens when cell chemistry, battery management software and manufacturing equipment remain imported. A policy justified by energy security should be measured by domestic energy used — and if each switched machine strengthens the politics around coal, Indonesia builds support for the system it later needs to retire, then describes the build-out as a transition.

Evidence

The numbers on the record

Four sets of figures, each of which can be checked against a published source.

EVIDENCE A
The emissions comparison
Indonesia's grid emission factor in 2024 was 680 grams of carbon dioxide per kilowatt hour, against an Asia-Pacific average of 559. Even so, the International Council on Clean Transportation reached the same conclusion for Indonesia: battery electric has the lowest life-cycle emissions in every segment here, including two-wheelers.
EVIDENCE B
The deployment rate
About 32,000 of the 6.5 million motorcycles registered last year were electric — roughly one for every two hundred petrol machines sold. The Ministry of Industry counted about 196,000 electric motorcycles on the road in June 2025, inside a fleet the Asian Development Bank puts above 120 million, of which perhaps 75 million are genuinely still running.
EVIDENCE C
The mechanism, stated plainly
En-ROADS states it directly: electrification raises primary energy demand for coal when rising electric demand is met by coal-fired generation. The saving of eighteen grams a kilometre is real, but it shrinks when new electricity demand is met the way Indonesia met last year's.
EVIDENCE D
The industrial base underneath
Manufacturing's share of Indonesia's economy was 22.1 percent in 2000, fell to around 18 percent in 2013 and now sits near 19.1 percent. A petrol motorcycle is not only a machine — it is a production chain, a repair chain, a financing chain and a labour market.
The Honest Summary

Electric motorcycles deliver a real efficiency gain on today's grid, then disappear inside a fleet where they account for a quarter of one percent. For energy security they solve a larger problem, while policy points them toward a deployment rate near one machine in four hundred. The bottleneck is deployment — but deployment without industrial conversion can create a different dependence.

Implications

Every objective has a number. None of them is units subsidised.

A policy that reports the wrong measure can succeed on paper for a decade while the objective goes untouched.

What Is Being Reported
✗Unit subsidies disbursed and task forces convened
✗Fleet share, rather than share of new sales
✗The grid average, rather than the marginal plant
✗Four-wheel assembly capacity and local content rules
What Should Be Reported
✓Tonnes avoided, for a carbon objective
✓Barrels not imported, for a sovereignty objective
✓Local value added, converted suppliers, workers moved
✓Contracted clean generation attached to each incentive
Where The Stock Is Created

Indonesia sells 6.5 million new motorcycles each year, almost all on credit. That is where emissions, fuel imports and long-term vehicle stock are created — and financing is where the state can intervene before petrol motorcycles enter the road for another decade.

Building What You Must Later Retire

If an electric motorcycle runs on Indonesian coal rather than imported petroleum, each switched machine strengthens the politics around coal: plants, mines, jobs and provincial revenue. Indonesia would build support for the system it later needs to retire, then describe the build-out as a transition.

Conclusion

Name the objective, publish its number, and attach the conditions.

The bottleneck is deployment, but deployment alone is not the policy. These measures make the claim testable.

1
Declare which objective the instrument serves
Carbon, fuel sovereignty and industrial policy are three different goals with three different measures. Publishing one number per objective would end the practice of defending an instrument with whichever claim is least embarrassed by the data.
2
Move from fleet headlines to the new-sales flow
The 6.5 million machines sold each year are where the next decade's emissions, imports and vehicle stock are decided. Almost all are bought on credit, which makes financing the point of leverage rather than the showroom price.
3
Tie every incentive to contracted clean generation
An incentive with no committed generation behind it converts imported petrol into coal-fired electricity. The generation contract and the incentive belong in the same policy document, with dates and delivery obligations.
4
Publish an industrial conversion plan alongside it
Engine plants, parts suppliers, mechanics, dealers and displaced workers need a named path. Accelerating electric motorcycles without one may reduce fuel imports while weakening the industrial base that should have absorbed those workers.
5
Measure the sovereignty claim by domestic content
If cell chemistry, battery management software and manufacturing equipment stay imported, the tank has changed but the creditor has not. A policy justified by energy security should report domestic energy and domestic value, not units sold.

The rider gets a quieter machine. The country still has dependence, coal demand and a weaker industrial base.
