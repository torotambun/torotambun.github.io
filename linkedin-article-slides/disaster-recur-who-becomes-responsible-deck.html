How Many Times Can a Disaster Recur Before Someone Becomes Responsible?

A no-blame investigation earns its justification only through the corrective action that follows. The 2026 haze shows that preventive commitments existed after earlier fires, but the available record does not show whether they were implemented or worked, so recurrence is a reason to investigate, and those responsible for prevention should publish findings, assigned actions, completion records and results.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

A no-blame culture is justified by the corrective action that follows an investigation, and the 2026 haze tests whether that action came.

At about 1 p.m. on 16 October 2026, five photographs of Singapore under haze; at 3 p.m. central Singapore’s 24-hour PSI reached 229, Very Unhealthy (CNA, 2026).

Situation

"When something went wrong, we were expected to establish what happened and why before assigning personal responsibility."

Complication

"I understood its justification to be the corrective action that followed: an investigation should change the conditions that allowed the incident to occur."

Question

Did that learning occur after previous fires, and who must account for the result?

Answer

Evaluate what the record establishes about prevention after earlier investigations, identify what remains unproven, and ask who must explain the outcome.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

Drought and drained peat made the fires foreseeable, but foreseeability alone cannot show whether a land manager met its duty.

Reuters reported severe El Niño heat and dryness; "removing water from peat makes it more vulnerable to fire" (Kopansky, 2017).

An inquiry "needs to examine how the land became vulnerable and how it was managed before the dry season." That requires the land-management requirements, warnings, water-management records, resources and evidence of response.

"Some fires may have exceeded reasonable containment capacity despite preventive work; others may expose omissions that were already known to be dangerous." In Indonesia, Reuters reported 283,270 respiratory illness cases linked to the fires across seven provinces as of 13 October.

What the photographs can show

"My photographs record reduced visibility; they cannot measure exposure or diagnose illness."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Gap

Pointing to the oxygen and other benefits of Indonesian forests, as officials did in 2015 and 2026, answers nothing about preventable fires.

Two separate remarks, eleven years apart, "both invoked forest benefits in response to complaints about smoke."

In 2015 a senior official suggested neighbours should be grateful for eleven months of forest benefits before seeking an apology, as recounted in Singapore’s National Day Rally. Reporting on 12 October 2026 described another official invoking the oxygen Indonesian forests supply.

"An answer about oxygen supplies no evidence that a concession holder met its obligations or that an earlier corrective measure worked. Accepting that answer would leave the decisions governing fire risk unexamined."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Evidence

Maps and notices identify concessions and companies that merit scrutiny, while none of them yet establishes who ignited a fire or breached a duty.

"The investigations identify places and relationships that merit scrutiny; responsibility depends on the duties, control and conduct established in each case."

RECORD A
Haze-source mapping
Greenpeace and Pantau Gambut (5 October) linked haze-source points to concessions associated with Malaysian and Singaporean interests; WALHI traced ownership behind hotspot-dense concessions.
RECORD B
What a hotspot is
A hotspot "represents a possible fire and may also arise from another high-temperature source." Hotspots, smoke-source points and burned areas are different observations.
RECORD C
Company responses
First Resources and Bumitama Agri cited severe dry conditions and fires spreading from outside their boundaries. "Those explanations also require testing against location-specific evidence."
RECORD D
Singapore’s notices
NEA issued Preventive Measure Notices to seven companies on 16 October and announced three more on 19 October. They "are preventive enforcement measures, not court findings that the recipients deliberately started fires."
The limit of a confirmed fire

"Even a confirmed fire inside a concession does not by itself identify who ignited it, establish negligent prevention, or prove responsibility at parent-company level."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

Preventive commitments have existed since 2002, but the record shows that they existed, not how consistently they were implemented or how much risk they cut.

"These records establish that preventive commitments and interventions existed; they do not establish how consistently they were implemented or how much risk they reduced."

2002
ASEAN haze agreement
Articles 7–9 address monitoring, assessment and prevention, including fire-prone areas and local fire management.
2015
Plans required of four companies
NEA required plans to extinguish fires or prevent recurrence: "an identifiable starting point for asking what was implemented, inspected and revised".
2016
Peatland restoration agency
Indonesia began rewetting drained areas (Kopansky, 2017).
2026
Emergency response
Personnel, aircraft and acceptance of foreign assistance, which "cannot establish whether earlier preventive obligations were fulfilled."
No verdict on 2015

"The sources examined here do not establish the subsequent performance of each plan, so I cannot conclude that those investigations either succeeded or failed to secure effective prevention."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Implications

An inquiry should trace each earlier finding through required action, implementation, enforcement and results under comparable conditions.

Reason (2000) separates individual errors from the conditions and safeguards around them; here it frames the inquiry and "supplies no finding about the causes of these fires".

1  ·  Earlier finding
"What risk or failure was identified, and at which location?"
2  ·  Required action
"Who had to act, by when, and with what authority and resources?"
3  ·  Implementation and enforcement
What inspections, water records, patrol logs and expenditure show, and whether sanctions and remediation followed.
4  ·  Results
"Did the action reduce fire occurrence, spread or severity under comparable conditions, and what changed after shortcomings emerged?"
A later fire is not proof of failure

"These are questions for an inquiry, not findings of non-compliance."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

Recurrence is a reason to investigate rather than a measure of guilt, and responsibility for reviewing safeguards exists before the next fire.

"My professional training makes me reluctant to accuse anyone before the evidence has been examined. But responsibility for implementing and reviewing agreed safeguards exists before the next disaster occurs."

I cannot specify how many recurring fires would make someone culpable, because recurrence is a reason to investigate, not a measure of guilt. I can ask the institutions responsible for prevention to publish the findings, assigned actions, completion records and results needed to judge whether they learned enough to reduce the next fire’s risk.

— Dr. Toronata Tambun, How Many Times Can a Disaster Recur?

"If the action was neglected, the inquiry concerns that omission; if it was completed but proved inadequate, the inquiry must examine its design, resources and revision."
