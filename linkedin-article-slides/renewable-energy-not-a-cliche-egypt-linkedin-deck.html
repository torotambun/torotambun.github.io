Renewable Energy : not a cliche

The article says solar power technology has increased dramatically and offers recent examples from Egypt, where the author assumes the regulatory body and business ecosystem overcame regulation and bureaucracy. In the second phase of Egypt's feed-in tariff solar project, 136 of 187 applicants were shortlisted and 10 investors offered to repay the costs of companies that invested earlier. The arrangements remind the author of cost recovery and risk and reward in Indonesia. From his oil and gas, nuclear engineering and MIT sustainability background he thinks oil and gas companies can enable a change of mind on the energy spectrum, and he names collaboration as one way forward.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus

Introduction

Solar power has improved dramatically, and Egypt has, the author assumes, overcome regulation and bureaucracy to reach a second feed-in tariff phase.

Situation

The article says solar power technology has increased dramatically, with some room for improvement still left. The author says he wishes to learn more about Indonesia's energy diversification journey plan for the next 25 years.

Complication

While learning, he offers recent examples from Egypt, where he assumes the regulatory body and the business ecosystem have managed to overcome regulation and bureaucracy to reach the stage they are at now.

Question

What does it take to diversify a country's energy mix toward renewables?

Answer

The article offers two things: an Egyptian example of a system that reached a second feed-in tariff phase, and the author's view that oil and gas companies can act as enablers, with collaboration as one way forward.

Evidence note

The article gives no source for the Egyptian account, and the author marks the explanation of Egypt's progress as his assumption.

Evidence

In Egypt's second feed-in tariff phase 136 of 187 applicants were shortlisted, and 10 investors offered to repay the costs of the companies that invested earlier.

The author says he believes the discussion on legislation was settled long ago, as Egypt is entering the second phase of the feed-in tariff solar energy project.

REPORTED A
The first phase
Egypt announced a few years ago 25-year power purchase agreements with 3 companies that were part of the first phase of the project.
REPORTED B
The second phase shortlist
136 of 187 applicants were shortlisted: 13 to provide solar plants under 20 MW, 87 to provide solar plants of 20 to 50 MW, and the remaining 36 to establish wind farms producing more than 20 MW each.
REPORTED C
The offer
10 investors from the US, China, France, the UAE and Egypt itself offer to pay the solar companies that invested earlier the costs those companies had paid to the New and Renewable Energy Authority and the Egyptian Electricity Transmission Company, which the author calls an equivalent of PLN.
REPORTED D
The amount
The calculation is based on how much revenue each company has achieved compared with how many permits it has obtained, and the amount ranges between 2 and 5 million USD.
Evidence note

The article gives no source for any Egyptian figure. It does not say whether the 2 to 5 million USD range applies per company.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

Each Egyptian arrangement reminds the author of an Indonesian concept, cost recovery and risk and reward, and a source in PLN told him the second is already implemented.

1  ·  Cost recovery
Investors offering to pay earlier investors their costs reminds the author of the cost recovery concept applied in Production Sharing Contract management in Indonesia.
2  ·  Risk and reward
The calculation from revenue against permits reminds him of a risk and reward concept in Indonesia. He says he learned recently from a reliable source in PLN that this concept has also been implemented there.
Evidence note

The article does not name the PLN source and gives no detail of how either concept operates in Indonesia. It states the parallels as reminders and not as findings.
