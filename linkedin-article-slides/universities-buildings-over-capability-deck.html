When Universities Prioritize Buildings Over Capability, the System Pays

The cost of choosing concrete over capability is not the construction bill. It is eight reinforcing loops that lock a university into permanent physical expansion and away from teaching, and that push out precarious students first. Freeing delivery from buildings removes the structure that hid teaching quality — after which the teacher, not the campus, is the differentiator.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

The cost is not the construction bill but the chain of burdens it starts.

A belief that is old, and costly in ways the budget line never shows.

Situation

"Indonesia still believes a university must be seen to be trusted. A gate, a hall, a tower, a parking lot. Without them, people assume the institution is not serious."

Complication

"This belief is old and costly. The problem is not construction expense. The problem is the chain of structural burdens created once a university chooses concrete over capability."

Question

If the money were available, would building still be the wrong move — and if so, what exactly does the concrete set in motion?

Answer

Eight self-reinforcing loops, each of which ends by demanding more construction. "Fixed assets create fixed obligations." Buildings lock the system into permanent enrollment pressure: more students require more facilities, more facilities raise fixed costs, and learning quality does not move while expansion continues.

The First Chain

The first group pushed out is precarious students, exactly where access matters most.

Before any of the loops close, one straight sequence runs from land clearing to exclusion.

"Physical expansion starts with land clearing. Trees go, gardens go, water control weakens. Flood risk rises. Heat climbs. Complaints increase. Political pressure follows. Compliance costs rise. Tuition follows."

"The first group pushed out is precarious students, so access drops exactly where it is most needed." The institution that built to look serious has made itself less reachable by the people it was arguably built for.

"Buildings attract cars. Cars demand parking. Parking demands more land. Land conversion repeats the ecological damage. Traffic grows. Local authorities impose controls. Controls increase cost." "The university becomes a physical-management institution instead of a teaching institution."

The Loops

Eight reinforcing loops, and every one of them ends by demanding more building.

"Then come the deeper loops." Each is stated as the article states it, and each closes on itself.

1  ·  Debt–Rigidity
"Construction drives long-term debt exposure. Debt forces revenue stabilization. Stabilization pushes conservative program choices. Risk-averse decisions weaken innovation. Weak innovation reduces differentiation. Low differentiation shrinks demand. Shrinking demand worsens the debt burden."
2  ·  Faculty Allocation
"Buildings create pressure for physical presence. On-site teaching is required to justify space. This cuts time for research and curriculum renewal. Weak renewal harms outcomes. Poor outcomes hurt reputation. Leadership responds by building again to signal strength. The loop resets."
3  ·  Maintenance–Deterioration
"Buildings age. Maintenance costs rise. Underfunded institutions delay repairs. Delays trigger deterioration. Deterioration weakens student satisfaction. Students demand upgrades. Upgrades require new capital. New capital restarts the maintenance burden a decade later."
4  ·  Scope-Creep
"Once facilities exist, leaders want to fill them. Empty halls look like failure. Departments open without relevance. More departments dilute focus. Dilution weakens quality. Weak quality erodes trust. Loss of trust triggers more superficial expansion. The institution drifts sideways."
5  ·  Regulatory Escalation
"Physical campuses trigger more regulatory categories. Categories bring audits. Audits require compliance staff. Compliance staff increases overhead. Overhead pressures tuition. Higher tuition excludes precarious students. Inequality rises. Government tightens regulation."
6  ·  Urban-Pressure
"A large campus concentrates people. Waste, noise, food demand, and security pressure increase. Zoning tightens. Universities rush to build before rules tighten further. Rushed construction intensifies ecological and social damage. Complaints rise. Goodwill falls."
7  ·  Cultural-Legitimacy Misalignment
"Parents trust buildings. Students want flexibility. This mismatch reduces engagement. Low engagement reduces outcomes. Poor outcomes push universities to build again to reassure parents. The system reinforces the wrong need."
8  ·  Digital Stagnation
"A physical-first model slows digital adoption. Slow adoption reduces digital competence. Low competence creates distrust of online pedagogy. Distrust slows digital investment. Weak digital capability increases dependence on physical expansion. The institution locks itself into analog logic."

Path Dependence

Concrete does not merely cost money; it creates path dependence.

Why the eight loops matter more than any one of their individual costs.

"These loops show why building universities the old way does not simply “cost money”. It shapes long-term behavior. It creates path dependence. It produces side effects that are expensive to unwind."

That is the difference between an expense and a commitment. An expense is settled once; a commitment reappears every budget cycle, and each reappearance narrows what the institution can still choose to become.

"Indonesia already struggles with heat, flooding, congestion, inequality, and weak learning outcomes. A concrete-heavy model amplifies all of them." The loops do not create new national problems so much as recruit the university into the existing ones.

The Shift

Freeing delivery from buildings removes what was hiding teaching quality.

"The Christensen shift enters here. The move toward disruptive learning is not about replacing classrooms with screens."

What the Campus Concealed
✗Campus size read as a proxy for institutional seriousness
✗Teaching quality invisible behind gates, halls and towers
✗Students choosing on facilities rather than on performance
✗Certification standing in for demonstrated competence
✗Weak teaching protected by the building it happens inside
What Is Exposed Without It
✓"When delivery is freed from buildings, the real differentiator becomes the teacher and the content."
✓"Strong teachers rise. Weak teachers do not."
✓"Students choose based on performance, not campus size."
✓"Word of mouth becomes the filter. Good content spreads. Poor content disappears."
✓"In an open, performance-based environment, people chase competence, not paper."
A claim the article marks as conditional

"People may chase badges today, but once a real learning movement forms, that behavior fades. When mastery produces results in work and life, the certificate becomes secondary. They study for understanding, not optics." The article states this as what would follow if such a movement forms, not as something already observed.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

Concrete builds obligation; capability builds opportunity.

The choice, stated in one line and given a horizon.

Concrete builds obligation. Capability builds opportunity. The choice decides the next twenty years.

— When Universities Prioritize Buildings Over Capability, the System Pays
Why the horizon is twenty years, not one budget
Because path dependence is measured in decades. A building committed to this year sets faculty allocation, debt service, maintenance and programme mix for a generation of students who have not yet applied.
What the argument does not claim
Not that classrooms should be replaced with screens, and not that construction is always wrong. The claim is narrower: that concrete chosen as a signal of seriousness buys obligation rather than capability, and that the obligation outlives the signal.
