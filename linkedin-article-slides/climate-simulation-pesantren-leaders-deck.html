LinkedIn Article  ·  Education · Pesantren as a Pool of Climate Leaders  ·  11 August 2023

Climate Change Simulation: Revealing the Noble and Potential Pool of Leaders in the Country

The article argues that pesantren, the Islamic boarding schools often taken to be conservative, are a source of the leaders a climate transition needs. Its evidence is an En-ROADS climate simulation workshop in which santris played eight stakeholder roles, three groups proposed pathways near 1.5 degrees and the government group targeted 2.0, together with four traits drawn from Gino's Rebel Talent: authenticity, curiosity, novelty and diversity. It links those traits to entrepreneurs and to environmental stewardship, lists nine espoused values that define the pool, and closes with sermon notes rooting stewardship in the Quran. The article words its case as potential, and it reports the workshop targets without participant counts or emissions figures.

Introduction

Pesantren are treated as conservative, and the article argues they are a source of climate leaders.

The article opens against a common view of pesantren. It describes them as institutions "often misunderstood as conservative and limiting", and says they "hold the key to nurturing a new generation of leaders uniquely equipped to tackle the challenges of the modern world".

Three claims are packed into the introduction: that pesantren education has real power, that it aligns with the Islamic perspective on climate change, and that it can cultivate leaders who will shape a sustainable future.

The evidence offered is an En-ROADS climate simulation workshop with pesantren students, a set of traits drawn from Gino's Rebel Talent, and a list of nine espoused values.

Details

A blame-free En-ROADS workshop let santris play eight stakeholder roles.

The article describes the workshop's method before it reports any outcome.

1  ·  Blame-free multi-solving
En-ROADS, by Climate Interactive and the MIT Sloan Sustainability Initiative, is described as dismissing the idea of a most righteous or most culpable group. It asks participants to adopt multi-solving and equity stakeholder engagement.
2  ·  Three guiding questions
The tool prompts "How does solving my problem help you solve yours?", then "Who needs to be here?", then a question about strengthening the connections between participants across silos, departments, industries and jurisdictions.
3  ·  Eight stakeholder sectors
Climate Hawk Justice, Industrialists and Manufacturers, Automobile Lobbyists, Conventional Energy, Technology and Innovation-Driven Startups, Agriculture, Forests and Animal Husbandry Industries, Renewable Industry, and Government as Policy Makers and Orchestrators.
4  ·  A glimpse of future roles
The division into sectors is described as a way to give the santris a glimpse of their potential future roles, each sector representing a critical stakeholder group.

Evidence

Three groups proposed pathways near 1.5 degrees while the government group targeted 2.0.

The article reports the workshop's outcome in two short passages, one for the ambitious groups and one for the policy makers.

Climate Hawk, Renewable and Technology groups
•Described as emerging with "particularly ambitious visions"
•Three groups proposed "extreme proposals that reach more or less 1.5 Degree without any hesitations"
•The article reads this as the pioneering and innovative spirit nurtured within pesantren
Government as policy makers
•Formulated a scheme that targets a 2.0-degree climate goal
•Reached it by incorporating diverse inputs and perspectives
•Described as embodying the equitable and multi-solving principles
•The article draws collaborative decision-making from this
Evidence note

In the extracted text the article gives the two targets and no participant counts, model settings or emissions figures. Images on several pages are not carried by the text extraction.

Framework

Authenticity, curiosity, novelty and diversity are the traits the article credits to pesantren.

Each trait is paired in the article with a passage from Gino (2018), Rebel Talent.

01
A legacy of authentic learning
Authenticity
Pesantren are called "dynamic centers of authentic learning" that blend traditional Islamic teachings with contemporary knowledge, giving students confidence to explore unfamiliar ground.
02
A catalyst for exploration
Curiosity
The article says pesantren "actively cultivate curiosity and critical thinking", so that students are encouraged to question, explore and seek answers.
03
Integrating old and new
Novelty
Far from resisting change, pesantren are said to integrate Islamic teachings with modern subjects, which sparks novel ideas and encourages students to challenge norms.
04
A confluence of perspectives
Diversity
Pesantren are said to have diverse student bodies, "providing a platform for different viewpoints to converge", which the article ties to empathy, collaboration and global citizenship.

Implications

The traits it observes in santris parallel those of entrepreneurs, and the article links both to stewardship.

The article says the traits observed in pesantren students, "open-mindedness, curiosity, and a balanced approach", parallel those of successful entrepreneurs.

It words the inference with a hedge: "This suggests that pesantren nurture traits that lead to innovative leadership in various fields, beyond traditional boundaries".

On the environmental side it says pesantren "uniquely equip students to become eco-conscious leaders", pointing to the exposure to both Islamic teachings and modern knowledge.

It traces its own link between entrepreneurship, education and environmental consciousness to an entrepreneurship bootcamp of the previous year, and calls the result "a twist of an unexpected connection between entrepreneurship, education, and environmental consciousness".

Evidence note

The trait claims rest on the workshop and on Gino's framework. The article reports no measurement of these traits among the santris.

Conclusion

The article presents the workshop as showing potential, and its appendix roots stewardship in the Quran.

The conclusion keeps to the language of potential, and the appendix adds a religious ground for the same stewardship claim.

The climate change simulation workshop exemplifies the potential of pesantren students to become leaders who prioritize collaboration, innovation, and responsible decision-making.

— Climate Change Simulation, 11 August 2023
How the article closes on the pool
The noble and potential pool of leaders is said to emerge "from the heart of pesantren education, ready to make a lasting impact on their communities and the world".
The appendix and its source
The appendix reproduces the author's notes from a sermon by Mr. Nur Munir on the Islamic perspective on climate change. The notes are credited to the speaker and are not the author's own argument.
Stewardship in the notes
The notes say "The Quran explicitly describes humans as stewards (khalifah) of the Earth", cite Surah Ar-Rum (30:41-42), and link moderation and accountability to environmental responsibility.
Unity beyond one faith
The notes add that "The Quran's call for unity transcends faith boundaries", and read it as matching the inclusive partnerships among students in pesantren.

Sources

The article carries a Further Reading list of three entries and reproduces five passages from Gino (2018). The Leutner et al. (2014) paper on the entrepreneurial personality is listed but not cited in the extracted text. En-ROADS is named with its developers and without a reference, and the sermon notes are credited to Mr. Nur Munir without a citation. Images, including one credited to @Nur Munir in the appendix, are not carried by the text extraction. One contextual note belongs with the Gino attribution for a reader coming to this deck later. Allegations of data fabrication in several of Francesca Gino's published papers were made public in June 2023, two months before this article appeared, and Harvard opened an investigation; the subsequent outcome should be checked against current sources rather than taken from here. The article itself makes no reference to any of this, and the deck reproduces its argument as written.
