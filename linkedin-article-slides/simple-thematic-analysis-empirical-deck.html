Simple Thematic Analysis - Empirical

Thematic analysis is normally applied to interview data. Applied instead to a researcher's own narrative, it is what separates autoethnography from reflection: a six-step coding procedure that makes the reading of one's own experience systematic, transparent and checkable, and that surfaces patterns the writer would not otherwise see.

Opening

Thematic analysis is for interview data, and this is an excerpt about using it on myself.

465 words, published as an excerpt of the author's own research proposal — the shortest article in this archive.

The piece announces exactly what it is in its first line: "This an excerpt of my research proposal Chapter III.3.1.4.4. Thematic Analysis". It is a methods section lifted out of a doctoral proposal and published on its own.

The starting point is the ordinary use of the method. Thematic analysis "is a commonly used method in qualitative research to identify patterns and themes within data. While frequently applied to interview data, it can also be effectively utilized in autoethnographic research."

And the object being analysed is unusual, which is the whole reason the excerpt exists. "Autoethnography involves introspective examination and analysis of one's identity and lived experiences to comprehend broader cultural phenomena." Interview data comes from other people. Here the data is the researcher.

The Claim

Autoethnography without a coding method is reflection, not research.

The argument for the combination is made twice, and both times in terms of rigour rather than convenience.

"By integrating thematic analysis into autoethnographic research, researchers can uncover recurring themes that are relevant to the research questions, providing a structured approach to interpreting data and identifying significant patterns within autoethnographic narratives" (Braun & Clarke, 2006; Leung, 2021; Vang et al., 2022).

What the method supplies is named precisely: "This method offers a systematic and transparent approach to understanding the underlying meanings and significant aspects of the researcher's experiences, thereby contributing to a more robust research methodology" (Kitto, 2023; Windhorst & Williams, 2015). Systematic and transparent are the two words doing the work — the first against arbitrariness, the second against a reader having to take the writer's word for it.

The claim is then put in its strongest form: the incorporation of thematic analysis "not only enhances the rigor of the study but also deepens the understanding by revealing underlying themes and patterns that may not be immediately apparent" (Braun & Clarke, 2006; Leung, 2021). Not merely a check on the writing — a way of finding what the writer had missed.

Why this matters beyond the proposal

Autoethnography is routinely dismissed as self-report. The answer offered here is procedural rather than defensive: apply an established coding method to the narrative and the findings become auditable by someone other than their author.

Details

Six steps run from familiarisation to a report that answers the research questions.

The procedure as the proposal sets it out, in the past tense of work already done on two documents.

1  ·  Familiarisation with the data
"The process began with familiarization with the data, where both documents were carefully read to understand the content comprehensively." Reading before coding, and reading whole.
2  ·  Generating initial codes
"This step was followed by generating initial codes, which involved identifying and noting down key points, phrases, or ideas that stood out in the documents."
3  ·  Searching for themes
"Next, the search for themes commenced by grouping related codes into potential themes." Codes are observations; themes are the groupings a reader could argue with.
4  ·  Reviewing themes
"Once these themes were established, a thorough review was conducted to ensure they accurately represented the data." The step that catches a theme the analyst wanted to find.
5  ·  Defining and naming themes
"Following this, each theme was clearly defined and named appropriately to reflect its essence." Naming is where a theme becomes portable and, therefore, contestable.
6  ·  Producing the report
"Finally, the process concluded with producing the report, which summarized the findings and linked them back to the research questions" (Clark & Braun, 2021; Merriam & Tisdell, 2016; Saldaña, 2021; Throne, 2019). The linkage back is what stops a theme list becoming an essay.

Conclusion

What the method adds is rigour, and what it reveals is patterns not immediately apparent.

The closing paragraph restates the case for the pairing and stops there.

The integration of thematic analysis into autoethnographic research provides a systematic and structured approach to analyzing narrative data, leading to a more comprehensive understanding of the recurring themes and patterns within the researcher's experiences.

— Simple Thematic Analysis - Empirical, 20 June 2024
The two things claimed
"This methodological combination enhances the rigor and depth of autoethnographic studies, offering valuable insights into personal narratives and contributing to the broader research landscape." Rigour and depth are separable — one is about how the work can be checked, the other about what it can find.
Where the procedure has since been used
Four weeks after this excerpt appeared, the author published an account of his proposal examination built on a thematic analysis of the recording script of the session. The method described here is the method applied there.
What the excerpt does not carry
No findings, no themes, no data. It is the methods chapter alone, published as a standalone note, and it makes no empirical claim of its own despite the word in its title.
