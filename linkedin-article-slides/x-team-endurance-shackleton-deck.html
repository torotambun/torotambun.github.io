X-Team in the "Epic Voyage of the Endurance" led by Ernest Shackleton: Toro's Case-study Note

Shackleton's 1914 expedition is the cleanest available demonstration of a 2007 leadership framework, because every one of its capabilities has a dated incident attached rather than an assertion. The pedagogical claim runs alongside it: sixteen hours of preparation and no PowerPoint produced three hours and forty minutes that nobody noticed passing, which is what a case discussion is supposed to do and rarely does.

Opening

Sixteen hours of preparation, no PowerPoint, and three hours forty that passed without notice.

MB5701 Entrepreneurial Leadership, SBM-ITB, 3 April 2024. The note records the teaching before it records the case.

The preparation is stated as a hedge against everything that could go wrong: "Approaching the class with a mix of anticipation and preparedness, I recognized the challenges inherent in engaging students who might not have pre-read the case or grasped its complexities. With 16 hours of meticulous preparation, I strategized for various scenarios, from lack of prior engagement to difficulties in understanding the case's nuances."

The instruments were chosen against the default: "Eschewing traditional PowerPoint presentations for a more interactive setup, I armed myself with an IPEVO overhead projector, multiple whiteboards, and a wealth of printed materials to facilitate a dynamic learning environment—all set against the backdrop of an amphitheater setup, conducive to vibrant discussion and engagement."

And the outcome is measured in the only unit that matters for a discussion class: "As time seemingly folded into itself, 3 hours and 40 minutes passed without notice, a testament to the riveting exploration of leadership in crisis and the power of collective inquiry." The fear he names beforehand — "any fears of hostility or disengagement" — was not realised.

The class is acknowledged, not named

The article lists roughly twenty-eight participants by name in credit, together with two faculty colleagues. Following this archive's standing practice, no student is named here and the cohort is acknowledged collectively; Dr. Leo Aldianto and Dr. Yos Sunitiyoso, who are faculty, are named.

The Case

Shackleton changed his objective from crossing Antarctica to bringing twenty-eight men home.

Leadership in Crisis: Ernest Shackleton and the Epic Voyage of the Endurance (Koehn, 2003), and what it is set up to examine.

The case "provides an opportunity to examine list of signature leadership so -called 4CAPS+ and entrepreneurship in the context of Ernest Shackleton's 1914 Antarctic expedition, a compelling story of crisis, survival, and triumph."

Its pivot is the fact everything else hangs on: after the vessel became trapped in ice and the crew abandoned ship, "the commander shifted his objectives and responsibilities from completing an historic march to ensuring the survival of all 28 expedition members." An objective abandoned entirely, and a new one adopted without loss of authority.

What the two years then required is stated as the case's real subject: "Shackleton's efforts to maintain his team's morale, loyalty, and commitment in the face of extraordinary mental and physical trials during almost two years in the Antarctic."

The second frame is the one the article adds: the crew is read as "a concept that works well if you an organization experiences distress" — an X-Team, an external team, whose ambassadorial, task-coordination and scouting functions are what carried it.

Framework

Every one of the four capabilities has an incident attached to it.

The 4-CAP+ mapping, worked through the expedition rather than asserted over it.

1  ·  Sensemaking
"His ability to understand and navigate the changing and challenging conditions of the Antarctic landscape demonstrates "Open-Mindedness" and "Experimenting," as he continuously adapted his plans based on the situation, such as when he decided to change the expedition's goal from crossing Antarctica to survival and rescue after the Endurance was trapped in ice."
2  ·  Visioning
"Shackleton's vision of crossing Antarctica and later ensuring the survival and rescue of his crew showcases his "Building and Sharing a Shared Vision" and "Creating Urgency for Change." Despite the extreme adversity faced, Shackleton maintained a clear vision for survival and inspired his men to persevere through seemingly insurmountable challenges."
3  ·  Relating
"This is evident in how he managed interpersonal dynamics, such as addressing grievances and ensuring that the crew's morale remained high through shared meals, entertainment, and equitable division of labor, demonstrating "Inquiring" and "Supporting & Coaching"." Shared meals and equitable labour are administrative decisions read as leadership ones.
4  ·  Inventing
""Inventing" was critical to the crew's survival. Shackleton and his men created solutions on the spot, from converting lifeboats for an open sea journey to improvising survival strategies on Elephant Island."
5  ·  Building credibility
"His decision-making process, particularly when abandoning the ship and choosing to risk a small crew to seek rescue, demonstrated "Acting as a Principled Leader" and "Committing to the Greater Good" by prioritizing his men's safety over the mission's original goals." Credibility here is built by what he gave up.
6  ·  Adaptability quotient, and the effectiveness index
"His leadership exemplified "Anti-Fragile," "Emotional Intelligence," and "Paradoxical Thinking" as he navigated the crew through physical and psychological extremes. The effectiveness of Shackleton's leadership can be measured by the survival of his entire crew against all odds." Every man came home, and the original mission did not.

Details

VUCA, TUNA and BANI are three names for the water he was actually in.

The article uses all three vocabularies rather than choosing one, which is a claim that no single acronym covers the case.

What the expedition faced
•Ice that closed without warning and did not open again
•A ship abandoned, then crushed, with no possibility of resupply
•An open-boat crossing improvised from lifeboats
•Almost two years with no external contact and no confirmed rescue
What the frameworks call it
✓VUCA — volatility, uncertainty, complexity, ambiguity, from the U.S. Army War College
✓TUNA — "turbulent, uncertain, novel, and ambiguous"
✓BANI — "brittle, anxious, non-linear and incomprehensible", named in the article alongside the other two
✓The article reads Shackleton as an X-Team "where the ambassador function, the task coordination function and the scouting function are predominant"
Why a 1914 case answers a 2007 framework

The article treats the X-Team as "a concept that works well if you an organization experiences distress or VUCA or BANI" or TUNA, and reads Shackleton's crew as exactly that. The claim runs both ways: a framework that fits a case a century older than itself is being tested, not merely illustrated.

What It Revealed

A 1914 expedition is the cleanest demonstration of the framework I can put in front of a class.

What the session produced, and why the pairing of case and framework is the pedagogical point rather than a decoration.

Shackleton's leadership during the Endurance expedition is a compelling case study of the 4CAPS+ framework, demonstrating how effective leadership can guide a team through unprecedented adversity.

— X-Team in the "Epic Voyage of the Endurance", 3 April 2024
The question the class opened on
"is Shackleton an entrepreneur not?" — put as a cold call and answered, the article records, warmly and firmly in the negative. A discussion that begins by denying the premise is one where the framework has to earn its application.
What the interactive setup produced
"This interactive approach not only enriched the learning experience but also highlighted the importance of adaptability and team cohesion, mirroring the very essence of Shackleton's leadership during the Antarctic expedition." The method and the subject matched.
