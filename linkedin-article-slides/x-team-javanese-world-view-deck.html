X-Team in Javanese World View

The four capabilities of X-Team leadership arrive as an American management framework, but each one already has a Javanese proverb attached to it — and the proverbs are older, more demanding and more specific than the capability names. Taught this way the framework is recognition rather than instruction, which is a different pedagogical proposition and a different claim about where leadership knowledge comes from.

Opening

The X-Team framework arrived from MIT and the values it needs were already here.

Entrepreneurial Leadership, framed through Javanese espoused values, capability by capability.

The method is stated as a deliberate re-reading rather than a comparison: the article sets out "To revisit and enhance our exploration of Sensemaking through the lens of Javanese espoused values with a laser focus", working through the sub-branches of each capability in turn.

The purpose is not to show that two traditions agree. It is to show what one supplies to the other: this approach "will provide a more comprehensive understanding of how Javanese cultural wisdom enriches the concept of Sensemaking." Enriches, not matches.

What follows takes the four capabilities of the 4-CAP+ model — sensemaking, visioning, relating, inventing — and finds, for each of their sub-branches, a Javanese saying that already names the behaviour and usually names it more sharply.

Sensemaking

Ora rumongso bisa asks you to be the stupidest in the room, which is open-mindedness stated as a duty.

Four sub-branches — open-mindedness, learning from others, creating meaning from uncertainty, experimenting — each with its saying.

1  ·  Ora rumongso bisa, nanging bisa rumongso
Glossed in the article as "one must always feel the stupidest in the room, so he can always have room to learn hungrily". What it produces: "a profound level of humility and openness, essential for letting go of existing mental models and being receptive to new insights and perspectives." Letting go of existing mental models is the whole of sensemaking, compressed.
2  ·  Ojo dumeh, tepa seliro, ngerti kuawalat
"one will always be aware of his position in the context, never conclude to know everything". The article reads it as self-awareness and considerateness toward others' viewpoints, "fostering an environment where open-mindedness prevails."
3  ·  Nyebar wiji sederakan lan wiji utamining kejawen ing manca negari
"one must strive to have a remarkably high networking density, welcoming new opportunities to networking". Read as expanding one's network to include diverse perspectives, "facilitating learning from a wide range of experiences and backgrounds." The saying makes network density a virtue, not a tactic.
4  ·  Empan papan
"always maintains 4 main consciences surrounding and place awareness, attitude and behavior awareness, role awareness and dynamic change throughout time awareness". Four simultaneous awarenesses is a far more demanding instruction than creating meaning from uncertainty.

Visioning

Manunggaling kawula gusti asks that your mission and your people's be the same thing.

Building a shared vision, creating urgency for change, and a strategic mindset.

1  ·  Manunggaling kawula gusti
"in his vision and mission, one must be in line with the aspiration of his team/people/organization". The article reads it as "the alignment of personal and collective aspirations, crucial for creating a shared vision that resonates with all members of a community or organization." Alignment is a precondition here, not an outcome of communication.
2  ·  Sepi ing pamrih, rame ing nggawe
"one must complete his tasks responsibly for the greater cause of improving the people's lives and welfare, without short sighted motives of seeking fame or simple monetary rewards". Its role in visioning: "essential for building a vision that transcends personal ambitions and focuses on broader societal or organizational welfare."
3  ·  Wani ngalah luhur wekasane
"willing to step back after a stagnant or temporary defeat so he/she can contemplate his failures, weakness and learn the best practice of the surroundings". Read as the source of urgency rather than of retreat — "the importance of learning from setbacks and using them as a catalyst for change".

Relating

Ngilo githoke dhewe asks you to see the back of your own neck.

Inquiring, influencing and negotiating, supporting and coaching, connecting outside.

1  ·  Tepa seliro
"one must be able to introspect", and to learn continuously from oneself and from one's ecosystem. Under Relating the article reads it as inquiry: it "emphasizes the importance of self-reflection and understanding others' perspectives, facilitating a culture of inquiry". The same saying does work under Sensemaking too, which the article does not hide.
2  ·  Ngilo githoke dhewe
"always project what are the impacts of my act to others. Be introspective." The literal image — looking at the back of your own neck — is of seeing what only others can see. It "Advocates for a heightened awareness of one’s actions on others, promoting empathy and the pursuit of understanding in interpersonal interactions."
3  ·  Nglurug tanpa bala, menang tanpa ngasorake
"one will not always apply one tactic... Instead, he considers many tactics around love and passion and welfare of the people". The article reads it as "a compassionate and flexible approach to influence and negotiation" — winning without humiliating, which is a constraint on victory rather than a route to it.

Inventing

Gurung bengawan weteng segara asks for a throat like a river and a belly like the sea.

Creating a learning culture, cultivating diversity and inclusion, execution, and making tough decisions.

1  ·  Gurung bengawan weteng segara
"one is always willing to accommodate any criticism or aspirations from all walks of life". The article reads it as "openness to feedback and diverse perspectives, a critical component of a culture that prioritizes learning and continuous improvement." The image sets the capacity required, not merely the willingness.
2  ·  Tan ban cindhe ban siladan
"one will not discriminate, one will inclusively uphold law and principle especially in the case of confronting his/her family, friends, networks". Inclusion here is tested precisely where it costs — among one's own — which is a harder standard than most diversity language sets.
3  ·  Alon-alon asal kelakon
"slow but sure, or patience ensures success". Placed under execution, it "underlines the significance of careful planning and patience in execution, advocating for a thoughtful approach to implementing ideas and ensuring that actions lead to meaningful and sustainable outcomes."
4  ·  Sepi ing pamrih, rame ing nggawe, again
Reappearing under a learning culture, where it "advocates for a focus on collective benefits and the pursuit of meaningful goals, essential for nurturing a culture that values learning and innovation for the sake of societal or organizational advancement, rather than personal accolades."
Sayings recur across capabilities, and that is a finding

Tepa seliro appears under both Sensemaking and Relating; sepi ing pamrih and wani ngalah luhur wekasane each appear twice. A management framework partitions capabilities; a body of proverbs does not, because it was never built as a taxonomy. The overlap is where the two systems fit least neatly, and the article leaves it visible.

Conclusion

Taught this way, the framework is recognition rather than instruction.

What the pairing buys is not validation of the Javanese values but adoptability of the imported model.

Ora rumongso bisa, nanging bisa rumongso

— "one must always feel the stupidest in the room, so he can always have room to learn hungrily" — X-Team in Javanese World View, 4 April 2024
Why this matters for teaching
A framework presented as foreign has to be justified before it can be used. A framework presented as already present in the culture's own sayings only has to be noticed. The article never states this consequence; it demonstrates it by doing the mapping four times.
The claim runs in both directions
The article's own phrasing is that Javanese wisdom "enriches" the concept of Sensemaking, not that it duplicates it. Empan papan asks for four simultaneous awarenesses where the framework asks for one capability; the saying is the more demanding instrument.
Where the argument continues
The same case is made in his Jakarta Post column arguing that Javanese values breed innovation, and in a companion video. This article is the worked-out version of that claim, capability by capability.
