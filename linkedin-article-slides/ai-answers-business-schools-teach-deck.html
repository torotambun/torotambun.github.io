When AI Can Answer Almost Anything, What Should Business Schools Teach?

The AI plans HBS described in June 2023 became identifiable curriculum, case-preparation and feedback activities by 2026. Implementation is not evidence of stronger independent judgment, so business schools should assess the capability each assignment targets and keep the case-method sequence: identify the problem independently, let AI challenge it, then defend the revised decision.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

AI can now produce a convincing answer before a student has understood the problem, which changes what management education must verify.

"An HBS alumnus examines the development of AI-supported management education from 2023 to 2026, and the responsibility to develop independent managerial judgment."

Situation

A Dean's Brief to the HBS Club of Singapore on 9 June 2023, recorded in a LinkedIn post, set out plans for digital transformation and AI-supported education.

Complication

"Three years and four months later, those publicly recorded plans provide a historical basis for examining what HBS has developed."

Question

"When AI can produce convincing answers before students have independently understood the problems, how should business schools develop their capacity to make defensible decisions?"

Answer

Separate what was proposed, what was implemented and "what evidence would establish that those developments improve management education."

Evidence

The 2023 plans became curriculum, case-preparation, feedback and benchmarking activities by 2026.

"These changes provide evidence of implementation. Their educational consequences require separate assessment."

Jun 2023
Announced intentions
D³, digital infrastructure and an HBS-tuned large language model to support students, faculty and case authors. "It did not establish their subsequent implementation or educational effectiveness."
Mar 2025
AI in the required MBA curriculum
Data Science and AI for Leaders, with a tutorbot on faculty-approved materials and Julius.ai: 492 tutorbot users and 930 Julius.ai users (Ryan, 2025).
Apr 2026
Course chatbots and assignment feedback
Course-specific chatbots to test assumptions, and a FIN1 Assignment Agent processing about 2,700 Excel assignments in 30 minutes (Ryan, 2026).
Aug 2026
Cases become AI benchmarks
The HBS AI Institute published BusinessCaseBench, evaluating AI on business-case analytical tasks.
What the record does not show

Processing capacity "does not establish that students subsequently become better at financial modeling without assistance." The reports "do not, on their own, demonstrate that students developed stronger independent judgment."

Gap

Two near-identical AI-assisted assignments can hide very different intellectual work, so assessment must name the capability it targets.

A student must decide whether a manufacturer should relocate production as profitability declines. "The finished assignments might look alike even though the intellectual work performed by the students differed substantially."

AI completes the analysis
✗A plausible comparison of labour costs, logistics, efficiency and regulation
✗The recommendation "might be defensible"
✗"Allowing it to complete the analysis that students were supposed to learn to perform may undermine the evidence provided by the assignment."
AI is checked and corrected
✓The student checks assumptions, corrects errors and rejects unsupported recommendations
✓For AI oversight, "Students must recognize unreliable outputs, identify omitted considerations and justify corrections."
✓"Using AI to evaluate a completed analysis may support learning."

Framework

The case method keeps independent problem identification first, and AI can serve it as a questioner that the student must still evaluate.

Hammond (2002, p. 2) emphasises identifying the real managerial problem. "A student may produce technically accurate calculations while analyzing an incorrectly defined problem."

An assistant could ask what evidence establishes labour costs as the cause of declining profit. If obsolete equipment and defects explain much of the gap, relocation may not address the problem. "Students must therefore learn to assess the AI's interventions rather than treat questioning by a machine as automatically authoritative."

The sequence matters: "A student might prepare an initial case analysis independently, use AI to challenge it, and then defend a revised position in class." The instructor can then assess "whether the student understands why the recommendation changed."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

Managerial judgment goes beyond analysis, because it decides whether evidence is sufficient for action and which objectives count.

"This does not establish that human judgment is inherently reliable." "The educational objective should therefore concern the quality of reasoning rather than whether an answer originated from a person or an AI system."

01
Sufficiency
Grounds for action
A facility closure may show large savings; management must still weigh disruption, contracts and employees, and decide what uncertainty is acceptable.
02
Practice
Testing assumptions in the field
AI can draft projections before customer demand is evidenced. "The document may be convincing while its central commercial proposition remains unsupported."
03
Responsibility
Objectives behind the recommendation
"If the objective is defined exclusively as short-term cost reduction, other consequential effects may be excluded from the beginning."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Implications

Business schools should assess demonstrated reasoning, including whether students perform well after AI support is withdrawn.

Schools must separate students who build AI, use it and decide on its deployment. These are "the author's proposed educational measures, not reported HBS learning outcomes."

1  ·  Financial modelling
Students explain their assumptions after receiving AI feedback.
2  ·  Case discussion
Students show they recognise weaknesses in AI-generated recommendations.
3  ·  Entrepreneurship
Students identify evidence that would disconfirm the opportunity they propose.
4  ·  Evaluating the AI itself
Whether students perform better after AI-supported learning, "including when they must subsequently work without assistance."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

The task of management education is to establish whether a student can independently judge when an AI answer is reliable enough to act on.

"Their implementation is evidence of institutional development. Whether they improve independent managerial judgment must still be established through appropriate educational assessment."

If AI can produce a persuasive answer before a student has understood the problem, the task of management education is to establish whether that student can independently determine when the answer is reliable enough to act upon.

— Dr. Toronata Tambun, What Should Business Schools Teach?
The standard for his own classroom
Students submitting an AI-supported business plan will explain its assumptions and "identify evidence that would justify abandoning or revising the proposal."
