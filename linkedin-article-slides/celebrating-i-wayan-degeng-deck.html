Celebrating Mr. I Wayan Degeng: Another Mentor Who Shaped My Career

A mentor who assesses his own career as unremarkable is assessing it on the scale his employer used — compliance, English, rebellious curiosity. On a different scale, one that counts contribution, continued learning and health, the same life is a model. The measure of success is not the wealth it produced but the journey, and the man who says he did not excel is the evidence for that claim.

Opening

In 1993 a General Field Engineer was assigned to mentor a vacation trainee, and he brought the world to me.

The article states in its third sentence whose story it is, and it is not the author's.

"In 1993, I embarked on a journey that would profoundly shape my career and life. It was during the summer when I, a vacation trainee at SLB , first met Mr. I Wayan Degeng." The name itself carries a small correction the article makes on the record: "I used to call him Mas Wayan, but only recently did I learn that he is commonly called “Degeng.”"

And then the sentence that sets the terms: "This story is not about me, but about Degeng, who at one time brought the world to me." He was then a General Field Engineer reporting to a Field Service Manager, and he and a senior field engineer were assigned to mentor the trainee.

What preceded that is compressed into one line, and the article spends the next section unpacking it: "Before this point, Degeng's life was one of perseverance and resilience, emerging from humble beginnings with relentless determination."

The Turns

He failed to get into ITB, went to ITS instead, and paid for it with 100,000 rupiahs of vocational scholarship savings.

The route is given with its costs in it, which is what makes it a record rather than a tribute.

School
The vocational route, taken deliberately
"Degeng didn't take the conventional path through high school; instead, he attended a vocational school, studying in the electrical major." He came from "a modest farming background, where his father managed to send his older brother to IKIP, a teaching school."
Family
An older brother who became the breadwinner
"Despite his limited means, Degeng's family was supportive of his ambitions. His older brother, who also completed vocational school, became the family's breadwinner." The support is material as well as moral, and the article records who paid for it.
Entry
Rejected by ITB, admitted to ITS on an uncle's counsel
"When Degeng aspired to attend Institut Teknologi Bandung but failed to secure admission, he didn't give up. He sought counsel from his uncle, who supported his plan to apply to Institut Teknologi Surabaya (ITS)." The two figures are given exactly: "a scholarship saving of 100,000 rupiahs from three years of scholarships during vocational school and an entrance fee of only 62,500 rupiahs."
Studies
A tailor, then an electrical repairer, then recruited
"Throughout his studies, Degeng worked tirelessly. He took up jobs as a tailor and later as an electrical repairer in an electronic shop." The job found him rather than the reverse: "It was during his frequent visits to the shop for components that the owner noticed his potential and offered him a job." His technical expertise and dedication "must have caught the attention of" the recruiter who took him on as a wireline engineer — a hedge the article states as a hedge.
After
Lecturer in Jakarta, then Denpasar, then retirement
"After leaving the company sometime between 2000 and 2010, he became an electrical engineering lecturer at a private university in Jakarta, before moving to Bali to teach at a Denpasar-based university in electrical engineering, and eventually retiring due to age." The departure date is given as a decade-wide range, not narrowed.

Field Notes

He signed my field curriculum on 2 September 1993, noting I was willing to get my hands dirty.

The observations are small, dated where dating is possible, and each is offered as a trace of character rather than as an anecdote.

RECORDED A
The document that still exists
"He never treated me harshly or unfairly. I still have the record of my VT/COOP Field Curriculum that he signed off on September 2, 1993, noting my good understanding of the subject and willingness to get hands dirty." It is the one piece of paper in the piece, and the author says he has it.
RECORDED B
How he supervised
"Degeng, an introvert, enjoyed observing and maintaining his rituals." And the combination the article credits: "He was a compliant person, yet open to new ideas, allowing me to work independently while building rapport with mechanics and operators." Compliance and autonomy-granting are usually opposed; here they sat together.
RECORDED C
The temple in Balikpapan
"I vividly remember observing a Hindu worship ceremony he attended at a temple in Balikpapan one Sunday morning." It is recorded as observation, not interpretation — a trainee watching what his mentor did on his own time.
RECORDED D
The untinted windows, 2024
At a reunion on 5 July 2024, "I noticed his car's untinted windows, a reminder of the safety culture ingrained during our Schlumberger days. He meticulously planned our meeting, reflecting the punctuality and respect for timing he developed over the years." Thirty-one years on, the training is still visible in a car window.

The Assessment

By his own account he did not excel: compliant, limited English, no rebellious curiosity.

The article gives the man's own verdict on his career before giving its own, and does not soften either.

His verdict on himself
•"in hindsight, he didn't consider himself as excelling in his Schlumberger career"
•"He mentioned that his compliant nature and limited English were drawbacks"
•"He felt that he lacked the rebellious curiosity and diversity of thought that often drive innovation"
•The criteria are the firm's: assertiveness, language, and a taste for breaking things
The author's answer
✓"To this, my response is that everyone chooses their own path"
✓"The fact that I see him contributing to life, continuing to learn, and maintaining his health already makes him a model"
✓"He was never a glamorous person despite his high salary back then"
✓"He lived a simple life, embodying values that serve as a good model"
What is actually in dispute

Not the facts — both men agree on the compliance, the English and the absence of rebellious curiosity. What differs is the scale. He was measuring himself against what an oilfield services company promotes for; the article measures against contribution, learning and health, and reaches the opposite conclusion from the same evidence.

What I Carry

The true measure of success wasn't wealth but the journey itself.

The generalisation is drawn explicitly, and it is the reason a private tribute was published.

The true measure of success wasn't wealth but the journey itself – navigating boulders, valleys, and peaks, cherishing the process, and finding fulfillment in health, social life, and solid friendships.

— Celebrating Mr. I Wayan Degeng, 24 July 2024
What the route proves
"Reflecting on Degeng's journey, I realized that success was accessible to those who dared to dream and work hard. Meritocracy provided opportunities, and the path to success was filled with challenges." Meritocracy is credited here as having worked — a claim the author tests hard in later work, and states plainly in this one.
Starting point against determination
"His story taught me that success isn't defined by one's starting point but by the resilience and determination to reach one's goals." The vocational school, the failed ITB application and the 100,000 rupiahs are the starting point being referred to.
The debt, stated as a debt
"I want to thank Degeng for the kindness he showed me during my early days at Schlumberger." And the closing line returns to the sentence the article opened with: "Degeng, at one time, brought the world to me, and his influence remains a significant part of my life."

Sources

Sources

The article carries no reference list and cites no works. Its material is the author's own memory of a 1993 vacation traineeship, one document he says he still holds — a VT/COOP Field Curriculum signed 2 September 1993 — a brief meeting around 2012, and a reunion on 5 July 2024 at which Degeng gave his own account of his career. The subject is named throughout at the author's choice, in tribute, as are three former colleagues, one of them recorded as deceased. Degeng's assessment of his own career is his, reported as his; the contrary assessment is the author's and is marked as such. The date he left the company is given in the source only as a range between 2000 and 2010, and is not narrowed here. The universities where he later taught are identified by city rather than by name in the article, and stay so here.
