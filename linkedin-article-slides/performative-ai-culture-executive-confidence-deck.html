Performative AI Culture: When Executive Confidence Exceeds Technological Understanding

Performative AI culture is a proposed pattern in which appearing knowledgeable about AI earns more recognition than demonstrating understanding. Executives need not master architectures, but they must name the technology, locate their organisation in the value chain, connect expertise to decision authority and keep human judgment answerable, because warranted confidence is shown when evidence can correct a claim and change a decision.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

A program that crawls websites and draws charts does not become AI by being called AI, though executives may approve and announce it as such.

"Suppose a team shows its executives a program that collects information from websites and turns it into charts. The team calls it artificial intelligence."

Situation

"Conventional web-crawling software can retrieve, extract, and organise those prices without using artificial intelligence." A RAG system can then build answers from the database.

Complication

"neither internet crawling nor RAG independently establishes whether a quoted price is current, contractually applicable, or clinically comparable."

Question

How can an organisation tell confident misunderstanding apart from careful caution?

Answer

Examine the claim, name the technology, understand how organisations learn, locate value in the chain, assign responsibility and "test whether evidence can change a decision."

Framework

Executive certainty becomes consequential because it commits others to purchases, advisers and dismissed warnings.

"The executive has the authority to commit other people to a decision. That authority makes unexamined certainty consequential."

In professional discussions, interest in AGI certification appeared "before participants had established what AGI meant or how it differed from existing AI systems." Elsewhere, legitimate domain requirements were raised without separating data acquisition, domain expertise, AI development and the commissioning organisation’s responsibilities.

Koentjaraningrat’s image of attention to the visible front of a house, with neglected interiors (1971, p. 38), is extended here to technological competence. "Koentjaraningrat did not formulate a theory of AI adoption; this extension is my interpretation."

Status of the claim

"The encounters cannot establish how widespread this behaviour is among Indonesian executives." "This remains a proposed explanation. Limited specialist support, competing demands and uncertainty may also explain inadequate preparation."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

Naming the technology changes the decision, because a desired result was being confused with the method that produces it.

"My concern was that AI had already been prescribed before anyone had established why the task required it."

The prescription
✗AI to search decades of records for profiles resembling a new underwriting applicant
✗A dataset from Hugging Face or Kaggle assumed fit for underwriting
✗A model’s pretrained knowledge treated as an authoritative source
✗"An agent will improve productivity"
What the task actually needs
✓With comparable attributes, "a mathematical measure such as cosine similarity could be used to rank their similarity." "Similarity alone would not justify an underwriting decision."
✓"Availability does not establish suitability."
✓Verified applicant facts, current policy terms and approved rules "must come from identified, checked sources."
✓The task, the model’s role, the permitted actions and the evaluation criteria
Four axes, not one hierarchy

Capability breadth, method, model or system class, and function "can coexist" (Tambun, 2026a). "Generative AI names a capacity to produce content; it does not settle the breadth or reliability of the system’s competence."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

Executive understanding is part of organisational learning, since access to expertise is not evidence that an organisation can use it.

Executives "do need enough understanding to judge the relevance of that knowledge, recognise its limits and organise its use."

01
Helfat & Peteraf (2015)
Managerial cognitive capabilities
Cognition connects sensing, seizing and reconfiguring. It "does not establish that confidence is a reliable indicator of these capabilities."
02
Durán & Aguado (2022)
Cognition and dynamic capabilities
A meta-analysis reports associations, but does not test this mechanism; its tables were later corrected (Durán & Aguado, 2024).
03
Zahra & George (2002)
Absorptive capacity
A workshop may enable acquisition; assimilation, transformation and exploitation still require understanding, changed procedures and use in practice.
The informative signal

"The quality of the reasoning, including its responsiveness to evidence, is more informative than the direction of the decision."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Gap

The value chain exposes what adoption leaves unfinished, because using, integrating, adapting and developing a model are different work.

"Which capability does the organization need to possess, and which can it obtain from elsewhere?"

Wu and Higgins (2023) separate architecture, training data, pre-training, fine-tuning, inference, hardware and guardrails. "Prompting a model is an inference-time interaction. Fine-tuning changes model parameters through additional training." "Purchasing access to a service does not mean that the purchaser has developed the underlying model."

In a hypothetical document review, someone must still define relevance and resolve disputed findings. "If management treats a successful model response as a completed organisational solution, the remaining work can disappear from the proposal."

On the HBS note

"its historical prices and market estimates should not be treated as current benchmarks."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

Expertise counts only when it has an effective route to alter action, otherwise specialist participation becomes the appearance of scrutiny.

NIST’s GOVERN function calls for clear roles, training and "executive responsibility for decisions about AI risks" (NIST, 2023).

A domain expert may see that an accurate output is unusable, a data specialist that evaluation records omit important cases, an engineer that an intervention cannot run reliably. "Management needs a process through which disagreements become testable questions." Whether a system is accurate enough "cannot be resolved by repeating an aggregate score."

"The culture becomes performative when specialist participation provides the appearance of scrutiny while credible objections have little influence on the decision."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Implications

Human judgment stays answerable only when reviewers have the time, information and authority to intervene, whichever loop is used.

"Human participation becomes meaningful only when the task and decision rights make intervention possible." Raisch and Krakowski (2021) caution against treating automation and augmentation as separable.

Human-in-the-loop
•An AI-mediated process routes selected cases to a reviewer
•"A reviewer may be formally present yet lack the time, information or authority needed to challenge an output."
•Can suggest reassurance without specifying what a person can do
AI-in-the-loop
•A human-led inquiry calls on AI for selected contributions
•People retain control over the problem, the evaluation of evidence and the consequential decision
•"The same workflow can contain both. Neither arrangement guarantees sound judgment."
Where to look

"The most revealing evidence may therefore appear after the demonstration: what the organisation changes when the technology fails, when a specialist disagrees, or when an apparently successful result cannot be reproduced."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

Confidence is warranted when evidence can correct a claim and change a decision, whether the decision is to proceed, revise or stop.

"Performative AI culture is a hypothesis about the relationship between technological claims and organisational behaviour."

Confidence becomes warranted when an organisation can explain what its system does, demonstrate its limits, assign responsibility and learn from failure. An informed decision to proceed, revise or stop can each meet that standard.

— Dr. Toronata Tambun, Performative AI Culture
What an executive should be able to do
"ask what the evidence establishes, identify whose expertise is needed and distinguish an unresolved limitation from a reason to abandon inquiry."
What a team should be able to say
"that a promising system failed an important test without having to disguise the result as success."
