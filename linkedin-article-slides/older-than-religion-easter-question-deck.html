Older Than Religion: An Easter Question I Did Not Expect to Ask

Religious persecution in Indonesia is not primarily a theological problem. It is a neurological one, activated by majority power rather than by creed — which is why the mechanism is identical in Tangerang and in South Minahasa, and why no tradition owns the solution. What suppresses it is not a tolerance programme but repeated personal encounter, of the kind a family performed at lunchtime in the 1970s without calling it anything.

Opening

Feeding a neighbour's child was the most radical political act I ever witnessed.

Sometime in the 1970s. It took perhaps ten minutes. It cost nothing.

Situation

A family fed their neighbour's child at lunchtime while his parents were away at work. Nobody asked them to. Nobody thanked them formally. They simply saw a child who needed a meal, and they gave one.

Complication

The author was that child — a member of a religious minority in a region where a different faith formed the overwhelming majority. He knows tepo seliro, the Javanese instinct to feel what another feels and act before being asked, from the inside. He also knows its absence.

Question

How can the brain capable of that quiet daily generosity be the same brain that seals a prayer house on Good Friday and destroys an ancestral worship site at dawn?

Answer

"Same organ. Same species. Completely different outcome." What changes between the two is not the faith held. It is whether the person holding it is in the majority.

The Question

The real question is what it costs to keep calling this a religious problem.

The reframe the piece insists on, and the reason it is uncomfortable for every reader it has.

The question is not simply why minorities are persecuted; social neuroscience has begun to answer that. The deeper question is what it costs to keep pretending this is primarily a religious problem — and what might be gained by looking elsewhere for the diagnosis.

That reframe has costs. For the reader who holds convenient explanations. For the policymaker who designs tolerance programs. For the diplomat who writes religious freedom reports. And for the believer who has confused persecution with identity.

"Neither the costs nor the benefits are small." The piece is written on Easter morning and it does not resolve into consolation.

The Mechanism

Fear runs faster than theology, and majority power is what amplifies it.

What social neuroscience has established, in the order the argument needs it.

EVIDENCE A
The amygdala computes us-versus-them in a fraction of a second
Estimated by evolutionary biologists at over 200 million years old and shared in its basic architecture with reptiles, its job is threat detection — faster than conscious thought, faster than theology, faster than any value we claim to hold (Amodio, 2014). "This is not evil. It is survival machinery…"
EVIDENCE B
Forty-five percent of adult moral capacity is set between ages three and seven
Early childhood experience in that window contributes approximately 45% to the moral capacity expressed in adulthood — the single strongest predictor of ethical behavior, outweighing cognitive biases, external pressures and even character (Tambun et al., 2025). The prefrontal cortex is being built precisely then. What enters does not merely teach. It wires.
EVIDENCE C
Inter-group bias appears in the majority brain and far less in the minority brain
Neural inter-group bias shows clearly in majority members, in regions governing attention, social categorization and emotional response, but is far less evident in minority members (Karinen et al., 2022). "Power does not merely change behavior. It changes neurology."
EVIDENCE D
The driver underneath the permits is not hatred but mortality salience
When the barely conscious terror of being replaced or made irrelevant is activated, negative stereotypes toward outgroups increase, hostility intensifies, and support grows for leaders who promise to manage the threat through force rather than dialogue (Haller et al., 2022).
Not a verdict

The prefrontal cortex arrived much later and is slower: the amygdala fires in milliseconds, the cortex takes seconds. Fear wins the first round automatically, and overriding it is a learned daily discipline. As Montagu observed, treating aggression as biologically inevitable becomes a pseudoscientific excuse for accepting what could be changed. "The amygdala is real. It is not a verdict."

The Mirror

The perpetrators changed faith; the mechanism did not.

Two cases, in two provinces, with the majority faith reversed between them.

Teluknaga, Tangerang
•July 2024: residents dispersed the POUK Tesalonika congregation gathering in a private home after their church lease expired
•Good Friday, 3 April 2026: Satpol PP sealed the congregation's prayer house, citing Perda No. 13/2022 on houses-of-worship permits
•PGI formally condemned the sealing as violating Articles 28E and 29 of the 1945 Constitution
•The instrument was not a mob with torches; it was government apparatus acting within the framework of law
•The perpetrators belonged to the majority faith of Java
South Minahasa, North Sulawesi
•Laroma practises the ancestral Malesung faith of the Minahasa, predating every missionary ship that entered these waters
•Since 2016: hate speech in houses of worship, at government events, and even at weddings
•June 2022: the sacred gathering place Wale Paliusan was physically destroyed by residents who stigmatised the believers as deviant
•A year after the destruction, discrimination continued in many forms
•The perpetrators belonged to the majority faith of that region
What was identical

Stigmatisation of the minority, pressure through social and institutional channels, the quiet removal of safety. "The amygdala does not check your faith before it activates" — "It only asks: are you inside or outside?"

The Count

The state has never counted, and the failure to count is itself an answer.

What the numbers are, and what they are not.

According to the Communion of Churches in Indonesia, more than 430 churches have been attacked, closed, or burned since 2004. In 2024 alone, SETARA Institute recorded 260 incidents and 402 violations of religious freedom — a significant increase over 2023.

"The government does not count. And the failure to count is itself a form of answer." An absent register is not neutral: it removes the basis on which a pattern could be established, and therefore the basis on which it could be answered.

What is estimated, not established

Local advocacy groups estimate the two-decade total may exceed 1,000 — but Indonesia's Ministry of Religious Affairs has never compiled official data. That figure is an advocacy estimate, and the article presents it as one. The 430 figure and the SETARA counts are attributed to named bodies; the 1,000 is not.

The Costs

Every reader pays a cost, including the one who came for validation.

The piece names its audiences and charges each of them separately.

01
The reader with a convenient diagnosis
Your comfortable explanation produces ineffective treatment
The brain doing the persecuting in Tangerang and the brain doing it in South Minahasa are running the same ancient program. Blaming a single tradition is more comfortable than confronting a mechanism that lives in every human skull, including your own.
02
The believer expecting validation
The mirror must turn before the mouth opens
You cannot speak with moral authority about what is being done to POUK Tesalonika while your own majority community does the same thing to Laroma. That sentence is addressed to the author's own side, not to someone else's.
03
The diplomat and the policymaker
The amygdala is not impressed by policy papers
Interfaith dialogue brochures do not rewire 200 million years of threat-detection circuitry. What the research consistently shows works is repeated, specific, personal encounter with the human being on the other side of the boundary, sustained long enough for the brain to learn that difference is not threat (Amodio, 2010).

The Older Answer

The deepest moral intuitions predate every religion that later claimed them.

Why the Malesung faith and the empty tomb belong in the same argument.

The Malesung faith carries its own flood story, its own account of the first man and woman — Dotu Lumimuut and Dotu Toar — and its own understanding of what the sacred demands, all arrived at independently through centuries of living close to land and sky. Its central teaching — "Si Tou Timou Tumou Tou, that humans live to be a blessing to other humans" — is not derived from any world religion.

These are not primitive superstitions. They are evidence that the deepest human questions are not the property of any imported religion. They predate all of them. They belong to the species.

"Religion does not create this mechanism. It inherits it." And then, across every tradition without exception, gives it a sacred vocabulary.

The crucifixion interrupted that logic. When the veil of the temple tore, from top to bottom and not by human hands, the entire architecture of mediated religion was abolished — the layered priesthood, the ritual requirements, the bureaucratic permission structure for approaching the sacred. Direct access. No majority required. No permit to be denied.

Implications

No tradition owns the solution, because no tradition created the problem.

The so-what, stated in the article's own sequence.

1  ·  The problem is neurological before it is theological
Shaped by millions of years of evolutionary history, activated by the experience of majority power, and suppressible only by the kind of sustained human encounter that rewires the threat response slowly and at great personal cost.
2  ·  Si Tou Timou Tumou Tou and the Sermon on the Mount are not competitors
They are independent human arrivals at the same recognition: that the prefrontal cortex must be trained, daily, to override what the amygdala does automatically.
3  ·  The believer must account for Laroma first
The reader rightly outraged by Tangerang has an obligation that comes before the next sentence about tolerance, and it points inward.
4  ·  The policymaker is funding the wrong organ
Programs are designed for the prefrontal cortex while the conditions being funded inflame the amygdala. The two efforts cancel, and the second is larger.
5  ·  The diplomat is documenting the wrong category
"It is a biological phenomenon dressed in religious clothing." Not the reverse — which is how the reports are currently written.

What I Carry

The family who fed me did what no tolerance programme has produced.

Without being asked, without ceremony, without theological justification.

They were training their prefrontal cortex. And mine.

That is the so-what
No permit system, no interfaith forum and no tolerance curriculum has ever reliably produced what a lunchtime meal produced in the 1970s. The mechanism that works is unglamorous, repeated, and impossible to fund into existence.
The only answer he actually believes
