Full Circle: From My First MIT Class to Sharing My Work with President Sally Kornbluth "Disciplined Storytelling" in Singapore

Presenting his own book to MIT's president closes a loop opened in 2016 by a single system-dynamics class, and each theme raised that evening lands against a different stretch of the same eight-year arc.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus

Opening

A personal moment of connection: presenting Disciplined Storytelling to President Kornbluth pays tribute to a teacher who never met her.

The 'Presidential Welcome Tour: Singapore' evening featured a conversation between MIT Alumni Association President Natalie Lorenz Anderson and MIT President Sally Kornbluth.

A deeply personal highlight came early in the evening: the honor of presenting my autograph book, Disciplined Storytelling, to President Kornbluth. Inspired by the late Professor Patrick Winston, the book embodies his principle: 'storytelling, writing ability, and quality of ideas — in that order.' Winston believed storytelling was essential for impactful communication and leadership, and handing this book to President Kornbluth felt like paying tribute to his legacy.

The event was made possible by the leadership and dedication of Ms. Hau Yee Ng (class of 1984) and the Executive Committee of the MIT Club of Singapore, whose efforts created an atmosphere of inclusivity and warmth that reflected the collaborative spirit of the MIT community.

The Turns

That moment transported him back to a 2016 system-dynamics course that changed his worldview.

The book itself carries an eight-year arc, from a first MIT classroom to this evening.

This moment transported me back to June 2016, when I first set foot on MIT's campus. Coming from a career in the oil and gas industry, I approached this new chapter with anticipation but little idea of how transformative the journey would be. My introduction to MIT was John Sterman's system dynamics course, which completely changed my worldview — revealing the complex, interconnected nature of systems and shaping how I viewed decision-making and problem-solving.

The transformation continued with my participation in the 'Entrepreneurship Development Program' led by Bill Aulet, which I attended twice, in 2017 and 2018. These experiences deepened my understanding of innovation, entrepreneurship, and resilience, ultimately guiding my decision to retire at 48 and pursue a life driven by purpose and the values instilled in me by MIT.

Field Notes

Seven themes from the evening each land against a different stretch of the same MIT arc.

President Kornbluth's conversation with Natalie Lorenz Anderson covered themes that resonate with the author's own professional and research interests.

1  ·  International collaboration
Partnerships like the Singapore-MIT Alliance for Research & Technology Centre let MIT leverage local expertise for global challenges like climate change and public health — aligning with the author's belief that entrepreneurship flourishes on diverse, collaborative foundations.
2  ·  Entrepreneurial spirit and innovation
MIT's culture of boundless innovation, where even first-year students arrive ready to pitch ideas, reinforced the importance of ecosystems that support budding entrepreneurs — echoing the author's own journey.
3  ·  Climate initiatives
MIT's 'Climate Project,' compared by President Kornbluth to a 'Manhattan Project' for sustainability, made the author feel at home, given his own involvement in MIT's Climate Interactive En-ROADS Simulation.
4  ·  AI in education and beyond
The surge of interest in applying AI to K-12 education connects directly to the author's own research on 'pruning neurons,' underscoring why early education is paramount.
5  ·  Graduate student support
President Kornbluth's emphasis on career development, faculty mentorship training, housing and wellness support reframed alumni engagement as more than donorship — fostering future innovators for life and leadership, not only academic training.
6  ·  Interdisciplinary collaboration
The MIT Humanistic Insight Collaborative (MITHIC) brings humanities, social sciences and STEM together — an integration the author sees as crucial for solutions that weigh social impact and ethics, not only technical feasibility.
7  ·  Alumni engagement
Both President Kornbluth and Natalie Lorenz Anderson underscored the vital role of alumni in expanding MIT's influence and supporting current students — an example, in the author's reading, of how alumni can be powerful advocates for innovation and education.

What I Carry

The evening reinforced that MIT's values continue to guide those who leave its halls.

The piece closes on gratitude to the organizers rather than a verdict on MIT's direction.

The 'Presidential Welcome Tour: Singapore' brought to life the shared vision and commitment that define the MIT community. From insightful discussions to reconnecting with fellow alumni, the event reinforced that MIT is a global network driven by curiosity, collaboration, and the pursuit of positive change.

Gratitude to Hau Yee, the Executive Committee of the MIT Club of Singapore, and the MIT Alumni Association team for making this gathering an inspiring success — the values and spirit that MIT fosters continue to guide and inspire long after leaving its halls.
