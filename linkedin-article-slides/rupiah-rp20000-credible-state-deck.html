Is Indonesia Safe if the Rupiah Reaches Rp20,000?

The number is not the question. A stable Rp20,000 with functioning liquidity is safer than a panicked Rp18,000. What decides safety is whether pressure becomes execution discipline — and replacing the finance minister or the Bank Indonesia governor answers no mechanism at all.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus · En-ROADS Climate Simulation Ambassador
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Changing the finance minister or the Bank Indonesia governor is not reform. It is theater.

When the rupiah weakens, politics quickly searches for a face.

Situation

"Someone must be blamed. Someone must be removed. Someone must be offered to the market as proof that the government is now serious. That temptation is growing again as the rupiah moves near levels that remind people of 1998."

Complication

"But Indonesia's problem is larger than one minister or one central banker. The serious question is whether the country can remain safe if the rupiah weakens further, even toward Rp20,000 per United States dollar."

Question

If the exchange rate is a symptom rather than the disease, what would actually have to be true for Indonesia to be safe at that level?

Answer

That liquidity stays normal, inflation stays contained, reserves stay adequate, bond markets keep working, and firms do not scramble for dollars. "The answer depends less on the number itself than on what happens around it."

Gap

A stable Rp20,000 would be better than a panicked Rp18,000.

The level is not the variable. The condition of the system around the level is.

"A stable Rp20,000 would be better than a panicked Rp18,000 if liquidity remains normal, inflation stays contained, reserves remain adequate, bond markets continue to work and firms do not scramble for dollars. But a stable Rp20,000 could also mean the market has simply repriced Indonesia downward and accepted a weaker country risk."

The 1998 memory should not be dismissed. "The rupiah crash then was not only a currency event. It was a banking crisis, a corporate foreign-debt crisis, a political crisis and a regime crisis." But memory is not a method: "To treat every movement toward Rp18,000 or Rp20,000 as the return of 1998 is weak analysis. It uses fear when diagnosis is needed."

The opposite error is to dismiss the exchange rate because most Indonesians do not transact in dollars. President Prabowo Subianto's statement that villagers do not use dollars is "economically incomplete but politically understandable." Villagers do not count daily transactions in dollars; farmers do not check exchange-rate screens before going to the field; warung owners do not price rice through Wall Street language.

Core Gap

Both readings answer the wrong question. One treats the number as a catastrophe; the other treats it as irrelevant. "The immediate question is no longer whether the rupiah can touch Rp20,000. It is whether the broader economic system can absorb that pressure without losing stability."

Evidence

Reserves cover 5.6 months and inflation is 3.08 percent; April's trade surplus was US$0.09 billion.

Indonesia is under pressure, although the public evidence so far does not show a full currency crisis. These are the indicators as the article reports them.

INDICATOR A
Reserves and outflows
"Foreign portfolio outflows are present, but that is not the same as crisis-level capital flight." Foreign-exchange reserves have fallen from about US$146.2 billion to US$144.9 billion, "but they still cover around 5.6 months of imports, well above the common three-month adequacy benchmark."
INDICATOR B
Inflation, still inside the corridor
"Inflation reached 3.08 percent year on year in May, still within Bank Indonesia's target range of 2.5 percent plus or minus one percentage point. Core inflation was lower, at 2.59 percent."
INDICATOR C
The trade account, the sharper warning
"Indonesia recorded only a US$0.09 billion trade surplus in April. Behind that thin surplus was a strong non-oil and gas surplus of US$3.53 billion and a large oil and gas deficit of US$3.44 billion." The rupiah story is not only about exchange-rate psychology; it is about imported energy and the dollar cost structure sitting under ordinary life.
INDICATOR D
The bond-market intervention, in proportion
Critics point to reported Finance Ministry intervention of around Rp2 trillion per day. "Indonesia's government securities market is worth thousands of trillions of rupiah, so Rp2 trillion per day is a stabilizing operation rather than a market takeover." The funds are linked to state cash management and government investment resources, including instruments associated with retail programs such as Savings Bonds Ritel (SBR).
Direct the Criticism at the Mechanism

"Whether one agrees with the policy or not, the criticism should be directed at the actual mechanism rather than at the finance minister personally." Bank Indonesia has also raised the BI-Rate to 5.50 percent and used instruments to defend rupiah stability. "These are not signs of silence."

Details

The dollar reaches the village through fuel, fertiliser and transport, not through cash.

Prabowo deserves a fairer reading here. His instinct is not empty — and the mechanism by which currency pressure arrives in a village is worth stating precisely.

1  ·  The transmission channel
"But the dollar enters the village through prices, not through cash transactions. It enters through fuel, fertilizer, imported feed, machinery, spare parts, medicines, transport, packaging and subsidies. A villager may never hold a dollar note, but imported costs can still reach the village through the price of planting, moving, cooking and selling."
2  ·  Why energy sovereignty belongs at the centre
"A country that imports too much fuel imports vulnerability. A country that depends too much on external inputs imports inflation. A country that cannot turn local resources into reliable production remains exposed no matter how confident its speeches sound."
3  ·  But not by opening forests
"Opening forests to produce feedstock for bioethanol may reduce one import bill while creating another national liability. Forests are not idle land. Watersheds, soil, biodiversity, microclimate, carbon storage and village livelihoods are economic assets. If fuel security is bought through forest destruction, the country has not escaped dependency. It has moved the cost from the foreign-exchange account to the ecological balance sheet."
4  ·  The upside, if it is used
For Singaporeans and other foreign buyers, Indonesia becomes cheaper — tourism, services, local food, furniture, textiles, creative goods and some exports may become more attractive, and Indonesians earning foreign currency gain domestic purchasing power. "There is something good in a weaker rupiah only if Indonesia uses it to rebuild production, not merely to celebrate cheapness."

Implications

This is stress, not collapse, and the clearest visible danger so far is political.

Stress becomes crisis when the signals multiply. Watching the right list matters more than watching the exchange rate.

What Stress Looks Like Now
✓Reserves adequate at around 5.6 months of imports
✓Inflation contained inside the target corridor
✓Markets continuing to function, even with weaker confidence
✓Portfolio outflows present but short of crisis-level flight
What Would Make It a Crisis
✗Reserve loss and unanchored inflation expectations
✗Forced selling in government bonds
✗Subsidy uncertainty and corporate dollar distress
✗Political attacks on institutions
Why Replacing a Name Makes the Signal Worse

"If investors demand higher yields because of oil risk, fiscal pressure, foreign outflows, policy uncertainty and concern over institutional credibility, changing names will not reduce the price of risk. It may even make the signal worse. Markets may read the move as panic."

The Discount

If Indonesia becomes cheaper but not better, the weak rupiah becomes a national discount.

Rp20,000 can become a harsh teacher. Whether it teaches anything depends on a capability Indonesia has rarely treated as a national habit.

"That lesson is difficult because Indonesia is not famous for disciplined execution in the way China is. Indonesia is good at resilience, improvisation, social absorption and survival under pressure. Those are real strengths." They explain why the country often does not collapse even when coordination looks messy. "But resilience is not industrial discipline. Improvisation does not automatically produce reliable execution."

"The familiar pattern appears in many sectors: large announcement, ceremonial launch, overlapping ministries, unclear chain of command, weak monitoring, local resistance, changing rules and another large announcement before the previous one matures." It is also connected to culture: harmony, consensus and accommodation are strengths for stability, "Yet those same tendencies can sometimes make institutions reluctant to enforce accountability, confront underperformance or insist on completion over symbolism. Indonesia often confuses policy declaration with policy completion."

"Cheapness alone is not competitiveness. Competitiveness means reliable production, consistent quality, export certification, shipping discipline, financing access, buyer trust, logistics reliability and policy predictability. If Indonesia becomes cheaper but not better, the weak rupiah becomes a national discount. Foreigners buy cheap assets, imported costs rise, poorer households pay more and the country congratulates itself for being affordable."

The Test Is Completion, Not Intention

If a weaker rupiah is supposed to boost exports, factories must increase output, ports must move goods on time, exporters must meet quality standards and ministries must remove bottlenecks quickly. "The challenge is not identifying the right goals. The challenge is finishing the work required to achieve them."

Conclusion

The rupiah does not need a scapegoat. It needs a credible state.

Prabowo's strategic instinct toward food, energy and villages has substance. It needs institutional help, and a different definition of success.

1
Judge stability by declining intervention, not by new appointments
"The best signal would not be a new official. The best signal would be a rupiah that stabilizes while intervention intensity declines." That would mean the market has started to carry the currency again, and that reserves are not being used to defend pride.
2
Let each institution do its own job visibly
"Bank Indonesia must defend price stability and rupiah stability without looking politically captured. The Finance Ministry must show credible fiscal arithmetic, especially on subsidies, debt issuance and spending priorities."
3
Make food security a productivity programme, not a land programme
"Food security cannot depend only on land expansion. It needs productivity, irrigation, seeds, storage, logistics and farmer income." If it is the goal, irrigation projects must be completed and storage must function.
4
Make energy security measurable in barrels not imported
"Energy security cannot depend only on new plantations. It needs demand management, refining discipline, public transport, renewable energy, biofuel governance, subsidy honesty and ecological restraint."
5
Ask the harder questions about what can actually be produced
Which farm products can meet export standards consistently? Which fisheries products can move up the value chain? Which manufacturing sectors can reduce imported inputs? "Which ministries can finish what they announce?"

And a credible state is not built by changing faces in a storm. It is built when pressure becomes discipline, and when sovereignty is earned in factories, farms, ports, forests, budgets and institutions.
