Prosecution Case Summary: The 12-Pillar Legal Rebuttal in the Nadiem Makarim Chromebook Case

This deck sets out one side of a live trial: the prosecution's rebuttal, pillar by pillar, with the statute each pillar invokes. The purpose is not to reach a verdict but to make a white-collar case legible — because the difficulty the public has with these cases comes from the distance between the money and the act.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

White-collar crime is hard for the public to read because the money sits far from the act.

A summary of the prosecution's rebuttal (Replik) as presented at trial. Everything below is the state's argument, not a finding.

Situation

The prosecution characterizes the defendant's actions not as a series of isolated policy decisions but as a white-collar crime as defined by Edwin H. Sutherland (1939) and forensic legal scholarship (Andi Saputra, 2025).

Complication

"The defense's Pledoi attempts a strategy of "Atomization" (Atomisasi) —intentionally fragmenting a singular criminal scheme into disconnected acts to dilute their illegal nature." A reader following the case through clips and headlines meets the fragments, not the scheme.

Question

If the prosecution's case only makes sense as a single structure, what does that structure look like when it is set out in full?

Answer

Twelve pillars. Six concern who was appointed and how the specification was locked. Six rest on financial and utilisation figures. Each names the statute it invokes and the act alleged to breach it.

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

The prosecution names three strategies: fraud, layering, and image.

The state argues these three were used together to execute the scheme. The rest of the case is an attempt to show each one operating.

01
Strategy one
Fraud
"The manipulation of regulations and financial reporting to create a facade of legality." The cited example is the 2022 SPT tax discrepancy, where the defendant is alleged to have recorded IDR 4.8 trillion in assets and IDR 5.2 trillion in shares specifically as "stock sales" to evade the "end-of-year assets" classification.
02
Strategy two
Layering
"Obscuring the link between the perpetrator and illicit benefits through shell companies and nominees." The prosecution alleges Andre Soelistyo and Kevin Aluwi served as nominees maintaining the defendant's substantive control over PT AKAP (Gojek/GOTO) while he occupied public office.
03
Strategy three
Image
"Constructing a "hero" persona through media and awards to frame legal accountability as "criminalization."" The state argues this secures public sympathy and distracts from the underlying allegation.
This Is Not a Judgment

This deck summarises the prosecution's rebuttal in a trial that was ongoing. It is not a court judgment and must not be read as a finding of guilt. The presumption of innocence stands. Every allegation below is the state's contention, and the defence's answer is set out in the companion deck on the full case. Pillars I–VI

Pillars I to VI allege that the ministry's own structure was replaced to lock the specification.

The governance half of the case. Each pillar names the law the prosecution says was violated and the act alleged to breach it.

1  ·  I · Administrative versus criminal liability — Article 613(3) of Law No. 1 of 2026; Perpres No. 16 of 2018
The prosecution rejects the defence's invocation of Primum Remedium. Per the expert testimony of Dr. Ahmad Redi, the actions are said to fall under "Criminal-Based Administrative Error." "The defendant did not merely commit a procedural lapse; he exhibited mens rea by issuing the "Go Ahead with Chromebook" directive."
2  ·  II · The directing mind — Law No. 28 of 1999; principles of corporate law
Using Prof. Nindyo Pramono's Identification Theory, the prosecution identifies the defendant as the senior organ who remained the substantive controller of his corporate interests. "The "Irrevocable Power of Attorney" was a sham." Nominees are alleged to have reported and sought approval for investor valuations and corporate actions.
3  ·  III · Manipulation of personnel — Law No. 39 of 2008; Law No. 5 of 2014 (ASN)
The prosecution asserts the “job auction” was a pre-meditated tool to purge opposition and install compliant subordinates. Echelon II officials Hamid Muhammad and Poppy Dewi, who resisted the Chrome OS shift on technical grounds, were replaced by Sri Wahyuningsih and Mulyatsah, "explicitly ordered to finalize technical specifications favoring Google."
4  ·  IV · The shadow organization — Perpres No. 68 of 2019
Before his inauguration on 19 September 2019 the defendant is alleged to have planned to replace official roles with external associates, then granted them unauthorized executive authority. Communicating through private WhatsApp groups including "Mas Menteri Kortim" and "Education Council," this group is said to have acted as "The Real Minister," dictating budget and technology policy while bypassing official Echelon channels.
5  ·  V · Coercion of technical specifications — Law No. 30 of 2014; Permendikbud No. 5 of 2021
"Despite technical warnings from Pustekkom regarding the 2018-2019 Chromebook failure and the lack of internet stability in 3T (underdeveloped) regions, the defendant coerced a shift to Chrome OS." The prosecution argues the devices' internet dependence rendered them useless for the students they were purportedly for.
6  ·  VI · Improper delegation and the legal trap — Article 8(2) and Article 5(2) of Law No. 39 of 2008
The prosecution rebuts the claim that responsibility shifted to KPA/PPK. Permendikbud No. 11 of 2020, which allowed Windows, is characterised as "a "legal shield" for himself, while concurrently forcing subordinates to sign Technical Guidelines ( Juklak ) that locked in Chrome OS." The alleged effect was plausible deniability for the minister and exposure for his subordinates.

Pillars VII–XII

Pillars VII to XII rest on numbers: 0.15 percent, IDR 809.6 billion, and IDR 11 trillion recorded as IDR 72 billion.

The financial and utilisation half. These are the figures the prosecution asks the court to treat as material facts.

PILLAR A
VII · Financial anomalies and substance over form
Applying the Substance Over Form doctrine (Dr. Mei Jati), the prosecution identifies a circular transaction. "PT Gojek Indonesia, a passive company with an asset base of only IDR 100 million, was changed from PMDN to PMA status solely to receive IDR 809.6 billion from Google. The funds entered and exited in a single day, after which the company reverted to PMDN status."
PILLAR B
VIII–IX · State loss and the utilisation failure
The state alleges "a “real and definite” state loss of IDR 5.2 trillion", including redundant Chrome Device Management licences and devices valued at IDR 4 million procured for up to IDR 8 million per unit. "Of the 1,634,260 units procured, real-time dashboard data (admin.belajar.id) showed a login rate of only 0.15% ." Usage is said to have spiked only briefly for AKM assessments.
PILLAR C
X · Systematic fraud in notarization
The prosecution alleges notary acts recorded foreign investments far below actual value: December 2017, $99.9M received, recorded as IDR 17B; January 2019, $349.9M recorded as IDR 6.1B; September 2021, $167.4M recorded as IDR 9.6B. "Total Contrast: Approximately IDR 11 Trillion in actual value vs. only IDR 72 Billion recorded."
PILLAR D
XI–XII · Unexplained wealth and constitutional mandate
Under Articles 37 and 37A the burden reverses. "He testified that his wealth stems solely from GOTO, yet GOTO consistently reported massive losses." Pillar XII alleges that by "providing unusable, internet-dependent hardware to 3T regions, he created a digital divide and betrayed his mandate as the Minister of Education."

The Disagreement

The defence answers by atomising a single scheme into disconnected acts.

The whole disagreement is about whether these twelve items are one structure or twelve separate questions. That is a question about how the case should be read before it is a question about guilt.

The Defence's Reading
•A series of isolated policy decisions
•Procedural lapses handled through government oversight, not a criminal court
•Primum Remedium: administrative remedy before criminal prosecution
•Responsibility devolved to KPA/PPK once delegation occurred
The Prosecution's Reading
•A singular scheme executed through fraud, layering and image
•Criminal-Based Administrative Error, with mens rea in the directive
•Anti-Corruption Law as lex specialis over general administrative rules
•Delegation as a shield, with the specification locked before it
The Prosecution's Final Demands

Eighteen years' imprisonment; a fine of IDR 1 billion or 190 days' additional prison; compensation for state loss of IDR 809.5 billion plus IDR 4.87 trillion, or nine years' additional prison. The prosecution moves to reject the defence's Pledoi entirely.

Why This Was Written

The question is not guilt; it is why this kind of case is so hard to read.

The article's own framing, and the author's answer to a reader who asked whether the same practices are prosecuted equally elsewhere.

The point is not whether Nadiem is guilty or innocent. That remains for the court. The question is why white-collar cases are harder for the public to read than crimes close to physical money.

— The author, replying in the article's comments
Distance is the mechanism
A theft is legible because the money and the act occupy the same moment. A procurement specification, a notarial deed, a nominee arrangement and a tax classification are separated by years, institutions and documents. The reader meets each fragment alone and cannot assemble the structure the prosecution alleges.
A reader's objection worth keeping
One co-learner asked why, if directing-mind arrangements, shadow structures and power abuse are practised by others, this defendant alone faces them. "i still questioning are the law are practiced in just and equal to other cases?" The article does not answer that, and the question survives the summary.
What this deck is not
It is a summary of one party's rebuttal in an ongoing trial. It contains no assessment of whether the allegations are true, no weighing of the defence's evidence, and no prediction. Those belong to the court, and the companion article of 28 June sets out both sides.
