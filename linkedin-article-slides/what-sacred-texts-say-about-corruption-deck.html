(2 of 3) What Sacred Texts Say About Corruption—for Those Who Begin with Religion as Their Framework

For readers who insist on reading corruption through religion, scripture does not explain why believers defraud — it indicts them. But the durable restraint is not scripture at all: it is early moral formation, laid down by imitation in childhood, long before any verse is read.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Faith here is externalised — it shows in ritual and not in restraint.

Situation

"Indonesia ranks high in religiosity (Pew Research Center, 2018) and low in governance integrity (Transparency International, 2023). Worship events are full; so are corruption investigations."

Complication

"This contradiction is not because Indonesians lack faith—it is because that faith has been externalized. It appears in ritual, but not in restraint."

Question

If belief is not the missing ingredient, what does restrain corruption — and can scripture supply it?

Answer

"What restrains corruption, then, is not belief—but early moral formation."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

Four findings, carried forward from part one, explain why devotion and restraint move independently.

The instalment compresses its predecessor before adding anything new.

01
Kohlberg
Reasoning stalls mid-level
"most people stop at mid-level reasoning: they obey rules to gain approval or avoid shame. Public religiosity often reinforces this stage."
02
Rest
Knowing is not doing
"One must also care enough to do it, and possess the will to follow through. Rituals may teach what is right, but they cannot install courage."
03
Schwartz
Conformity outranks truth
"in collectivist cultures, values like conformity and security often take precedence over truth or fairness. These values stabilize groups—but suppress moral independence."
04
Benedict and Koentjaraningrat
Exposure, not the act
"In such systems, wrong is wrong only when it is seen. Exposure matters more than the act itself." "The shame lies not in the injustice—but in being caught."
What that permits

"In this cultural mix, religion can easily be repurposed. It becomes armor for reputation, not conscience. When faith fuses with authority—whether political, familial, or religious— ethical space narrows."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Evidence

Ethical reflexes are laid down by childhood exposure, not built by adult character.

This is the instalment's own contribution, and it is where the argument leaves theology for neurodevelopment.

CLAIM A
Children copy; they do not receive instruction
"Children learn through imitation, not instruction. They copy fairness, humility, and accountability when they see it modeled."
CLAIM B
The counter-lesson is learned just as fast
"If they observe adults praying but also lying, fasting but also cheating, they learn that religion and ethics can be separated."
CLAIM C
The mechanism is developmental, not moral
"Ethical reflexes are not products of adult character, but outcomes of synaptic pruning, myelination, and patterned exposure during childhood—what I have termed the neurodevelopmental scaffolding of ethical behavior (Tambun et al., 2025)."
CLAIM D
What ritual can and cannot do
"Rituals may stabilize identity and belonging, but they cannot substitute for the formation of a value-based mindset shaped through early moral conditioning."

Details

Scripture is offered as an indictment, not an explanation — and the piece says so before quoting a line.

"These verses are not offered as solutions. They are indictments. They do not answer why believers commit fraud. They answer what is violated when they do."

1  ·  Proverbs 11:1
"The Lord detests dishonest scales, but accurate weights find favor with him." Toro's gloss: "Fraud—economic or procedural—is detestable to God. Not merely wrong. Detestable."
2  ·  Micah 6:8
"Act justly, love mercy, walk humbly with your God." "Justice, mercy, humility—fraud abandons all three."
3  ·  Luke 16:10
"Whoever is dishonest with very little will also be dishonest with much." "Dishonesty is cumulative. It rarely begins in high office. It is tolerated early, then grows."
4  ·  Ephesians 4:28
"Steal no longer, but work honestly… and share." "Paul demands reversal. Not belief alone, but concrete restitution and redirection of purpose."
5  ·  Exodus 20:15–16
"You shall not steal. You shall not bear false witness." "Fraud is both theft and falsehood. It breaks two of the most basic ethical prohibitions."
6  ·  James 5:4
"The wages you failed to pay… cry out… their cries have reached the Lord Almighty." "Scripture takes the side of the defrauded."
7  ·  Luke 19:8–9
"If I have cheated… I will repay fourfold." "Zacchaeus’s repentance is not verbal. It is mathematical. Without restitution, there is no salvation."
The charge against believers in power

"When Christians in power commit fraud, it is not only an ethical breach. It is theological perjury. They invoke the name of God while contradicting the voice of God."

The Replies

Three readers answered from outside the Christian frame, and each moved the argument further from ritual.

The piece reports responses to the earlier instalment and declines to adjudicate between them.

What the readers said
•A retired Professor of Mechanical Engineering pointed to Surah At-Takathur, which opens "The rivalry for piling up [worldly gains] distracts you, until you visit the graves." and ends "Then, you shall surely be asked about the pleasures you indulged in."
•His summary: "Wrong lifestyle encourages corruption."
•A former rector argued that one who fails to follow the Qur'an's teachings cannot be called a real Muslim, since ihsan is a defining mark of faith
•A business school dean observed that Japan and Singapore hold low corruption "not through prayer but through everyday modeling of respect and fairness"
•"Banyak orang yang beragama tapi lupa ber-Tuhan."
What Toro does with them
✓He declines the question of who counts as a real believer: "But who are we to judge who qualifies as real? That is not the task of this article."
✓He narrows the charge to conduct: "What can be observed—and must be challenged—is the use of religious identity to shield unethical behavior."
✓"In that sense, the warning is not theological. It is ethical."
✓The dean's contrast becomes the mechanism: where families model shortcuts, "religion becomes cognitive, not formative"
✓The verdict follows: "Corruption committed by those who wear religious identity is not an inconsistency. It is the result of moral formation that never happened."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

Reform begins in the household, generations before it reaches a courtroom or a pulpit.

Religion has not failed. But it has been performed without reflection, recited without reasoning, and displayed without depth.

— (2 of 3) What Sacred Texts Say About Corruption
Where the lever actually sits
"The only durable reform begins far from the courtroom or the pulpit. It begins in how adults live, how children observe, how families speak about fairness, how mistakes are admitted, and how power is questioned."
On the timescale
"Culture changes slowly. Moral values take root across generations." The claim is not that this is fast, only that nothing quicker holds.
What it would produce
"if conscience is nurtured early—quietly, repeatedly, and without spectacle—then the next generation may not need to borrow ethics from ritual. They will have learned it from character." The article's closing line is a single instruction: "CHANGE ONE CHILD AT A TIME, AT HOME"
