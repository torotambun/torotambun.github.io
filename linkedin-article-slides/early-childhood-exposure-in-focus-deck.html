Early Childhood Exposure in Focus: When an Interview Becomes a Learning Journey

A CEO's five goals for a life read as private aspiration until you notice that not one of them can be secured by an individual alone. Nutrition in the womb, dignified marriage, schools that form conscience, affordable housing, a just neighbourhood — each depends on the state doing its part. If these are the objectives of every citizen, government cannot stand apart from them.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Opening

A CEO named five goals for a life, and they are not only private.

From an interview with Dr. Joko Triraharjo, CEO of PT Putra Perkasa Abadi.

Situation

He reflected on what he called "five goals of becoming a human in economy": to be a pious man (ṣāliḥ), to have a pious wife (zawjah ṣāliḥah), to raise pious children (walad ṣāliḥ), to own good vehicles (markab hani'), and to live in a good house (maskan wāsi').

Complication

He attributed this to Islamic tradition. The list is coherent, memorable, and framed entirely as personal attainment — the kind of thing a successful man says about his own life.

Question

If these are the objectives a citizen is expected to reach, can any of them actually be reached by a citizen acting alone?

Answer

"If these are understood as the objectives of every citizen, then government and authority cannot stand apart. They must strive to make these goals achievable for all, not just for a privileged few."

The Check

The hadith tradition adds a sixth he did not name: the good neighbour.

"Out of curiosity, I checked."

"The hadith tradition does speak of these themes, though not in exactly this five-part form. His version is a fusion of authentic teachings on self-piety, family, housing, and vehicles." The five-part list is his own synthesis rather than a canonical enumeration, and the article says so plainly rather than letting the attribution stand unexamined.

"In the hadith, one element often listed alongside spouse, house, and mount is the good neighbor." It is the one item the five-part version drops — and it is the only item on the list that cannot be acquired at all, because it depends entirely on other people.

"While he did not state this explicitly, he addressed it implicitly when speaking of his networks, his circles of connection, and even his political aspirations — all of which reflect the broader meaning of cultivating good neighbors."

The Cost

The children once mistook their father for one of their mother's patients.

After the five goals, the interview turned to his own family and how he raised his children. "One of them was my student."

"When I asked if his son was raised with good nutrition, he answered yes — but then he told me the cost. Because of his demanding career, he came home only every two weeks."

"Once, his young children even mistook him for one of their mother's patients." The detail is offered by the father himself, unprompted and without softening, in the middle of an account of successful parenting.

"Still, he and his wife held firmly to their parenting choices…" What follows is not a defence of the absence but a description of what was built around it.

The Record

Adab mattered more than knowledge, and childhood was deliberately protected.

The parenting choices as the interview reported them.

REPORTED A
Faith was structured, not left to chance
"His children were taught to memorize the Qur'an. Religious learning was not left to chance or to the family alone." An Ustad accompanied and guided them in Qur'an, religious practice and Muslim tradition — "This made faith a structured and consistent part of their upbringing, extending beyond the home environment."
REPORTED B
Responsibility was framed as training for adulthood
"They were also taught that every decision carried responsibility. Choices were not taken lightly; discipline was framed as accountability, empowering the children to live with the consequences of their own actions. Responsibility was treated as training for adulthood, not something to be postponed."
REPORTED C
Difference sat at the same table
"A Chinese Christian classmate named Daniel, from a lower economic background, was always welcome in their home, eating together at the same table. The children did not see him as a foreigner; he was simply part of their circle of friends." "Adab (manners, discipline, character) mattered more than raw knowledge."
REPORTED D
Childhood was protected, and play was physical
"Childhood was protected: no TV except Sundays, no mobile phones in the house." "Physical play replaced digital distractions." They joined neighbours' children in traditional games, wrestled in the field, swam in the river, ran in the rain. "By junior high, the children were sent to an Islamic boarding school (pesantren) where discipline around TV and phones was even stricter."
Who was actually present

"The mother's role was central. A general practitioner, she was the daily presence at home. The children grew very close to her, observing how she worked with patients inside their own house." And the children were not confined to it: as they grew older their father took them to project sites, client meetings and the office. "They saw both the doctor's care and the miner's business, learning from each."

The Translation

If these are every citizen's objectives, government cannot stand apart.

Each of the five goals, read as a condition the state either creates or withholds.

01
Piety and family
Formation and dignity
"To cultivate piety and moral grounding, education must combine technical knowledge with ethical formation." "To secure righteous families, men and women must be able to marry with dignity, supported by health, safety, and stable income."
02
Children
Nutrition in the womb, and schools that form conscience
"To raise righteous children, there must be nutrition in the womb, the eradication of stunting, and schools that nurture both skill and conscience." None of the three is available to a parent who wants it but cannot buy it.
03
House, vehicle, neighbour
Livelihood, infrastructure, and justice
"To provide houses and vehicles, citizens must have fair access to livelihood, affordable housing, and infrastructure free from the distortions of corruption or greed." And the good neighbour "depends on a system of justice and fairness that government is bound to uphold."
What makes the goals hollow

"Without tackling corruption, greed, injustice, and neglect of basic education and health, these goals remain hollow. A nation can rise in corporate ranking yet fail its people in the most human of measures."

Conclusion

Otherwise the five goals become dreams for the privileged few.

"When a corporate leader frames life in terms of religious and moral objectives, it forces a wider reflection: are these goals only private aspirations, or should they be seen as public responsibilities?"

Otherwise, these five goals risk becoming personal dreams for the privileged few, instead of achievable benchmarks for all citizens.

— Early Childhood Exposure in Focus: When an Interview Becomes a Learning Journey
What governance is being asked to do
"If every man and woman is expected to be pious, raise good children, and secure housing and livelihood, then the role of governance is to clear the path — through justice, fairness, and strong public institutions." Clearing the path, not walking it.
Why the interview became the argument
The five goals were offered as one man's account of a life well aimed. Read as a specification for every citizen, they turn into a list of things a state must underwrite — which is how a private conversation became a public claim.
