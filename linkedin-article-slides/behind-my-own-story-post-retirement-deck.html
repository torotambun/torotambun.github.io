Behind My Own Story: A Journey of Challenges and Reflection Post-Retirement

A dinner after a networking event turned into an account of a friend who retired at forty-eight with the career, the respect and the financial plan intact, and without anyone who noticed. What the evening establishes is that the problem was never his record; it was that meaningful work and financial sustainability had come apart, and that the smallest act of recognition was the thing worth writing down.

Dr. Toronata Tambun

Opening

The evening's real conversation began after we left the networking room.

A reunion with Adithya Teguh Wicaksono, Corporate Strategist and Market Intelligence at Schlumberger Asia, at a Welcome to Your City event run by the Harvard Alumni Association with the Harvard Club of Singapore and the HBS Club of Singapore.

We had first been introduced at the HCI Global Networking Night on 15 February 2020, a month before COVID-19 was declared in Jakarta, and connected over a shared background at SLB — I had retired, he was about to begin his master's at Harvard.

The evening was well organised and dynamic, and not the most comfortable setting for introverts. Despite having served as VP of sales for many years, I have grown more introverted over time, especially in daily life. We excused ourselves and continued over dinner at a newly renovated five-star hotel on Scotts Road, where a more intimate conversation was possible.

We talked about the newspaper pieces I had written, and about what shared values are — individual or national — and how they shape the way we navigate our lives. We explored how people make quick, automatic judgments through system 1 thinking. What I learned is that perseverance, conscientiousness and maintaining utmost credibility are embedded in his.

The Turn

The talk moved to a friend who retired at forty-eight, and I could feel it as my own.

As the night drew on, the conversation shifted to a mutual friend who, like me, retired several years ago.

"The more we talked, the more I could feel his struggles as my own." He had retired six years earlier, at only forty-eight, after a relatively successful career spanning decades.

On the surface his life seemed planned out. Global experience, widely respected, what was meant to be a sustainable financial retirement plan, and some investment income that to a degree sustains him and his family. Despite that buffer, after stepping away from the corporate world things had not unfolded as smoothly as expected.

Field Notes

Every door he knocked on had a reason that was not about his record.

Six attempts to re-enter, and what each of them returned.

1  ·  The fund that failed
He once attempted to raise a $100 million fund across four countries and failed miserably when he could not adequately answer questions such as whether this was his first fund, and — collaborating with a former colleague — whether it was the first time the two had worked together.
2  ·  The venture capital world
Attempts to break into it were met with statements of the order of "We have our own way of building pipeline," which closes the conversation without addressing anything he had brought to it.
3  ·  The private equity firm
He offered his extensive network to a lower-tier private equity firm in Singapore and was dismissed with "You are overqualified." The disqualification was the experience itself.
4  ·  The cold calls
Cold calls within each ecosystem he visited yielded no responses. He was not part of the right crowd — an oil and gas background attempting to navigate the finance sector. Investors either expected large financial contributions or favoured younger professionals with more recent relevant experience.
5  ·  The academic world
Despite numerous keynote speeches and several books, the academic community often prioritised those with published research and peer-reviewed articles over practical industry knowledge. The divide between academia and industry added to the sense of exclusion.
6  ·  The platform meant to hold the connections
LinkedIn, where he had hoped to maintain professional connections, felt inauthentic — surface-level engagement rather than meaningful interaction. He grew disillusioned with digital spaces and began considering whether his own teaching platform, or more books, might be a more genuine way to share what he knows.

The Cost

Recognition mattered to him, and admitting that was the hardest part.

The frustration was not financial, and the article is exact about what it was.

Despite the experience and the connections, he found himself feeling unnoticed, even invisible. Being neglected and overlooked was the primary source of the frustration, especially since recognition had played such a key role in his sense of fulfilment.

He also struggled to fit into a professional landscape that often thrived on a culture of corruption, nepotism and relational politics. His commitment to his principles, with an INTJ personality characterised by strategic thinking, independence and strong values, set him apart. While many adapted to the system, he stood firm, which sometimes cost him opportunities and inclusion.

"Although it's hard to admit, external recognition does matter deeply to him." The lack of acknowledgment stirred feelings of uselessness, affected motivation and happiness, and over time made him question the value of twenty-six years of global experience. He had expected recognition, respect and perhaps new opportunities to emerge naturally. They did not.

The Ledger

He simplified the life and the arithmetic still did not close.

What was let go, set against what still did not pay.

What he released
•Seldom-used luxurious cars
•Top-tier watches
•Unproductive properties
•Travel, enriching but a significant expense, to be more selective about
•Framed not as necessity but as optimising resources for what mattered
What still did not pay
•Teaching — incredibly fulfilling, generating no income, and remaining a spending activity
•Writing and helping others — deeply meaningful, financially unsustainable
•A startup venture that, if it succeeded, could make teaching viable — and had not
•Investment income providing family stability, not a livelihood for the work he wants
•A belief that acts of helping others would be reciprocated, held alongside the need for boundaries

The Discussion

The advice was not to become someone else but to make this life pay.

What the two of us arrived at over the table, offered to one person and not as a prescription.

1  ·  Make the teaching financially viable
Teaching was already the most fulfilling thing he did, which made it the best candidate — turned into something that generates income, such as consulting or a curriculum, rather than replaced by something else.
2  ·  Go where the values are already recognised
Rather than trying to fit into spaces that did not fully see his worth, he might find more fulfilment contributing in niche areas aligned with his strengths and passions.
3  ·  Put boundaries on the generosity
Helping others was central to his life and financial sustainability was equally essential. Clear boundaries on giving would let him keep supporting others without putting his own well-being at risk.
4  ·  Live within the means while the alignment is built
Budgeting wisely is critical, and so is exploring professional opportunities that align with his skills. The goal is not to change who he is but to let the passions and the financial stability coexist.

What I Carry

What I took from the evening was that someone had bought me dinner.

The article ends on the smallest thing in it.

Not so many people treat me for dinner or lunch, and Adithya’s gesture was not only kind but also meaningful.

— Behind My Own Story, 11 September 2024
The recognition the piece is about, received
An article whose central subject is being unnoticed closes by recording an act of being noticed. The gesture is small, it is named, and it is the last thing the reader is given.
The connection continuing
Adithya has also contributed to one of the author's EL4244 batches at ITB, where all the students have since graduated — one of them going on to work for Schlumberger, extending the shared background the two men started from.
What is left unresolved
The friend's situation is not solved in the article. Trust that things would work out is held alongside the need for practical financial planning, and the piece stops at the balance rather than at an outcome.
