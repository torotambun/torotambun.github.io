Criteria of the New Marketer Breed : D-A-R-C

The article says a company entering the digital marketing wave should hire a new breed of marketer, and it adopts the D-A-R-C framework, which it takes from Halligan and Shah's book on inbound marketing: hire digital citizens, hire for analytical chops, hire for web reach and hire content creators. It answers a manager's doubt about digital channels by saying the survey behind the doubt dates from the traditional marketing era and that the test is fit to the new breed, not loyalty to a school. It gives a practical test for each criterion and says marketers who resist the new information have no future in the new journey, so an executive decision must reorganize an unfit team.

Introduction

The digital marketing wave calls for a new breed of marketer, and the article adopts the D-A-R-C criteria to define it.

Situation

The article says the key for a company embarking on an unprecedented new digital marketing wave is to hire a breed of marketer who fits the functions described in the author's post of 24 February 2017. Such marketers hold a crucial role in the customer conversion process, which the article calls the ultimate objective. The criteria can define the next recruit of a CRM champion, a marketing manager, a VP marketing and a marketing team.

Complication

After seeing the author's presentation, someone titled Sales and Marketing Manager at a global oil and gas service company doubted social media, web events, blogs and interactive media as marketing channels.

Question

What defines the new marketer breed a company should hire?

Answer

Fit to the four criteria of a framework the article calls D-A-R-C, which it takes from the book Inbound Marketing by Brian Halligan and Dharmesh Shah: digital citizens, analytical chops, web reach and content creators.

Evidence note

The article marks its three sources (1), (2) and (3): a book on B2B professional sales, Running Lean, and the Halligan and Shah book. The deck reports the framework as the article credits it and did not open the book.

Gap

The data behind the manager's doubt came from the traditional marketing era, so the test is fit to the new breed and not loyalty to a school.

The article says his attitude does not show which school of thought he follows.

What the doubt rested on
✗Doubt about social media, web events, blogs and interactive media as marketing channels
✗A survey by Forrester Research conducted in the traditional marketing era, before the digital wave became so apparent
What the article says matters
✓Not whether one follows the traditional school, which believes in outbound channels: SEM, print and TV ads, trade shows and cold calling
✓Not whether one follows the digital school, which also believes in inbound channels: blogs, SEO, ebooks, white papers and webinars
✓But whether one fits the criteria of the new marketer breed or not

Framework

Four criteria define the new marketer: a digital citizen, analytical chops, web reach and content creation.

The article credits the framework to Halligan and Shah and words the four as hire digital citizens, hire for analytical chops, hire for web reach and hire content creators.

01
D
Digital citizens
The new breed must not be digital tourists who pass through and take in a site here or there. They must be committed to the internet, very comfortable on the web, and live there actively for some portion of their lives.
02
A
Analytical chops
The new breed must know what to do with big data, and must inherently be friendly with it.
03
R
Web reach
The article calls it a digital Rolodex: good inbound marketers make the most of a personal network of loose connections, such as Twitter followers and blog subscribers, and tight ones, such as LinkedIn networks, within their industries.
04
C
Content creators
They must write well, preferably at the level of a journalist and not of a technical writer of manuals. The article adds that a new marketing team benefits from an in-house video content creator.

Details

Each criterion has a practical test, from questions on blogs and networks to a pivot graph and a trial blog article.

The article gives its interview questions in the second person, so they are paraphrased here.

1  ·  Digital citizen
Ask which blogs the candidate reads and how the candidate keeps up with news. Ask whether the candidate ranks first in Google for the candidate's own name, and whether the candidate keeps a blog, a recently updated LinkedIn profile, a Twitter account and a YouTube channel, each to be shown.
2  ·  Analytical chops
Ask the candidate to make a graph from pivot data in an Excel database. Then check whether the candidate can analyze the data for patterns and lessons, and tries to draw insights, even a wrong one.
3  ·  Web reach
Ask how many subscribers the candidate's blog has and how many LinkedIn connections the candidate holds. Ask whether the candidate writes about the industry or about personal matters, takes part actively in groups, and joins online professional communities of marketing or of the industry.
4  ·  Content creators
Ask the candidate to write a blog article, and see how many links it attracts, how many views it gets and how many comments it receives relative to the company's other blog content.
Evidence note

The article gives no result from applying these tests and no threshold for any of them.

Implications

Marketers who resist the new information have no future in the new journey, so an executive decision must reorganize an unfit team.

1
Define the next recruit by the four criteria
The article says the criteria can define the next recruit of a CRM champion, a marketing manager, a VP marketing and a marketing team.
2
Show existing marketers the new information and read the reaction
If they react positively to the new information and opportunity, the article says they can fit the new journey. If they are resistant and defensive and sit back and take it easy, it says there is no future for them in it.
3
Reorganize the unfit team by executive decision
The article ties the decision to optimizing the customer cost of acquisition. For marketers who show none or a minimum of the digital-citizen behaviours, it says to replace them.

In this time of crisis, in order to optimize the Customer Cost of Acquisition, an executive decision must be taken to reorganize unfit marketing team.
