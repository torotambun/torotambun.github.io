LinkedIn Article  ·  Entrepreneurship · Free Cash Flow and the Unicorn Measure  ·  9 October 2019

It is time to tell startups to focus on: free cash flow. For the country : Stop using number of unicorns as measure for entrepreneurial success.

The article urges startups to focus from day one on creating value and free cash flow rather than on reaching a valuation of one billion, and says the valuation follows. It holds that a country which uses the number of unicorns as its measure of entrepreneurial success may lack basic knowledge of the ecosystem, and it lists ten foundational-institution measures to meet instead. A note added on 3 November 2019 cites a piece on Airbnb and WeWork.

Introduction

Valuation excitement pulls startups away from creating value, so free cash flow is the measure to return to.

Situation

The author tells audiences not to be owned by valuation excitement and its euphoria. Startups are to focus on adding and creating value in the product, service or offering, and the article says this focus should be aimed at from day 1.

Complication

The alternative the article warns against is being merely busy with reaching the 1B valuation mark. It says "Being entrepreneurs is not about making money or valuation." The calling it describes is creating value for society, leaving a legacy and providing good jobs to people and the earth, and it says startups should not be drawn into a bubble or a quick-rich scheme.

Question

What should startups measure instead of valuation, and what should a country measure instead of unicorns?

Answer

For startups, free cash flow: the article says that if the right thing is done right the first time, creating free cash flow, the valuation will follow. For a country, the number of unicorns should stop serving as the measure, and the ten foundational-institution criteria should be met.

Framework

Free cash flow is the purest measure of a business, so startups create it and let the valuation follow.

The article rests the measure on Prof. Mihir Desai of Harvard Business School, who wrote in May on the Uber IPO and later on WeWork, and who taught free cash flow as the purest measure of business and a basis for valuation.

The valuation chase
✗Aiming at the 1B mark as the goal
✗Being busy with valuation instead of adding value to the product, service or offering
✗Treating entrepreneurship as a way of making money
✗Joining a bubble or a quick-rich scheme
The value-creation path
✓Adding and creating value from day 1
✓Revenue, profit and free cash flow as what counts at the end of the day
✓Free cash flow as the purest measure of business and a basis for valuation
✓Value to society, a legacy, and good jobs for people and the earth
Evidence note

The article names Desai's Uber piece by title and refers to a later WeWork piece with no title or outlet. It gives no figures from either, so the support is his authority and the measure he taught.

Details

Counting unicorns may show that a country lacks basic knowledge of its ecosystem, whose foundation is ten institutional measures.

The article says that using the number of unicorns to show a country's innovation or entrepreneurial success may confirm a lack of basic knowledge of the ecosystem itself. It points to an earlier post, Innovation Entrepreneur Ecosystem of a Country, and asks for these ten criteria to be met "with flying colors". A passage quoted after a second citation calls the billion-dollar mark arbitrary: "that $1 billion mark? It's arbitrary", an indicator that a company has raised a lot of money and that investors believe in it.

1  ·  Ease of doing business
Composite country ranking from the World Bank across 10 topics relevant to ease of operating private-sector firms.
2  ·  Starting a business
Ranking of the simplicity of starting a new business, incorporating and registering a new firm.
3  ·  Paying taxes
Ranking of tax rates and the administrative burden of paying tax for typical medium-size firms.
4  ·  Resolving insolvency
Ranking of the weaknesses in insolvency law and the main bottlenecks in the process.
5  ·  Enforcing contracts
Ranking of the time and cost of resolving a commercial dispute, including the degree of good practice in the court system.
6  ·  Property rights
Score across the strength of laws that let individuals accumulate five types of property rights, including IPRs.
7  ·  Government integrity
Score capturing levels of trust, transparency and absence of corruption.
8  ·  Labor freedom
Score capturing the flexibility and efficiency of the labor market, including hindrance to hiring.
9  ·  Trade freedom
Score capturing tariff and non-tariff barriers to imports and exports.
10  ·  Corruption Perceptions Index
Overall ranking of countries by composite level of perceived corruption. A high ranking implies high corruption.

The passage on the billion-dollar mark is quoted in the article without naming its speaker, and the article gives no scores for any country on the ten criteria.

Implications

Startups create free cash flow from day one, and the country stops counting unicorns and meets the ten criteria.

The article prescribes for two audiences: the startup founder and the country.

1
Startups: put value creation ahead of valuation
Add and create value in the product, service or offering from day 1, instead of being busy with reaching the 1B mark.
2
Startups: build free cash flow
With valuation set aside, what counts is revenue, profit and free cash flow, and the article says the valuation follows from them.
3
The country: stop using unicorn counts
The number of unicorns should not be used to show a country's innovation or entrepreneurial success.
4
The country: build the foundational institutions
Build the foundational institutions correctly, meeting the ten criteria "with flying colors".

Do the right thing right in the first time : creating free cash flow and the valuation will follow !
