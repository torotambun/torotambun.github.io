"Why Are You Still Looking Up?" – A Midrashic Homiletic on the Ascension of Christ for Vocational Witness

Read through the older patterns it echoes — Elijah's mantle, Moses in the cloud, Daniel's enthronement, the psalms of ascent, and a grave no one can find — the Ascension is not departure but transference. Authority moved; the Spirit was distributed rather than located. Which relocates the question from where Jesus is to who reigns in the room where the decisions get made.

Opening

The angel does not comfort; he prods.

"Men of Galilee, why do you stand here looking into the sky?" (Acts 1:11)

Situation

"The disciples had just witnessed the resurrection, received 40 days of instruction from Jesus, and now—He's gone. Not in death, but in ascension. A lifting. A transfer."

Complication

"This is not just a question. It is a confrontation. A disruption." "And the angel does not comfort. He prods."

Question

"Today is Ascension Day. Most calendars ignore it. Many churches skip it." What does a day the calendar skips have to say to people in the marketplace?

Answer

"But for those in the marketplace—leaders, founders, professionals—this day holds keys to understanding spiritual authority, vocational purpose, and visible absence as a form of divine presence." "To understand that, we must read the Ascension like the early church did: through midrash. Not as an isolated event, but as an echo of older patterns."

The Patterns

Five older patterns read the Ascension as transference, not departure.

Each is a midrashic template — an earlier lifting that gives the later one its grammar.

1  ·  Elijah and the mantle (2 Kings 2)
"Elijah, the prophet of fire, is taken up by whirlwind. Elisha, his successor, stands watching. There's no memo. No strategy deck. Just a falling mantle." Elisha asks not for position but for a double portion of spirit. "Succession in the Kingdom is not appointment—it is impartation. One must see rightly to receive rightly. The Ascension is not loss. It is transference."
2  ·  Moses and the cloud (Exodus 24)
"Moses ascends Mount Sinai. He enters the cloud. No one sees what happens inside. But when he comes down, his face shines." "In midrash, the cloud is not concealment. It is concentration. A place of convergence between heaven and earth. The absence of clarity is the presence of intimacy."
3  ·  Daniel's vision (Daniel 7)
"I saw one like a Son of Man, coming with the clouds of heaven… and he was given authority, glory, and sovereign power..." "Early Christians saw the Ascension as the actualization of this moment. Jesus does not vanish. He is presented to the Ancient of Days." "This is not escape. This is enthronement."
4  ·  The psalms of ascension
Psalm 24, Psalm 47, Psalm 110 — "These are enthronement psalms. Used in coronations. Liturgically read during Ascension." "Ascension is not just about Jesus leaving earth. It is about earth acknowledging a new economy of power. The real question is not: “Did He go?” but: “Did we lift up the gates?”"
5  ·  Moses on Mount Nebo (Deuteronomy 34)
He sees the land but does not enter, and God buries him alone and secretly. "To this day, no one knows where his grave is" (Deut. 34:6). "There is no funeral. No monument. No farewell tour." "In midrash, this is not loss—it is protection."

The Spirit

Located, He was in one place; ascended, He is distributed.

"Jesus says: “It is better for you that I go” (John 16:7). That line still stuns. Better?"

"Yes. Because when He was present physically, He was located. But in ascension, He becomes distributed. Through the Spirit, Jesus becomes present wherever believers carry His name and purpose."

"In Jewish thought, the Spirit (Ruach) is not passive. It animates. It builds. It empowers. The Spirit does not merely console—it constructs."

The mantle changes form but not function. "The mantle is now internal. Fire falls not on a prophet, but on a body—the Church." And the consequence for the worker is immediate: "Stop waiting for “anointing” to hit in a service. Look at your product strategy. Your hiring policy. Your public tone."

What the cloud means for someone in it

"Leadership in faith often means disappearing from visibility, not from responsibility." "Marketplace believers often feel they're “in the cloud”—not of glory, but of confusion. But midrash teaches: the cloud is where God meets the faithful. It is not abandonment. It is assignment."

The Gates

The gates that need lifting are in the boardroom.

"For a business leader, this is pivotal."

"Every project, every decision, every boardroom has gates. Places of access. Who is allowed in? Who rules here?"

"Ascension is a liturgical re-alignment of decision-making." This domain — finance, law, medicine — "is not spiritually neutral. It must ask: who reigns here?"

And it changes the question being asked. "For the marketplace, that changes the question from: “Where is Jesus now?” To: “Who holds the board seat of heaven?”" "To act in one's field—law, media, finance, education—with delegated dominion is not arrogance. It is accurate theology."

Conclusion

The Ascension is not closure; it is commission without supervision.

"So why are you still standing here—looking up? Elijah is gone. Moses is buried. Jesus is lifted. Not so the church can admire—but so it can inherit."

You already have the mantle. You already stand at your Nebo. You already lead from your cloud.

What Nebo teaches about legacy
"Leadership is not measured by how long you stay—but how well you prepare others to continue. Moses fades. Joshua leads. The mission goes on." "You may be invisible—but you are not irrelevant. The greatest legacy is often the one that leaves no statue, but changes generations."
What has to stop
"Marketplace believers must stop waiting for permission. Stop looking for signs. Stop outsourcing conviction."
The economy of Ascension
"Authority has transferred. The Spirit has landed. The throne is occupied." "Now, the boardroom must look different. Now, your hiring decisions must signal a new kingdom. Now, your speech, pricing, contracts, policies—must prove who reigns."
