For Startups : High Priority Should be given in Assembling Founding Team

The article grants that an entrepreneur needs great discipline, hence the steps of Disciplined Entrepreneurship taught by Prof Bill Aulet, but argues that the number one skill an aspiring founder must prioritize is founding team assembly: choosing co-founders, splitting equity, recruiting advisors and managing a board. Drawing on a class by Prof Matt Marx of MIT Sloan and on two chief executives at the MIT Sloan Executive Education EDP 2018, it says co-founders' skills and networks must complement each other while their objectives must be similar, and that the objective cannot be observed. It says the framework's steps will succeed provided three takeaways are covered: paying customers, cohesive founders and the innovator trait, and it closes by urging readers to connect to find a good co-founder.

Introduction

The Disciplined Entrepreneurship steps demand discipline, but the number one skill for an aspiring founder is assembling the founding team.

Situation

The article grants that an entrepreneur needs to be very disciplined, hence the 24 steps of Disciplined Entrepreneurship taught by Prof Bill Aulet.

Complication

The author describes a eureka moment that grew as he became more and more convinced: the number one skill an aspiring founder must prioritize is founding team assembly, which he lists as choosing co-founders, splitting equity, recruiting advisors and managing a board.

Question

Which skill should an aspiring founder prioritize first?

Answer

Founding team assembly. The article says Thomas R. Eisenmann, Rob Howe and Beth Altringer articulate this in an article titled What Does an Aspiring Founder Need to Know?

Evidence note

The article prints the framework as 24 steps and also says a product is finalized after 23 steps. The deck reports both as printed and does not reconcile them.

Framework

Co-founders' skills and networks should complement each other while their objectives should be similar, and the objective is the one that cannot be observed.

The article gives this as the observation Prof Matt Marx of MIT Sloan outlined in his class Dilemmas in Founding New Ventures, described as a full semester in 80 minutes.

What should complement, and can be observed
✓The skills of the founders must complement each other
✓Skills are easily observed
✓The networks of the founders must complement each other
✓Networks are also rather easy to observe if they are probed
What should be similar, and cannot
✗The objective must be similar among the founders
✗The raison d'être of a co-founder is not observable
✗Determining it takes a more systematic and lengthy approach
✗The article calls that very okay, considering the degree of importance
Evidence note

The article does not make clear whether the sentences on what can be observed are Marx's or the author's. It says Marx gave examples from Smartix, Segway, Wily Technology and Zipcar.com that could have conducted the founding team assembly stage better, in a garbled sentence, and it develops none of the four.

Evidence

Two chief executives at the MIT EDP 2018 stressed finding a good co-founder, and cohesive founders became one of the course's three takeaways.

The article reports two of the author's LinkedIn posts, dated 7 and 8 February 2018, and does not reproduce either.

REPORTED A
Brian Halligan, CEO of HubSpot
In one class at the MIT Sloan Executive Education EDP 2018, he specifically emphasized the importance of finding a good co-founder, as the 7 February post quoted him.
REPORTED B
Jeff Vagnan, CEO of Accomplice
In a separate class at the same programme, he emphasized the same point. The article glosses good as complementing each other.
REPORTED C
The three takeaways
The 8 February post named the cohesiveness of the team of founders as one of the three takeaways of the course. The other two are the existence of paying customers and the innovator trait.
REPORTED D
The innovator trait
The article credits Shana Lebowitz with a piece on five key skills that separate innovators from imitators, and says the trait shows in networking properly and being genuine. It points to Prof Hal Gregersen for further learning on networking.
Evidence note

The two posts were not opened, and the article gives no words from Halligan or Vagnan. It numbers the other two takeaways 1 and 3 and gives cohesiveness no number.

Implications

The framework's steps will succeed provided the three takeaways are covered, and the article closes by urging readers to connect to find a good co-founder.

1
Cover the three takeaways
Paying customers, cohesiveness among founders as group dynamics, and innovative traits, meaning being genuine, attentive and inquisitive. The article says all of the framework's steps will take place successfully provided these are covered.
2
Take the framework's iterations diligently
The article says a product is only finalized after the steps have been taken into account diligently, because it requires many iterations: during the PMR, in determining the total addressable market and the beachhead, in multiple visits to determine the Decision Making Unit, and in long hours of discussion to determine the Core, the Competence and the MVP.
3
Connect to find a good co-founder
The article ends on an invitation to make connections, addressed to readers who have not yet found a co-founder.

So, let's make connection to find good co-founder
