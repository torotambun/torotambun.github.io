Checklist of a star-performer Sales Manager

The article portrays a star-performer sales manager, in executing monitoring, directing, evaluation and rewarding functions, as five roles adopted from The CIM Handbook of Selling and Sales Strategy: company leader, customer champion, process guru, organization architect and course corrector. It sets this beside the author's earlier portrait of the star-performer sales person, which it says can be described differently using the same book, through four traits. It says both portraits emphasize physical interaction with people, the clients, instead of relying merely on sales activity behind a desk.

Introduction

The article portrays the star-performer sales manager as a checklist adopted from a sales handbook, and sets it beside its earlier portrait of the star-performer sales person.

Situation

The article portrays a star-performer sales manager in executing monitoring, directing, evaluation and rewarding functions. The checklist is adopted from The CIM Handbook of Selling and Sales Strategy, edited by David Jobber.

Complication

The manager and the sales person are portrayed in two separate lists, the second from an earlier article of the author's that the handbook can describe differently, and the article then compares them.

Question

What does a star-performer sales manager look like, and how does that portrait relate to the star-performer sales person?

Answer

The manager fills five roles, the sales person shows four traits, and the article says both emphasize physical interaction with clients and not sales activity behind a desk.

Evidence note

The article states no problem the checklist answers. The complication above is the deck's description of how the article sets two lists side by side, and is not a claim the article makes.

Framework

A star-performer sales manager is a company leader, a customer champion, a process guru, an organization architect and a course corrector.

The article gives the five roles as a numbered list and says they portray the manager in executing monitoring, directing, evaluation and rewarding functions.

1  ·  A company leader
Focused on revenue generation and on building the sales troops.
2  ·  A customer champion
A trusted advisor, described in the article as a lighthouse. Involved in product, pricing, place and promotion activity, and able to take a good role as Customer Service Officer.
3  ·  A process guru
Goes through a stack of proposals from a consultant or sales training company to improve the database, CRM tools, KPI definition and process mapping. The article prints 20% in this sentence, but the sentence is incomplete and does not say what the 20% measures.
4  ·  An organization architect
Must ensure that the organization has the right size to meet the corporate strategic goal.
5  ·  A course corrector
Reminds the team not to miss the annual revenue and the goal margin, and always calibrates with short-cycle data and analysis. Hence invests in people, the CRM tool, pipeline management, forecasting and quota allocation, and leads with a strong hand to organize the sales team by segment and by channel and to make the right sales process available.

Details

The star-performer sales person in the author's 22 February article can be described through the same book as having four traits.

The article says its earlier portrait can be described differently using the book referred to here, and it lists four traits.

01
Trait
High motivation
A sense of accomplishment and a sense of personal growth and development.
02
Trait
Customer orientation
They are very customer oriented.
03
Trait
Team orientation
A super team orientation: not a lone wolf or a road warrior, but always cooperative and willing to accept direction and review from a manager or higher authority.
04
Trait
Opportunity management
They commit to opportunity management, such as the sales plan and CRM. They spend a substantial amount of time planning sales calls, are effective in non-selling activities, and follow up well on sales support activities.
Evidence note

The article refers to its article of 22 February. The star-performer piece in the archive is dated 2017-02-23, and the deck records the mismatch without repairing it. The article does not say whether the four traits come from the same book.

Conclusion

Both portraits emphasize physical interaction with clients and not sales activity behind a desk.

The article draws one comparison between the two lists.

Both emphasize on the PEOPLE (client) physical interaction, instead of relying merely on sitting behind the desk sales activities.

— Checklist of a star-performer Sales Manager, 24 February 2017
The manager
Five roles: company leader, customer champion, process guru, organization architect and course corrector.
The sales person
Four traits: high motivation, customer orientation, team orientation and opportunity management.
What the article does not give
No study, sample or measure for either list, no page reference to the handbook, and no role-by-role match between the manager's list and the sales person's.
