The Technology Machine: The Future Arrived, but Where Are Indonesia’s Technology Entrepreneurs?

The intelligent manufacturing Moody and Morley foresaw for 2020 has arrived as equipment that Indonesia can buy. Inventing and commercialising that equipment requires enterprises willing to bear years of uncertainty, and a cultural preference for secure employment and quick returns limits how many such enterprises form.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Indonesia can buy the machines Moody and Morley predicted, but buying machinery is not the capability to invent it.

"Moody and Morley anticipated intelligent manufacturing. Industrial experience and doctoral research explain why importing machines is easier than producing enterprises that invent them."

Situation

In 1999, The Technology Machine described how manufacturing would work in 2020. "The predicted year has passed. Industrial robots are now deployed in the millions".

Complication

"the ability to purchase sophisticated machinery is not the same as the ability to invent and manufacture it."

Question

"why technological opportunities and entrepreneurship programs do not reliably produce enterprises that create industrial technology."

Answer

Experience in oil and gas, followed by research into entrepreneurial formation, "suggests that the answer extends beyond machines, capital and engineering education."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

At Schlumberger, building industrial technology for paying customers was the business, not a purchase from suppliers.

"Schlumberger’s priorities were people, technology and profit."

Between 1994 and 1999 the work spanned well-logging operations and assignments associated with the seismic division, facilities and field engineering. The company "engineered equipment and systems rather than simply buying finished technologies from suppliers." It also acquired companies and developed capabilities within them.

"I do not retrospectively classify every system as artificial intelligence. What mattered was the company’s ability to build and improve industrial technology for paying customers." Current work on AI and large language model modules meets the same test: "a functioning demonstration is not sufficient."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

Moody and Morley placed the next manufacturing advantage in the connections across an enterprise, not in the machines inside one factory.

Lowell’s point, as the authors present it, "was organizational as much as mechanical."

01
Origin
Lowell’s coordinated factory
Francis Cabot Lowell brought dispersed textile processes into one factory. The next transition would reorganise connections across design, procurement, logistics, production and customer fulfilment.
02
Model
The Island of Excellence
"not simply a factory filled with advanced machines. It was a model of enterprise capability." Shared competence supports coordination, yet conformity "can also discourage dissent and experimentation."
03
Analogy
The multilayer circuit board
"Sophisticated components are insufficient when the connections between them fail." A firm may own excellent machinery while suffering from inaccurate data or poorly coordinated suppliers.
What the book does not prove

"A programmable logic controller executes industrial control instructions and need not be an AI system." Only Chapter 1 was closely analysed; "The other chapters’ full arguments have not been independently inspected for this paper".

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Evidence

China installed 59% of the world’s new industrial robots in 2025, and its own suppliers built about 195,000 of them.

The figures come from the International Federation of Robotics and separate robot adoption from domestic supply.

What the numbers do not measure
"They do not measure the number of original inventions, the profitability of robot manufacturers or the extent of embodied AI."
Embodied AI raises the bar

"A laboratory demonstration may show technical feasibility without establishing a commercially viable product."

Gap

Innovation multiplies invention by commercialisation, so selling imported robots does not establish the capability to make them.

Aulet (2013) adapted Edward B. Roberts: Innovation = Invention × Commercialization. "The equation is a conceptual heuristic, not a measured mathematical relationship."

Enterprises that use technology
✓"Small firms provide employment, goods and services."
✓Trading and brokerage "can serve legitimate economic functions."
✓"selling imported robots does not, by itself, establish the capability to invent and manufacture them."
✓"Size alone does not determine whether a firm is innovative."
Enterprises that create it
✗"Innovation-driven firms may require specialized engineering, investment and years of experimentation."
✗A working prototype shows feasibility; commercialisation needs paying customers, reliable production and continued technical support
✗Must sustain both technological development and commercialisation
✗Need "a willingness to tolerate uncertain financial returns over extended periods."
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

The dissertation traces the shortage to a preference for secure employment and short-horizon returns that displaces long invention.

The Dynamic Feedback Theory of Entrepreneurial Formation in Loss-Averse, Short-Horizon Cultures (Tambun, 2026).

"The preference for stable employment is a central cultural indicator in this account." An engineering graduate may choose a secure position over an uncertain venture, and "A founder may withdraw from a startup when a safer employment opportunity appears."

Trading and brokerage serve legitimate functions. "The problem arises when the search for immediate returns repeatedly displaces the willingness to invest in uncertain, prolonged invention and commercialization."

Status of the claim

"This is the theoretical claim advanced by the dissertation, not a measured estimate of how many Indonesians hold any particular attitude." The model "does not claim that Indonesian technology entrepreneurs cannot succeed."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Implications

Universities and corporations can supply facilities and graduates, but they cannot substitute for entrepreneurs who persist after funding fades.

"the presence of ecosystem institutions does not guarantee that their expected feedback relationships become active" (Tambun & Aldianto, 2025).

"A university may produce an invention without identifying a paying customer. A corporation may offer support but withdraw it when development takes longer than expected. A graduate may leave a venture for secure employment."

Corporations can contribute engineering facilities, specialists, customer access and long-term resources, and universities can provide research and graduates. "But these institutions must support entrepreneurs who continue testing and commercializing technology after the initial enthusiasm and funding have faded."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

The technology machine can be imported as equipment, while the capacity to build the next one must be formed in enterprises.

"Purchasing machines, establishing laboratories and expanding entrepreneurship programs do not automatically change the cultural preference for secure employment or the demand for quick returns."

The future described in The Technology Machine can be imported as equipment. The capacity to create the next generation of machines must be developed through enterprises willing to bear technological uncertainty.

— Dr. Toronata Tambun, The Technology Machine
