Beyond Presentations: A Reflection on Doctoral Learning

Student presentations have real value for preparation and explanation, but in the observed format they extended coverage without testing understanding. Doctoral learning needs claims to be questioned, compared with original literature and connected to each candidate’s research, and generative AI makes it harder to know from a polished presentation whether that understanding exists.

Dr. Toronata Tambun
Director, Yayasan Mens et Manus
© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Introduction

Four doctoral sessions built on student presentations prompted a reconsideration of what a presentation can show about theoretical understanding.

Doctoral study attempted in three disciplines at three universities shaped one educator’s preferences; four classroom sessions tested them.

Situation

In a professionally oriented doctoral program attended mainly by students with industry experience, assigned PowerPoint presentations were the principal classroom activity.

Complication

Preferences formed elsewhere favour early engagement with research questions, while the observed method had a credible rationale of its own.

Question

What can presentations contribute to doctoral education, and "what further evidence is needed to establish theoretical understanding and independent research capability"?

Answer

"The benefits of preparation and public explanation are worth retaining. My question concerns the intellectual work expected after the explanation has been delivered."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Field Notes

In the observed sessions, presentations followed presentations, and Indonesian and international theories were never required to meet.

No conventional lectures or case-method discussions were observed in those sessions.

"The discussants sometimes delivered another prepared presentation instead of examining the first student's claims. Indonesian and international perspectives were introduced separately, without a required critical comparison." Few students asked questions when invited.

"There were also moments of inquiry." Some students drew on professional experience to question theories, "However, I did not observe sustained examination of those questions or an explicit requirement to connect assigned topics with each student's proposed doctoral research."

What four sessions cannot show

"I did not review the assessment rubric, midterm or final examinations, supervision arrangements, or work outside the four sessions. Consequently, these observations cannot establish the effectiveness of the course as a whole or what individual students learned elsewhere."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Framework

Preparing a presentation has genuine learning value, though the evidence shows a small, contested benefit that accrues mainly to the presenter.

The rationale given: Indonesian managers regularly communicate through presentations, so students should practise. "That explanation made me reconsider my reaction."

Ribosa and Duran (2022) reviewed 23 articles and 62 comparisons and reported a small overall benefit from generating teaching materials for others, varying with materials and comparison. Kobayashi (2023) questioned that meta-analysis’s definitions and comparisons. "Neither paper establishes that every slide presentation improves understanding or that the audience learns as much as the presenter."

Exposure to Indonesian and international perspectives is also worthwhile. "Yet the place where a theory originates is not a test of its validity. Its assumptions, evidence and explanatory limits provide the basis for comparison."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Gap

A discussant who adds another presentation extends coverage, while one who tests the first claim tests understanding.

Chi and Wylie’s (2014) ICAP framework examines what learners do with information: passive, active, constructive or interactive engagement.

Extends coverage
✗An additional presentation that "could provide useful material without examining the argument already presented"
✗Frameworks presented "in geographical categories"
✗Listeners whose learning goes unassessed
Tests understanding
✓A discussant who tests "a claim's assumptions, identify contradictory evidence or ask why an alternative theory might explain the same observation"
✓A comparison of where each theory works, the strength of its support and what professional observation challenges
✓Assessment of whether all students "can explain relevant theories, evaluate claims against sources and apply their reasoning to a new research problem."
Restraint on the silence

"Independent study could have supplied substantial learning outside class."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Details

A professional observation becomes a research gap only through critical engagement with original theory, which concise summaries cannot replace.

"But a professional observation does not itself establish a research gap."

Students "need to read the relevant original works and understand their assumptions, evidentiary bases and limits. Artificially concise summaries, whether prepared by humans or AI, cannot replace this disciplinary reading." Brodin and Frick (2011) frame doctoral scholarship as critical creativity, going beyond accurate repetition.

A developing proposal should align problem, questions, objectives, literature and method, and its gap "is provisional." Banerjee and Morley (2013) connect professional doctorates with practice, "but a DBA should not be treated as identical to a research PhD."

A preference, not a rule

Engagement with research questions began at matriculation in the program completed, which "does not establish that every doctoral curriculum must use the same sequence."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Implications

Lectures, presentations and research-led inquiry each serve doctoral learning, and each needs evidence of what students can independently defend.

"a familiar method is not necessarily the best choice for every educational objective." Methods should be compared by the work they ask students to perform.

01
Organise
Conventional lectures
Can organise unfamiliar theory and correct misconceptions. "But receiving an explanation does not necessarily demonstrate the capacity to challenge it."
02
Articulate
Student presentations
Demand preparation; become demanding "when its claims are questioned, compared with primary literature and defended against alternatives."
03
Investigate
Research-led inquiry
Connects coursework with developing questions; early novelty claims may be premature, so feedback must come at meaningful stages.
Generative AI

"a polished presentation does not establish whether the student has independently understood the underlying knowledge." Guo et al. (2026) found benefits for selected skills but inconsistent evidence on deeper dispositions; "Those findings do not establish how the observed doctoral cohort used AI."

© 2026 Dr. Toronata Tambun
Conclusion

The unresolved question is what evidence would show doctoral students progressing toward independent theoretical reasoning.

"The four observed sessions showed educational merits that this preference should not dismiss." "I cannot answer that question from classroom observation alone."

How can different teaching methods support independent theoretical reasoning and research capability, and what evidence would show that doctoral students are making progress?

— Dr. Toronata Tambun, Beyond Presentations
When understanding becomes assessable
"when a candidate can defend an interpretation, address an objection or revise an argument in response to evidence without relying on a prepared summary."
On the Harvard case method
Case teaching can be designed to develop theory, yet a decision-focused case is no substitute for systematic inquiry. The research does not show Harvard-style cases to be categorically inappropriate for PhD education.
